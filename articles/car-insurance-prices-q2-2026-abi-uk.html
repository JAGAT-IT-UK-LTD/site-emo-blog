The Average Car Insurance Premium Is £566. Your Renewal Says £900. Both Numbers Are True, And Here Is Why.

Ruhul Amin · Published 4 September 2026 · 6 min read · Figures checked September 2026

Every quarter the Association of British Insurers publishes the only UK tracker built on what drivers actually paid, not what they were quoted. The latest, for April to June 2026, says the average premium was £566 and has barely moved in a year. Anyone whose renewal letter says something very different is not being lied to by either side. Here is what the number is, what sits behind it, and what to do with your own figure.

The short version

  • Average premium paid in Q2 2026: £566, up £6 (1%) on the quarter. Adjusted for inflation it is £14 lower than Q2 2025. Source: ABI Motor Insurance Premium Tracker, published 29 July 2026.
  • Claims paid hit a record £3.2 billion in the quarter, 5% up on Q1 and 7% up on a year earlier. The average claim payout rose 4% to £4,900; the average windscreen repair rose 7% to £283.
  • The tracker is built on 28 million policies a year and on price paid, not price quoted. Quoted averages from comparison sites run higher, and that is the source of most of the confusion.
  • The average hides a spread. Age, postcode, car, claims history, mileage and how you pay all move an individual premium by hundreds of pounds either way. £566 is a midpoint, not a promise.
  • Since 1 January 2022 an insurer cannot quote a renewing customer more than it would quote a new customer for the same policy. That FCA rule stopped loyalty penalties; it did not stop premiums rising with claims costs.
  • Nothing here is a product recommendation. The useful action is to compare your renewal against last year's price and against two fresh quotes, not against a national average.

1. What the £566 is, and what it is not

The ABI's Motor Insurance Premium Tracker collects the price actually paid on more than 28 million private car policies a year. The ABI is explicit that this is what makes it different: it is "the only collection that is based on the price customers pay for their cover rather than what they are quoted (which typically delivers higher averages)". A comparison site's average is the average of quotes shown, including the ones nobody bought.

For the second quarter of 2026, April to June, the average premium paid was £566. That is £6, or 1%, higher than the first quarter. Adjusted for inflation the ABI puts it £14 below the same quarter of 2025. In cash terms, the market has been roughly flat for a year after the sharp rises of 2023 and 2024.

So the £566 is a real, paid-for figure. It is also an average across every driver in the country: a 55-year-old with a ten-year-old hatchback in a rural postcode and a 19-year-old with a hot hatch in a city are both in it.

2. What is behind it: a record quarter for claims

The same release explains why premiums are not falling faster. Insurers paid out a record £3.2 billion in motor claims in the quarter, 5% more than the previous quarter and 7% more than a year earlier. The average claim payout rose 4% to £4,900. The ABI attributes that to repair costs: modern cars carry cameras, sensors and driver assistance systems that make a minor collision an expensive repair. The average windscreen repair alone rose 7% in the quarter to £283, because a windscreen is now where the cameras live. That average is also the reason small damage is better kept off the policy: for wheel and suspension damage from a road defect, claiming against the council instead protects both the excess and the no-claims discount. The MOT is the other annual car bill where the headline figure is not what most people pay, and the maximum a garage is allowed to charge for an MOT is fixed in law even though plenty charge less.

The ABI's Chris Bose points to the Motor Insurance Taskforce, whose report in December 2025 set out recommendations for government and industry, and to the industry's funded partnership with the National Vehicle Crime Intelligence Service on vehicle crime and fraud. The honest reading of the release is that insurers are holding premiums flat while claims costs rise, and that the room for cuts is limited unless repair costs, theft and fraud come down.

3. Why your renewal is not £566

A national average tells you the direction of the market. It does not tell you your price. The main reasons an individual premium sits far from £566:

  • Age and experience. Young and newly qualified drivers pay multiples of the average; drivers in their fifties and sixties with long no-claims histories pay well under it.
  • Postcode. Theft and claims rates vary sharply by area, and insurers price by it.
  • The car. Repair cost, theft risk and insurance group. The same technology that pushed the average payout to £4,900 pushes the premium on cars that carry it.
  • Claims and convictions. A fault claim in the last few years moves the price a long way.
  • How you pay. Monthly instalments carry interest, which adds to the annual total; compare the total of twelve payments with the single annual price, not the monthly figure with the average.
  • Mileage, use and extras. Business use, high mileage, courtesy car and legal cover all add.

One thing that no longer explains a high renewal is loyalty. Since 1 January 2022 the FCA's pricing rules have required that a renewing customer is not quoted more than the insurer would quote a new customer for the equivalent policy through the same channel. That ended the practice of quietly raising prices on people who did not shop around. It did not, and could not, stop everyone's price rising with claims costs, which is why renewals still went up through 2023 and 2024.

4. A worked example in Wolverhampton

Priya in Wolverhampton is 31, drives a five-year-old Nissan Juke, has four years' no-claims bonus and no convictions. Her renewal letter quotes £742 a year, up from £698 last year, a 6.3% rise. Against the £566 headline it looks like she is being overcharged.

The right comparison is different. First, against her own last year: 6.3% against a market that the ABI says rose about 1% in the quarter and fell slightly in real terms over the year. That is worth a question. Second, against fresh quotes for identical cover: she runs two, and gets £655 and £690 for the same excess and extras. Third, against the FCA rule: her insurer must not be quoting her more than it would a new customer, so she asks her current insurer directly what its new customer price would be for her policy. It comes back at £719.

On our arithmetic, switching to the £655 quote saves £87 a year against the renewal, and the insurer's own new-customer figure of £719 means the renewal was £23 above what the FCA rule appears to allow, which is a question for the insurer and, if unanswered, for the Financial Ombudsman. Nothing about the national average of £566 told her any of that. Her price is her price; the average only told her the market was flat while her quote was not.

Had she paid monthly, the £655 would have carried interest. Her cheapest quote's monthly total came to £728, which is £73 for the credit. Paying annually from savings, if she has them, is the biggest single saving in the whole exercise.

5. What is worth doing with a renewal letter

Compare it with last year's price, which the letter must state. A rise well above the ABI's roughly flat market is worth challenging, and a fall is worth noticing before you switch for the sake of it.

Get two fresh quotes for identical cover, including the same voluntary excess, and check the total if you pay monthly, not the headline. Check whether your current insurer's new customer price is lower than your renewal; under the FCA rules it should not be.

Do not buy cover on price alone. A cheaper policy with a higher excess or without a courtesy car can cost more the first time you claim, and the ABI's £4,900 average payout is a reminder of what a claim now costs.

And ignore the national average once you have your own three numbers. It is a useful barometer for the market and a useless one for a single policy.

6. The honest verdict

The average car premium is £566 and the market has been flat for a year, on the ABI's paid-price data. Claims costs are at a record £3.2 billion a quarter, so do not expect big falls.

Your renewal is priced on you, not on the average. Compare it with last year, with two fresh quotes, and with your insurer's own new-customer price, which the FCA rule says it cannot undercut you with.

The biggest single saving for most people is paying annually rather than monthly, and it has nothing to do with which insurer you pick.

Frequently asked

Where does the £566 figure come from? The ABI's Motor Insurance Premium Tracker for Q2 2026, published 29 July 2026, based on the price paid on more than 28 million private car policies a year.

Are car insurance prices going up or down? On the ABI's data the average paid rose 1% in the quarter and is £14 lower than a year earlier after inflation, so broadly flat. Claims costs are rising, which limits room for cuts.

Can my insurer charge me more at renewal than a new customer? Not for the equivalent policy through the same channel. FCA pricing rules in force since 1 January 2022 prohibit it. Ask your insurer for its new customer price if you suspect a difference.

Is paying monthly more expensive? Usually, because instalments carry interest. Compare the total annual cost of monthly payments with the single annual price before deciding.
