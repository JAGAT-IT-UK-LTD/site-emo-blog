Average Car Insurance Cost UK: £562 on the Latest ABI Figures. Your Renewal Says £900. Both Are True.

The average car insurance premium paid in the UK was £562 in July to September 2026, £4 less than the quarter before, on the Association of British Insurers tracker. That is the price drivers actually paid, not what they were quoted. If your renewal letter says something very different, neither side is lying. Here is what the number means, why claims costs keep it from falling, and what to do with your own figure.

The short version

  • Average premium paid in Q3 2026: £562, down £4 on the quarter. It is £11 (2%) higher than a year earlier in cash, and about £7 lower after inflation. Source: ABI Motor Insurance Premium Tracker for July to September 2026, published in November 2026. The Q2 figure was £566.
  • Claims paid stayed at a record £3.2 billion in the quarter, level with Q2 and 8% up on a year earlier. The average claim payout was £4,900, up 1% on the year. The average windscreen repair rose 5% in the quarter to £298.
  • The tracker is built on 28 million policies a year and on price paid, not price quoted. Quoted averages from comparison sites run higher, and that is the source of most of the confusion.
  • The average hides a spread. Age, postcode, car, claims history, mileage and how you pay all move an individual premium by hundreds of pounds either way. £562 is a midpoint, not a promise.
  • Since 1 January 2022 an insurer cannot quote a renewing customer more than it would quote a new customer for the same policy. That FCA rule stopped loyalty penalties; it did not stop premiums rising with claims costs.
  • Nothing here is a product recommendation. The useful action is to compare your renewal against last year's price and against two fresh quotes, not against a national average.

1. What is the average car insurance cost in the UK?

The ABI's Motor Insurance Premium Tracker collects the price actually paid on more than 28 million private car policies a year. The ABI is explicit that this is what makes it different: it is "the only collection that is based on the price customers pay for their cover rather than what they are quoted (which typically delivers higher averages)". A comparison site's average is the average of quotes shown, including the ones nobody bought.

For the third quarter of 2026, July to September, the average premium paid was £562. That is £4 lower than the second quarter, when it was £566. It is £11, or 2%, higher than a year earlier in cash, and about £7 lower once inflation is taken out. In plain terms the market has been roughly flat for a year after the sharp rises of 2023 and 2024.

We could not open the ABI's own Q3 release, which blocks automated readers. The Q3 figures here are as reported by Insurance Times and Which? in November 2026, both quoting the ABI. The Q2 figures come from the ABI release itself.

So the £562 is a real, paid-for figure. It is also an average across every driver in the country: a 55-year-old with a ten-year-old hatchback in a rural postcode and a 19-year-old with a hot hatch in a city are both in it.

2. Why are car insurance prices not falling?

The same release explains why premiums are not falling faster. Insurers paid out £3.2 billion in motor claims in the third quarter, matching the record set in the second and 8% more than a year earlier. The average claim payout was £4,900, up 1% on the year. The ABI attributes that to repair costs: modern cars carry cameras, sensors and driver assistance systems that make a minor collision an expensive repair. The average windscreen repair rose 5% in the quarter to £298, and 9% on the year, because a windscreen is now where the cameras live. That average is also the reason small damage is better kept off the policy: for wheel and suspension damage from a road defect, claiming against the council instead protects both the excess and the no-claims discount. The MOT is the other annual car bill where the headline figure is not what most people pay, and the maximum a garage is allowed to charge for an MOT is fixed in law even though plenty charge less.

The ABI's Chris Bose points to the Motor Insurance Taskforce, whose report in December 2025 set out recommendations for government and industry, and to the industry's funded partnership with the National Vehicle Crime Intelligence Service on vehicle crime and fraud. The honest reading of the release is that insurers are holding premiums flat while claims costs rise, and that the room for cuts is limited unless repair costs, theft and fraud come down.

3. Why your renewal is not £562

A national average tells you the direction of the market. It does not tell you your price. The main reasons an individual premium sits far from £562:

  • Age and experience. Young and newly qualified drivers pay multiples of the average; drivers in their fifties and sixties with long no-claims histories pay well under it.
  • Postcode. Theft and claims rates vary sharply by area, and insurers price by it.
  • The car. Repair cost, theft risk and insurance group. The same technology that pushed the average payout to £4,900 pushes the premium on cars that carry it.
  • Claims and convictions. A fault claim in the last few years moves the price a long way.
  • How you pay. Monthly instalments carry interest, which adds to the annual total; compare the total of twelve payments with the single annual price, not the monthly figure with the average.
  • Mileage, use and extras. Business use, high mileage, courtesy car and legal cover all add.

One thing that no longer explains a high renewal is loyalty. Since 1 January 2022 the FCA's pricing rules have required that a renewing customer is not quoted more than the insurer would quote a new customer for the equivalent policy through the same channel. That ended the practice of quietly raising prices on people who did not shop around. It did not, and could not, stop everyone's price rising with claims costs, which is why renewals still went up through 2023 and 2024.

4. A worked example in Wolverhampton

Priya in Wolverhampton is 31, drives a five-year-old Nissan Juke, has four years' no-claims bonus and no convictions. Her renewal letter quotes £742 a year, up from £698 last year, a 6.3% rise. Against the £562 headline it looks like she is being overcharged.

The right comparison is different. First, against her own last year: 6.3% against a market that the ABI says fell £4 in the latest quarter and rose 2% in cash over the year. That is worth a question. Second, against fresh quotes for identical cover: she runs two, and gets £655 and £690 for the same excess and extras. Third, against the FCA rule: her insurer must not be quoting her more than it would a new customer, so she asks her current insurer directly what its new customer price would be for her policy. It comes back at £719.

On our arithmetic, switching to the £655 quote saves £87 a year against the renewal, and the insurer's own new-customer figure of £719 means the renewal was £23 above what the FCA rule appears to allow, which is a question for the insurer and, if unanswered, for the Financial Ombudsman. Nothing about the national average of £562 told her any of that. Her price is her price; the average only told her the market was flat while her quote was not.

Had she paid monthly, the £655 would have carried interest. Her cheapest quote's monthly total came to £728, which is £73 for the credit. Paying annually from savings, if she has them, is the biggest single saving in the whole exercise.

5. What is worth doing with a renewal letter

Compare it with last year's price, which the letter must state. A rise well above the ABI's roughly flat market is worth challenging, and a fall is worth noticing before you switch for the sake of it.

Get two fresh quotes for identical cover, including the same voluntary excess, and check the total if you pay monthly, not the headline. Check whether your current insurer's new customer price is lower than your renewal; under the FCA rules it should not be.

Do not buy cover on price alone. A cheaper policy with a higher excess or without a courtesy car can cost more the first time you claim, and the ABI's £4,900 average payout is a reminder of what a claim now costs.

And ignore the national average once you have your own three numbers. It is a useful barometer for the market and a useless one for a single policy.

6. The honest verdict

The average car premium paid is £562 and the market has been roughly flat for a year, on the ABI's paid price data. Claims costs have sat at a record £3.2 billion a quarter for two quarters running, so do not expect big falls.

Your renewal is priced on you, not on the average. Compare it with last year, with two fresh quotes, and with your insurer's own new-customer price, which the FCA rule says it cannot undercut you with.

The biggest single saving for most people is paying annually rather than monthly, and it has nothing to do with which insurer you pick.

Frequently asked

What is the average car insurance cost in the UK in 2026? £562 a year in July to September 2026, on the ABI's Motor Insurance Premium Tracker, which is based on the price paid on more than 28 million private car policies a year. It was £566 in April to June. Young drivers and city postcodes pay well above it.

Are car insurance prices going down? Slightly, and only just. On the ABI's data the average paid fell £4 in the third quarter of 2026 and is about £7 lower than a year earlier after inflation, though £11 higher in cash. Claims costs are at a record, which limits room for cuts.

Can my insurer charge me more at renewal than a new customer? Not for the equivalent policy through the same channel. FCA pricing rules in force since 1 January 2022 prohibit it. Ask your insurer for its new customer price if you suspect a difference.

Why is my car insurance renewal so high? Because it is priced on you, not the average. Age, postcode, the car, claims history, mileage and how you pay all move it. Compare it with last year's price, two fresh quotes for identical cover, and your insurer's own new customer price.

Is paying monthly more expensive? Usually, because instalments carry interest. Compare the total annual cost of monthly payments with the single annual price before deciding.
