Do I Need a VPN in the UK? What It Does, What It Costs, and When to Skip It

Ruhul Amin · Updated 26 November 2026 · 9 min read · Figures checked November 2026

Most people in the UK do not need a VPN at home, but it is worth about £1.50 a month if you use public wifi, travel, or want privacy from your broadband provider. VPNs are legal, and in July 2026 the government said it will not ban them. Here is what one really does, what the adverts oversell, and how to avoid the renewal trap.

The short version

  • Using a VPN is legal in the UK. In its July 2026 response on children's online safety, the government said it will not ban or age gate VPNs.
  • It does three things well: hides your browsing from the network you are on, hides your location from sites you visit, and stops your broadband provider seeing which sites you use.
  • It does not make you anonymous, stop viruses, stop scams or protect your banking. Those four claims sell subscriptions and none of them hold up.

  • Mostly avoid free VPNs. Proton VPN's free tier is the honest exception, with no data limit and one device.

1. Do I need a VPN in the UK?

Most people at home on their own broadband do not need a VPN. Your bank, your email and almost every site you use already encrypt the connection.

You do have a good reason for one in three cases. You often use public wifi. You travel and want your usual services to work as they do at home. Or you do not want your broadband provider to see which sites you visit.

Here is the quick test.

Your situationVerdict
You regularly use public wifi in cafes, hotels, trains or airportsBuy one. This is the strongest case
You travel and want your usual services working as at homeBuy one
You do not want your broadband provider logging every siteBuy one. Legitimate, and a personal call
You run a business needing a stable location for suppliers or clientsBuy one, and it is an allowable business expense
You only browse at home on your own connectionSkip it. The money is better spent elsewhere
You want protection from viruses or scamsSkip it. Wrong tool. It does not do that

If you skip it, there are cheaper habits worth changing first. The daily shop coffee costs about £860 to £910 a year, which pays for a VPN many times over.

2. Is a VPN legal in the UK, and will it be banned?

Using a VPN is legal in the UK. There is no law against it. Businesses have used them for decades so staff can reach work systems from home.

The ban question came up in 2026. The government's "Growing up in the online world" consultation asked whether VPNs should be restricted for children or need age checks.

The answer came in the government response published in July 2026. It says the government will not ban or age gate VPNs. Instead it will ask VPN providers to make their products less accessible to children, and it has asked Ofcom to research how sites can spot people getting round age checks.

The government also said it will review this within a year of its planned under 16 social media restrictions starting, and act further if it has to. So the honest position for November 2026 is simple. Legal now, no ban planned, but watched.

A VPN does not make anything illegal legal. Copyright infringement and fraud are still crimes whatever your IP address says. Getting round a streaming service's regional limits usually breaks its terms rather than the law, so the risk is to your account, not a police matter.

3. What changed with the Online Safety Act age checks?

From 25 July 2025, sites that allow porn, or content such as self harm and suicide material, must use what Ofcom calls highly effective age assurance. In practice that means a face scan to estimate age, a photo ID match or a credit card check.

That weekend, UK VPN sign ups jumped. Proton told TechRadar its hourly UK sign ups rose by more than 1,400% from 25 July 2025. VPN apps briefly topped the UK App Store charts.

Many of those people were adults who did not want to hand a selfie or a passport to a website. That is a privacy choice, not a hacker problem.

Two honest points follow. First, a VPN does not change your age, and the government says sites must take robust steps to detect people getting round their checks. Second, if you use one to avoid a face scan, you are trusting the VPN company with your browsing instead. Pick one with an independent audit.

4. What does a VPN actually do?

  1. It hides your browsing from the network you are on. On public wifi in a cafe, hotel or airport, whoever runs that network can otherwise see a good deal about your traffic. This is real protection and the strongest case for buying one.
  2. It hides your real location from the sites you visit. This is how travellers keep their usual services working as they do at home.
  3. It stops your broadband provider seeing which sites you visit. How much that matters is your call, and for some people it is the entire reason.

The National Cyber Security Centre puts it plainly. A VPN is one way to secure data in transit across an untrusted network. That is its job, and it is a narrow one.

5. What does a VPN not do, despite the adverts?

The claimThe reality
"Makes you anonymous"No. Log into your Google or Facebook account through a VPN and they know exactly who you are. The account is the identifier, not the IP address
"Protects you from viruses and scams"No. If you download something bad or type your card details into a fake site, the VPN watches it happen and does nothing
"Secures your online banking"No. UK banking apps and websites are already encrypted. The padlock in your browser was doing that job before you paid anyone
"Stops you losing money online"No. UK Finance says £576.4 million was lost in 2025 to scams where people were tricked into sending money, up 19%. A VPN cannot stop you being talked into a payment

Total payment fraud losses in 2025 were £1.28 billion, according to the UK Finance Annual Fraud Report 2026. Of that, £703.4 million was unauthorised fraud, such as stolen card details. Stolen card details come from fake messages, fake sites and data breaches, none of which a VPN can see.

A VPN protects the pipe. Almost every way UK households actually lose money happens at one end of it or the other, where the VPN cannot see and cannot help.

6. How much does a VPN cost in the UK?

VPN pricing is strange, and understanding why saves you most of the money. The headline monthly price is almost always a long plan paid upfront.

How you buyWhat happensExample checked November 2026
Monthly rollingHighest price per month, cancel any timeGood for a one off trip only
One year planCheaper per month, paid upfrontProton VPN Plus: $35.88 for the first year, then $83.88 a year
Two year planCheapest per month, paid upfrontSurfshark Starter: £40.23 for the first 27 months, about £1.49 a month
Renewal, any planOften far higher than the first termCharged automatically to the card you gave them

Proton's page showed prices in US dollars when we checked. The point is the ratio, not the currency. Its renewal price was 2.3 times the first year price.

Surfshark's page notes that VAT may apply and that you pay "the applicable renewal price" when the term ends, without saying what that is. Assume it will be higher.

Two warnings on price. The cheap headline rate is for the whole term paid upfront, so you hand over the full sum on day one. And renewal is usually much dearer, so put a reminder in your calendar before it renews. That single reminder is worth more than the choice between providers.

7. Worked example: the Hughes household

Sam and Leah Hughes, Nottingham, one laptop each and two phones

The need. Sam works from hotels and trains about two nights a week. Leah uses the gym and cafe wifi. Neither cares about streaming abroad. They have a real public wifi reason, so a VPN is worth having.

Option one, a monthly plan. They sign up monthly to "try it" and never cancel. Surfshark lists an "old price" of £372.33 for the same 27 months, about £13.79 a month. That is roughly what paying full rate looks like.

Option two, the two year plan. Surfshark Starter at £40.23 covers 27 months. That is about £17.88 a year. Before paying, they check the device limit covers two laptops and two phones.

Option three, free. Leah only needs cafe wifi cover on one phone, so Proton's free tier would do for her. It allows one device at a time.

The trap. If they had chosen Proton's one year plan and forgotten the renewal, year one costs $35.88 and year two costs $83.88. Year two alone costs $48 more than year one. One calendar reminder, and a look at the market before renewal, avoids most of that.

The verdict for them: one two year plan, set a reminder for month 26, and spend nothing extra on "identity protection" add ons.

8. Is a free VPN safe to use?

Mostly avoid them. Running servers costs money, so a free service is being paid another way, often by logging what you do and selling it. That is the opposite of what you came for.

The exception is Proton VPN's free tier. It is paid for by people on Proton's paid plans, not by selling data. Proton's pricing page lists one device at a time, servers in 10 countries chosen for you, medium speed and no data limit.

If your need is "I use hotel wifi four times a year", start there and spend nothing.

9. How do you buy one without overpaying?

  1. Pick the two year plan, never the monthly one, once you are sure you want it.
  2. Use the money back window, usually 30 days, to test it on your own devices and your own connection. Speed varies a lot by provider and location.
  3. Set a calendar reminder a week before renewal. The second term rarely costs what the first did, and this is where providers make their margin.
  4. Check the device limit if you want it on phones, laptops and a TV. Most cover several, some do not.
  5. Look for an independent audit of the no logs claim, not just the claim itself.
  6. Ignore the countdown timers. The deal is always on. A permanent sale is just the price.

If you run a side hustle you may need one for a real reason. Some suppliers, marketplaces and payment tools behave differently by country, and freelancers with overseas clients often need a stable location. That is a business expense, which reduces your taxable profit, covered in our side hustle tax guide. If your side income is small, compare it with the trading allowance first, because you cannot claim both.

The honest verdict

Buy one if you use public wifi regularly, travel, or object to your broadband provider seeing your browsing. On a two year plan that is roughly £1.50 a month, which is fair for what it does.

Skip it if you browse at home on your own connection and are not bothered who knows which sites you visit. Skip it entirely if you were buying it to stop viruses, scams or bank fraud, because it does none of those.

Whatever you decide, never buy a single month by accident, and put the renewal date in your diary the day you sign up. Those two habits are worth more than the choice of provider.

Frequently asked

Are VPNs illegal in the UK? No. Using a VPN is legal in the UK for adults and businesses. In July 2026 the government said it will not ban or age gate VPNs, though it will ask providers to make them harder for children to reach. What you do through a VPN is still covered by the normal law, so a crime stays a crime.

Do I need a VPN at home in the UK? Usually not. On your own broadband, sites you use for banking, shopping and email already encrypt the connection. A home VPN mainly stops your broadband provider seeing which sites you visit. If that does not bother you, skip it and spend the money on a password manager or nothing at all.

Does a VPN get round UK age verification? It can hide that you are in the UK, but it does not make you an adult, and the government says sites in scope must take robust steps to detect and respond to underage circumvention. It has also asked Ofcom to research what more sites can do about VPNs. Many adults use one for privacy instead, which is legal.

Will a VPN slow my internet down? Somewhat, because traffic takes a longer route and is encrypted. On a decent provider with a nearby server the difference is usually small and hard to notice for browsing or video. On a free tier, at busy times or with a distant server, the slowdown can be severe, so test it inside the refund window.

Do I need a VPN on my phone as well? Yes, if public wifi is the reason you bought it, because the phone is where most people use public wifi. Most paid plans cover several devices, so it costs nothing extra. On mobile data a VPN adds less, because your data connection is not shared with strangers the way cafe wifi is.
