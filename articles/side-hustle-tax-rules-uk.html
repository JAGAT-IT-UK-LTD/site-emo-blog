Side Hustle Tax Rules UK: The £1,000 Allowance Now, and the £3,000 One From April 2027

Ruhul Amin · 26 August 2026 · 11 min read · Updated 18 November 2026 · Figures checked November 2026

For the 2026 to 27 tax year, the side hustle tax rules in the UK are unchanged: the first £1,000 of trading sales is tax free, and above that you must tell HMRC. From 6 April 2027 that allowance triples to £3,000, confirmed in the Budget on 28 October 2026. Selling your own old things is not trading at all, so most Vinted clearouts owe nothing either way.

The short version

  • For 2026 to 27 the trading allowance is £1,000 of gross sales, not profit. Sell £1,200 of candles and spend £500 on wax, and you are over the line. This catches more people than every other rule combined.
  • From 6 April 2027 it rises to £3,000. The Budget on 28 October 2026 confirmed it, and the Self Assessment reporting threshold for this income rises to £3,000 too. It covers trading and miscellaneous income.
  • All your side hustles share one allowance. Freelancing, tutoring, market stalls and online selling add together. You do not get £1,000 each.
  • Selling your own used possessions is not trading and is not taxable, however much it comes to. Buying or making things to sell is trading from the first sale.
  • There are three separate £1,000-ish allowances and people merge them constantly: trading (£1,000), property (£1,000), and Rent a Room (£7,500).
  • The platforms already report you. Since January 2024, Vinted, eBay, Etsy, Airbnb and similar pass seller data to HMRC above 30 sales or about £1,700 a year.
  • The £3,000 is a real tax free allowance, not just a reporting line. But it does not reach back. Sales made before 6 April 2027 are still tested against £1,000.
  • Register by 5 October following the end of the tax year in which you crossed the threshold. For 2026 to 27 that means 5 October 2027.

1. What is the side hustle tax allowance in 2026 to 27?

Every person in the UK gets a trading allowance of £1,000 per tax year. The tax year runs from 6 April to 5 April, so the current one ends on 5 April 2027.

That £1,000 is the side hustle tax free allowance people search for. It is the heart of the side hustle tax rules in the UK, and it stays at £1,000 until the £3,000 version starts on 6 April 2027.

One sponsorship slot can swallow the whole allowance in a single payment, so what UK newsletters actually charge sponsors is worth knowing before you agree a rate.

If your total side income stays at £1,000 or below, you owe no tax on it and you do not need to tell HMRC anything. No forms, no registration, nothing.

Two details catch people out, and the first one is the single most misunderstood rule in UK personal finance.

First, the £1,000 means your total sales, not your profit. If you sold £1,200 worth of candles and spent £500 on wax and jars, you are over the limit. Your sales were £1,200. Your profit of £700 is what gets taxed, but the threshold that decides whether you must report at all is measured on the £1,200. Letting a room or a whole place has its own allowance and its own reporting rules, which is where what Airbnb hosting actually costs a UK host starts to matter.

Second, all your side hustles share one allowance. Freelance work, market stalls, tutoring, online selling, dog walking, it all adds up together. Royalties and subscriptions sit in the same pot, so what a KDP ebook actually pays per sale and what a paid Substack leaves after the platform and card fees are both trading income. You do not get £1,000 per activity. Platform fees come off after that test, not before it, which matters most on freelance marketplaces: what Upwork actually takes from a UK freelancer is deducted from money HMRC has already counted. Digital products sit in exactly the same pot, so whatever selling Canva templates brings in over a year is added to the rest before the allowance is applied, not counted separately.

Gross, not net. The threshold is measured on money in, before you have paid for a single thing. That one word is the difference between "I made £300, I am fine" and a Self Assessment return.

2. Do I have to pay tax on a side hustle, or am I just decluttering?

This causes the most needless panic, so here it is plainly.

Clearing out your own possessions is not trading. Selling your old wardrobe, your old phone, or unwanted gifts on Vinted is not income. You are selling personal possessions, usually for less than you paid. There is no tax on that, no matter the amount, and the £1,000 limit is not relevant to it at all.

Trading means you buy things to resell, or you make things to sell, with the aim of profit. Buying ten pairs of trainers to flip is trading. Selling your own trainers because they no longer fit is not.

What you didTrading?Does the £1,000 apply?
Cleared your wardrobe and sold 40 items for £600NoNo. Not taxable at any amount
Sold your old sofa and a bike for £450NoNo
Bought stock at car boots to resell on eBayYesYes, from the first sale
Made and sold candles, jewellery or printsYesYes
Did freelance design work at weekendsYesYes
Walked neighbours' dogs for paymentYesYes
Sold one personal painting for £8,000NoNo, but Capital Gains rules can apply above £6,000

The distinction is intent, and for a reseller it starts from the first item you deliberately sourced. If you began by selling your own things and then started buying stock, the trading starts at that point, not retrospectively.

One exception exists for single valuable items. If you sell a personal possession for more than £6,000, such as a painting, jewellery or an antique, Capital Gains Tax rules can apply. For everyday clearouts this almost never comes up.

3. Which allowances do people confuse?

These are separate. Using one does not consume another, and merging them in your head is how people either over-report or miss a threshold. Anyone claiming benefits has a second set of sums to do, because the Universal Credit minimum income floor treats self-employed earnings on different terms from HMRC.

AllowanceAmountCovers
Trading allowance£1,000 for 2026 to 27, then £3,000 from 6 April 2027Self-employment and casual trading income of any kind, combined, plus miscellaneous income
Property allowance£1,000, no rise announcedProperty income such as renting a driveway, garage or storage space. Entirely separate from trading
Rent a Room scheme£7,500Letting furnished accommodation in your own main home to a lodger

So someone earning £900 from a side hustle, £800 from renting their driveway and £6,000 from a lodger is inside all three and has nothing to report. Add those three figures together and it looks like £7,700 of untaxed income, which is exactly why people get confused. Fostering sits outside all three again, because qualifying care relief runs on its own weekly formula rather than on the trading allowance.

The Budget 2026 measure covers trading and miscellaneous income only. It says nothing about the property allowance, so treat that as £1,000 until GOV.UK says otherwise.

One important limitation: if you claim the trading allowance, you cannot also deduct expenses. You choose one or the other.

The allowance still works once you are over the line. You can take £1,000 off your sales instead of your real costs. So below £1,000 of costs the allowance usually wins. Above it, deducting properly wins, and that crossover arrives fast once you buy equipment. From April 2027 the same choice applies with £3,000 in place of £1,000. That choice bites hardest in side hustles carrying real running costs, and pet sitting and home boarding is the clearest example, because the council licence and the insurance are unavoidable cash out. Two others carry costs that people underestimate in the same way: what a coaching business really costs to set up in the UK, and what Twitch affiliate status pays against partner status. That choice is easiest where the costs are close to nothing, which is the shape of income from selling Notion templates: the allowance beats an expense claim almost every time. Employment income on the side has its own trap, which is why a BR tax code on a second job takes more than it should.

4. Over the line: when must I register for Self Assessment?

Once your gross side income passes £1,000 in a tax year, you need to register for Self Assessment with HMRC. It takes about ten minutes on GOV.UK. You will need a Government Gateway account and your National Insurance number. HMRC is not always the only registration involved either: selling food you have made at home means registering with your council at least 28 days before you start. Construction is the other exception worth knowing: a CIS subcontractor has 20% or 30% deducted at source and still files a return to claim the difference back.

The deadline to register is 5 October after the end of the tax year in which you passed the limit. For anyone who crossed £1,000 during 2025 to 26, that date was 5 October 2026, and it has now passed. The penalty for missing it is a percentage of the tax owed rather than a flat fine.

If you cross £1,000 in the current year, 2026 to 27, you must register by 5 October 2027. The £3,000 allowance does not help here, because it only starts with the 2027 to 28 tax year.

Missed the date? Register as soon as you realise. Penalties are generally based on the tax owed and how late you are, so acting quickly limits them. Deliberately not registering is treated very differently from being late.

Registering does not always mean paying. If your profit is small and your total income sits under the Personal Allowance of £12,570, you may owe nothing at all. The return still has to be filed. Note also that the Personal Allowance is held at £12,570 until 5 April 2031, so more people cross into tax each year without any rate changing, as covered in our guide to frozen thresholds. Some people are sent a Simple Assessment letter instead of being asked to file a return at all, and that letter carries a deadline of its own. If what lands is a Simple Assessment rather than a return demand, you have 60 days to challenge the figure before it hardens.

The key dates once you are in the system:

DateWhat happens
5 AprilTax year ends
5 OctoberDeadline to register for Self Assessment for the year just ended
31 OctoberPaper return deadline
31 JanuaryOnline return deadline, and the tax is due
31 January and 31 JulyPayments on account, if your bill was over £1,000

The last row surprises people in year two. If your bill is over £1,000, HMRC asks for next year's tax in two instalments on top of this year's. Your first January as a self-employed person can be a bill you did not budget for. The return itself has two dates rather than one. For 2025 to 26, the paper deadline of 31 October 2026 has passed, and 31 January 2027 is the online deadline, with the tax due on 31 January either way. If a first payment on account is part of that bill, what the 31 January 2027 payment on account means is worth reading before you file.

5. What do Vinted, eBay and Airbnb tell HMRC?

Since January 2024, digital platforms in the UK must report seller information to HMRC. This covers eBay, Vinted, Etsy, Depop, Airbnb, Uber, Deliveroo and similar sites. Crypto exchanges have been pulled into a parallel regime with its own thresholds and start date, covered in our guide to the HMRC crypto reporting rules. Subscription platforms fall inside the same reporting rules, which is why the tax side is the first thing to settle before running a paid Patreon membership from the UK.

A platform reports you when you pass either of these in a calendar year: 30 sales, or roughly £1,700 in earnings (the rule is set at 2,000 euros). They send your name, address, total earnings and transaction count. Reports go in by 31 January for the previous year.

This is not a new tax. It is a visibility tool, and reporting is not the same as taxation. Being reported does not mean you owe anything. If you were selling your own belongings, the honest answer to any HMRC letter is exactly that, and it is a complete answer.

What it does mean is that HMRC can now compare what platforms say you earned against what you declared. If those numbers do not match and you were trading, expect a letter. Guessing is no longer a strategy.

If one has already arrived, our guide to the HMRC letter to online sellers and how to answer it walks through the reply.

6. Is the trading allowance going up to £3,000?

Yes. The Budget on 28 October 2026 confirmed that the trading allowance rises from £1,000 to £3,000 from 6 April 2027, for the 2027 to 28 tax year onwards.

Earlier headlines spoke only of a reporting threshold. The government first announced that idea at the spring 2025 tax update. The Budget 2026 version goes further, and it matters which one you remember.

According to the HMRC policy paper, three things change at once:

  • The tax free allowance itself rises to £3,000. It is not only a reporting change. Up to £3,000 of gross trading sales a year will be free of Income Tax.
  • The Self Assessment reporting threshold for this income also rises to £3,000. Below it, you will not need a return for this income.
  • Miscellaneous income is included, such as one off paid jobs that are not a trade.

HMRC estimates up to 1.4 million people could benefit. About 400,000 of them would see a lower tax bill, by around £380 on average. About 100,000 would no longer need to report this income at all.

Two things have not changed, and both are routinely got wrong:

  • It does not reach back. Everything you sell between now and 5 April 2027 is still tested against £1,000.
  • It still measures gross sales. The new £3,000 line is money in before costs, exactly like the old £1,000 line.

The change is being made through the Finance Bill 2026 to 27. Our separate guide on what the £3,000 trading allowance from April 2027 means for a side hustle runs the numbers in more detail, and our guide to what was already decided before the 28 October 2026 Budget sets out the wider background.

7. What can I actually deduct?

If you are past the allowance and deducting expenses instead of claiming it, the test is that a cost must be wholly and exclusively for the business. Common allowable costs for a small side business:

  • Stock and materials, including the wastage you did not sell
  • Postage, packaging and courier fees
  • Platform and payment fees, such as Etsy's cut or card processing. Our Etsy fees guide breaks those down per sale
  • Travel to buy stock or see clients, at the approved mileage rate or actual cost
  • A proportion of your phone and broadband, based on genuine business use
  • Software, hosting, domains and professional subscriptions
  • Insurance such as public liability or professional indemnity
  • Use of home, either the simplified flat rate or a calculated proportion

What is not allowable: your entire laptop if you also use it personally, ordinary clothing even if you wear it for work, client entertaining, or fines and penalties. And a company structure does not widen this list, as our sole trader versus limited company guide explains.

If you drive for it: rather than itemising fuel and wear, most people claim HMRC's flat mileage rate. For 2026 to 27 that rose to 55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles, up from 45p, then 25p a mile after that.

8. Worked example: three people, three answers

Same tax year, very different positions

Leah cleared her flat. She sold 63 items on Vinted and eBay for a total of £840: old clothes, a games console, a bookcase. Vinted reported her to HMRC because she passed 30 sales. She owes nothing and needs to do nothing. These were her own possessions, so it is not trading, and the £1,000 threshold does not even apply. If HMRC writes, one sentence answers it.

Marcus started reselling. He bought stock at car boots and resold it, taking £1,450 in sales and spending £590 on stock, fuel and postage. His profit was £860. He is over the threshold on the £1,450, not the £860, so he must register by 5 October following the end of that tax year. Because his profit is small and his employed salary already uses his Personal Allowance, he will pay tax on what is left at his marginal rate. He should claim the £1,000 allowance instead of his £590 of costs, because it is the bigger deduction. That leaves £450 taxable rather than £860. Affiliate commission is treated exactly the same way, and there is a step by step on declaring affiliate income to HMRC if that is where your money comes from.

Priya did three things at once. Tutoring brought £700, selling handmade prints brought £450, and she rented her driveway for £600. She assumed she was fine on all three. She is not. The tutoring and the prints are both trading and share one £1,000 allowance, so £1,150 of trading income puts her over. The driveway sits in the separate property allowance and is fine. She must register, on the strength of two hobbies she thought were too small to count.

The pattern: the person selling the most money's worth of goods owed nothing, and the person who felt smallest is the one who had to register. Gross versus net, and one allowance versus several, decide it.

Now move all three into 2027 to 28. Leah still owes nothing. Marcus, with £1,450 of sales, sits under the new £3,000 allowance, so he owes no tax on it and has no return to file for it. Priya's £1,150 of trading sales is under £3,000 too, so she drops out of Self Assessment for that income. Her driveway still uses the separate £1,000 property allowance.

One more case, for scale. Say Daniel earns £3,600 a year teaching guitar at weekends, with £400 of costs. His salary already uses his Personal Allowance and keeps him a basic rate taxpayer. In 2026 to 27 he registers, and the £1,000 allowance beats his £400 of costs, so £2,600 is taxable. From 2027 to 28 he can take £3,000 off instead, so only £600 is taxable. At the 20% basic rate that is £120 of tax instead of £520.

9. What happens as it grows? NI, VAT and MTD

Three further thresholds arrive later, and it helps to know they exist before you meet them.

  • National Insurance. Class 4 is 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270, and 2% above that. Class 2 is no longer charged. If your profits are £7,105 or more, you get the State Pension credit without paying. Below £7,105 you can choose to pay £3.65 a week to protect your record.
  • VAT. Registration is compulsory once taxable turnover passes £90,000 in any rolling 12 month period. Not per tax year, rolling, so it can arrive mid-month if you are growing. Once you are registered, check whether the VAT flat rate scheme is worth it for your trade before you pick a method. One important exception: if you dropship low-value consignments direct to GB customers, you may need to register from your first sale regardless of turnover, as our dropshipping guide explains.
  • Making Tax Digital for Income Tax. Live since 6 April 2026 for sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000. The threshold drops to £30,000 from April 2027 and £20,000 from April 2028. Qualifying income is gross turnover before expenses, the same trap as the £1,000 rule. Our free software guide covers which tools can actually file.

Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. The allowances, National Insurance and VAT rules are UK wide, and so is the new £3,000 allowance. Scotland sets its own income tax bands, so what you pay on the profit differs there. Scotland runs six bands against three for 2026 to 27.

10. A 60 second self check

  1. Am I selling my own used possessions? If yes, relax. No tax, no forms, whatever the amount.
  2. Am I making or buying things to sell, or doing paid work on the side? If yes, add up your total sales for the tax year, across every activity, before costs.
  3. Is that total over £1,000? If yes, register for Self Assessment by 5 October following the end of that tax year. If no, you are done. From 2027 to 28, ask the same question with £3,000.
  4. Is any of it property income? That has its own separate £1,000, and a lodger has a separate £7,500.

Keep simple records either way. A spreadsheet with dates, amounts and costs takes minutes to maintain and settles any question HMRC ever asks. Reconstructing two years of it in January is miserable, and it is the single most common regret among people who started a side hustle that worked.

Two things sit alongside the record keeping. If the numbers are the part you dread, it is worth knowing what you would be paying someone else for: bookkeeping as a business of its own carries a supervision requirement most guides skip, and that requirement is why the going rate is what it is. Separately, one of the insurances a small side business needs is compulsory and it is not the one most people assume, which is far cheaper to sort out before it is needed than after.

The honest verdict

Most people worrying about this owe nothing. If you are clearing your own clutter, you are not trading, the platforms reporting you changes nothing, and you can stop reading.

If you are actually trading, the rule is simpler than the fear: add up what came in, before costs, across everything. Over £1,000, register by 5 October. Under it, do nothing. From April 2027 the line moves to £3,000, which is a genuine tax cut for small sellers, but not a reason to stop keeping records now. Then open a separate account for the money and put a share of every payment aside from day one, because the bill arrives in January whether you saved for it or not.

Frequently asked

Is the trading allowance going up to £3,000? Yes. The Budget on 28 October 2026 confirmed it rises from £1,000 to £3,000 from 6 April 2027, for the 2027 to 28 tax year onwards. The Self Assessment reporting threshold for this income rises to £3,000 as well. Sales made before that date are still tested against the £1,000 limit.

I sell on Vinted and got an HMRC letter. What do I do? If you were selling your own used possessions, reply saying so. Platform reporting is triggered by 30 sales or roughly £1,700 a year, not by any judgement that you were trading. Keep a note of what you sold and roughly what you originally paid, in case HMRC asks a follow up question.

Does my employer find out about my side hustle? No. Self Assessment is between you and HMRC. Your tax code can change if HMRC collects side hustle tax through PAYE, which an employer could notice, but they are not told why. You can ask HMRC to collect it separately instead. If the new code looks wrong, here is how to get a wrong tax code corrected.

Do I have to register if my side hustle made a loss? If your gross sales passed £1,000 in 2026 to 27, yes, because the test is sales rather than profit. Registering also lets you record the loss, which can be set against future profits from the same trade. That makes it worth doing properly rather than ignoring, even when no tax is due.

Do I need an accountant for a side hustle? Not for a small, simple one, and from April 2027 many will drop out of Self Assessment entirely. Once you are dealing with VAT, MTD, employees or a limited company, an hour with an accountant costs far less than the mistake. MTD for Income Tax reaches the £20,000 threshold in April 2028.
