A dog walking side hustle needs no council licence in England, because the dog stays in its own home. Boarding a dog in your house is different: that is licensed, and a new licence costs £533 at Derby City Council. Here is where the line falls, what insurance costs, how many dogs you may walk and what you can really earn around a job.

The short version

  • Dog walking needs no licence in England. DEFRA's statutory guidance, updated 1 June 2026, excludes "businesses that look after dogs in their owner's homes, such as, dog sitters and dog walkers". No council licence, no inspection, no fee.
  • The moment a dog spends the day or the night at your address, it becomes licensable. That is dog day care or home boarding under the Animal Welfare (Licensing of Activities Involving Animals) (England) Regulations 2018.
  • A new home boarding licence costs £533 at Derby City Council for 2026/27, and £378 to renew. Commercial day care is £689. Vet fees come on top, and every council sets its own fees.
  • Below the HMRC trading allowance you need no licence at all. The allowance is £1,000 today and rises to £3,000 from 6 April 2027. Above it, the council still has to decide you are trading.
  • Your council caps what you earn per walk. There is no national limit. Reigate and Banstead allows four dogs per person, Sefton six. At an assumed £14 a dog that is £56 or £84 for the same hour.
  • Rover keeps 15% of a one off booking but only 5% of a recurring weekly one. On £3,600 of turnover, that gap is £360 a year for the same work.

1. Do you need a licence for a dog walking side hustle?

Search for how to start a dog walking business and you find two kinds of article. One says you need a council licence. The other never mentions licensing at all.

Both are wrong, because the answer depends on a distinction neither of them makes. It is not about how much you earn. It is about whose house the dog is in.

If the start up cost is what puts you off, proofreading as a side hustle needs a laptop and nothing else.

The Animal Welfare (Licensing of Activities Involving Animals) (England) Regulations 2018 license five activities. Two matter here. Providing or arranging boarding for cats or dogs, which includes home boarding, and providing day care for dogs.

DEFRA's statutory guidance for councils, last updated on 1 June 2026, then lists what falls outside. The exact wording is worth reading.

Activities that do not require a licence include "businesses that look after dogs in their owner's homes, such as, dog sitters and dog walkers".

Sitting in the owner’s home is therefore a different business on paper, with its own insurance and rates. We set that out in what pet sitting pays and what cover you need.

The reason is simple. The dog stays in its own home. You collect it, walk it and bring it back. Nothing about your home is inspected, because your home is not part of the service.

Turn that around and the picture changes. Home boarding, under the guidance, means a dog staying in a domestic home that is not its usual home. That is your house, and that is licensed.

So the fork is physical, not financial. Lead in hand on a public footpath, no licence. Dog asleep on your sofa for money, licence.

There is one more exemption almost nobody quotes. The same guidance says that if your trading income is below the HMRC trading income allowance, you do not need a licence.

That allowance is £1,000 a year today. Board a neighbour's spaniel a handful of weekends a year for under £1,000 and you are outside the regime.

From 6 April 2027 the trading allowance rises to £3,000, confirmed in an HMRC policy paper on 28 October 2026. The guidance links the exemption to the HMRC allowance, so the line may move with it.

DEFRA had not said so directly by late November 2026, so ask your council before relying on the higher figure. Above the line, councils judge whether you are trading using HMRC's nine badges of trade.

2. How much does a home boarding licence cost?

Fees are set by each council, so there is no national figure. Derby City Council's schedule, valid from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027, is a fair example.

Home boarding for dogs£533£378
Day care for dogs, domestic premises£533£378
Day care for dogs, commercial premises£689£533
Boarding in kennels for dogs£689£430

Derby's fees exclude vet fees, which are charged on top where a vet inspection is needed. A re-score inspection is a further £257.

Your own council will charge something different. Search your council's name with "animal licence fees" before you plan anything.

Put £533 next to an assumed home boarding price of £45 a night. The licence is covered by about twelve nights of boarding, and the £378 renewal by about eight.

Framed that way the fee is not the barrier people imagine. The standards are.

The home boarding guidance sets out what your house must have. At least one working smoke detector on each floor and at least one carbon monoxide detector.

Dogs need at least two secure physical barriers between them and any exit. Records for every animal must be kept for at least three years. Your house stops being just your house.

The licence also states a maximum number of dogs. It must be reasonable for your space and staffing, and your own dogs count towards it.

There is a reward for going further. Businesses that meet the higher standards get a four or five star rating. That earns a licence lasting two or three years instead of one, which lowers the cost.

If you are serious about boarding, aiming straight at four stars is cheaper over five years than renewing a one star licence every twelve months.

3. How many dogs can you walk at once?

This is the rule that decides the economics of walking, and most guides miss it. There is no national legal limit on how many dogs one person may walk.

Instead, individual councils make Public Spaces Protection Orders. Reigate and Banstead cut its limit from six to four dogs per person after a 2023 review. Sefton's order limits one person to six.

Some boroughs vary the limit park by park. DEFRA's day care guidance sets six as the standard for licensed businesses: "a dog walker may walk no more than 6 dogs at the same time".

Multiply that out. At an assumed £14 a dog for a group walk, a four dog cap means £56 for the hour. A six dog cap means £84.

Same hour, same effort, same fuel, 50% more money, decided by which side of a boundary you live on. Check your council's order before you price anything.

Prices vary a lot by area. Before you set yours, look at what walkers within a few miles charge on Rover and local listings, and pitch near the middle.

4. What does it cost to start a dog walking business in the UK?

Public liability insurance. Not a legal must, but you are taking someone's dog into a public place. Protectivity advertises dog walking cover from £5.41 a month. Cliverton advertises dog walkers insurance from £129.26 a year, with £5 million public liability as standard.

Treat those as starting prices, not quotes. Our guide to public liability insurance for a side hustle explains what the cover does.

Car insurance, the first thing people forget. A social, domestic and pleasure policy may not cover carrying other people's animals for money. Tell your insurer exactly what you carry, and get business use confirmed in writing.

Key cover, the second thing people forget. Most walking clients give you a key. If one is lost and locks have to be changed, check whether your policy pays.

Kit. A crate or boot guard, a coupler lead, spare slip leads, a canine first aid kit, high visibility clothing and plenty of waste bags. Most of the spend is in year one.

What you do not need. No DBS check is legally required to walk dogs, and no qualification either. Anyone telling you to buy an accreditation before you can start is selling you something.

Platform commission. On Rover in the UK, walkers and sitters take home 85% of a booking. On recurring weekly services they take home 95%. Owners separately pay a 15% service fee, capped at £49 a booking.

That 10 point gap is the most important number on the platform. Your whole plan should be turning one off bookings into standing weekly slots, because that is where the fee drops from 15% to 5%.

5. Do you pay tax on dog walking income?

Yes, once it passes the trading allowance. Walking and boarding are both trading income. The first £1,000 of gross trading income in a tax year is tax free, and you do not need to tell HMRC about it.

From 6 April 2027 that allowance rises to £3,000. Above it, you register for Self Assessment and either take the allowance or deduct your real costs, whichever is better.

6. Worked example: Sadia in Watford, weekends only

Sadia works full time and wants to earn from dogs around her job. She has two realistic options, and they are not equal. Her prices are our assumptions, so swap in your local rates.

Option A, walking. The problem is timing. Most walking work is booked for weekdays, while owners are at work, which is when Sadia is at work too. At weekends many owners walk their own dogs.

Say she fills one group walk each weekend day with three dogs at £14: 3 × £14 × 2 days = £84 a week. Over 46 weeks that is £3,864 gross.

Take off £129.26 of insurance at Cliverton's starting price and an assumed £400 of fuel and kit. She nets about £3,335. No licence, no inspection.

Option B, home boarding. One dog, Friday evening to Sunday evening, at £45 a night. That is £90 a weekend. Across 40 weekends, £3,600 gross.

Year one costs: the £533 Derby style licence plus £129.26 of insurance. She nets about £2,938. Year two, on the £378 renewal, about £3,093.

Confirm any policy covers boarding in your home. Walking cover alone may not.

The turn. On one dog, boarding is worse. But Sadia's licence states a maximum number of dogs, and the fee does not change if that number is two.

Two dogs a weekend: £7,200 gross, less £533 and £129.26, is about £6,538 in year one. The fixed cost has not moved. That is the whole point.

All of these figures are before tax. Each option is above the £3,000 allowance that starts in April 2027, so Sadia files a Self Assessment return either way.

The honest verdict

Dog walking is a poor side hustle and a decent full time job. The demand sits in the working day, the one block of time an employed person does not have. Weekend walking will pay for a food shop and not much more.

Home boarding is the opposite. It fits around a job, because the work happens in the evenings and at weekends, in a house you already pay for.

The licence is real, the inspection is real, and at Derby's £378 to £533 the fee is worth about twelve nights of trade. The honest question is not whether you can afford it.

It is whether you will have your home inspected, keep written records for three years, and lose the ability to say no on a Friday night.

If the answer is no, walk dogs at weekends, keep it under the trading allowance, and enjoy it for what it is. There is no shame in a side hustle that stays small.

Frequently asked

Do I need a licence to walk dogs in England? No. DEFRA's statutory guidance expressly excludes dog walkers and dog sitters who look after dogs in the owner's home. You will still want public liability insurance, and if you drive dogs anywhere, business use on your car policy. Check your council's Public Spaces Protection Order for the dog limit.

How much does a dog boarding licence cost? Each council sets its own fee. At Derby City Council a new home boarding licence costs £533 for 2026/27 and a renewal £378, with vet fees on top if needed. Commercial premises day care is £689. Higher star ratings earn a two or three year licence, which cuts the yearly cost.

What if I only board a dog at my house now and then? If your total trading income stays below the HMRC trading allowance, DEFRA's guidance says no licence is needed. That allowance is £1,000 now and £3,000 from 6 April 2027. Above it, the council decides whether you are trading using HMRC's nine badges of trade. Ring the licensing team first.

Is it the same in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland? No. The 2018 Regulations apply to England only. In Scotland and Wales, boarding is still licensed under the Animal Boarding Establishments Act 1963. That Act does not extend to Northern Ireland, which has its own rules. Wales has consulted on licensing home boarding more widely, so check with your council.

How many dogs can I legally walk at once? There is no national limit. Your council's Public Spaces Protection Order sets it, often at four or six, and it can differ between parks. Reigate and Banstead allows four per person and Sefton six. DEFRA's licensed day care standard is a maximum of six dogs at one time.
