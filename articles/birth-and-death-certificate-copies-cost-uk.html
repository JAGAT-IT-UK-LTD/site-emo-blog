One Reference Number Decides Whether Your Certificate Takes Four Days or Three Weeks

Ruhul Amin · Published 15 September 2026 · 5 min read · Figures checked September 2026

A copy of a birth, death, marriage or civil partnership certificate from the General Register Office costs 12.50 pounds. What changes the price and the wait is whether you already have the GRO index reference number, and almost nobody dealing with a death has it to hand.

Share Facebook X WhatsApp Pinterest LinkedIn

The short version

  • A certificate costs 12.50 pounds and is sent 4 days after you apply if you have the GRO index reference number.
  • Without the index reference it is 3.50 pounds extra for each search, and the certificate is sent 15 working days after you apply.
  • The priority service costs 38.50 pounds and is sent the next working day if you order by 4pm.
  • Extra copies are the same price. There is no bulk discount, so five death certificates is five times 12.50 pounds.
  • This covers England and Wales only. Scotland goes through National Records of Scotland, Northern Ireland through nidirect.
  • A birth or death registered abroad with the FCDO cannot be obtained from the GRO until the November of the following year. Sooner than that, it is roughly 50 pounds plus courier fees through the Overseas Registration Unit.

1. The fees, in full

These are the General Register Office fees published on GOV.UK, checked on 15 September 2026. They apply to birth, adoption, death, marriage and civil partnership certificates in England and Wales.

What you are orderingCostHow long
Certificate, with a GRO index reference number£12.50Sent 4 days after you apply
Additional search, where you do not have the reference£3.50 per searchSent 15 working days after you apply
Priority service£38.50Sent next working day if ordered by 4pm
Extra copiesSame price eachAs above

You register on the GRO website, which takes around ten minutes and needs a debit or credit card. You can also order by phone on 0300 123 1837, or by post using the GRO certificate application form, or from the local register office where the event was registered.

2. The index reference number, and why it matters so much

The GRO index reference is the number that tells the office exactly which entry in the register you want. With it, the order is a lookup. Without it, someone has to search, and each search costs 3.50 pounds on top of the certificate fee.

The time difference is larger than the money difference and is the part that actually hurts. With the reference, the certificate is sent four days after you apply. Without it, fifteen working days, which with weekends is three calendar weeks before it is even sent.

The trap is obvious once you say it out loud: the people who need death certificates urgently, to close bank accounts and start probate, are exactly the people who have never heard of a GRO index reference. You can find index references through the family history route on GOV.UK, and doing that before you order is worth the half hour if you are not in a rush.

The other half of the trap is the search fee itself. The 3.50 pounds is charged per search, not per certificate. If the details you give are vague enough that more than one search is needed, that is more than one 3.50 pound charge.

3. When the 38.50 pound priority service is worth it

Priority costs 38.50 pounds against the standard 12.50 pounds, so you are paying 26 pounds for speed. Ordered by 4pm, it is sent the next working day.

Whether that is worth it comes down to one question: is something else waiting on the certificate that costs more than 26 pounds to delay? Some honest cases where it is:

  • A bank or pension provider will not release funds without an original certificate and the estate has bills running.
  • A passport application has a deadline and the birth certificate is the missing document.
  • A property transaction or a probate application is sitting on hold.

And the cases where it is not: you want it for your own records, you are researching family history, or you simply feel that three weeks is too long. Those are real feelings but they are not 26 pounds of value.

One combination worth doing deliberately. If you need several copies and only one of them urgently, order one on priority and the rest standard. There is no discount for ordering in bulk anyway, so splitting the order costs you nothing in unit price.

4. Worked example: Yvonne in Ashton-under-Lyne

Yvonne's father died in August. Three organisations have each asked for an original death certificate, and she does not have a GRO index reference.

Standard route without the reference. Three certificates at 12.50 pounds is 37.50 pounds, plus 3.50 pounds for the search, which is 41 pounds. Sent fifteen working days after she applies.

If she finds the index reference first. Three certificates at 12.50 pounds is 37.50 pounds, with no search fee, sent four days after she applies. She saves 3.50 pounds and, far more usefully, about a fortnight.

If one is urgent. One on priority at 38.50 pounds plus two standard at 12.50 pounds is 63.50 pounds. The urgent one is sent the next working day if she ordered by 4pm.

There is a cheaper route she should check first. The local register office where the death was registered can also issue certificates, and if she is ordering shortly after the registration, that is often both quicker and simpler than going through the GRO at all. Certificate costs are one of several charges that stack up in the weeks after a death, and we have set out the rest in our guide to what a funeral actually costs.

5. What this does not cover

Being clear about the limits matters more here than usual, because getting it wrong means ordering from the wrong office and waiting for nothing.

Scotland and Northern Ireland are separate. The GRO covers England and Wales. Scottish certificates come from National Records of Scotland, Northern Irish ones through nidirect. Different processes, different fees.

Births and deaths registered abroad. If the event was registered with the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office Overseas Registration Unit, you cannot get a copy from the GRO until the November of the following year. If you need it before that, you email the Overseas Registration Unit with the name, the registration date and the reference number, and the cost is usually around 50 pounds plus courier fees. That is a materially different price and timescale from the domestic one.

A certificate is not probate. Getting death certificates is one of the first administrative steps after a death, but it does not by itself give you authority over the estate. That is a separate application with its own fees.

The honest verdict

12.50 pounds for a certificate is not a bad price and there is nothing to negotiate. The decision that is actually yours to make is whether to spend half an hour finding the GRO index reference before you order. That half hour saves 3.50 pounds and turns a fifteen working day wait into four days, which is the better bargain by a distance.

The priority service at 38.50 pounds is fairly priced for what it is, but only buy it when something that costs more than 26 pounds is genuinely waiting on the certificate. And if the death was registered recently, check the local register office before the GRO. Same document, often faster.

Frequently asked

Do I get a discount for ordering several copies? No. GOV.UK states that extra copies are the same price, so five certificates cost five times 12.50 pounds. There is no bulk rate.

How do I find a GRO index reference number? Through the family history research route on GOV.UK, which lets you search the indexes. Doing this before you order avoids the 3.50 pound per search fee and cuts the wait from fifteen working days to four days.

Can I use a photocopy instead of an original certificate? Usually not. Banks, pension providers and probate registries generally want an original certified copy, which is why people end up ordering several. Some organisations will accept a certified copy made by a solicitor, so it is worth asking before ordering more.

What if the death was registered in Scotland? The GRO fees on this page do not apply. Scottish certificates are ordered through National Records of Scotland, which has its own fee scale and process.
