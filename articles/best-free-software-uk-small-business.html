MTD for Income Tax Started in April. Only Three Free Tools Actually Cover It

1. What changed on 6 April 2026

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax Self Assessment began on 6 April 2026. If you are a sole trader or landlord with qualifying income over £50,000, you must now keep digital records and send quarterly updates to HMRC using compatible software.

Two consequences people underestimate. A spreadsheet on its own is no longer enough unless it is bridged by compatible software. And "compatible" is a specific technical status, not a claim a company can simply make, so a tool being good at bookkeeping does not mean it can file.

If your qualifying income is under the threshold, none of this applies to you yet and you can choose software purely on features. Thresholds step down in later phases, so it is worth choosing something you will not have to abandon.

3. The FreeAgent loophole worth checking

This is the one that saves the most money and the fewest people use.

FreeAgent is free for NatWest, RBS and Ulster Bank business account holders. Not a trial, not a limited tier. It is a full accounting package, normally a paid product, included with the business account.

If you already bank with one of those, you may be paying for software you could have for nothing. If you are choosing a business bank account, this is worth roughly £150 to £300 a year and should be part of the comparison rather than an afterthought.

Before you subscribe to anything, check whether your bank already gives you an accounting package. A surprising number of UK sole traders pay twice for the same thing.

Price the plan that does what you need, not the one in the advert. One thing worth paying for: if your accountant works daily in a particular package, using it will usually cost you less in their time than you save on the subscription. Ask them before you choose.

5. Free tools for everything that is not accounts

Accounts is where free is now constrained by law. Everywhere else, free is genuinely good.

  • Documents and spreadsheets. LibreOffice, free and offline, opens and saves Microsoft formats. Google Docs and Sheets if you want collaboration. Neither costs anything and both are enough for almost every small business.
  • Email and domain. Free webmail is fine to start, but an address at your own domain changes how you are read. That is the cost of a domain, not of software.
  • Design. Canva free tier covers social posts, flyers and simple logos. GIMP if you need proper image editing and refuse to pay for Photoshop.
  • Video calls. Google Meet and the Zoom free tier are enough for client calls. The 40 minute limit on Zoom free is a real constraint for long meetings.
  • Project and task tracking. Trello, Notion and ClickUp all have free tiers that comfortably handle a business with under five people.
  • Password management. Bitwarden free tier is genuinely full featured and syncs across devices. This is the free tool most worth adopting today.
  • Scheduling. Cal.com and Calendly free tiers remove the email chain of finding a time.
  • Invoicing without full accounts. Wave and Zoho Invoice both do free invoicing well if your bookkeeping lives elsewhere.

6. A worked stack for a real business

Rachel, sole trader photographer in Sheffield. Turnover £41,000. Banks with NatWest.

Her MTD position. Under the £50,000 qualifying income threshold, so quarterly filing does not apply to her yet. But she is close enough that she wants software she will not have to leave.

Accounts: FreeAgent, £0. Free with her NatWest business account. She had been about to sign up for Xero Grow at £33 a month. Saved: £396 a year.

Documents: Google free tier, £0. She pays £12 a year for the domain and uses free forwarding rather than a paid mailbox.

Design: Canva free, £0. Enough for her Instagram and client galleries.

Passwords: Bitwarden free, £0. Scheduling: Cal.com free, £0.

What she does pay for: £108 a year. Adobe Lightroom at £96, because free alternatives genuinely do not do what she needs for editing, and £12 for the domain.

Total software cost £108 a year against roughly £600 for a comparable paid stack. The single biggest saving was not a clever free tool, it was checking what her bank already included.

7. Where free stops being free

  • A turnover ceiling you will cross. Zoho Books Free ends at £35,000. If you are at £30,000 and growing, you are choosing where you will be in eighteen months, not where you are now.
  • Payroll. The moment you employ anyone, almost every free accounting tier stops covering you. Payroll is where free reliably ends.
  • Data export. Before committing, check you can export your records in a usable format. A tool you cannot leave is not free, it is deferred cost.
  • MTD compatibility changing. A compatible status is not permanent. Check the GOV.UK list at the start of each tax year rather than assuming.
  • Free tiers that fund themselves with your data. Read what a free product does with your customer list. In accounting this is rare, in marketing tools it is not.
  • Support. Free tiers usually mean forum support. In the week before a filing deadline that difference is worth money.

The honest verdict

Do these three things in order. Check whether your bank already gives you FreeAgent, because if you are with NatWest, RBS or Ulster Bank the accounting question is already answered and it costs nothing. If not, and you are under £35,000 turnover, Zoho Books Free is a real accounting package that can file for MTD. Above that, price the plan you actually need rather than the headline tier, and ask your accountant which package they work in before you decide.

Everything outside accounts, documents, design, passwords, scheduling, calls, task tracking, can be run for nothing at small business scale without compromise. That has not changed and probably will not.

What did change on 6 April 2026 is that for anyone over £50,000, accounting software stopped being optional. Choose it on whether it can file, then on price.

Frequently asked

Can I still use a spreadsheet for MTD? Only with bridging software that connects it to HMRC. A spreadsheet alone does not satisfy the digital records and quarterly filing requirement.

What counts as qualifying income for the £50,000 threshold? Gross income from self employment and property before expenses, not profit. This catches people out in the same way the £1,000 trading allowance does.

Is free accounting software safe for my business records? Reputable free tiers from established companies run on the same infrastructure as their paid tiers. The risk is not security, it is the tier being withdrawn or capped.

Do I need software at all if I am under the threshold? Not legally, yet. But thresholds step down in later phases, so starting on something compatible now saves a migration later.
