Clear Books, Zoho Books and Sage offer free plans that can, and FreeAgent is free with some business bank accounts. That matters because MTD reaches anyone over £30,000 from April 2027.

  • Several free tools can file MTD quarterly updates: Clear Books free plan, Zoho Books Free (up to 1,000 invoices a year) and Sage's Sole Trader Free plan. Landlord Studio's free plan covers up to three units but charges £5 per MTD submission.

The best free software for a UK small business is mostly about accounts now. Everything else, from documents to passwords, has been free and good for years.

Accounts changed because of Making Tax Digital. For a growing number of sole traders and landlords, free accounting software has to be able to file with HMRC, not just keep records.

1. What changed on 6 April 2026, and what comes next?

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax began on 6 April 2026. Sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000 in the 2024 to 2025 tax year must now keep digital records.

They also send quarterly updates to HMRC using compatible software. GOV.UK sets the deadlines as 7 August, 7 November, 7 February and 7 May.

The net widens fast. GOV.UK says the threshold drops to £30,000 from 6 April 2027, based on 2025 to 2026 income, and to £20,000 from 6 April 2028, based on 2026 to 2027 income.

Our guides to the April 2027 £30,000 threshold and the 2028 £20,000 threshold show who gets caught in each wave.

There is a small breathing space. For the 2026 to 2027 tax year, GOV.UK says HMRC will not give penalty points for late quarterly updates.

Two points people underestimate. A spreadsheet on its own is not enough unless compatible bridging software links it to HMRC.

And "compatible" is a specific status, not a marketing claim. A tool being good at bookkeeping does not mean it can file.

If your qualifying income is under the threshold, none of this applies yet. But with the threshold stepping down, choose something you will not have to abandon.

3. Can you get FreeAgent free through your bank?

This is the one that saves the most money, and the fewest people use it.

FreeAgent says its software is free for sole traders with a NatWest, Royal Bank of Scotland or Ulster Bank business current account, for as long as you keep the account.

Mettle customers qualify too, as long as they make at least one transaction a month. It is the full package, not a trial or a cut down tier.

The normal sole trader price is £19 a month plus VAT, which is £228 a year before VAT. Some add ons, such as unlimited receipt capture, can still cost extra.

If you already bank with one of these, you may be paying for software you could have for nothing. If you are choosing a business bank account, put this in the comparison.

Before you subscribe to anything, check whether your bank already gives you an accounting package. It is worth £228 a year before VAT, and it costs nothing to ask.

5. What free software covers everything else?

Accounts is where free is now constrained by law. Everywhere else, free is genuinely good.

  • Documents and spreadsheets. LibreOffice, free and offline, opens and saves Microsoft formats. Google Docs and Sheets if you want to work with others. Both are enough for almost every small business.
  • Email and domain. Free webmail is fine to start, but an address at your own domain changes how clients read you. That is the cost of a domain, not of software.
  • Design. Canva's free tier covers social posts, flyers and simple logos. GIMP if you need proper image editing and refuse to pay for Photoshop.
  • Video calls. Google Meet and the Zoom free plan are enough for client calls. Zoom ends free meetings at 40 minutes, which is a real limit for long sessions.
  • Project and task tracking. Trello, Notion and ClickUp all have free tiers that handle a very small team comfortably.
  • Password management. Bitwarden's free tier is full featured and syncs across devices. Our guide to password managers explains what risk it removes.
  • Scheduling. Cal.com and Calendly free tiers end the email chain of finding a time.
  • Invoicing without full accounts. Wave and Zoho Invoice both do free invoicing well if your bookkeeping lives elsewhere.

6. A worked stack for a real business

Rachel, sole trader photographer in Sheffield. Turnover £41,000. Banks with NatWest.

Her MTD position. She is under £50,000, so quarterly updates did not start for her in April 2026.

But her 2025 to 2026 turnover of £41,000 is over £30,000. Unless her turnover falls, MTD is due to apply to her from 6 April 2027, so she needs compatible software within months.

Accounts: FreeAgent, £0. Free with her NatWest business account. She had been about to sign up for Xero Grow at £39 a month after the offer. Saved: £468 a year before VAT.

Documents: Google free tier, £0. She pays only for her domain name and uses free forwarding rather than a paid mailbox.

Design: Canva free, £0. Enough for her Instagram and client galleries.

Passwords: Bitwarden free, £0. Scheduling: Cal.com free, £0.

What she does pay for: Adobe Lightroom. Adobe lists the Lightroom plan at £11.99 a month on an annual plan billed monthly, so £143.88 a year. Free editors do not do what she needs.

The sums. Xero Grow plus Lightroom would have cost her £611.88 a year before the domain and VAT on Xero. Her stack costs £143.88 plus the domain.

The biggest saving was not a clever free tool. It was checking what her bank already included.

7. Where does free stop being free?

  • A limit you will cross. Zoho Books Free stops at 1,000 invoices a year and one user. Landlord Studio's free plan stops at three units. Choose for where you will be in eighteen months.
  • Per submission charges. A free plan that charges per filing, like Landlord Studio's £5, adds up across four quarterly updates.
  • Payroll. The moment you employ anyone, most free accounting tiers stop covering you. Clear Books says payroll needs a paid plan.
  • VAT registration. Sage's free plan is for non VAT registered sole traders, and Clear Books free does not file VAT. Registering for VAT usually means paying.
  • Data export. Before committing, check you can export your records in a usable format. A tool you cannot leave is not free, it is deferred cost.
  • MTD compatibility changing. Compatible status is not permanent. Check the GOV.UK finder at the start of each tax year.
  • Support. Free tiers usually mean help articles and community forums. In the week before a deadline, that difference is worth money.

The honest verdict

Do three things in order. First, check whether your bank already gives you FreeAgent. With NatWest, RBS, Ulster Bank or an active Mettle account, the accounting question may already be answered for nothing.

If not, try Clear Books free or Zoho Books Free, and confirm each on the GOV.UK finder. Landlords with up to three units can look at Landlord Studio, at £5 a submission.

Above those limits, price the plan you actually need after any introductory offer ends. Ask your accountant which package they use before you decide.

Everything outside accounts, documents, design, passwords, scheduling, calls and task tracking, can run for nothing at small business scale.

What changed on 6 April 2026 is that accounting software stopped being optional for anyone over £50,000. From April 2027 that line is £30,000. Choose on whether it can file, then on price.

Frequently asked questions

Is there free MTD software for sole traders?

Yes. Clear Books, Zoho Books and Sage all describe free plans that can send MTD for Income Tax quarterly updates, each with limits. FreeAgent is free if you bank with NatWest, RBS, Ulster Bank or Mettle. Always confirm a product on HMRC's software finder on GOV.UK before you rely on it.

Can I still use a spreadsheet for Making Tax Digital?

Only with bridging software that connects the spreadsheet to HMRC and is itself compatible. A spreadsheet alone does not meet the digital records and quarterly update rules. Bridging suits people who already keep tidy spreadsheets, but a free accounting package often does the same job with less copying and fewer errors.

What counts as qualifying income for the MTD threshold?

GOV.UK says qualifying income is the amount before expenses, also known as turnover. It adds together self employment and property income. Employment pay, dividends, pensions and partnership profit shares do not count. So a sole trader with £45,000 turnover and £20,000 profit is judged on the £45,000, not the profit.

Do I need accounting software if I am under the threshold?

Not legally, yet. But GOV.UK says the threshold falls to £30,000 from 6 April 2027 and £20,000 from 6 April 2028. Starting on compatible software now saves a rushed switch later. Separately, the trading allowance stays £1,000 for 2026 to 2027 and rises to £3,000 from 6 April 2027.

Is free accounting software safe for my business records?

Established providers generally run free and paid plans on the same systems, so the bigger risk is not security. It is a free plan being capped, withdrawn or given new charges. Check you can export your records in a usable format, and keep your own backup copy at least once a quarter.
