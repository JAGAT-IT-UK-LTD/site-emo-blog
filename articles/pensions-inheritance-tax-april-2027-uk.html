From 6 April 2027 An Unused Pension Counts For Inheritance Tax. HMRC Says 10,500 Estates A Year Will Pay Who Never Would Have. Here Is The Arithmetic.

For a decade the advice was simple: spend your ISA, keep your pension, because the pension passed to your children free of Inheritance Tax. From 6 April 2027 that stops. Most unused pension funds and death benefits join the rest of the estate. The change is in law, HMRC has published who it expects to hit, and executors get a new job. Here is what it does, what it leaves alone, and what it costs a family with a £300,000 pot.

The short version

  • From 6 April 2027 most unused pension funds and death benefits are part of the estate for Inheritance Tax. Legislated in the Finance Act 2026, Royal Assent 18 March 2026. Source: HMRC policy paper, 26 November 2025.
  • HMRC's own estimate for 2027 to 2028: 213,000 estates with inheritable pension wealth, 10,500 newly liable, 38,500 paying more, average extra bill about £34,000. The Treasury expects £710 million in the first year, rising to £1,665 million by 2030 to 2031.
  • Anything left to a spouse or civil partner stays exempt. Death in service payments from a registered scheme and dependants' scheme pensions from defined benefit schemes are excluded entirely.
  • The executor, not the pension scheme, reports and pays. They can order the scheme to withhold 50% of the taxable benefit for up to 15 months while the tax is settled.
  • The £325,000 nil-rate band and 40% rate are unchanged. A pension only creates a bill where the whole estate, pension included, clears the thresholds.
  • Income tax on inherited pensions where death is at 75 or over still applies. That is the double charge the headlines mean, and it is real for higher-rate beneficiaries.

1. What changes on 6 April 2027, and what does not

Today most UK pension schemes are discretionary: the trustees decide who receives an unused pot, so it sits outside the estate for Inheritance Tax. HMRC's policy paper says this has let pensions be "marketed as a tax planning vehicle to transfer wealth", especially since the 2015 pension freedoms and the end of the lifetime allowance in 2023. Families already inside the estate rules have one more lever worth knowing about, because redirecting an inheritance with a deed of variation has to be done within two years of the death.

From 6 April 2027, for deaths on or after that date, most unused pension funds and pension death benefits are added to the value of the estate. The rules are in the Finance Act 2026, which received Royal Assent on 18 March 2026. The rate and thresholds do not change: 40% above a £325,000 nil-rate band, plus up to £175,000 of residence nil-rate band where a home passes to direct descendants, with unused bands transferable between spouses.

What is left alone matters as much:

  • Spouse and civil partner exemption stays. A pension passing to a surviving spouse or civil partner is exempt, as any other asset would be.
  • Death in service benefits from a registered pension scheme are excluded from the estate entirely.
  • Dependants' scheme pensions from a defined benefit scheme, the NHS or teachers' pension paid to a widow for example, are excluded.
  • Charity exemption continues.
  • Continuing annuities and funds under £1,000 are outside the withholding process described below.

So a married couple where everything passes to the survivor sees no change on the first death. The bill, if any, arrives on the second.

2. Who is actually caught: HMRC's numbers

The tax information and impact note gives the government's own estimate for 2027 to 2028. Of around 213,000 estates with inheritable pension wealth, about 10,500 will have an Inheritance Tax liability where previously they would not, and about 38,500 will pay more than they would have. The average liability rises by around £34,000 when pensions are counted. HMRC calls these static estimates and a maximum, because people will draw pensions down faster or plan differently.

The Exchequer figures in the same note are £710 million in 2027 to 2028, £1,485 million the year after, and £1,665 million by 2030 to 2031, certified by the Office for Budget Responsibility.

Read against the roughly 600,000 to 650,000 deaths a year in the UK, that is a change for a minority: estates already near or above the thresholds, and estates where the pension is the asset that tips them over. A single homeowner with a £250,000 house and a £150,000 pension, no spouse, children inheriting, was untaxed under the old rules and is over the £500,000 combined bands under the new ones. That is the profile the 10,500 figure describes.

3. The executor's new job, and the 50% withholding

The original consultation proposed making pension scheme administrators liable. The government instead moved the duty to personal representatives, the executors or administrators of the estate. They must find out what pension funds exist, get valuations from each scheme, include them in the IHT400 return, and pay the tax.

Two tools come with that. First, where personal representatives reasonably expect Inheritance Tax to be due, they can direct a scheme to withhold 50% of the taxable benefit for up to 15 months from the date of death, and to pay the tax to HMRC out of it before releasing the rest to the beneficiaries. Second, once they have clearance from HMRC, personal representatives are discharged from liability for pensions discovered later.

For a family that means two things. Probate for an estate with a pension will take longer, because a valuation from every scheme is now on the critical path. And whoever is named as executor should have a list of every pension the person holds, including the small forgotten ones, because the executor is now responsible for reporting them. Executors should budget for the paperwork as well, which we price in what extra copies of a birth or death certificate cost.

There is a second tax to keep in view. Where the person dies at 75 or over, the beneficiary already pays income tax at their own marginal rate on money drawn from an inherited pension. That does not change. From April 2027 the pot can suffer 40% Inheritance Tax and then income tax on what is left, which for a higher-rate beneficiary is the double charge the headlines describe.

4. A worked example in Norwich

Margaret in Norwich is 78, widowed, with a house worth £320,000, £60,000 in savings, and £300,000 in a drawdown pension she has barely touched because she was told to spend other money first. Everything goes to her two children.

Under today's rules. Her estate for Inheritance Tax is £380,000. Her own nil-rate band is £325,000 plus her late husband's unused £325,000, plus the residence band because the house goes to her children. Well under. Tax: nil. The £300,000 pension passes to the children outside the estate; because she is over 75 they pay income tax as they draw it, but no Inheritance Tax.

If she dies on or after 6 April 2027. Her estate becomes £680,000. Her combined nil-rate bands with the transferred allowance are £650,000, plus residence nil-rate band of up to £350,000 with her husband's transferred. Total available: £1,000,000. Tax: still nil, on our arithmetic.

Now change one fact. Margaret was never married. Her allowances are £325,000 plus £175,000 residence band: £500,000. Estate £680,000. Taxable £180,000 at 40%: £72,000. Under the old rules her estate paid nothing. The children then draw the remaining pension and pay income tax on it as well. The difference between the two Margarets is not the pension. It is the transferable allowance from a spouse.

5. What is worth doing before April 2027, and what is not

Worth doing: writing down every pension you hold and telling your executor where the list is; checking your nomination forms so the scheme knows who you want the money to go to, especially for a spouse where the exemption applies; and, if you are over 75 with a large pot and no spouse, asking a regulated adviser whether drawing more now and gifting it under the seven-year rule beats leaving it. That is a personal decision and depends on how long you live. Giving money away while you are alive has its own timetable, which we set out in the seven year rule on money you give away.

Not worth doing: panic-withdrawing a pension into a bank account. Money in the bank is already in the estate, so the move gains nothing for Inheritance Tax and can cost income tax at a higher rate in the year you take it. And nothing needs doing at all if your estate including the pension is under £325,000, or under £500,000 with a home going to children, or if everything goes to a spouse.

6. The honest verdict

For most people this changes nothing. HMRC expects 10,500 estates a year to become liable and 38,500 to pay more, out of 213,000 with pension wealth and well over 600,000 deaths.

It bites where there is no spouse to pass to, and the pension is what carries the estate over the bands. That is a single homeowner with a decent pot, and for them the bill can be tens of thousands.

Do the free things now: a pension list for your executor, nominations checked. Leave the drawdown decisions to a regulated adviser, and do not empty a pension into a bank account because of a headline.

Frequently asked

Does this affect someone who dies before 6 April 2027? No. The rules apply to deaths on or after 6 April 2027. The date of death decides, not when the pension is paid out.

My pension is going to my wife. Is it taxed? No. Transfers to a spouse or civil partner remain exempt from Inheritance Tax. The change matters on the second death, when the pot passes to children or others.

Who pays the tax, the scheme or my family? The personal representatives of the estate report and pay it. They can direct the scheme to hold back 50% of the taxable benefit for up to 15 months and pay the tax from it.

Is income tax on inherited pensions changing too? No. Where the person dies at 75 or over, beneficiaries still pay income tax on withdrawals. From April 2027 both taxes can apply to the same pot.
