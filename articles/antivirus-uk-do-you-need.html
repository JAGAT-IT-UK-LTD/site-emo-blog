Do I Need Antivirus in the UK? Is Microsoft Defender Enough in 2026?

The short version

  • Most UK households on an up to date Windows 11 PC do not need to buy antivirus. Microsoft Defender is built in, free and switched on by default.
  • Defender blocked 396 of 400 live attacks, 99.0%, in AV-Comparatives' February to May 2026 real world test. The best paid suite, Kaspersky, blocked 399, or 99.8%. Bitdefender managed 99.5%.
  • Defender's weak spot in 2026 is false alarms, not missed malware. In the September 2026 malware test it caught 99.89% of samples but raised 28 false alarms, which cut its award to Standard.
  • At AV-TEST, Defender scored full marks for protection in all four 2026 rounds. It lost half a point on performance in June and August. Avast Free scored full marks on every count.
  • Fraud by persuasion is the bigger money risk. UK Finance counted £1.28 billion stolen in 2025, and £576.4 million of it was push payment fraud, where victims send the money themselves. No antivirus stops that.
  • Windows 10 users get free security updates until 12 October 2027 if they enrol in Extended Security Updates. Without them, no antivirus fixes an unpatched system.

Do I need antivirus in the UK at all?

You need antivirus protection running on any Windows PC. You almost certainly have it already.

Microsoft Defender comes with Windows 11 and Windows 10. It is on by default and updates itself through Windows Update.

So the real question is not whether you need antivirus. It is whether you need to pay for a second one on top. For most UK households, the answer is no.

Microsoft says the same in its own guidance. Which? is more cautious, and its testers rate some paid products higher. Both points are fair, and the lab results below show why.

Two labs are worth reading. AV-TEST is in Germany and AV-Comparatives is in Austria.

Three things stand out. First, Defender now sits in the same band as products that charge a yearly fee.

Second, a free third party product, Avast Free, scored perfectly at AV-TEST all year. Free and weak are no longer the same word.

Third, Defender had a rough September. It still caught 99.89% of samples, but it wrongly flagged 28 clean files. In March it flagged only 3.

A false alarm is a nuisance, not a breach. It can block a program you trust until you tell Windows to allow it. That is annoying, but it does not cost you money.

In 400 live attacks, Defender let through four and the best paid suite let through one. That is the whole size of the argument.

How big is the gap between free and paid antivirus?

Be fair to the paid products. The gap is genuine.

In the real world test, Defender missed four attacks out of 400 and Kaspersky missed one. If you meet the attack in that gap, the difference is total.

But translate it. These labs throw hundreds of live threats at each product on purpose. A typical home user meets a handful of risky moments a year.

Those moments look like a dodgy download, an email attachment or a fake update prompt. Most of them are stopped by browser warnings, by Defender, or by not clicking.

So the useful question is not which product wins in a lab. It is whether your own habits put you near the gap.

If you download cracked software, install browser extensions casually, or share a family PC with someone who does, you meet more threats. Then the extra protection starts to matter.

If your machine is used for email, banking, shopping and streaming, you are a long way from the gap.

What are UK households really losing money to?

This is the part that changes the whole purchase.

UK Finance's Annual Fraud Report 2026 counts £1.28 billion stolen through payment fraud in 2025, up 4% on 2024.

  • Unauthorised fraud, such as card misuse, cost £703.4 million, down 5%, across 3.81 million cases, up 11%.
  • Authorised push payment fraud rose 19% to £576.4 million across 248,070 cases. That is fraud where the victim is talked into sending the money.
  • Personal victims lost £500.8 million of that push payment total.
  • Investment scams rose 40% to a record £221.5 million, across 14,893 cases.

Put those numbers side by side. Push payment fraud alone is about 45% of all losses, and that money leaves because a person was persuaded.

Divide the totals by the cases and the average push payment loss is about £2,324. The average investment scam loss is about £14,873.

Antivirus does not step in when you willingly transfer money to someone pretending to be your bank. No subscription protects you from that. Habits do.

If it does happen, banks must now reimburse most push payment victims. Our guide to the £85,000 APP fraud reimbursement rules explains what you can claim back.

What does Defender include, and what does it leave out?

Microsoft Defender sits inside the Windows Security app. If you have never installed anything else, it is already running.

Included: real time malware scanning, cloud delivered protection, and ransomware protection through Controlled Folder Access, which is worth switching on yourself.

You also get a firewall, SmartScreen reputation checks in Edge, and parental controls through Microsoft Family Safety.

Not included: a VPN, a password manager, dark web monitoring, identity theft cover, or one licence covering your phone and a partner's Mac.

Those extras are most of what a paid suite is really selling you at renewal. If you want a VPN, read what a VPN does and does not do in the UK before paying for one in a bundle.

Do I need antivirus on Windows 10 now?

Windows 10 main support ended on 14 October 2025. Microsoft then extended the free consumer Extended Security Updates programme by a year.

Enrolled home PCs now get security fixes until 12 October 2027. Devices already enrolled roll over automatically.

That matters more than any antivirus. Antivirus scans files, but it cannot patch holes in the operating system underneath.

Check that your PC is enrolled in Settings, under Windows Update. Then plan a move to Windows 11 or a supported system before October 2027. Our guide to what changed with Windows 10 support walks through it.

When is paid antivirus worth it?

There are real cases, and it would not be honest to pretend otherwise.

  • You want one licence across several devices and people. A family plan covering Windows, Android and macOS is a genuine convenience Defender does not offer.
  • You are buying the bundle, not the engine. If a suite includes a VPN and a password manager you would otherwise buy, compare it with buying those two alone. Sometimes the bundle wins.
  • You share the machine with someone who clicks anything. A teenager downloading game mods does meet more threats. The extra protection earns its place.
  • You want someone to phone. Paid products come with support. For a less confident user, or a parent you help from a distance, that has value no test score shows.

When it is not worth it: one modern Windows 11 machine, one careful adult, used for ordinary things. You would be paying every year for a few attacks in 400, plus a heavier program on your laptop.

What does a sensible family set up cost?

The Okafors in Leeds: two adults, two teenagers, one Windows 11 laptop and one older Windows 10 desktop.

Step one, costs £0. They keep Microsoft Defender on both machines and switch on Controlled Folder Access. They enrol the Windows 10 desktop in Extended Security Updates, which keeps it patched until 12 October 2027.

Step two, costs £0. They turn on two step verification for every email account and for online banking. Email comes first, because it resets every other password.

Step three, $47.88 a year. They take Bitwarden's Families plan, which covers up to six people and is billed in US dollars. The free Bitwarden plan would also work for each adult on their own.

What they skip. A paid antivirus suite. Bitdefender's own terms say its first year price is followed by a renewal price that may be higher, so the cheap offer is not the real cost.

What they are now protected against. Leaked passwords tried against their email, account takeover, and the reused password problem. Defender still blocks the vast majority of malware.

Why it adds up. One average push payment loss, about £2,324, would pay for decades of that password manager. Their biggest risk was never the four attacks in 400. It was a convincing message about their money.

What are the traps in antivirus pricing?

  • The renewal jump. First year offers are common, and the renewal price can be much higher. Put the renewal date in your diary the day you buy.
  • Auto renewal that is hard to cancel. New UK subscription rules on easy cancellation are still being brought in. Check how to cancel online before you subscribe.
  • Scareware in free versions. Some free products show alarming pop ups about issues that only an upgrade can fix. Defender does not do this.
  • Bundled VPNs with limits. The included VPN often has a small data allowance unless you upgrade again.
  • Paying for a scan you already have. Never pay a phone caller or a pop up to clean your PC. That call is the fraud.

The honest verdict

Do you need to buy antivirus in the UK? For a single, up to date Windows 11 machine used by a careful adult, no. Microsoft Defender is enough.

It blocked 99.0% of live attacks in 2026 real world testing, scored full marks for protection at AV-TEST all year, and costs nothing. Its September false alarms are a real mark against it, but they are an annoyance rather than a danger.

Buy a paid suite if you want family cover across phones and Macs, if you truly want the bundled extras, or if someone in the house downloads things they should not. Those are good reasons.

And keep the £1.28 billion in view. Most of the money UK households lose is lost to persuasion, not infection.

Two step verification on your email, unique passwords, and a habit of never acting on an urgent message about money will protect you far more than any upgrade.

Frequently asked questions

Is Windows Defender enough for online banking?

For most people, yes. Defender blocks known malware and SmartScreen warns about fake sites. The bigger banking risk is being persuaded to send money, which no antivirus stops. Turn on two step verification, use your bank's own app, and never move money because a caller or message tells you to.

Do I need antivirus for a Mac?

Most Mac users do not need to buy one. macOS has built in malware checks and blocks unsigned apps by default. Mac malware does exist, so keep macOS updated and install apps from the App Store or known developers. As on Windows, account security and careful habits matter more than the product.

Do I need antivirus on my iPhone or Android phone?

On an iPhone, no. Apps run in a locked box and an iPhone antivirus app cannot scan the system. On Android, install from the Play Store, keep the phone updated and check app permissions. Paid mobile security apps mostly sell extras like VPNs and call blocking rather than extra protection.

Is Windows Defender the same as Microsoft Defender?

Yes. The built in antivirus was called Windows Defender and now appears as Microsoft Defender Antivirus inside the Windows Security app. Older articles calling Windows Defender weak usually describe a version from years ago. Its 2026 test results are far stronger than its reputation.

Should I run two antivirus products at once?

No. Two real time scanners can clash, slow your PC and flag each other as threats. If you install a paid product, Defender's real time scanning switches itself off automatically. If you later remove the paid product, check that Defender has switched back on in Windows Security.
