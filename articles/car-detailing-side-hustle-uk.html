Car Detailing Side Hustle in the UK: What It Really Pays

Published 21 July 2026 · Updated 14 November 2026 · 12 min read · Figures checked November 2026

A car detailing side hustle in the UK can clear around £1,000 a month from weekends once you are booked out, but it is hard physical work, not passive income. You can start with roughly £250 of kit. The first tax point to know is HMRC's £1,000 trading allowance: go over it in gross income and you must register for Self Assessment.

This guide covers what to charge, what to buy first, the water and weather problems nobody mentions, and the tax and waste water rules you must get right.

The short version
  • A mobile valet earns roughly £20 to £80 per car. A proper detail with machine polishing earns £180 to £300 and takes most of a day.
  • You can start with about £250 of kit. You do not need an expensive machine polisher in month one.
  • The two things that actually limit you are water access and weather. Solve those before you buy anything.
  • Public liability insurance is non negotiable. You are working on someone's second most valuable possession, and one slip can cost far more than the job paid.
  • Tax: once gross trading income passes £1,000 in a tax year, register for Self Assessment by 5 October after that tax year ends. Class 4 National Insurance is 6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270.
  • Waste water must never go into a surface water drain. That rule comes straight from GOV.UK pollution guidance.
  • The money is in repeat maintenance customers and pre sale preparation, not one off deep cleans.

1. Valeting or detailing: which business are you starting?

People use the two words as if they mean the same thing. They are different businesses, with different customers and different margins.

Valeting is cleaning. Wash, wheels, interior vacuum, glass, dress the trim. One to three hours. The customer wants a clean car.

Detailing is correction. Decontamination, clay bar, machine polishing to remove swirl marks and light scratches, then a sealant or ceramic coating. Six hours to two days. The customer wants the paint to look better than it did.

Most beginners call themselves detailers, charge valeting prices and do valeting work. Decide which you are.

The valeting customer wants speed and a fair price. The detailing customer wants proof you know what you are doing, and will pay several times as much for it.

The sensible path is to start as a valet. Build a round of repeat customers. Add detailing once you have practised machine polishing on your own car and a few friends' cars.

Polishing badly can burn through the lacquer. That is a respray, not a refund.

3. What kit do you need to start a car detailing side hustle?

Buy in three stages. Spend on the next stage only when the last one is paying for itself.

Stage one, about £250, enough to take money.

  • Two buckets with grit guards, and a wash mitt. The two bucket method is the difference between cleaning a car and scratching it
  • pH neutral shampoo, wheel cleaner, iron fallout remover, glass cleaner, interior detailer, tyre dressing
  • A stack of microfibre cloths and two large drying towels. Buy more than you think, they get filthy
  • A decent wet and dry vacuum with a crevice tool
  • A pressure washer, entry level is fine to start
  • Kneeling mat, detailing brushes, an applicator set

Stage two, once you are booked out. A snow foam lance, a water tank and 12V pump so you are not dependent on the customer's tap, and a decent extractor for interiors.

Stage three, only after practice. A dual action polisher, a pad set and compounds. A dual action machine is far more forgiving than a rotary and is what you should learn on.

You can put a starter kit together from Amazon UK, though for chemicals the specialist UK detailing suppliers are usually better value per litre and sell in 5L containers. As an Amazon Associate we earn from qualifying purchases.

What not to buy in month one: a ceramic coating kit, a rotary polisher, a branded uniform, or a van wrap. People buy all of those instead of finding customers.

Keep every receipt. Kit, chemicals and fuel for the business are costs you can set against your income for tax, as long as you do not also claim the trading allowance.

4. How do you handle water, power and the weather?

This is the part that quietly kills mobile detailing businesses. Hardly anyone writes about it.

Water. Many customers will not want you using their outside tap. Plenty of flats and terraces do not have one.

A 100 to 200 litre tank in the back of the van with a 12V pump removes that objection. It lets you say yes to jobs your competitors turn down. After the basics, it is the best equipment purchase you can make.

Power. Same problem with extension leads through a window. A leisure battery and an inverter, or a petrol pressure washer, makes you independent.

Weather. This is the real one. In a British winter you will lose working days to rain.

Detailing in the cold is miserable. It also gives worse results, because many products behave differently when it is cold. Plan for it:

  • Push interior work in winter. It does not depend on the weather and it pays well.
  • Build a rain policy into your booking messages, so cancellations are not awkward.
  • If you can use a garage, a unit or even a carport, your winter income can be far higher.

6. Where do car detailing customers come from?

  1. Facebook local groups and Marketplace. In our experience, the biggest source for mobile detailers. Free, and the audience is already local.
  2. Before and after photographs. This trade sells on images more than almost any other. Same angle, same light, side by side. Post every job.
  3. Google Business Profile. Free, and "car valeting near me" is a search people make when they are ready to book. Ask for reviews from day one.
  4. Small used car dealers. Steady repeat work preparing stock. Lower price per car, but it is volume and it fills weekdays.
  5. Private sellers. Someone about to list a car will often pay for a pre sale clean to help it sell. Tell them to have the paperwork ready too, since a buyer will check the MOT history before they view, and a clean car with a messy history still sits unsold.
  6. Street presence. Working on a driveway in good weather gets neighbours asking. Have cards in your pocket.

Repeat customers are the goal. A monthly maintenance wash for a round of cars in one area cuts your travel time and fills your diary in advance.

7. Do you need insurance or a licence, and what about tax?

These are the rules that catch new detailers out. Get them right before your first paid job.

  • Public liability insurance. Cover of at least £1m is a common starting point. Prices depend on the cover and your turnover, so get several quotes. This is the one thing you must not skip. Damage claims on paintwork are expensive.
  • Motor trade or customer vehicle cover if you ever move a customer's car, even a few feet on a driveway. Standard public liability does not usually cover driving it.
  • Waste water. GOV.UK pollution guidance lists vehicle washing as a source of contaminated water. That water must not go into a surface water drain, because those drains run straight to rivers and streams. It can go to a foul drain only with your water company's permission. In practice, never wash where run off reaches a roadside gully, and ask what the customer's drive drains to.
  • Self Assessment. HMRC's trading allowance lets you earn up to £1,000 a year in gross trading income tax free. Above that, you must register for Self Assessment by 5 October after the tax year ends. For income earned in the 2026 to 2027 tax year, that is 5 October 2027.
  • Allowance or expenses, not both. You can deduct the £1,000 allowance or your real expenses, never both. With fuel, chemicals and kit, most detailers are better off claiming expenses.
  • National Insurance. Class 4 is 6% on profits from £12,570 to £50,270 and 2% above that. You do not pay Class 2 if your profits are £7,105 or more. Below that, it is optional at £3.65 a week.
  • COSHH. If you take on help, the law on hazardous substances puts duties on you around the chemicals. Wheel acids and fallout removers are not harmless.

This is general information, not legal or tax advice. Insurance wording varies, so read what you are actually buying. For more on cover, see our guide to public liability insurance for side hustles.

Myths that cost people money

"I need a ceramic coating certification to charge properly." Maker accreditations are largely a sales channel for the coating brands. Your before and after photographs sell the work. Learn to polish properly first, and worry about branded coatings later.

"Buy the van and wrap it, then customers come." A wrapped van is an expensive advert for a business with no customers. Get the customers, then let the work pay for the van.

"Undercut the local competition." A very cheap price attracts customers who complain and never rebook. Price in the middle, be reliable, and photograph everything.

"It is cash work, HMRC will never know." Card payments, bank transfers and online booking tools all leave a trail. Registering is simple, and the first £1,000 of gross income is tax free anyway.

Is car detailing a good side hustle? The honest verdict

This works if you are physically fit, genuinely fussy about detail, and have somewhere to work in poor weather. In our worked example, a full weekend diary nets about £1,074 a month after tax. The ceiling is higher if you go full time with a unit.

This does not work if you have no covered space, no transport, and you expect it to be passive. It is manual labour outdoors in a country with a lot of rain, and the winter is genuinely difficult.

Start with about £250 of kit and one very well photographed car. Do not spend more until people are paying you.

Frequently asked

Do I need a van to start car detailing? No. A car with a decent boot works for valeting, and many people start that way. The van becomes worthwhile when you add a water tank, a pump and a proper extractor, because those let you take jobs where the customer has no outside tap or power. Wait until bookings justify it.

How much can you earn from a car detailing side hustle? It depends on hours and the mix of work. In our worked example, 17 jobs a month across weekends grosses £1,770 and nets about £1,074 after costs, income tax and National Insurance. That is roughly £18 an hour. Polishing work lifts the hourly rate.

Do I need to tell HMRC about car detailing income? Yes, once your gross trading income goes over the £1,000 trading allowance in a tax year. You then register for Self Assessment by 5 October after that tax year ends and file a return. Below £1,000, the trading allowance usually means you do not need to register.

How do I learn machine polishing? Practise on a scrap panel from a breaker's yard, then on your own car, then on friends' cars. Use a dual action polisher, which is far more forgiving than a rotary. A paint depth gauge is worth buying once you charge for correction work, because it shows how much lacquer you have left.

What is a realistic first month? Expect a handful of jobs, mostly from Facebook and word of mouth, at valeting prices. Use them to build your before and after photographs and ask every customer for a review. Anyone promising a full diary in week one is probably selling a course, not reporting real results.
