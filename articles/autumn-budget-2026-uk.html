Autumn Budget 2026 Summary: What Happened to the Five Figures We Watched

The Autumn Budget 2026 went ahead on Wednesday 28 October, Chancellor John Healey's first. Tax on wages stayed the same, the trading allowance triples to £3,000, electricity stays VAT free until 1 July 2027, and the cash ISA cut was scrapped. Here is what happened to each figure we watched, and what to do before most changes start on 6 April 2027.

The short version

  • It happened on 28 October 2026. Chancellor John Healey presented Budget 2026 that day, and the OBR published its Economic and fiscal outlook alongside it.
  • No rise to the headline rates of income tax, National Insurance or VAT. The personal allowance stays £12,570 and the higher rate still starts above £50,270 in 2027 to 2028.
  • Our five watched figures: 0% VAT on home electricity extended to 1 July 2027; the trading allowance tripled to £3,000 from 6 April 2027; the Capital Gains Tax exempt amount kept at £3,000; the Plan 2 threshold freeze left in place, with interest capped at 6%; the National Living Wage set at £13.21 from April 2027.
  • One "already decided" item was reversed. The planned cash ISA cut to £12,000 is not going ahead. The cash ISA limit stays £20,000.
  • Where tax went up: savings interest and property income are taxed at 24%, 44% and 49% from 6 April 2027.
  • Help with bills: a £100 Household Energy Payment from January 2027 for homes in council tax bands A to D in England, and the 5p fuel duty cut kept to 1 July 2027.
  • Our verdict: the noise before the day was mostly wrong. Most changes start on 6 April 2027, so there is time to plan calmly.

1. When was the Autumn Budget 2026, and what came with it?

The date was fixed by a one page letter from the Chancellor, John Healey, to the Treasury Select Committee, published on GOV.UK on 31 July 2026. It named Wednesday 28 October 2026. That is the day it happened.

It was his first Budget. The full Budget document, the speech, the tax papers and a Treasury summary are all on GOV.UK. We link each one at the bottom of this page.

The Office for Budget Responsibility published its Economic and fiscal outlook, October 2026, the same day. In its central forecast, real GDP growth slows from around 1¼% in 2026 to 1% in 2027.

The OBR expects CPI inflation to average 3½% in 2027, with a quarterly peak of 3¾% in early 2027. It says higher energy prices are the main reason. That explains why so much of this Budget is about bills.

The Treasury says the government meets its fiscal rules, with a £15 billion buffer against the stability rule. Its headline promise was simple: no increase to the headline rates of income tax, National Insurance or VAT.

2. What was already decided before the day?

Before the Budget we listed the changes that were already law or already announced. A Chancellor can reverse them, but it is rare. This time one of them was reversed.

Student finance sat in the same settled column. Tuition fees at £9,790 for 2026-27 and what that actually costs was never on the table on Budget day.

3. What happened to the five figures we watched?

Before the day we picked five figures on this site that a Budget could settle. Here is what the Budget document says about each one.

1. The electricity VAT zero rate. VAT on domestic electricity fell from 5% to 0% on 1 October 2026. The Budget extends the 0% rate by three months, to 1 July 2027.

The Treasury expects that to take around £50 off the annual Ofgem price cap in April 2027. Northern Ireland gets funding for comparable support. Our VAT cut guide has the unit rate arithmetic.

2. The trading allowance. This went further than expected. Before the day, the government had only talked about raising the reporting point to £3,000.

Instead the allowance itself triples, from £1,000 to £3,000, from 6 April 2027. The Budget document says people will be able to receive up to £3,000 of trading and miscellaneous income tax free each year. It will be legislated in Finance Bill 2026-27.

Until 5 April 2027 the old £1,000 rule still applies. The Self Assessment registration rules still run on £1,000 for this tax year. Our full guide is the £3,000 trading allowance from April 2027.

Once side income clears £3,000 by a wide margin, the question becomes structural. Whether a limited company beats sole trader for affiliate income turns on numbers most people guess at.

3. The Capital Gains Tax annual exempt amount. No change. HMRC's rates table shows £3,000 for 2027 to 2028, with rates of 18% and 24%.

The changes were aimed at business owners. A new Founders' Relief gives a 10% rate on up to £10 million of lifetime gains from 6 April 2027. The Business Asset Disposal Relief lifetime limit falls from £1 million to £500,000 for disposals from the same date.

4. The Plan 2 student loan threshold. The repayment threshold is £29,385 a year on GOV.UK today. Budget 2025 froze it from 2027 to 2028 until 2029 to 2030, and this Budget did not lift that freeze.

What is new is a cap on interest. Plan 2 and Plan 3 interest rates are capped at 6% from 1 September 2026 to 31 August 2027. Our student loan guide explains why the threshold still matters more than the rate.

5. The National Living Wage. This one was certain to be announced, and it was. From April 2027 the rate for workers aged 21 and over rises 3.9% to £13.21 an hour.

The rate for 18 to 20 year olds rises to £11.27. The rate for 16 and 17 year olds and apprentices rises to £8.31. Until April, £12.71 is still the rate to check a payslip against, and what the minimum wage should show on a payslip explains how.

4. What else changed for households?

Some of the biggest household measures were not on our list of five. These are the ones most readers will feel.

Savings and property income tax. From 6 April 2027 savings interest above your allowances is taxed at 24%, 44% and 49%, up from 20%, 40% and 45%. Property income gets the same new rates.

The Treasury says 84% of taxpayers will see no increase. The £1,000 and £500 Personal Savings Allowances do not change. We explain who pays in savings tax at 24% from April 2027, and landlords can read property income tax at 24%.

The £100 Household Energy Payment. Homes in council tax bands A to D in England get £100 from January 2027, paid through the council tax system. It is worth £2.6 billion and reaches up to 20 million households.

Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland get their share of the money through the Barnett formula. Our guide to who gets the £100 payment covers the detail.

Fuel duty. The plan to add 5p a litre back in January 2027 is cancelled. The 5p cut now runs to 1 July 2027, and the planned inflation rise is deferred to the same date.

Gas standing charges. The government has asked Ofgem to abolish them on domestic bills, aiming for the end of 2027. The cost moves into the price per unit instead.

Mileage rates. The 55p a mile tax free rate for the first 10,000 business miles is now permanent. Above 10,000 miles the rate rises to 35p from 6 April 2027. Our mileage rate guide has the sums.

Pensioners. A new Pension Tax Allowance runs from 6 April 2027 to 5 April 2030. Pensioners whose total income is at or below the full new State Pension will not have to pay small amounts of income tax.

First time buyers. From 29 October 2026 the Lifetime ISA withdrawal charge falls from 25% to 20% for buyers above the £450,000 price cap. From April 2029 a new First Time Buyer ISA replaces the Lifetime ISA for new savers, with up to £4,000 a year and a 25% bonus.

One change was already dated rather than debated: payroll reporting of benefits. From April 2027 a company car shows on the payslip itself, which moves when the tax is collected rather than how much it is.

5. Did the rumours come true?

Before the day the same subjects circulated: property tax reform, the pension tax free lump sum, pension tax relief, wealth taxes and Inheritance Tax thresholds. We said to treat them as rumours. Most of them stayed rumours.

The Budget document has no wealth tax and no cut to the pension tax free lump sum. The one property tax in the papers, the High Value Council Tax Surcharge on homes worth over £2 million, was announced at Budget 2025. This Budget only confirmed its deferrals, discounts and exemptions from 1 April 2028.

The rise in tax on savings and property income did happen. But it was a 2 point rise in rates, not the large wealth tax some previews described.

The lesson holds. People who withdrew a pension lump sum or sold a property because of a headline paid real tax for a change that never came. The same mistake is being made over car finance compensation, and what the pause on motor finance redress actually means for a claim is a better guide than any preview.

6. A worked example in Sunderland

Aisha, 34, lives in Sunderland. She earns £38,000 as a team leader and makes about £2,400 a year selling handmade candles online. She has £9,000 in a cash ISA. Her flat is in council tax band A.

Before the Budget she wondered whether to "do something". She did nothing, which turned out to be right. The household is an illustration; the rates are the official ones.

Her candle income. In 2026 to 2027 she can deduct the £1,000 trading allowance instead of her small costs. That leaves £1,400 taxed at 20%, which is £280. She must be registered for Self Assessment.

From 6 April 2027 the allowance is £3,000. Her £2,400 sits inside it, so it is tax free. She saves £280 a year. She should check HMRC's guidance on reporting once Finance Bill 2026-27 becomes law.

Her energy bill. Band A in England means she should get the £100 Household Energy Payment from January 2027. Her electricity also stays free of VAT until 1 July 2027.

Her savings. All her savings sit in a cash ISA, so the new 24% savings rate does not touch her. The cash ISA limit stays £20,000, so she can keep saving on the same terms.

Her pay. Her £38,000 is under the frozen £50,270 higher rate threshold, so the rate she pays does not change. The freeze still means any pay rise is taxed in full.

Add it up. £280 saved on candle tax plus the £100 payment is £380 better off over the year. She did not need to act before 28 October to get any of it.

7. What should you do before 6 April 2027?

Most Budget changes start with the new tax year. That leaves time to read the real documents and then act.

Use the allowances that exist now, because they expire on 5 April 2027. That means the £20,000 ISA allowance, which the Budget kept in full.

If you hold savings outside an ISA and pay tax on the interest, moving money into an ISA before April shields it from the new 24% rate. Our guide to tax on savings interest shows who pays.

Pension contributions are worth a look if you are near £50,270 or £100,000. They pull assessed income back under those cliffs.

Claim Marriage Allowance if one partner earns under the personal allowance. And check your tax code on your Personal Tax Account, because wrong codes are common and the money is yours whatever the Chancellor says.

If you run a small payroll, the new minimum wage rates start in April 2027. Knowing what a small employer can reclaim on statutory payments helps before rates move.

8. The honest verdict

The Autumn Budget 2026 was mostly about bills, not big tax rises. The headline rates of income tax, National Insurance and VAT did not move.

Of our five watched figures, two went the reader's way: the trading allowance tripled and electricity VAT stays at 0% for longer. The minimum wage rose as expected. The Capital Gains Tax allowance and the Plan 2 freeze did not change.

The real cost is quieter. Savers with interest outside an ISA and landlords pay more from April 2027, and the threshold freeze keeps working to 2031. Household costs work the same way: the branded school uniform limit was already in force, not waiting on the Chancellor.

Use your allowances before 5 April, check your tax code, and ignore anything that asks for a permanent decision based on a prediction.

Frequently asked

What was announced in the Autumn Budget 2026? The main household measures were a £100 energy payment for council tax bands A to D in England, 0% VAT on home electricity to 1 July 2027, the trading allowance rising to £3,000, and the cash ISA limit kept at £20,000. Savings and property income tax rises to 24%, 44% and 49% from April 2027.

Did income tax go up in the Autumn Budget 2026? Not on wages. The Budget document says there is no increase to the headline rates of income tax, National Insurance or VAT. The personal allowance stays £12,570 in 2027 to 2028. The rates on savings interest and property income do rise by 2 points from 6 April 2027.

Did the Budget change stamp duty? Not for ordinary home buyers. The Budget document lists only technical Stamp Duty Land Tax changes, such as group relief rules for companies and removing the charge on rent under assured tenancies. The share version of stamp duty will be replaced by a new Securities Transfer Tax from late 2027.

When do the Budget changes take effect? A few started straight away, such as the lower Lifetime ISA charge from 29 October 2026. The energy payment starts in January 2027. Most tax changes, including the £3,000 trading allowance and the new savings rates, start on 6 April 2027. The National Living Wage rises in April 2027.

Will the electricity VAT cut be extended again? Nobody knows yet. The Budget extended the 0% rate to 1 July 2027 and gave no further date. The Treasury expects it to take around £50 off the annual price cap in April 2027. Plan on the 5% rate returning in July 2027 and treat any further extension as a bonus.
