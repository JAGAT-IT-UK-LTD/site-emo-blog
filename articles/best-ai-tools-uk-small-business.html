The best AI tools for a UK small business are usually one general assistant and the AI already built into software you pay for. Bookkeeping apps and Canva's free tier do more than most people expect.

Everything else has to pass one test: can you name the hour it saves you each month? This guide prices the main tools as listed today, so you can run that test in minutes.

1. How do you know if an AI tool is worth paying for?

Every AI tool sounds essential on its own landing page. Here is the arithmetic that cuts through it.

Work out what an hour of your time is worth. If you charge £40 an hour, an hour is worth £40.

Then ask how many minutes a month the tool genuinely saves you.

Tool cost per monthMinutes it must save to break even at £30/hr
£1020 minutes
£2040 minutes

£1505 hours

A general assistant that drafts your emails and first drafts clears 40 minutes easily.

A niche £50 a month tool that generates social captions almost certainly does not. Writing a caption takes four minutes, and you will spend three editing what it produced.

Joining the tools together is where the rest of the time goes. We cost that in running Zapier automations in a one person business.

Run the free tier for a month and count. Actually count.

Most people upgrade on a feeling and then forget the subscription exists. That is how a small business ends up with £120 or more a month of software it does not use.

3. Which AI tools help with which jobs?

Writing and email. This is where the time genuinely goes. A general assistant drafting quotes, follow ups, listings and first drafts saves real hours.

Do not publish anything unedited. It reads like it, customers notice, and Google's spam policies name scaled content abuse as a problem.

Bookkeeping. The AI features inside FreeAgent, Xero and QuickBooks that auto categorise transactions and match receipts are the highest value AI most small businesses will use.

They come bundled with software you already need. No separate subscription.

Design. Canva. The free tier does more than most small businesses need.

Pro at £13 a month on Canva's UK page is worth it if you make graphics weekly and want brand kit, background removal and resizing.

Meeting notes. Genuinely useful if you do several calls a week, worthless if you do two a month. Several tools offer free tiers with a monthly transcription limit. Start there.

Customer support. A chatbot on a small site usually annoys more people than it helps. A well written FAQ page costs nothing and answers the same questions.

Images. Fine for backgrounds, mockups and abstract graphics.

Bad for anything a customer will think is a real photograph of your actual product or premises. That is a trading standards problem, not a taste one.

6. Is it legal to put customer data into AI tools?

Yes, but if you put customer information into an AI tool, you are processing personal data and UK GDPR applies to you.

  • Do not paste customer names, addresses, health information or payment details into a general chat tool. Anonymise first. "Draft a reply to a customer whose delivery is late" needs no names.
  • Check whether your data trains the model. Business and team tiers generally exclude your data from training. OpenAI's business page says no training on your business data by default. Free consumer tiers often do not offer that. This is the strongest argument for paying if you handle client data.
  • Update your privacy notice if AI processing is part of how you handle customer data.
  • Watch where the data is processed. International transfers have rules, and your customers may reasonably ask.
  • You are responsible for the output. If an AI drafted quote contains a wrong price, that is your quote and potentially your contract.

The ICO has clear guidance on AI and on small organisations, and it is free to read. This is general information, not legal advice.

7. Where is AI bad for a small business?

Being honest about this is more useful than another list of tools.

  • Anything requiring current, specific facts. Prices, legislation, tax thresholds, opening hours. It will produce something plausible and wrong, and confidently.
  • Legal documents. A generated contract that misses a clause costs far more than a solicitor would have.
  • Anything numerical you have not checked. Quotes, measurements, dosages, tax calculations.
  • Bulk website content. Google's scaled content abuse policy targets exactly this. Publishing fifty generated pages is an active ranking risk, not a shortcut.
  • Your actual expertise. If you are a kitchen fitter, the thing that wins you work is that you know what goes wrong in a 1930s semi. No model has that, and outsourcing your voice to one removes the reason people hire you.

Myths that cost people money

"I need AI or I will fall behind." This is marketing.

The businesses winning in 2026 are the ones answering the phone, quoting quickly and turning up when they said they would. AI helps at the edges of that. It does not replace it.

"The expensive tier must be better." For small business use the free and entry tiers are very capable.

The expensive tiers buy seats, admin controls and compliance features, which matter at fifty people and not at two.

"I should use several so I get the best of each." The overlap is enormous and the switching cost is real. One, used properly, beats three used shallowly.

The honest verdict

Buy one general assistant, around £14 to £20 a month, and only if you write a lot.

If you already live in Microsoft 365, Copilot Business at £16.10 per user, or £13.80 in the first year, makes sense. If you do not, a standalone assistant is cheaper because you are not stacking it on a licence.

Use Canva free until you genuinely need brand kit and background removal weekly.

Cancel everything else that you cannot point at and say what hour it saved you this month.

The realistic outcome here is not new revenue. It is two or three hours a week back on admin, and a subscription bill that is smaller than it was.

Frequently asked

What is the best AI tool for a small business in the UK? For most, one general assistant such as ChatGPT, Claude or Microsoft 365 Copilot, plus the AI already inside your bookkeeping software. Pick by where you work: Copilot if you live in Outlook and Excel, a standalone assistant if not. Start on the free tier and upgrade only when a limit blocks you.

Is the free tier good enough? For most sole traders, yes. Free tiers draft emails, quotes and listings well. Upgrade when you hit a usage limit that actually blocks your work, or when you need business data protection such as no training on your data, not before. Count the minutes saved for a month first.

Can I claim AI subscriptions as a business expense? Yes, where they are used wholly and exclusively for the business. GOV.UK lists software among allowable expenses for the self employed. Keep the invoices, and split the cost if a tool is also used privately. Our side hustle tax rules guide covers the basics. General information, not tax advice.

Will AI written content hurt my website? Unedited, mass produced content will. Google's spam policies target scaled content abuse, meaning many pages made mainly to rank, not the use of a tool. Content you have edited, fact checked and added your own experience to is fine, and usually reads better too.

What if the price changes? It will. All the figures here were checked on the providers' own pages on 22 November 2026, and this category reprices constantly. Microsoft's offer is first year only. Confirm on the vendor site before you commit to an annual plan.
