Probation is a word in your contract, not a status in law

Ruhul Amin · Published 12 September 2026 · 5 min read · Figures checked 27 September 2026

Being on probation feels like a legal category with fewer rights attached. It is not one. Here is what actually applies from your first shift, and the date in January that changes the calculation for everybody.

1. What probation actually is

Acas, whose guidance on this was last updated on 24 July 2026, defines a probation period as a time at the start of employment to check whether someone is suitable for a job. Then it says the part nobody repeats: there is no legal requirement to have a probation period, and they can be any length of time.

So probation is a contractual arrangement, not a legal status. Three months, six months, a year, none at all, all equally lawful. It does not create a tier of employment with fewer rights.

Acas adds that probation periods are usually for people with the legal status of employee, and that someone is not likely to be an employee if they are an agency worker, a casual worker or on a zero hours contract. An employer may run a probation for workers too, and should then follow fair processes for everyone.

2. What you already have from day one

Acas lists day one employment rights that apply during a probation period, meaning from the moment you start:

  • Minimum wage.
  • Paid holiday, which accrues from the first day.
  • Protection against discrimination.
  • Protection against detriment for whistleblowing.
  • The right to join a trade union.

Acas also confirms two things treated as negotiable that are not. You can take time off sick during probation, and you are entitled to sick pay. And an employer can delay automatic pension enrolment for up to 3 months, but must enrol you if you ask. That second point is money: three months of missed employer contributions is not trivial, and the fix is one email.

Notice runs both ways. Per GOV.UK you must give at least a week's notice once you have been in the job more than a month, and notice usually runs from the start of the day after you hand it in.

3. The date that actually matters

Acas states plainly that the Employment Rights Act 2025 does not affect probation periods. There is still no legal requirement to have one and they can still be any length.

What the Act changes is the thing probation is often used as a proxy for. From 1 January 2027, protection from unfair dismissal becomes a right after 6 months in a job, and Acas is specific: you are protected if you already have at least 6 months' service on 1 January 2027.

Work that backwards. If you started on or before 1 July 2026, you will have six months' service on 1 January 2027, so you are covered from the day the new rule starts. If you start after that, you reach protection six months into the job.

Acas also anticipates the obvious employer reaction and answers it: employers might want probation periods to end before someone gets protection from unfair dismissal, but this does not avoid legal risks, because other protections, including discrimination, whistleblowing and breach of contract, apply during probation anyway.

Aisha, Wolverhampton. Started on 15 June 2026 with a six month probation ending 15 December. She assumes she is most exposed in December, just before the new rules. In fact her start date is before 1 July, so on 1 January 2027 she has more than six months' service and is protected from that day. The three weeks between her probation ending and the new right starting are the gap, not the whole winter. That changes how she handles a difficult review, and it comes from two dates.

4. What to do if probation is going badly

  • Ask for the review in writing. A record of what was said and what you were asked to improve is what makes any later argument possible.
  • Check whether an extension was contractual. Probation can be extended, but the power to extend usually has to come from the contract. If the contract is silent, ask on what basis.
  • Separate performance from discrimination. If the issue relates to a protected characteristic, that is a day one protection and probation does not touch it.
  • Ask about the pension. If enrolment has been postponed and you want in, say so in writing. Your employer must then enrol you.
  • Count your notice. Whether you leave or are let go, statutory notice starts once you have been there more than a month.

5. What each day-one right is actually worth

Day one rights are usually listed and never priced, which is why they get ignored. Every figure below was checked against GOV.UK on 27 September 2026.

The sick pay line is the one that has actually moved. GOV.UK now states the rate as £123.25 a week or 80% of normal weekly earnings, whichever is lower, and lists the eligibility test as being an employee, having done some work for the employer and having been ill for at least one full working day. If you are working from an older article that tells you about three unpaid waiting days and a lower earnings limit, it is describing a scheme that no longer looks like that. On probation this matters more than for anyone else, because new starters are exactly who used to fall through the old earnings floor.

6. Kelly in Sunderland, six months in

Kelly starts at a Sunderland logistics firm on 4 August 2026, 37.5 hours a week at £12.71 an hour, and is told she is on six months' probation with "no holiday and no sick pay until you pass". Three of those four words are wrong, and here is the bill.

  • Weekly pay: 37.5 × £12.71 = £476.63. That is the legal floor, not a starting offer.
  • Holiday accrued by 4 February 2027: half of 28 days is 14 days, worth 14 × 7.5 × £12.71 = £1,334.55. Her employer can control when she takes it. It cannot make her lose it, and if she leaves it is paid out.
  • A week off sick in October: 80% of £476.63 is £381.30, so the £123.25 cap bites and she gets £123.25, not nothing. Over a five-day absence that is the difference between a missed food shop and a missed direct debit.
  • Unfair dismissal: she reaches six months on 4 February 2027, after the 1 January commencement, so from that date she is covered. Dismissed in December 2026 at four months, she would not have been.

Total value of the three things she was told she did not have: £1,457.80 before anyone argues about performance.

7. Where probation genuinely does leave you exposed

The word does nothing, but the clause underneath it does. Probation has no standing in law. What has standing is the notice clause your contract applies during probation, commonly one week against a month afterwards. That clause is lawful as long as it is not below the statutory minimum, and it is the real reason probation feels precarious. Read that clause, not the paragraph about objectives. Read that clause against the floor it cannot drop below, because the notice you are legally owed when you leave or are let go starts at one week after a month of service and climbs with every full year.

Under six months and no discrimination in play, you often have no legal lever at all, and pretending otherwise wastes your time. If the problem is genuinely performance, the useful move is a short written note asking for specific, measurable objectives and a review date, not a grievance. A grievance raised to create a paper trail against a dismissal you cannot challenge tends to bring the dismissal forward.

Two exceptions have no qualifying period and never have. If the real reason is a protected characteristic, or that you raised a protected disclosure, service length is irrelevant: those claims are available on day one, on day three, and in month five. If that is your situation, the length of your probation is not the question to be asking.

And if you are not an employee, most of this table changes. The SSP test above is written for employees. Agency workers may be entitled, but workers and the genuinely self-employed sit on a different footing, and a contract headed "probation" does not settle which of those you are.

The verdict

Probation changes the tone of the first few months. Legally it changes far less than people assume, and Acas says so in as many words: no requirement, no fixed length, day one rights throughout.

The date worth writing down is 1 January 2027, and the date worth checking is your own start date. Before 1 July 2026 and you are covered by the new unfair dismissal rule from the moment it begins. And whatever your probation says, if your pension enrolment has been postponed, ask to be enrolled. That is the one item on this page that puts money in an account this month.

Frequently asked

Can my employer extend my probation? Probation can be extended, but the basis for extending should come from your contract or written statement. Acas covers extension as part of a fair process.

Do I get holiday during probation? Yes. Acas lists paid holiday among the day one rights that apply during probation. It accrues from your first day whatever your contract calls the period.

Does a shorter probation mean I am protected from unfair dismissal sooner? No. Probation is contractual. Unfair dismissal protection is statutory, and from 1 January 2027 it starts at 6 months of service whatever the probation clause says.
