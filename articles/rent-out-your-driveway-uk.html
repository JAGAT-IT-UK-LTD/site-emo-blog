Rent Out Your Driveway in the UK: The £1,000 Tax Free Rule Is Counted on a Number You Never See

Ruhul Amin · Published 29 August 2026 · Updated 26 November 2026 · 10 min read · Figures checked November 2026

Renting out your driveway in the UK is tax free up to £1,000 a year, but HMRC counts that £1,000 before platform fees, not what lands in your bank. The allowance does not rise to £3,000 in April 2027, and JustPark and YourParkingSpace report every host to HMRC from the first booking. Here is what that means for your money.

The short version

  • The allowance is £1,000 a year, and it is the property allowance, not Rent a Room. The £7,500 Rent a Room threshold covers furnished accommodation in your home, and whether taking a lodger is worth it is a different calculation from letting a strip of tarmac. A driveway is not accommodation, so you get £1,000, per GOV.UK.
  • It does not triple in April 2027. Budget 2026 raises the trading allowance from £1,000 to £3,000 from 6 April 2027. The property allowance is not part of that change, so driveway rent stays at £1,000.
  • HMRC measures it gross. JustPark tells you to measure it net. HMRC defines gross income as the total "before any allowances or expenses are taken off". JustPark's own guide, dated 7 May 2026, says the allowance "is from the earnings that you have received directly into your bank, after any fees are deducted". HMRC writes the rules.
  • There is no small seller exemption for a driveway. The 30 sales or €2,000 let off covers goods only. Parking is rental of immovable property, and JustPark says it "has to report anyone who has received at least one booking since 2024".
  • Two lines to remember: gross above £1,000 means you must contact HMRC. Gross above £2,500 means you must register for Self Assessment. That second line is about £208 a month of bookings.
  • From April 2027 the tax rate on the slice above the allowance goes up. Property income is taxed at 24%, 44% and 49% from 2027 to 2028, four points above the main rates.
  • The sums are small. On the worked example below, a host who crosses the line owes £4 this tax year and £4.80 next year. The paperwork is the problem, not the bill.

1. How is tax on renting out your driveway worked out?

Every guide to renting out your driveway says the same comforting thing. The first £1,000 a year is tax free.

That is true. It is the property allowance, worth up to £1,000 of gross property income a year since 6 April 2017.

The problem is how you are told to count to £1,000.

JustPark's resource centre guide to the allowance, dated 7 May 2026, tells space owners this. Its help centre article "How much tax do I pay for earnings from my space?" said the same when we read it in August.

"The £1,000 allowance is from the earnings that you have received directly into your bank, after any fees are deducted."

Now here is HMRC, on the GOV.UK page JustPark itself links to:

"Gross income means the total amount you would put on your tax return before any allowances or expenses are taken off."

A platform fee is an expense. So HMRC's £1,000 test runs on what the driver paid for your space. JustPark's instruction runs on what reached your current account after the fee.

On a 3% fee those numbers are about 3% apart. That sounds like nothing until your year lands between £1,000 and about £1,031.

In that band your bank statement says you are fine, and you are not. Whatever you earn, the figure that decides your obligations is always higher than the figure you received.

When we read it in August 2026, the same JustPark help article also called this "the property trading income allowance". There is no such thing. GOV.UK has two separate £1,000 allowances, and says "if you have both types of income, you'll get a £1,000 allowance for each".

Driveway rent is property income. Your eBay flipping, if you do any, is trading income with its own allowance. Merging the names invites people to assume one pot, and that matters more than ever from April 2027.

2. Does the £1,000 allowance rise to £3,000 in April 2027?

No. This is the most common mix up since the Budget on 28 October 2026.

HMRC's policy paper "Increasing the trading allowance", published on Budget day, says the government is raising the trading allowance "to £3,000 from 6 April 2027". The Self Assessment reporting line for that income rises to £3,000 too.

The paper says nothing about the property allowance. The GOV.UK allowances page still shows £1,000 for property income, and the Budget tax overview lists no change to it.

So a driveway, a garage or a parking bay you let out stays at £1,000 a year. Selling crafts or doing odd jobs moves to £3,000. Our guide to the trading allowance rising to £3,000 in April 2027 covers who gains.

The Budget did change one thing for driveway hosts. From 6 April 2027, property income has its own income tax rates. The Budget 2026 overview of tax rates sets them at 24% basic, 44% higher and 49% additional, against 20%, 40% and 45% on wages.

That only bites on the slice above the allowance, so for most hosts it is pennies. The detail for landlords is in the new 24% property income tax rate from April 2027.

3. Will JustPark or YourParkingSpace report you to HMRC?

Yes, and from your very first booking.

GOV.UK says the digital platform reporting rules "started in the UK from 1 January 2024". Platforms collect your details, then send them to HMRC by the following 31 January. The first deadline, for 2024, was 31 January 2025. You also get a copy of what was sent.

Most people have heard of the exemption. Platforms need not report sellers who get "2,000 euros or less (approximately £1,700) for fewer than 30 sales of goods in a year". Plenty of casual earners assume they are safely under it.

That exemption is for goods. GOV.UK's reporting rules say it does not cover "the provision of services", "rental of transport" or "rental of immoveable property". HMRC's own international exchange manual lists parking spaces as immovable property.

JustPark puts it plainly on its HMRC reporting page:

"This means we have to report anyone who has received at least one booking since 2024."

One booking. Not thirty. Not €2,000. One.

So hosts are asked for their full legal name, date of birth, address and National Insurance number. GOV.UK confirms platforms need your National Insurance number and "the address of each property for rent on the platform". JustPark also runs a photo ID check, and says it may hold back payouts if you do not complete it. YourParkingSpace has its own page explaining why it needs your tax details.

To be fair to the platforms, none of this changes what you owe. GOV.UK says "a platform reporting your details to HMRC does not automatically mean you owe tax".

What changed is what HMRC can see. It now holds your gross figure and your National Insurance number, whether you earned £40 or £4,000.

4. How much can you earn renting out a driveway?

The honest position: there is no independently audited figure for what a UK driveway earns. What exists is marketing.

JustPark's rent out your driveway page promises you can "earn up to £1,000 a year tax free, with no earnings cap" and says it has "65,000+ space owners". "Up to" is doing all the work there.

The £1,000 is not a JustPark achievement either. It is the statutory allowance, and it applies whichever platform you use.

Fees are where the two big platforms differ, and only one of them tells you the number.

PlatformWhat the host paysWhat the driver paysPublished?
YourParkingSpace3% payment processing fee, taken from earningsA 20% booking fee added to your priceYes, on its help centre
JustPark"A small service fee from each booking"Not stated on the host pageNo percentage given

YourParkingSpace says on its help centre that you decide what you want to earn, and it adds a 20% booking fee to the price drivers see. A 3% processing fee then comes off your earnings. Long term bookings carry an extra arrangement fee of £4.99 to £9.99, also paid by the driver.

That 20% is not free to you, even though you never pay it. It is 20% on the price a driver compares with the council car park down the road.

JustPark does not publish a percentage. Its host page says "It's 100% free to list. We take a small service fee from each booking." Its owner agreement gives no rate either.

Older press figures for JustPark disagree with each other, so we do not use any of them. Ask for the fee in writing before you list.

Location does the rest. A driveway near a commuter station, a hospital, a stadium or an airport is a real asset.

One on a quiet street with free kerbside parking is worth close to nothing. No listing trick changes that. Both platforms show a local price guide before you commit, which costs nothing and is the only number worth trusting.

5. Worked example: the Hussain driveway in St Albans

Tanvir Hussain has a second parking space on his driveway in St Albans, about eight minutes' walk from the station. He lists it on YourParkingSpace as a monthly commuter space at £85 a month, and it is taken all year.

What he sees:

  • Gross bookings at his price: £85 × 12 = £1,020
  • 3% processing fee: £30.60
  • Paid into his bank: £989.40

Tanvir follows the "count your bank" advice he read on a platform help page. He totals £989.40, sees it is under £1,000 and does nothing. Reasonable. Also wrong.

His gross property income is £1,020. GOV.UK says gross property income "over £1,000 up to £2,500" means you must contact HMRC.

He has a duty to tell HMRC that he does not know about. The platform has already reported him.

What he actually owes. He takes partial relief: £1,020 minus the £1,000 allowance leaves £20 taxable.

  • 2026 to 2027, basic rate 20%: £4.00. Higher rate 40%: £8.00.
  • 2027 to 2028, property basic rate 24%: £4.80. Property higher rate 44%: £8.80.

Four pounds. We are not going to dress that up as a crisis, because it is not one. The point is the mismatch, not the money.

Now scale it up. Suppose the space is better placed and goes for £150 a month. That is £1,800 gross, and £54 of processing fees. Tanvir now has a real choice.

MethodTaxableTax 2026 to 2027 (20%)Tax 2027 to 2028 (24%)
Claim the £1,000 allowance£800£160.00£192.00
Deduct the £54 fee instead£1,746£349.20£419.04

The allowance wins by £189.20 this year and £227.04 next year. It will almost always win for a driveway, because a driveway has almost no running costs.

You cannot do both. GOV.UK says "you cannot deduct any other expenses or allowances if you claim the allowances".

He is still under £2,500, so he contacts HMRC rather than registering for Self Assessment. Divide £2,500 by twelve and you get the line nobody publishes: a space earning more than about £208 a month gross puts you into Self Assessment.

Below about £83 a month you are inside the allowance and have nothing to tell HMRC at all.

6. Do you need permission, planning or insurance?

Permission. JustPark's host page sets the condition: "If you own the space or have permission from your landlord, you're free to list it."

A tenancy may forbid subletting any part of the property. A lease often limits what a parking space can be used for and by whom, and a managing agent can enforce it. Read the document before you list, not after a neighbour complains.

Planning. There is no national permitted development right to run a commercial car park from your front garden.

Occasional, small scale letting is not generally treated as a material change of use. That is a judgement your local planning authority makes on the facts.

Letting one space part time, you are almost certainly fine. Letting several spaces all day near a stadium, ring the council's planning team first. A free phone call is cheaper than an enforcement notice.

Insurance. Your home policy is written for your household, not for a stranger's car on your land for ten hours a day.

JustPark says it "provides protection for space owners" and that "all drivers are verified with licence plate checks". That is worth having, but it is not your own insurer agreeing to the arrangement. Tell your insurer in writing and keep the reply.

The mirror image is worth knowing too. Damage to your own car on the road outside is nobody's problem until you make it one. A claim against the council for pothole damage runs on whether the authority knew about the defect.

The honest verdict

If your driveway is near something people need to park next to, this is one of the better small income streams in the UK. No skill, no stock, no hours, and the asset already exists.

If you are thinking of letting a room or a whole property the same way, the commitment is far bigger. What Airbnb hosting actually costs a UK host sets out the fees, the cleaning and the rules before the income.

If your driveway is on an ordinary street with free parking outside, there is nothing here. You will get a handful of bookings a year, and the honest advice is not to bother. No listing trick fixes a location problem.

A car parked outside earns nothing sitting still, either. What an Amazon Flex block really pays once fuel and wear come off is the honest comparison for anyone hoping the vehicle and the address would pay for themselves.

On the tax, keep it in proportion. The worked example owes £4. What deserves ten minutes is that HMRC now holds a figure for your driveway larger than the one in your bank, with your National Insurance number attached, from one booking.

Keep your own record of gross bookings, not bank credits. If the gross crosses £1,000, tell HMRC. That is the whole job, and the April 2027 changes do not alter it.

There is nothing to buy here.

Frequently asked

Is renting out your driveway tax free in the UK? Up to a point. The first £1,000 of gross property income each tax year is covered by the property allowance, so you owe nothing and need not tell HMRC. Above £1,000 you must contact HMRC, and above £2,500 you must register for Self Assessment. The £1,000 is measured before platform fees come off.

Can I use the £7,500 Rent a Room allowance for my driveway? No. GOV.UK describes Rent a Room as letting furnished accommodation in your home. A parking space is not accommodation, so driveway income sits under the £1,000 property allowance. GOV.UK also says you cannot use the property allowance on income you already put through the Rent a Room Scheme, so the two never stack on the same money.

Does the property allowance go up to £3,000 in April 2027? No. Budget 2026 raised the trading allowance to £3,000 from 6 April 2027, but HMRC's policy paper covers trading and miscellaneous income only. The property allowance stays at £1,000. What does change in April 2027 is the rate: property income above the allowance is taxed at 24%, 44% or 49%.

I have a buy to let. Does the £1,000 allowance still help? Take advice first. GOV.UK says if you claim the property allowance in one business "you may not claim actual expenses in respect of the other business". Claiming it on your driveway could cost you real expenses on your rental. You also cannot use it at all if you claim the finance cost tax reducer on a residential let.

Do I get reported if I only let my space out twice? Yes. Parking counts as rental of immovable property, not a sale of goods, so the 30 sales and €2,000 exemption does not apply. JustPark says it must report anyone with one booking since 2024. Being reported is not the same as owing tax. Under £1,000 gross a year, you owe nothing and have nothing to tell HMRC.
