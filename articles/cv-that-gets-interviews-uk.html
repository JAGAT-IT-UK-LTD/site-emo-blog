How to Write a CV in the UK That Gets Interviews in 2026

Ruhul Amin · Published 21 August 2026 · Updated 24 November 2026 · 10 min read · Figures checked November 2026

To write a CV that gets interviews in the UK, keep it single column and plain, and rewrite the top third for every advert with one number in it. Software reads your CV before a person does, and the person gives it about seven seconds, so being readable beats being designed.

1. What are you actually up against?

Before changing a word, look at the scale. Most people decide their CV is terrible when the real answer is arithmetic.

The ONS release of 17 November 2026 put UK vacancies at 690,000 in August to October 2026. That is down 22,000 on the previous three months.

There were 2.4 unemployed people for every vacancy in July to September 2026, against 2.5 in April to June. Slightly better, but still more than two people chasing each open job before you count those already in work.

And many applicants are already in work. They apply for the same roles you do.

So if you have sent forty applications and heard nothing, that is roughly what the numbers predict. It does not mean your CV is fine.

It does mean the fix is about getting through a filter, not about being a better person. Our guide to what falling vacancies mean if you are job hunting covers the market side.

2. What is the filter you never see?

Most large employers run applications through an applicant tracking system, usually called an ATS.

Jobscan checks the Fortune 500 every year. In 2026 it detected an ATS at 97.4% of them. That is a US list, but the same software, such as Workday and SuccessFactors, runs at large UK employers too.

You will often read that 75% of CVs are rejected by software before a person sees them. That figure is widely repeated but has no solid public source, so treat it with care.

What is true is simpler. These systems parse text and match it against a job description. When they cannot read you, a recruiter searching the system will not find you.

So the failures are boring and mechanical.

  • Text inside images or graphics is invisible. A designed CV with your skills in an infographic parses as a blank skills section.
  • Tables and multi column layouts scramble the reading order. Your job titles end up mixed in with your dates.
  • Headers and footers are often skipped entirely. If your phone number and email live in the header, they may simply not exist.
  • Unusual section names fail to map. Call it "Work Experience", not "My Journey". Call it "Education", not "Where I Learned".
  • Missing the words in the advert. If the advert says "stock control" and your CV says "inventory management", a keyword search will not find you.

Fixing all five takes about twenty minutes. It is worth more than any redesign.

The software is not deciding whether you are good. It is deciding whether it can read you. Only one of those is in your control.

3. What happens in the seven seconds?

Once a human does open it, the widely cited figure for the first pass is 7.4 seconds.

That comes from the 2018 eye tracking study by Ladders, a US jobs site. Its earlier 2012 study found about six seconds. Ladders is a commercial firm and did not publish much detail on its method, so take the exact number loosely.

The finding that matters is where the eyes went. Recruiters looked at job titles and section headings, and simple layouts with clear headings did best.

In that time a recruiter is answering three questions, in order:

  1. Has this person done roughly this job before? They look at your most recent job title and the company.
  2. For how long, and recently? They scan the dates for gaps and for job hopping.
  3. Is there a reason to keep reading? One concrete, quantified thing near the top.

Everything on your CV that does not help answer one of those three questions is, for the first pass, decoration.

4. How do you rewrite the top third?

The single highest value change is to rewrite the top third of page one for every application.

Not the whole CV. The top third: your name block, your profile paragraph and your most recent role.

The profile paragraph. Three lines at most. It should contain the job title from the advert, your years of relevant experience, and one number.

Delete every "hard working", "passionate", "team player" and "results driven". They appear on almost every CV, so they distinguish nobody. They also burn two of your seven seconds.

Weak: "A hard working and passionate professional seeking a challenging role where I can utilise my skills."

Better: "Warehouse team leader, six years, currently running a shift of eleven. Cut stock discrepancies by 40% in twelve months by moving to weekly counts."

The second one is not better written. It is better because it contains facts, and facts are what a stranger can act on.

5. Duties or outcomes, which win?

Most CV bullets describe what the person was told to do. Hiring managers already know what the job involves.

What they cannot know is whether you were any good at it.

Duty, which says nothingOutcome, which does the work
Responsible for stock controlCut stock discrepancies by 40% over twelve months by introducing a weekly count
Handled customer complaintsResolved 30 to 40 complaints a week, keeping escalation to under 5%
Assisted with social mediaGrew the shop's Instagram from 400 to 3,100 followers in nine months, which brought roughly 15 enquiries a month
Trained new staffWrote the onboarding checklist now used for all new starters, cutting time to competent from three weeks to ten days

If you think you have no numbers, you usually do. You have just never counted them.

How many customers a day? How many orders? How much did the thing cost before you changed it? An estimate you can defend in an interview beats no number at all.

That applies hardest at the start of a career, where an opening shift in retail produces more countable detail than it looks like: covers opened, deliveries checked, tills reconciled.

6. What is the right CV UK format?

Rules that genuinely matter in the UK:

  • Two pages at most. One page if you have under five years of experience.
  • Reverse chronological order. Most recent role at the top. CVs that hide dates make recruiters assume you are hiding something.
  • No photo, no date of birth, no marital status, no nationality. UK employers generally do not want these, and they can complicate equality duties. This differs from much of Europe, which is why templates from abroad get it wrong.
  • Simple single column, standard headings, real text. Save as PDF unless the advert asks for Word.
  • An email address that is your name. Not the one you made when you were fourteen.

The National Careers Service CV guide uses the same sections and is free.

Things people agonise over that do not move the needle:

The font, as long as it is legible. "References available on request", which is assumed either way. Hobbies, which are neutral unless genuinely relevant. Colour, as long as it prints well and does not break parsing.

Which CV template should you use in the UK?

The plainest one you can find. A free Word or Google Docs template with one column and standard headings is enough.

Avoid templates with sidebars, skill bars, icons or a photo box. They look good on screen and read badly to software.

7. What does a real rewrite look like?

Sam, 29, from Manchester, applying for customer operations roles. 46 applications, 2 replies, both rejections.

Sam works 37.5 hours a week on the National Living Wage of £12.71, which is about £24,785 a year. The roles he wants pay around £27,500. That is roughly £2,700 a year more before tax, or £225 a month.

From April 2027 the National Living Wage rises to £13.21, about £25,760 a year at the same hours. Moving up a level still beats waiting for that rise.

Before, the top of page one: a two column layout with a photo, a skills bar chart showing "Communication 90%", and a profile reading "A passionate and dedicated individual with excellent communication skills looking for an opportunity to grow within a dynamic company."

What was wrong. The skills chart was an image, so the parser saw no skills at all. The two column layout mixed his job titles with dates.

His phone number was in the header, which many systems skip. The profile contained no job title, no number, and nothing from the advert.

After. Single column, no photo, contact details in the body. Profile rewritten to: "Customer operations coordinator, four years in ecommerce. Handled 120 to 150 tickets a week at a 94% first contact resolution rate. Rebuilt the returns process, cutting average handling time from 9 minutes to 5."

Skills moved to a plain text list using the exact words from each advert.

Result in the example: 22 applications, 6 first stage interviews. The work history did not change. Not one job, not one date. Only what a machine could read and what a human saw in the first seven seconds.

Sam is a composite example built to show the method, not a client case study. The point stands: the change that works is almost never "write better". It is "be readable and be specific".

8. How do you write a CV with no experience?

Lead with what you can prove, not with the job history you do not have yet.

Put a short profile first, then a skills section in plain text, then education. Part time work, volunteering, school projects and caring roles all count.

Give each one a number where you can. "Served around 60 customers a shift" says more than "customer service".

9. Which mistakes cost UK applicants interviews?

  • Sending the identical CV to everything. Volume with no tailoring is the most common pattern among people getting no replies, and it is the one that feels most productive.
  • Hiding gaps. A gap is fine. An unexplained gap invites the worst assumption. One line, plainly stated, closes it.
  • Keyword stuffing in white text. Modern systems can flag it, and a recruiter who spots it will reject you outright.
  • Listing every job since 2009. Anything older than ten years can be a single line, or nothing.
  • Writing "I" throughout. UK CV convention drops it. "Managed a team of six", not "I managed a team of six".
  • Letting a generative tool write the whole thing. A Robert Walters survey reported in May 2025 found 73% of hiring managers say they can spot a CV or cover letter written with AI. Use it to tighten your own sentences, not to invent your experience.

The honest verdict

Your CV has two audiences and they want opposite things. Software wants plain, readable, keyword matched text. A human wants one specific, quantified reason to keep reading.

A heavily designed CV usually fails the first. A generic CV fails the second.

The version that works is deliberately boring to look at and unusually specific to read. Single column, standard headings, real text, and a top third rewritten for each advert with at least one number in it.

And keep the market in view. With 690,000 vacancies and 2.4 unemployed people for each one, twenty tailored applications will beat a hundred identical ones. The silence you have been getting is mostly arithmetic, not a verdict on you.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a CV be in the UK?

Two pages at most, and one page if you have under five years of experience. Recruiters decide on the first pass from the top of page one, so length does not help you. Cut anything older than ten years to a single line, and remove duties that do not match the advert you are applying for.

Should I use a CV template from Canva or Word?

Only the plainest single column ones. Anything with columns, sidebars, icons or charts risks failing the software that reads it. If you love a design, keep a designed version for people you hand it to directly, and a plain text version for every online application.

Is a cover letter still worth writing?

When there is a box for it, yes. In a CV Genius survey of 625 UK and Irish hiring managers, 85% called cover letters vital, but only 49% expected one. Four short paragraphs, specific to that employer, are enough. Our UK cover letter guide shows the format.

How do I explain a career break on my CV?

Directly and briefly. "Career break, 2024 to 2025, caring responsibilities" is complete. Do not stretch other dates to cover it, because dates get checked when references are taken. If you learned something or volunteered during the break, add one line about it, with a number if you have one.

Does applying early actually matter?

Often, yes. Many employers review applications in batches as they arrive, and some close adverts early once they have enough. Seasonal work is the extreme case: big retailers and delivery firms recruit for Christmas from early autumn and fill roles in waves, as our Christmas temp jobs guide explains.
