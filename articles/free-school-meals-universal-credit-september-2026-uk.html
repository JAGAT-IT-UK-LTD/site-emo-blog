Free School Meals Just Opened To Every Universal Credit Family In England. You Still Have To Register.

Ruhul Amin · Published 3 September 2026 · 6 min read · Figures checked September 2026

The autumn term that started this week is the first under the expanded free school meals rules. The £7,400 household earnings limit is gone for families on Universal Credit. But the entitlement is not automatic, the old transitional protection ended in July, and the new category comes with a funding difference that matters to schools and, indirectly, to your child.

The short version

  • From the start of the 2026 to 2027 academic year, every child in a household receiving Universal Credit in England is entitled to free school meals, regardless of household earnings. Source: Department for Education guidance, updated 1 September 2026.
  • The old £7,400 net earnings limit still exists, but only to split the entitlement into two categories: targeted (£7,400 or less) and expanded (above it).
  • A child is only eligible once a claim has been made and verified by the school or local authority. Nobody is enrolled automatically.
  • Transitional protection ended at the close of the 2025 to 2026 academic year. Families protected since 2018 must now meet the criteria afresh.
  • Expanded free school meals do not count towards pupil premium. Ever 6 FSM, which drives disadvantage funding, is based on targeted FSM only.
  • The government's own estimate is around £500 a year per child. That is Downing Street's figure, not ours, and it depends on what your school would otherwise charge.

1. What actually changed on 1 September

Until July, a family on Universal Credit in England only qualified for benefits-based free school meals if their annual household earnings, after tax, were £7,400 or less. That threshold was set in 2018 and never moved. A parent working 25 hours a week at the minimum wage sailed past it and got nothing.

The Department for Education's guidance for local authorities and schools, updated on 1 September 2026, now says this: from the start of the 2026 to 2027 academic year, eligibility is expanded to all children from households in receipt of Universal Credit regardless of household earnings.

The qualifying benefits list is therefore short. Universal Credit, or support under Part VI of the Immigration and Asylum Act 1999. Households on income-related Employment and Support Allowance or the guarantee element of Pension Credit may also be entitled, and there is separate guidance for families with no recourse to public funds.

Children in reception, year 1 and year 2 in England were already fed under universal infant free school meals, and that has not changed. The new rules matter from year 3 upwards, and for older siblings in secondary school.

2. Why you still have to register

This is the part the headlines skipped. The guidance is explicit that a pupil is only eligible to receive a free school meal where both a claim for this support has been made and their eligibility has been verified by the school or local authority.

Being on Universal Credit does not put a meal in front of your child. Registering does. The DfE has told schools to promote the new entitlement, to accept applications as early as possible using a model registration form, and to process new applications from 1 June. A family found eligible on or after 1 June is entitled to provision for the whole of the 2026 to 2027 academic year.

If you have never applied because you assumed your earnings ruled you out, apply now, through your local authority's free school meals page or your school office. Most authorities check eligibility electronically against DWP data, so you usually do not need to send payslips or award letters.

There is a second reason to act this month. Transitional protections, in place since 1 April 2018, ended at the close of the 2025 to 2026 academic year. Those protections kept children on free meals even when household circumstances changed. From this term, households must meet the criteria to continue. Schools have been told to recheck eligibility annually and to complete checks ahead of the October census. A child whose family has drifted off Universal Credit since 2018 may lose the meal, and a child whose family is now on it, at any earnings level, gains one.

3. Targeted versus expanded: the difference schools will feel

The £7,400 figure has not gone away. It now marks the boundary between two categories inside the same entitlement.

CategoryWhoWhat it brings
Targeted FSMUniversal Credit households with annual earnings of £7,400 or lessA free meal, plus pupil premium and other disadvantage funding for the school
Expanded FSMUniversal Credit households with earnings above £7,400A free meal only

Pupil premium is calculated on the Ever 6 FSM cohort, meaning pupils who are or have been eligible over the last six years. The DfE guidance states that, following the expansion, Ever 6 FSM will be based on targeted FSM. Expanded eligibility does not feed into it.

Why does a parent care? Because pupil premium is money the school spends on your child's cohort, and because some schools attach extras to it: help with uniform, trips, music lessons, or revision materials. If your school runs those schemes off the pupil premium list rather than the free meals list, an expanded-category child may not be included. Ask the school which list it uses. It is a fair question and the answer is not always the same. Uniform also has its own rule from September 2026 limiting branded items, which is worth reading before you buy: the branded item limit and what it saves a family.

Schools receive a new FSM expansion grant for 2026 to 2027 to cover the extra meals. The guidance stresses that pupils must be captured in the October census for that funding to follow. That is the practical deadline behind the polite requests to register: your school's money for your child's lunch depends on the paperwork being in before October.

4. A worked example: two children in Luton

Priya lives in Luton with two children, one in year 4 and one in year 8. She works part time and the household receives Universal Credit with annual take-home earnings of roughly £19,000.

Last year she was £11,600 over the £7,400 limit and paid for both lunches. This term both children are entitled, in the expanded category.

How much is that worth? The government has said the change puts around £500 a year back into a parent's pocket per child. That is Downing Street's figure, published when the policy was announced in June 2025, and it is an average, not a promise. The saving depends on what your school charges and how many days your child actually buys lunch. Using the government's own number, Priya's household is around £1,000 a year better off, provided she registers and her children eat the meals rather than bringing a packed lunch.

Her children are in the expanded category, so the school gains the meal funding but not pupil premium for them. Nothing about that reduces what Priya's children eat. It only affects the extras the school may or may not offer off its pupil premium list.

5. Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland

Everything above is England. School meals are devolved. Wales already provides free meals to all primary pupils. Scotland provides them to all pupils in primary 1 to 5 and to some older pupils on a benefits basis. Northern Ireland runs its own income-based test through the Education Authority. If you live outside England, check your own nation's scheme rather than the DfE guidance.

6. The honest verdict

If you are on Universal Credit in England and have not registered, do it this week. The entitlement is real, it is for the whole school year, and it is not automatic. There is nothing to buy and nothing to pay.

If your child was on free meals through transitional protection, check. That protection ended in July. If your household is no longer on Universal Credit, the meal may stop, and you should hear from the school rather than find out at the till.

Ask which list the school uses for extras. Expanded free school meals bring the lunch but not the pupil premium. That is the one detail in this policy that a parent can do something about, by asking.

Frequently asked

Does my child get the meal automatically because we are on Universal Credit? No. The DfE guidance says eligibility requires both a claim to have been made and verification by the school or local authority. Apply through your council or school.

We earn more than £7,400. Are we really included? Yes, in England, from the 2026 to 2027 academic year. The £7,400 line now only decides whether your child is in the targeted or expanded category, and both get the meal.

What is the difference between targeted and expanded? Targeted covers Universal Credit households with annual earnings of £7,400 or less and attracts pupil premium for the school. Expanded covers households above that and brings the meal only.

My child had free meals under transitional protection. Do I need to do anything? Yes. Transitional protection ended at the end of the 2025 to 2026 academic year. Your household must now meet the criteria, which for most families means being on Universal Credit.
