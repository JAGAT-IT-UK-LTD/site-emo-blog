Free School Meals and Universal Credit in 2026: Every Family in England Now Qualifies, If You Register

Since the start of the 2026 to 2027 school year, every child in a Universal Credit household in England can get free school meals, whatever the household earns. The £7,400 earnings limit now only decides which category your child is in. The meal is not automatic, so you still have to apply, and it is not too late to do it this term.

1. What changed for Universal Credit families in September 2026?

Until July 2026, a family on Universal Credit in England only qualified for free school meals if annual household earnings, after tax, were £7,400 or less. That threshold applied to claims made from 1 April 2018 and never moved.

A parent working part time at the minimum wage sailed past it and got nothing. That is the gap the change closed.

The government announced the expansion on 4 June 2025. Its press release said over half a million more children would benefit, and that the change would lift 100,000 children in England out of poverty.

The new rules started with the autumn term in September 2026. The Department for Education's guidance for local authorities and schools, last updated on 7 October 2026, says eligibility now covers all children from households in receipt of Universal Credit, with no earnings test for the expanded group.

GOV.UK's own apply page now says the same in plain words. From the start of the 2026 to 2027 academic year, your child will qualify for free school meals if you get Universal Credit.

The qualifying benefits list is short. Universal Credit, or support under Part VI of the Immigration and Asylum Act 1999. Households on income-related Employment and Support Allowance or the guarantee element of Pension Credit may also be entitled. There is separate guidance for families with no recourse to public funds.

Children in reception, year 1 and year 2 in England were already fed under universal infant free school meals, and that has not changed. The new rules matter from year 3 upwards, and for older siblings in secondary school.

2. Do you still have to apply for free school meals?

Yes. This is the part the headlines skipped. The guidance is explicit that a pupil is only eligible where both a claim has been made and eligibility has been verified by the school or local authority.

Being on Universal Credit does not put a meal in front of your child. Registering does.

The good news is that a late claim is not wasted. The guidance says a family found eligible on or after 1 June 2026 is entitled to provision for the whole 2026 to 2027 academic year. Apply in November and your child is covered until the summer.

If you have never applied because you assumed your earnings ruled you out, apply now. Use your local authority's free school meals page, which GOV.UK links to, or ask your school office.

Most authorities check eligibility electronically against DWP data. You usually do not need to send payslips or award letters.

There is a second reason to check. Transitional protections, in place since 1 April 2018, ended at the close of the 2025 to 2026 academic year. Those protections kept children on free meals even when household circumstances changed.

From this term, households must meet the criteria to continue. A child whose family has drifted off Universal Credit since 2018 may lose the meal. A child whose family is now on it, at any earnings level, gains one.

Schools must recheck eligibility every year ahead of the October census. For autumn 2026 only, the DfE allowed checks completed by 2 November 2026 to count. That date has now passed, but it affects school funding, not your child's right to the meal.

3. What is the difference between targeted and expanded free school meals?

The £7,400 figure has not gone away. It now marks the boundary between two categories inside the same entitlement.

CategoryWhoWhat it brings
Targeted FSMUniversal Credit households with annual earnings of £7,400 or lessA free meal, plus pupil premium and other disadvantage funding for the school
Expanded FSMUniversal Credit households with earnings above £7,400A free meal only

Pupil premium is calculated on the Ever 6 FSM cohort, meaning pupils who are or have been eligible over the last six years. The DfE guidance states that, following the expansion, Ever 6 FSM will be based on targeted FSM. Expanded eligibility does not feed into it.

Why does a parent care? Because pupil premium is money the school spends on your child's cohort. Some schools attach extras to it: help with uniform, trips, music lessons or revision materials.

If your school runs those schemes off the pupil premium list rather than the free meals list, an expanded category child may not be included. Ask the school which list it uses. It is a fair question, and the answer is not always the same.

Uniform also has its own rule from September 2026 limiting branded items, which is worth reading before you buy: the branded item limit and what it saves a family.

Schools receive a new FSM expansion grant for 2026 to 2027 to pay for the extra meals. The guidance sets it at £505 per pupil per year for mainstream schools, worked out from the change in eligibility between the October 2025 and October 2026 censuses.

That is why schools pressed families to register before the census. The money for your child's lunch follows the paperwork, so registering helps your school as well as your budget.

4. A worked example: two children in Luton

Priya lives in Luton with two children, one in year 4 and one in year 8. She works part time, and the household receives Universal Credit with annual take home earnings of roughly £19,000.

Last year she was £11,600 over the £7,400 limit and paid for both lunches. This year both children are entitled, in the expanded category.

How much is that worth? The government said the change "puts £500 back into parents' pockets every year". That is its own figure from June 2025, and it is an average, not a promise.

The real saving depends on what your school charges and how many days your child buys lunch. Using the government's number, Priya's household is around £1,000 a year better off, as long as she registers and her children eat the meals.

5. Does this apply in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland?

No. Everything above is England, because school meals are devolved.

Wales already offers free meals to all primary pupils, through its Universal Primary Free School Meals scheme. Older pupils there still use a means tested scheme.

Scotland gives free lunches to all pupils in primary 1 to 5 at council schools. In primary 6 and 7 it depends on benefits such as Scottish Child Payment. In secondary school, Universal Credit counts only where monthly earned income is not more than £995.

Northern Ireland runs its own income based test through the Education Authority. If you live outside England, check your own nation's scheme rather than the DfE guidance.

6. The honest verdict

If you are on Universal Credit in England and have not registered, do it this week. The entitlement is real, it covers the whole school year, and it is not automatic. There is nothing to buy and nothing to pay.

If your child was on free meals through transitional protection, check. That protection has ended. If your household is no longer on Universal Credit, the meal may stop, and you should hear from the school rather than find out at the till.

Ask which list the school uses for extras. Expanded free school meals bring the lunch but not the pupil premium. That is the one detail in this policy that a parent can do something about, by asking.

Frequently asked questions about free school meals and Universal Credit

Does my child get free school meals automatically on Universal Credit? No. The DfE guidance says eligibility needs both a claim and verification by the school or local authority. Apply through your council's free school meals page or your school office. Most councils check your Universal Credit electronically, so you rarely need to send payslips or letters.

We earn more than £7,400. Are we really included? Yes, in England, from the 2026 to 2027 academic year. Since September 2026 the £7,400 line only decides whether your child is in the targeted or the expanded category. Both categories get the free meal. Only targeted pupils also bring pupil premium funding to the school.

Is it too late to apply for free school meals this year? No. The guidance says a family found eligible on or after 1 June 2026 is entitled for the whole 2026 to 2027 school year. A claim made in November still covers the rest of the year. The 2 November census date affected school funding, not your child's right to eat.

What is the difference between targeted and expanded free school meals? Targeted covers Universal Credit households with annual earnings of £7,400 or less, and it attracts pupil premium for the school. Expanded covers Universal Credit households above that line and brings the meal only. The school gets a separate expansion grant of £505 per pupil for mainstream schools.

My child had free meals under transitional protection. Do I need to do anything? Yes. Transitional protection ended at the end of the 2025 to 2026 academic year. Your household must now meet the criteria again, which for most families means being on Universal Credit. If you are still on it, at any earnings level, your child keeps the meal.
