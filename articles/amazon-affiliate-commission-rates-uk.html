Amazon Affiliate Commission Rates UK 2026: The Full Rate Card, and Who Pays More

Published 22 August 2026 · Updated 14 November 2026 · 14 min read · Figures checked November 2026

Amazon affiliate commission rates in the UK run from 6% down to zero. Fashion, shoes and watches pay 6%. Books, kitchen, home, furniture, tools and automotive pay 5%, and anything Amazon does not list pays 3%. The rate that matters is the one for the item your reader actually buys, which is often not the one you linked.

The short version
  • The UK rate card tops out at 6% for fashion and luxury. Books, automotive, furniture, home, kitchen, tools and jewellery pay 5%. Everything not listed is 3%.
  • The card is unchanged when checked against Amazon's own page on 14 November 2026.
  • Amazon pays least where prices are highest. Appliances and Fire TV pay 2.5%, video games and grocery 1%, gift cards and wine nothing.
  • The rate follows the item the reader actually buys, not the item you linked to.
  • The Netherlands, Belgium and Ireland pay up to 14% for the same clothing a UK link pays 6% on.

  • The UK payment threshold is £25 by bank transfer, paid about 60 days after the month ends.

What are the Amazon affiliate commission rates in the UK?

Amazon Associates does not pay a flat percentage of the price. It pays a rate it has set by category.

That rate is lowest, on purpose, where prices are highest. Once you see that, most beginner advice about promoting expensive products falls apart.

It is also why chasing costly products backfires, which we work through in why high ticket items are a trap on Amazon.

Here is the standard UK commission rate list, copied from Amazon's own Standard Commission Income Statement. Germany uses the identical card, which we also checked today.

Compare that with the US card. There the catch all is 4%, kitchen, automotive and physical books pay 4.5%, and Luxury Beauty pays 10%.

The UK card is simpler and, across most of the catalogue, a little weaker. Keep that in mind before you assume an American guide applies to you.

Which rules decide what you are actually paid?

Two things in the commission statement decide which rate you get. Neither is printed on the rate table itself.

Direct against indirect. A purchase is direct only when the item bought sits in the same product category as the page your link pointed at.

Send someone to a book page and they buy a saucepan, and that is an indirect qualifying purchase. A link to a category page or the Amazon home page can never be direct.

The free Kindle rule nobody quotes. You earn no commission at all in any month where two things are both true.

First, 10,000 or more free Kindle ebooks are ordered through your links. Second, at least 80% of all Kindle ebooks ordered through them are free.

A free ebook roundup site can hit both and be paid nothing for the month. Amazon is not the only programme worth testing against your own traffic, and what the eBay Partner Network pays on a sale is built on a different model entirely.

Rule one: the item purchased sets the rate, not the item you linked. You write a careful 3,000 word review of a £2,500 television. The reader clicks, then buys a £30 HDMI cable and a week's groceries.

You are paid at cable and grocery rates. The useful half cuts the other way. A reader who arrives through a kitchen link and fills a basket earns you commission on the whole qualifying cart.

Because the link does that much work, people ask whether to route it through a redirect or a tidier URL. Here is what cloaking an affiliate link actually gains and costs.

Rule two: the click window is 24 hours, the cart window is 89 days. A session ends when 24 hours pass, when the customer orders a physical product, or when they click another Associate's link.

Anything added to the basket inside those first 24 hours stays yours if the order is placed within 89 days of the first click. Amazon's own policy text says 89, so plan around 89.

Cookie windows differ sharply between networks, so do not carry the 24 hour assumption anywhere else.

Since 14 April 2026 there is a third rule. The product must also be shipped, streamed or downloaded, and paid for, within 180 days, or it does not qualify.

The strategic consequence is not subtle. The best paying format on Amazon is not the single product review.

It is the build guide: the garage gym, the first flat kitchen, the camera kit, the car audio install. That is content that honestly puts six items in one basket, and gives the reader a reason to add to basket today.

It also decides where your hours go. A build guide that already ranks is usually worth more attention than a new one, as we set out in refreshing affiliate content against writing new posts.

Do other Amazon countries pay more?

Yes, several pay far more. The UK card is one of the weaker ones in the programme.

Stated plainly, a £250 handbag on Amazon.nl pays 14%. The same handbag on Amazon.co.uk pays 6%.

A kitchen appliance in Australia pays 10% against 5% here. A Canadian bag of dog food pays 9%, against 3% on the UK catch all.

But read the catch all column before you get excited. Canada and Australia have the worst catch all rates in the programme, at 2%.

Anything not explicitly listed there earns you almost nothing. A high top band with a poor catch all is a trap if your content is broad.

Two practical limits apply. First, each marketplace has its own Associates account, its own tax and payment setup, and its own three qualifying sales test.

Open accounts in five countries on day one and you may fail the test in all five. Clear it in one market first.

Second, link localisation tools send a visitor to their own country's store. That store's card then applies, so a UK visitor redirected from a US link earns UK rates, not US ones.

Earning across marketplaces also means being paid in more than one currency. Here is what actually lands in a UK account when you are paid in dollars, which can quietly remove the advantage you chased.

What does the Influencer onsite rate pay?

Amazon runs a second, lower card for onsite commission. That is money earned from shoppable videos and content placed on Amazon's own pages through the Influencer programme.

In the UK, fashion still pays 6% and handmade 5%. Home, kitchen and tools drop to 4%.

Books, beauty, grocery, toys, sports and car items pay 3.5%. Electronics, computers and Amazon devices pay 2%, consoles 0.5%, and the onsite catch all is 1.5%.

Since 14 April 2026, onsite commission only applies when the buyer purchases the same product variant you linked. The full trade off is in the Amazon Influencer programme against Associates.

How much do Amazon bounties pay in the UK?

Bounties are flat payments for a signup, with no product purchase needed. Almost every beginner ignores them.

Amazon's UK Audible page sets the standard rates at £5 per free trial and £8 per full price £5.99 a month membership.

From 1 October to 16 December 2026, Audible paid signups earn £20 instead of £8. Promotional £0.99 for three months signups count as full memberships during that window.

An Amazon Music Unlimited free trial pays £4.50, whatever plan the customer picks. It still pays if they cancel inside the 30 days.

Put that next to the rate card. A standard £8 bounty is what you earn on a £160 kitchen sale at 5%, or a £267 sale at the 3% catch all.

At the current £20, one Audible signup matches a £400 kitchen basket. For a books, study or commuting article, that is the fastest money on the UK programme.

The trial must start from your link. Trials started through other routes, such as Amazon's own checkout, do not earn the bounty.

When and how does Amazon pay you?

  • Threshold: £25 by bank transfer or Amazon.co.uk gift certificate. Below that, the balance rolls into the next month.
  • Cheques: only for people who are not UK residents, with a £50 threshold. A UK site takes the bank transfer.
  • Timing: payment lands roughly 60 days after the end of the month you earned it. January's commission arrives around the end of March.
  • Commission accrues only after the order ships, not when it is placed.
  • Returns reverse your commission, deducted from later earnings, sometimes months on.
  • Subscriptions pay nothing unless the agreement says otherwise. Amazon's commission statement excludes products bought as a subscription.

Before any of that, your application is only reviewed once you have made three qualifying sales within 180 days of signing up. Personal orders do not count.

The fuller set of account rules is in the rules that get Amazon affiliate accounts closed.

Did Amazon cut commission rates in 2026?

For some large publishers, yes. In May 2026 Adweek reported that Amazon had cut affiliate commissions by up to 50% for some publishers, and eMarketer followed it up.

The reports also said milestone bonuses and some reporting detail had gone. Amazon's reply was that the changes affect a tiny fraction of Associates.

Here is what can be verified. The public UK rate card is unchanged. We checked it line by line on 14 November 2026 and it matches the card above.

The evidence points to cuts in negotiated rates for big publishers, not the standard card a new site sits on. If you are starting now, those reports do not describe your situation.

The published changes that did land on 14 April 2026 are set out in what changed in the April 2026 Associates agreement.

The reports do show the direction of travel, though. That is the strongest argument in this whole subject for never building a business on Amazon alone.

A programme that can rewrite your income overnight, with no notice and no negotiation, is a revenue stream you rent rather than own.

What should a UK site do with this?

  1. Pick a niche inside the 5% band. Kitchen, automotive, tools, home and furniture. Decent rate, real ticket sizes, and readers who buy in multiples.
  2. Avoid the categories the card punishes. Televisions, computers, games consoles and grocery are traffic bait. They look impressive and pay badly.
  3. Write build guides, not single product reviews. The 89 day basket rule rewards content that fills a basket.
  4. Add one bounty angle. If your topic touches books, study, commuting or driving, an Audible article is the fastest realistic income on the UK card, and it pays £20 a signup until 16 December 2026.
  5. Do not open five country accounts. Clear three sales in one market first.
  6. Test a second network. For the same click, a brand's own programme often pays more. Our Amazon Associates against Awin comparison shows where.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Amazon affiliate commission rate in the UK? It depends on the category of the item bought. Fashion and luxury pay 6%, books, kitchen, home, tools and automotive pay 5%, and beauty and sports pay 4%. Everything Amazon does not list pays 3%. Appliances pay 2.5%, games and grocery 1%, and gift cards nothing.

Can I use the US rates on my UK site? No. Your commission is set by the marketplace where the purchase happens. A UK reader buying from Amazon.co.uk earns you UK rates, whatever card you read. If a link tool redirects that reader to their home store, that store's card applies instead, so plan around the UK card for a UK audience.

Is 3% worth bothering with? On a £40 product, 3% is £1.20, so you would need a lot of buyers to make a living. On a £900 kitchen basket at 5%, it is £45. The rate matters less than the basket size, which is the whole argument for build guide content that fills a basket in one visit.

Can I write Amazon prices in my article? Not by hand. Amazon only allows prices where its own link serves them, or where you pull them through its product API, and you must show a date and time stamp beside them. Typed or scraped prices break the rules. Write check the current price and link instead.

How long before Amazon pays anything? Longer than most people expect. You need three qualifying sales within 180 days for your application to be reviewed, then £25 of earnings to trigger a payment, then a wait of about 60 days after the month ends. Plan for around six months before money reaches your account.

The verdict

The UK card is workable, not generous. Build inside the 5% band, write content that fills a basket rather than sells one item, take the bounties seriously, and treat Amazon as one income stream among several rather than the business itself. Anyone promising more than that from a 3% catch all rate is selling you something.
