Missed the Self Assessment Registration Deadline of 5 October 2026? Register Now and You Can Still Pay Nothing Extra

The late registration penalty is a percentage of tax unpaid on 31 January 2027, so if you file and pay by then it is usually nil. That matters because waiting for an HMRC letter raises the minimum penalty from 0% to 10%, and late payment interest rises to 8% from 24 November 2026.

1. Who should have registered by 5 October 2026?

GOV.UK put the rule in one line. You had to tell HMRC by 5 October 2026 if you needed a tax return for the previous tax year.

That applied if you had never sent one, or if you had registered before but did not need to file for 2024 to 2025. The previous tax year ran from 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026.

So the question is not whether you have side income now. It is whether you had enough of it in the twelve months to last April.

For most readers of this site, the trigger is the trading allowance. You can earn £1,000 of gross trading income in a tax year without telling HMRC. Not profit, gross.

Sell £1,200 of prints on Etsy and spend £400 on materials and postage, and you are over the line. The £1,200 is what counts. We go through the allowance, and when selling your own old clothes counts at all, in our side hustle tax rules guide.

The same deadline caught lodger landlords above the Rent a Room limit. It also caught anyone whose untaxed savings interest or dividends first pushed them into owing tax. HMRC's factsheet puts the self-employment case as "within 6 months of the end of the relevant tax year", which is 5 October by another route.

What about the £3,000 figure in the news? The Budget on 28 October 2026 confirmed the trading allowance and the Self Assessment reporting threshold will rise to £3,000. That starts on 6 April 2027, with the 2027 to 2028 tax year. It does not reach back. For 2025 to 2026, the £1,000 figure still decides whether you should have registered.

2. Missed the Self Assessment registration deadline: what now?

Register now. That is the whole answer, and GOV.UK offers no other route. The same online service works in November as it did in September.

It is not too late to register for Self Assessment. GOV.UK only says that if you tell HMRC after 5 October 2026, you "could get a penalty". Could, not will.

The reason is how the penalty is built. It is a share of the tax you have not paid on time. If you register, file and pay before 31 January 2027, there is usually nothing left unpaid for the penalty to bite on.

So your plan has three steps. Register this month. File the return when your notice arrives. Pay any tax by 31 January 2027.

The one thing not to do is wait. Online platforms now report seller data to HMRC, and a letter turns your late registration from voluntary into prompted. That raises the floor of the penalty from 0% to 10%.

3. How big is the late registration penalty, in numbers?

There is no £100 flat fine for registering late. That fixed penalty belongs to filing the return late.

Registering late is a "failure to notify". HMRC's factsheet CC/FS11, updated 19 March 2026, sets the penalty as a percentage of the potential lost revenue. That means the tax your late registration put at risk.

Your behaviourHow HMRC found outPenalty range
Non-deliberateYou told them, within 12 months of the tax being due0% to 30% of tax
Non-deliberateYou told them, 12 months or more after10% to 30%
Non-deliberateThey found it, within 12 months10% to 30%
Non-deliberateThey found it, 12 months or more after20% to 30%
DeliberateYou told them / they found it20% to 70% / 35% to 70%
Deliberate and concealedYou told them / they found it30% to 100% / 50% to 100%

Read the first row again. Miss the date through not knowing, come forward before HMRC writes, and the penalty can be zero.

Within each range, HMRC reduces the penalty for telling, helping and giving access. Someone who registers late, explains why and files a full return has done all three.

Because the penalty is a percentage of tax, a return with no tax due produces no penalty. Think of a student under the £12,570 personal allowance, or a part timer whose expenses ate the profit. They still have to register. The sum at the end is nil.

The picture changes when HMRC finds you first. Since 1 January 2024, Vinted, eBay, Etsy, Airbnb and similar platforms have had to collect seller data. The first reports reached HMRC in January 2025. The letters that followed are covered in our nudge letter guide. Registering before a letter arrives is worth real money.

4. How do you register late, and what arrives afterwards?

Go to GOV.UK's "Check how to register for Self Assessment" page and press Start now. It asks a handful of questions and picks your route: self-employed, not self-employed, or partner.

You need your National Insurance number, your address history and a few details about the work. Allow about 20 minutes.

A ten digit Unique Taxpayer Reference then arrives by post. An activation code follows if you have no online account yet. GOV.UK's reply times page shows this can take a while in a busy period, so do not leave it until January.

If you registered in an earlier year, reactivate your old record rather than register again. Two UTRs cause more trouble than a late registration.

Once you have the UTR, keep your records together. A spreadsheet of sales, a folder of receipts and the platform's annual statement are enough at this size. If your income is mostly from links and adverts, our guide on declaring affiliate income to HMRC shows where it goes on the return.

5. Which deadlines still apply to you?

Three dates matter now. Two of them have not moved.

WhatDeadline for 2025 to 2026Status on 11 November 2026
Register for Self Assessment5 October 2026Passed. Register now.
Paper return31 October 2026Passed. File online instead.
Online return, to pay through your PAYE tax code30 December 2026Still open, if you qualify
Online return31 January 2027, or 3 months after a late noticeStill open
Pay the tax31 January 2027Still open. This date does not move.

The filing date is the one that can shift. GOV.UK says that if you register after 5 October 2026, HMRC sends a letter or email with a new deadline. The return is then due three months from the date on that letter or email.

The payment date does not shift. The tax is still due by 31 January 2027, even if your notice gives you until spring to file.

That matters because of interest. HMRC's late payment interest rate is 7.75% today and rises to 8% from 24 November 2026. The change follows the Bank of England raising Bank Rate to 4% on 5 November 2026.

If your tax bill is over £1,000, HMRC may also ask for payments on account towards the next year. Half falls due on 31 January and half on 31 July. Our guide to the 31 January 2027 bill and payments on account works through the sums.

The paper route closed on 31 October 2026. We explain why paper was rarely worth it anyway in our paper deadline guide.

6. A worked example: Priya in Colchester

Priya lives in Colchester. She started reselling refurbished games consoles in June 2025, alongside her job.

By 5 April 2026 she had sold £4,800 worth. The stock cost £3,100 and postage and packaging £260. Her salary was £31,000, so her personal allowance was already used and she pays basic rate.

Her gross of £4,800 is well over the £1,000 allowance. She should have registered by 5 October 2026. She did not.

Her profit using actual expenses is £4,800 less £3,360, which is £1,440. Using the £1,000 allowance instead would give £3,800, so expenses win. Tax at 20% on £1,440 is £288.

There is no Class 4 National Insurance, because profit is under £12,570. She pays no Class 2 either. Her profit is below the 2025 to 2026 small profits threshold of £6,845, so she could pay it voluntarily to protect her State Pension record.

Now the three paths from here.

Path one: she registers in November 2026, files, and pays £288 by 31 January 2027. Nothing is unpaid on that date, so the failure to notify penalty is nil. No late filing penalty either, as long as she files by the date on her notice. Total extra cost: £0.

Path two: she registers now, but pays three months late, at the end of April 2027. Interest at 8% on £288 for about 90 days is roughly £5.68. After 30 days a 5% late payment penalty adds £14.40. The failure to notify penalty can also apply to the unpaid £288, at 0% to 30%. With full cooperation it should sit near the bottom.

Path three: she waits, and an HMRC letter reaches her first. The floor rises to 10%, so at least £28.80 and up to £86.40. If her return is also late, the separate £100 late filing penalty applies on top. That one actually hurts.

The gap between path one and path three is well over £100 on a £288 tax bill. Registering this week is how Priya stays on path one.

7. The honest verdict

If your gross side income first went past £1,000 between April 2025 and April 2026 and you missed 5 October, do not panic. Register for Self Assessment now. It is free, it takes about 20 minutes, and your penalty range still starts at zero.

Then do the one thing that really decides the bill: pay any tax by 31 January 2027. Do that and a late registration usually costs nothing.

If you are under the line, or you were only clearing out your own belongings, this does not apply to you. If you are over it and the profit is small, register anyway. A return showing £50 of tax is a far better place to be than a letter from HMRC next spring.

Frequently asked questions about late Self Assessment registration

What happens if you miss the deadline to register for Self Assessment?

Nothing automatic. You may get a failure to notify penalty, worked out as a percentage of tax unpaid on 31 January 2027. If you register yourself, file on time and pay by that date, the unpaid tax is usually nil, so the penalty is nil too. Waiting until HMRC writes raises the minimum to 10%.

Is there a £100 fine for registering late?

No. The £100 fixed penalty is for filing the return late. Late registration is a separate failure to notify, charged as a percentage of the tax at stake. For a non-deliberate failure that you disclose yourself within 12 months of the tax falling due, HMRC's range starts at 0%, and full cooperation keeps it near the bottom.

Is it too late to register for Self Assessment for 2025 to 2026?

No. You can register at any time using the normal GOV.UK service. If HMRC issues your notice after 31 October 2026, you get three months from that notice to file online. The tax is still due by 31 January 2027, so register soon enough to pay on time and avoid interest.

I only made £1,100 in sales. Do I really have to register?

Yes, if it was trading rather than selling your own belongings. The 2025 to 2026 allowance is £1,000 of gross sales, and £1,100 is over it. The tax on the profit may be small or nil. The registration is still required, and it protects you from the higher penalty floor if platform data flags you first.

Does registering mean I have to pay tax now?

No. Registering gets you a Unique Taxpayer Reference and puts you on HMRC's list for a return. It does not create a bill. Any tax for 2025 to 2026 is due by 31 January 2027, the same day as the normal online filing deadline. Late payment interest runs at 8% from 24 November 2026.
