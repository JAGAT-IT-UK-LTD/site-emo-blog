Winter Fuel Payment 2026: The Opt Out Has Closed. When You Get Paid and What HMRC Takes Back

If you were born on or before 27 June 1960 and live in England, Wales or Northern Ireland, £200 or £300 should reach your bank in November or December 2026. If your own income is over £35,000, HMRC will take it back through your tax code or Self Assessment, and two dates still matter: 31 March 2027 to opt back in, and 21 December 2026 when next winter's opt out opens.

Update, 11 November 2026: the deadline has passed

When this guide first went up, the opt out deadline was days away. It closed at 11:59pm on Sunday 20 September 2026. The GOV.UK opt out page now reads, in its own words, that you "can no longer opt out" for 2026 to 2027.

So the choice for this winter is made. Payments are now going out. This page explains what that means for your bank account and your tax code, and the two dates still open to you.

1. What happened to the Winter Fuel Payment 2026 opt out?

The Winter Fuel Payment 2026 opt out ran until 20 September. There were three ways in: the Manage your State Pension service, the online form on GOV.UK, or the helpline, which closed earlier at 6pm on 18 September.

If you did nothing, you are in for this winter. You will get a letter and then a payment. If your own income is over £35,000, HMRC will take that payment back later.

If you did opt out, nothing will arrive this winter. You stay opted out for future winters too, until you opt back in.

The next window is already set. GOV.UK says you will be able to opt out of the payment for winter 2027 to 2028 from 21 December 2026.

2. Who gets the Winter Fuel Payment 2026 and how much?

You are eligible if you were born on or before 27 June 1960 and usually live in England, Wales or Northern Ireland. Scotland has its own scheme, the Pension Age Winter Heating Payment, run by Social Security Scotland.

What you get depends on your date of birth and who you lived with during the qualifying week, 21 to 27 September 2026. That week has now passed, so your amount is already fixed.

You do not get it if you were in hospital receiving free treatment for the whole qualifying week and the year before it, were in prison for the whole qualifying week, or your leave to be in the UK says you cannot claim public funds.

Care home residents are excluded only when two things are both true. They get Pension Credit, Universal Credit or income-related ESA, and they had lived in a care home the whole time from 29 June 2026 or earlier.

The money does not affect any other benefit you get.

3. When will the Winter Fuel Payment 2026 be paid?

Most payments are made automatically in November or December 2026. Before the money, you should get a letter. It says how much you will get and which bank account it goes into. That is usually the account your State Pension or other benefits are paid into.

GOV.UK sets one clear checkpoint. If you have had no letter, or no money, by 27 January 2027, contact the Winter Fuel Payment Centre on 0800 731 0160, Monday to Friday, 8am to 6pm.

Most people never need to claim. But some do. If you do not get the State Pension, Pension Credit, Universal Credit or certain disability or carer benefits, you must claim if you have never had the payment before, or you have deferred your State Pension since your last one. The claim deadline for this winter is 31 March 2027.

One warning from GOV.UK itself. Scam calls, emails and texts pretend to be about this payment. The Winter Fuel Payment Centre will not ask you for passwords. If a message asks for bank details to "release" your payment, treat it as a scam.

4. The £35,000 test and how HMRC claws it back

This is the part most people search for. If your total income for the tax year is over £35,000, you still receive the payment. Then HMRC takes it back.

GOV.UK is plain on two points that headlines blur. The test is on your income alone: your partner's income does not count. And you cannot simply hand the money back; HMRC collects it.

Income means the total you expect for the 2026 to 2027 tax year before deductions: State Pension, private pensions, earnings, savings interest, rent. HMRC's online checker tells you whether you are over the line and how it will collect.

How HMRC collects depends on how you pay tax:

  • PAYE, which covers most pensioners with a private pension or a job: HMRC changes your tax code. More tax comes out of your pension or wages. If it cannot collect the full amount in the tax year, it sends a tax calculation letter.
  • Self Assessment: the payment goes on your return as a "Winter Fuel Payment charge" and is added to your bill. It is usually added automatically.
  • Making Tax Digital for Income Tax: you cannot pay it back through your software. HMRC writes to you with the amount and how and when to pay.

The clawback can stack. GOV.UK gives its own example. If HMRC is already collecting your 2025 payment through your tax code, the code changes again in January 2027 to start collecting the 2026 one. If both payments were £200, you pay roughly £30 to £33 more tax a month.

If you do not opt out of the next one, the code changes again in April 2027. It keeps collecting the 2026 payment and starts collecting the 2027 one in advance.

Keep the two recovery routes apart in your head. This one runs through HMRC and your tax code. Money the department itself says you owe is taken under separate rules, covered in what DWP debt recovery can take from a benefit or a bank account from 26 October 2026.

Note the shape of the rule. It is a cliff, not a taper. £35,001 of income means the whole payment comes back. £34,999 means you keep all of it.

If your new tax code looks wrong, check it. Our guide on how to fix a wrong tax code walks through it, and what an HMRC P800 tax calculation letter means covers the year end letter.

5. What does opting out commit you to?

This is the part people miss. You do not opt out for one winter. You stay opted out in future years until you opt in again.

That matters if your income falls. Say a part time job ends and you drop below £35,000. Nothing happens automatically. You have to contact the Winter Fuel Payment Centre by phone or post.

To get the 2026 to 2027 payment after opting out, you must contact them before 31 March 2027. They will ask for your name, address, date of birth and National Insurance number.

Opting out never affected your State Pension. It also does nothing for anyone else. It does not change your partner's payment and it does not free up money for other pensioners.

6. A worked example in Stevenage

Bernard in Stevenage is 68, lives alone and was born in 1958, so his payment is £200. His income for 2026 to 2027 is £12,548 of full new State Pension (£241.30 a week), a £19,200 company pension and about £4,000 from three days a week at a garden centre. Total: £35,748.

He is £748 over the line. He did not opt out in September, so £200 arrives in November or December.

HMRC then adjusts his tax code. The £200 comes back out of his company pension. Spread over twelve months that is about £16.67 a month, and more per month if the change starts late in the year. Net effect: zero.

If he had opted out, nothing would arrive and nothing would be collected. Net effect: also zero, with less paperwork.

Now the version that matters. Suppose his garden centre work stops in January 2027. His income drops to £31,748, under the threshold. Because he stayed in, he should keep the £200. He should tell HMRC his income has fallen so his tax code is corrected. Any extra tax taken should come back after the year ends.

His neighbour Pauline made the other choice. She opted out in September, expecting the same job income. Hers stopped too. She has until 31 March 2027 to phone 0800 731 0160 and opt back in, or she loses this winter's £200 and next winter's too. The opt out is the option with the downside.

7. What should you do now?

If your income is under £35,000 and you did nothing: wait for your letter and the payment. If nothing has come by 27 January 2027, call the Winter Fuel Payment Centre.

If you opted out but your income is now under £35,000: opt back in before 31 March 2027. Otherwise you lose this winter's payment and every one after it.

If your income is over £35,000: do nothing with the payment itself. Watch for HMRC's email or letter about your tax code, and check the new code is right. On Self Assessment, expect the charge on your return.

If you are comfortably over £35,000 every year: you can opt out for winter 2027 to 2028 from 21 December 2026. That saves admin, not money.

If you are near the line: stay in. Income can fall, and being wrong in the opt out direction costs you the payment and a phone call to undo it.

8. The honest verdict

The 20 September deadline was a deadline to refuse money. If you ignored it and your own income is under £35,000, you made the right call. Let the payment arrive.

If you are over £35,000, doing nothing cost you nothing in total. HMRC takes the payment back through your tax code or your return. Opting out saves admin, not money, and it stays in force until you undo it.

The one thing worth doing this month is checking your letter, and your tax code if you are over the line. If you opted out and now regret it, the date to remember is 31 March 2027.

Opting out of this payment does not touch anything else you are owed this winter. The £150 Warm Home Discount is settled on its own qualifying date and applied to the electricity bill automatically, on a different test entirely. Nor does it touch what your council can give: the Crisis and Resilience Fund that replaced the Household Support Fund is applied for locally on its own rules. Frequently asked questions about the Winter Fuel Payment 2026

When is the Winter Fuel Payment 2026 paid?

Most eligible people are paid automatically in November or December 2026, into the account their State Pension or benefits go into. A letter comes first, in October or November, stating the amount. If you have had no letter or no money by 27 January 2027, GOV.UK says to contact the Winter Fuel Payment Centre on 0800 731 0160.

Can I still opt out of the Winter Fuel Payment for 2026?

No. The opt out closed on 20 September 2026 online and 18 September by phone. GOV.UK says you can no longer opt out for 2026 to 2027. If your income is over £35,000, you will get the money and HMRC will take it back. The opt out for winter 2027 to 2028 opens on 21 December 2026.

Is the £35,000 threshold household income?

No. GOV.UK states that your partner's income does not count towards your total. Each person is tested on their own income for the 2026 to 2027 tax year, before deductions. That includes State Pension, private pensions, earnings, savings interest and rent. One partner can keep the payment while HMRC takes the other partner's back through their tax code.

What if I opted out and my income has dropped?

You stay opted out until you contact the Winter Fuel Payment Centre to opt back in. To receive the 2026 to 2027 payment, you must do that before 31 March 2027, by phone on 0800 731 0160 or by post. Have your name, address, date of birth and National Insurance number ready when you call or write.

Do I have to repay it for someone who has died?

Only if their income was over £35,000. GOV.UK says that if the person who died received the payment and their income was over the threshold, it is usually paid back when their estate is settled. If their income was under £35,000, the payment is theirs to keep. People in Scotland are covered by the separate Pension Age Winter Heating Payment instead.
