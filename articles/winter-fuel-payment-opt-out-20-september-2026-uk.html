Winter Fuel Payment: The Opt-Out Closes At 11:59pm On 20 September. Most People Should Ignore It.

Ruhul Amin · Published 4 September 2026 · 6 min read · Figures checked September 2026

The Winter Fuel Payment for winter 2026 to 2027 goes automatically to anyone born on or before 27 June 1960 who lives in England, Wales or Northern Ireland. There is a deadline in the news, 20 September, but it is a deadline to give the money up, not to claim it. Here is who the opt-out is actually for, what HMRC does if you earn over £35,000 and do nothing, and why for most people the right action is none.

The short version

  • You get £200 if you were born between 28 September 1946 and 27 June 1960, or £300 if you were born before 28 September 1946, provided you live in England, Wales or Northern Ireland. Couples who both qualify usually get £100 each. Source: GOV.UK, read 4 September 2026.
  • Nobody needs to claim. The payment is automatic, a letter arrives in October or November, and the money lands in November or December.
  • The £35,000 test is on your own income, not your household's. GOV.UK states plainly that your partner's income does not count.
  • Over £35,000, HMRC takes the whole payment back through your tax code or your Self Assessment bill. You cannot return it yourself.
  • The opt-out deadline is 11:59pm on Sunday 20 September 2026 online, or 6pm on Friday 18 September by phone. Opting out is permanent until you opt back in.
  • If your income is under £35,000, opting out simply gives away £200. If it is over, opting out changes the timing, not the total.

Five days left

Checked on 15 September 2026. The opt-out deadline is 20 September 2026, which is this coming Sunday. If you want to opt out of the Winter Fuel Payment rather than receive it and have it clawed back through the tax system, that is the date the decision has to be made by. Everything below still applies, and none of the figures have changed since this guide was written.

1. Who gets it and how much

The rules for winter 2026 to 2027 sit on GOV.UK and were last updated on 3 September 2026. You are eligible if you were born on or before 27 June 1960 and usually live in England, Wales or Northern Ireland. Scotland has its own scheme, the Pension Age Winter Heating Payment, run by Social Security Scotland.

What you get depends on your date of birth and who you live with during the qualifying week, 21 to 27 September 2026:

Your situationBorn 28 Sep 1946 to 27 Jun 1960Born before 28 Sep 1946
Live alone, or nobody else eligible£200£300
Live with another eligible person, no qualifying benefits£100 each£150 each
Get Pension Credit, Universal Credit or income-related ESA£200£300
Live in a care home£100£150

You do not get it if you were in hospital receiving free treatment for the whole qualifying week and the whole previous year, were in prison for the qualifying week, or have no recourse to public funds. Care home residents on Pension Credit, Universal Credit or income-related ESA who have been in the home since 29 June 2026 or earlier are also excluded.

There is nothing to apply for. If you are eligible you get a letter in October or November stating the amount, and most people are paid in November or December 2026. The money does not affect any other benefit.

2. The £35,000 test and how HMRC claws it back

This is the part that generates the searches. If your total income for the tax year is over £35,000, you still receive the payment, but HMRC takes it back. GOV.UK is explicit on two points that the headlines blur. First, the test is on your income alone: your partner's income does not count towards your total. Second, you cannot simply return the money; HMRC collects it.

Income means the total you expect to receive in the 2026 to 2027 tax year before deductions: state pension, private pensions, earnings, savings interest, rent. HMRC's online checker tells you whether you are over and how it will collect.

How it collects depends on how you pay tax:

  • PAYE, which covers most pensioners with a private pension or a job: HMRC changes your tax code and the extra tax comes out of your pension or wages across the year. HMRC contacts you by email or post when it does this.
  • Self Assessment: the payment goes on your return as a "Winter Fuel Payment charge" and is added to your bill. If you file online, HMRC usually adds it automatically.
  • Making Tax Digital for Income Tax: you cannot pay it through your software. HMRC writes to you after you submit and tells you how to pay.

The clawback stacks. GOV.UK gives its own example: if HMRC is already collecting your 2025 payment through your tax code, the code changes again in January 2027 to start collecting the 2026 payment as well, and you pay roughly £30 to £33 more tax a month if both payments were £200. If you still have not opted out by then, the code changes again in April 2027 to keep collecting the 2026 payment and start collecting the 2027 one in advance. Worth keeping the two recovery routes apart in your head: this one runs through HMRC and your tax code, while money the department itself says you owe is taken under a separate set of rules, which is what DWP debt recovery can take from a benefit or a bank account from 26 October 2026.

Note the shape of that: it is a cliff, not a taper. £35,001 of income means the whole payment comes back. £34,999 means you keep all of it.

3. The opt-out: how, when, and what it commits you to

Three routes, three deadlines:

  • The Manage your State Pension service, if you get a State Pension, before 11:59pm on 20 September 2026.
  • The online opt-out form on GOV.UK, before 11:59pm on 20 September 2026.
  • The Winter Fuel Payment Centre helpline, 0800 731 0160, before 6pm on 18 September 2026.

You need your National Insurance number for the form and the phone. Opting out does not affect your State Pension.

The commitment is the part people miss. You do not opt out for one winter. GOV.UK states that you will not receive the payment in future years unless you opt in again. If your income falls below £35,000 in a later year, say because a part-time job ends, nothing happens automatically. You would have to contact the Winter Fuel Payment Centre, and to get the 2026 to 2027 payment after opting out you would need to do that before 31 March 2027.

4. A worked example in Stevenage

Bernard in Stevenage is 68, lives alone and was born in 1958, so his payment would be £200. His income for 2026 to 2027 is £12,548 of new State Pension, a £19,200 company pension and about £4,000 from three days a week at a garden centre. Total: £35,748.

He is £748 over the line. If he does nothing, £200 arrives in November and HMRC adjusts his tax code so that £200 comes back out of his company pension across the tax year, about £16.67 a month over twelve months, or more per month if the change lands late in the year. Net effect: zero. If HMRC also has a 2025 payment to recover, the monthly figure roughly doubles for a period.

If he opts out by 20 September, nothing arrives and nothing is collected. Net effect: also zero, with less paperwork and no tax code changes.

Now the version that matters. Suppose Bernard's garden centre work stops in January 2027. His income drops to £31,748, under the threshold. If he did nothing in September, HMRC's year-end reconciliation should leave him with the £200. If he opted out, he gets nothing for this winter and nothing next winter either, unless he remembers to opt back in. The opt-out is the option with the downside.

5. Who should opt out, and who should not

Do not opt out if your own income is under £35,000. There is no clawback, and opting out gives away £200 or £300 for no reason. The deadline coverage is aimed at a minority and reads as if it applies to everyone.

Do not opt out if you are near the line either way. Income can fall, and the cost of being wrong in the opt-out direction is a lost payment and a form to reverse it.

Consider opting out if you are comfortably over £35,000 every year and would rather not have your tax code altered, or you are on Self Assessment and want a cleaner return. The saving is administrative, not financial.

Opting out does nothing for anyone else. It does not free up money for other pensioners and it does not change your partner's payment. Each person is assessed alone.

6. The honest verdict

The 20 September deadline is a deadline to refuse money. If your own income is under £35,000, ignore it and let the payment arrive.

If you are over £35,000, doing nothing costs you nothing in total. HMRC takes the payment back through your tax code or your return. Opting out saves admin, not money, and it stays in force until you undo it.

The one thing worth doing this week is the GOV.UK income checker, so you know which side of the line you are on before the letter arrives.

Frequently asked

Is the £35,000 threshold household income? No. GOV.UK states that your partner's income does not count towards your total. Each person is tested on their own income for the 2026 to 2027 tax year.

What if I opt out and then my income drops? You stay opted out until you contact the Winter Fuel Payment Centre to opt back in. To receive the 2026 to 2027 payment after opting out you must do that before 31 March 2027.

Opting out of this payment does not touch anything else you are owed this winter. The £150 Warm Home Discount is settled on its own qualifying date and applied to the electricity bill automatically, on a different test entirely, so nothing you do here affects it. Nor does it touch what your council can give: the Crisis and Resilience Fund that replaced the Household Support Fund is applied for locally and assessed on its own rules. If you are still working out whether the £35,000 threshold catches you at all, what actually changed for this winter, claim dates included, is the place to start.

I live in Scotland, does this apply? No. Scotland has the Pension Age Winter Heating Payment run by Social Security Scotland, with its own rules.

I am dealing with the estate of someone who died. Do I have to repay it? If the person received a payment and their income was over £35,000, GOV.UK says it is usually paid back when the estate is settled.
