1. How much does a slow cooker cost to run per hour?

About 6p an hour for a typical model. Here is the sum, so you can redo it for your own.

Under Ofgem's price cap for 1 October to 31 December 2026, the average direct debit electricity rate is 26.32p per kWh. Your own rate is on your bill and may differ if you are on a fixed or time of use tariff.

A typical slow cooker is rated at 200W to 250W. That is 0.2 to 0.25 kWh an hour, so 5.3p to 6.6p an hour. Small 150W models come in nearer 4p. Big 300W models are nearer 8p.

Those are the most it can cost. A slow cooker's thermostat also cycles once the pot is hot, so the real figure is often a little lower.

The surprising part is that a longer cook is not much dearer. The wattage is so low that ten hours while you are at work costs about 60p. That is the whole appeal.

The rate may move in January. Ofgem sets the cap every three months, and the level for January to March 2027 had not been published when we checked on 24 November 2026. Our January 2027 price cap forecast covers what could change.

2. Is a slow cooker cheaper than the oven?

Yes, clearly, for any long cook. The gap is about five to nine times per hour, not the twenty times some claims suggest.

A common electric oven is rated at about 2.1kW. At full power that is about 55p an hour. Once it is hot, the thermostat switches the element on and off. At 60% of the time on, the average is about 33p an hour. Preheating for 12 minutes at full power adds about 11p.

The payback. Say your slow cooker cost £25 and replaces two oven meals a week. If each saves a conservative 80p in electricity, that is £1.60 a week, or about £83 a year. It pays for itself in about four months on electricity alone.

A breadmaker is the same sum with a different answer, because the saving depends on how many loaves you get through: whether a breadmaker beats shop bread once the electricity is counted.

Where the oven still wins: anything that needs browning, crisping or roasting. A slow cooker cannot make a roast potato. Use both, and fill the oven with several dishes when it is on.

Eight hours in a slow cooker costs about 48p. Three hours in a typical electric oven costs about £1.10 to £1.65.

3. What does one slow cooker meal cost?

A beef stew that feeds four, with leftovers.

Ingredients: say about £6. Braising steak, onions, carrots, potatoes, stock and tinned tomatoes. Prices vary by shop, so check your own.

Electricity: eight hours on low at up to 250W is 2 kWh, so about 48p to 53p.

Total: about £6.50 for four portions plus leftovers, so about £1.63 a head including the electricity.

The same dish in a 2.1kW oven for three hours costs about £1.10 with the thermostat cycling, or up to about £1.65 if it runs hard. The cheap cut is also more likely to dry out.

For a household cooking four slow cooker meals a week at about 40p each, the electricity comes to roughly £83 a year for 208 meals.

4. Where does slow cooker batch cooking save the most?

Batch cooking means one cook, many meals. In a slow cooker, the energy cost of eight portions is the same as four.

The savings come from four places, and people usually only count the first:

  • Energy per portion. Eight portions for 48p is 6p a portion. Four portions is 12p each.
  • Bulk ingredient prices. Bigger packs are usually cheaper per kilo, and cooking in volume is what lets you buy that way.
  • Food waste avoided. The largest one. WRAP puts UK household food and drink waste at about £1,000 a year for a family of four. Cooking to a plan stops vegetables going off in the drawer.
  • Takeaways not ordered. The real rival to a slow cooker meal is not an oven meal. It is a delivery on a Wednesday because nothing was ready. A portion in the freezer removes that choice.

The practical version: cook double, freeze half, label with the date. Two sessions a week can cover most evenings.

If you freeze a lot, check what the freezer itself costs to run. Our chest freezer running cost guide does that sum.

5. Which cheap cuts work best?

Long, low cooking breaks down connective tissue. That is exactly what makes cheap cuts tough. So a slow cooker turns the cheapest meat in the shop into the best it can be, and this saves more than the electricity.

Instead ofUseWhy it works
Beef steakBraising steak, shin, brisketFar cheaper per kilo and better after long cooking
Chicken breastThighs, drumsticks, a whole chickenCheaper, more flavour, does not dry out
Lamb chopsShoulder, neckMade for slow cooking
Pork loinShoulder, bellyCheap and forgiving
Any meatHalf meat, half pulsesLentils and beans stretch a dish for pennies

Adding a tin of lentils to a mince dish is the cheapest way to turn four portions into six. In a slow cooked sauce, most people do not notice.

Vegetarian batch cooking is cheaper still. A chickpea curry or a bean chilli needs no meat at all, and both freeze well.

6. A worked month for a family of four

The Hendersons, Sunderland. Two adults, two children. Before: three oven meals and one takeaway a week.

Before. Three oven meals a week at about £1.40 of electricity each, so £4.20. One takeaway at £29. Weekly cost of those four evenings: £33.20 plus ingredients.

After. Two slow cooker sessions a week, each cooking double. Electricity about 48p a session, so 96p. Four evenings covered, because half goes in the freezer. One oven meal kept at £1.40. No takeaway, because there is always something ready.

Weekly energy and takeaway cost: £2.36. Saving £30.84 a week. Almost all of that is the takeaway, not the electricity, and that is the honest breakdown.

Ingredients. Moving to braising steak and chicken thighs cuts their meat bill by about £6 a week. That is their own estimate, so check yours against your receipts.

Realistic monthly saving: about £160. Of that, about £8 is electricity, about £26 is cheaper cuts, and about £126 is the takeaway that no longer happens.

The honest note: if you never ordered takeaways, your saving is the energy and the cheaper cuts. That is about £34 a month. Still worth doing, and about a fifth of the headline.

The sum, so you can check it: £30.84 plus £6 is £36.84 a week. Times 52, divided by 12, is about £160 a month.

7. How do you batch cook safely?

Batch cooking means more food sitting around, so the safety rules matter more. These follow Food Standards Agency advice.

  • Do not put frozen meat straight in. It spends too long in the temperature range where bacteria grow. Defrost in the fridge first.
  • Boil dried red kidney beans hard for at least 10 minutes before slow cooking. A slow cooker alone may not get hot enough to destroy the natural toxin. Tinned beans are already cooked and are fine.
  • Fill between a half and three quarters. Too empty can burn. Too full may not reach temperature.
  • Cool leftovers quickly and get them in the fridge within two hours. Split them into shallow containers to cool faster.
  • Reheat until steaming hot all the way through, and reheat only once.
  • Freeze in portions and label with the date, so the oldest gets eaten first.

The honest verdict

The energy case is real: about 6p an hour against roughly 33p to 55p for a typical oven. A slow cooker that replaces two oven meals a week pays for itself in a few months, then keeps saving.

But be clear about where the money comes from. The electricity saving is perhaps £80 to £100 a year. The bigger money is in cheap cuts becoming worth eating, food not being wasted, and the takeaway you do not order.

If you buy one, buy a plain one. Timers and searing functions are nice, and none of them change the arithmetic. The saving is in the method, not the model.

Frequently asked

How much does a slow cooker cost to run for 8 hours? About 42p to 53p for a typical 200W to 250W model, at the price cap rate of 26.32p per kWh for October to December 2026. That is the most it can use. The thermostat often cycles once the pot is hot, so your real cost may be a little lower. Check your own unit rate on your bill.

Is it cheaper to use a slow cooker or an oven? A slow cooker, for any long cook. Per hour it uses about a fifth to a ninth of the electricity of a typical 2.1kW oven. A stew that takes three hours in the oven costs about £1.10 to £1.65, against under 50p for eight hours in a slow cooker. The oven only wins for roasting and crisping.

Does a slow cooker use more electricity than an air fryer? Per hour, no, a slow cooker uses far less. An air fryer draws much more power but runs for a short time, so for one quick meal for one or two people it can work out cheaper. For a long cook that feeds a family, or a batch for the freezer, the slow cooker wins clearly.

Is it safe to leave a slow cooker on all day? Yes, they are designed for it, and at 200W to 250W they run cool. Put it on a hard, heatproof surface away from the wall and anything that can burn. Keep the cable tidy, follow the maker's instructions, and never leave a damaged one running while you are out.

Can you batch cook in a slow cooker and freeze it? Yes, and that is where most of the saving comes from. Cool the food quickly, split it into shallow containers and freeze it within two hours of cooking. Label each one with the date. Defrost in the fridge and reheat once until steaming hot all the way through.
