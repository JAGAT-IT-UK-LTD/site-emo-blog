Cover Letter UK: The Four Paragraph Format That Gets You Read

1. Is a cover letter still worth writing in the UK?

Yes, in most cases. The people who decide who gets an interview still say it matters.

CV Genius surveyed 625 hiring managers in the UK and Ireland for its CV and Cover Letter Trends report, published in July 2024. 85% said cover letters are vital in their hiring decisions.

Yet only 49% said they expect applicants to send one. So many employers value a letter without demanding it.

That gap is the opportunity. When the employer would welcome a letter but does not insist, plenty of applicants skip it. A short, specific one puts you in the smaller pile.

The same survey found 56% believe an applicant who sends a cover letter is more passionate about the job. That is a signal you can send in twenty minutes.

The wider US research points the same way. Resume Genius surveyed 625 US hiring managers and found 83% read most cover letters, even where they are not required. It is a US sample, so treat it as a direction rather than a UK figure.

And the UK market is crowded. The ONS counted 690,000 vacancies in August to October 2026, with 2.4 unemployed people per vacancy in July to September. When roles are scarce, anything that separates you without needing more experience is worth doing. 2. Who actually reads it?

Most confusing cover letter advice comes from lumping two different jobs together.

WhoWhat they doWhat the letter does for them
Recruiter or screenerFirst pass, high volume, often matching against a checklistLittle. They are checking the CV against the essentials
Hiring manager, the final decision makerChooses who to interview and who to hireA lot. It is how they choose between similar shortlisted CVs
Agency recruiterPitches you to their clientGives them the line they will repeat about you

In the CV Genius survey, 61% of hiring managers said cover letters help them understand an applicant's soft skills. That is the job a CV cannot do.

The practical reading: your cover letter will not get you through a keyword screen. That is not its job.

Its job is to work on the person choosing between six shortlisted CVs that all look alike.

49% of UK and Irish hiring managers said they would consider an underqualified applicant who sent a highly persuasive cover letter.

3. What is the UK cover letter format?

One page. Four paragraphs. Roughly 250 to 350 words. Never longer.

This is the cover letter UK structure that works for almost any job application.

Paragraph one: the specific opening. Name the role. Give one concrete reason you are applying to this employer.

Not "I was excited to see your advert". Something that proves you looked: their new site, their product, a change in their sector.

Paragraph two: your strongest matching evidence. One achievement, with a number, that maps onto the main requirement in the advert.

Not a summary of your CV. One thing, in detail.

Paragraph three: the second requirement, and the gap. Cover the other thing they stressed. If you are missing something they asked for, address it here in one sentence.

Silence on an obvious gap reads worse than a plain explanation.

Paragraph four: the close. Short. What you would bring in the first months, and that you would welcome the chance to discuss it.

No pleading. No "I would be grateful for the opportunity".

The cover letter UK layout

Use the same font and header as your CV, so the two look like a set.

Put your name, phone number and email at the top. You do not need your full home address. A town is enough.

Address a named person if you can find one. Use "Dear Hiring Manager" if you cannot. Never "To Whom It May Concern".

Sign off with "Yours sincerely" if you used their name, and "Yours faithfully" if you did not. Many UK employers now accept "Kind regards" in an email.

Paste it into the application box as well as attaching it. Attachments sometimes do not open, and a pasted letter is read without a download.

The National Careers Service gives the same basic layout and is free to use.

4. What does a good cover letter look like?

Tom, 34, from Leeds, applying for a facilities coordinator role at a local manufacturer.

Tom earns £26,500 a year. The new role pays £31,000. That is £4,500 a year more before tax, about £375 a month. Twenty minutes on a letter is a small price for that.

Before, paragraph one: "I am writing to apply for the position advertised on your website. I am a hard working and motivated individual with excellent communication skills and I believe I would be a great asset to your team."

What is wrong with it. It does not name the role. It could be sent to any employer in Britain. And it uses three phrases that appear on almost every application, so it sets him apart from nobody.

After, paragraph one: "I am applying for the Facilities Coordinator role. I saw that you brought your maintenance in house last year, which is the same move I ran at my current site, and the reason the job interests me."

After, paragraph two: "At my current employer I manage planned maintenance across a 40,000 square foot site and a team of four contractors. Moving from reactive callouts to a planned schedule cut unplanned downtime from about 14 hours a month to under 4, and reduced our contractor spend by roughly £11,000 a year."

After, paragraph three, addressing the gap: "Your advert asks for experience with a CAFM system and I have used Excel and a supplier portal rather than a dedicated platform. I learned our supplier portal in a fortnight and would expect the same here, and I am happy to be tested on it."

After, paragraph four: "In my first three months I would aim to have your planned maintenance calendar fully mapped and your top five contractors on agreed response times. I would welcome the chance to talk it through."

Why the second version works. Nothing about Tom changed. The before version asked to be believed.

The after version gave two numbers, proved he had researched the employer, and dealt with his weakest point openly instead of leaving the reader to find it.

5. What if the advert says optional?

Send one. Treat "optional" as "this is how we find out who is serious".

In the US Resume Genius survey, 72% of hiring managers said they expect a cover letter even when the job posting says it is optional. There is no equivalent UK figure, but the UK survey points the same way, with 85% calling letters vital.

The one time to skip it: when there is genuinely no field for it, no email address, and the application is a structured form only.

In that case put your effort into the free text answers. They are read for the same reason, and the same four point method works in a 250 word box.

6. Can you use AI without sounding like everyone else?

Yes, but only as an editor. Recruiters now read many machine written applications, and they notice.

A Robert Walters survey reported in May 2025 found 73% of hiring managers say they can spot when a candidate used AI to create a CV or cover letter. Impersonal language and perfect formatting were the giveaways.

35% said AI use would harm their opinion of the candidate.

The version that works: write your own bullet points first, with your real numbers. Then use a tool to tighten the sentences.

What you must not outsource is the specific detail. That is the only part carrying any weight, and a model that does not know your job cannot invent it.

A simple test before you send: could this letter go to a different company with only the name changed? If yes, it is not doing anything yet.

7. Which mistakes get you rejected?

A weak letter can sink a strong CV. In the Resume Genius survey, 18% of hiring managers said a weak cover letter could make them reject an otherwise strong candidate.

That is why a generic letter is not a free extra. These are the errors to catch.

  • The wrong company name. The most common fatal error, caused by editing a previous letter. Read the first and last lines aloud before sending.
  • Repeating your CV in prose. They have the CV. This is your only chance to say something that is not on it.
  • Making it about what you want. "This role would help me develop" is about you. "You need someone who can do X, here is when I did X" is about them.
  • Length. Over one page and a busy recruiter will not start it.
  • Explaining a gap defensively. One plain sentence. Long justifications draw attention to the thing you want them to move past.
  • No name, no sign off, no contact details. Basic, and it still happens.
  • Personal details nobody asked for. Age, marital status and health have no place in a UK cover letter. GOV.UK sets out what employers should not ask.

The letter sits alongside your CV, so fix both together. Our guide to how to write a CV in the UK that gets past the filter covers the other half.

The honest verdict

Fewer employers insist on a cover letter than once did. What has not changed is how much it counts when the person deciding reads one.

85% of UK and Irish hiring managers call them vital, but only 49% expect one. That gap rewards the applicant who writes a good letter.

But write a real one. A generic letter can do harm, and most hiring managers say they can spot one written by a machine.

If you cannot say something specific about that employer and back it with one number of your own, the letter is not ready to send.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a cover letter be in the UK?

One page, four paragraphs, roughly 250 to 350 words. Shorter is fine if it is specific, and a pasted email version can be even tighter. Longer is not. Hiring managers who read hundreds of applications stop at the first page, so anything past it is unlikely to be read at all.

Is a cover letter still necessary in 2026?

Not always required, but usually worth it. In a CV Genius survey of 625 UK and Irish hiring managers, 85% said cover letters are vital in hiring decisions, while only 49% expected one. Send one whenever there is a field or an email address, and make it specific to the employer.

How do you start a cover letter in the UK?

Use "Dear" and the hiring manager's name if you can find it on LinkedIn or the company site. Otherwise "Dear Hiring Manager" is fine. Then open with the job title and one specific reason you want to work for that employer, not a line about being hard working or passionate.

Should I attach my cover letter or paste it into the email?

Both where possible. Paste it into the body of the email or the application box so it is read without a download. Attach a PDF that matches your CV formatting as well. Name the file with your name and the role, so it is easy to find among other applications later.

Do I need a cover letter for an agency application?

A shorter version, yes. The agency recruiter uses it to pitch you to their client, so give them the one specific line you want repeated. Three short paragraphs are enough: the role you want, your strongest matching result with a number, and when you are available to start.
