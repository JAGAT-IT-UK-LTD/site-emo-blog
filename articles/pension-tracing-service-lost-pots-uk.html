A Quarter of Your Pension for an Afternoon of Letters

The government will tell you where your old pension scheme lives, for nothing. It will not tell you whether you have a pension or what it is worth, and that gap is exactly what tracing firms sell back to you, sometimes for a quarter of the pot. Here is the arithmetic.

The short version

  • The government Pension Tracing Service is free. There is an online service, a phone line on 0800 731 0175 and a postal address.
  • It gives you contact details and nothing else. GOV.UK states plainly that the service will not tell you whether you have a pension, or what it is worth. Everyone should know that before they start.
  • You need the name of an employer or a pension provider to use it. If you cannot remember who you worked for, the free route stalls and that is when paid tracers look attractive.
  • Commercial tracers typically charge a percentage of what they find. On a £9,000 pot, a 10% fee is £900, and a 25% fee is £2,250.
  • The break-even is your own time. Doing it yourself takes perhaps three to five hours of letters and phone calls. A £900 fee values that time at £180 to £300 an hour.

1. What the free service does, and what it does not

The Pension Tracing Service is run by the Department for Work and Pensions. You give it the name of an employer or a pension provider, and it gives you back current contact details for the scheme, which may have changed name, merged or moved several times since you left.

Three things it explicitly does not do, all stated on GOV.UK:

  • It will not tell you whether you have a pension.
  • It will not tell you what it is worth.
  • It cannot help if you cannot name an employer or provider.

That is the whole of the free service. It is a directory lookup, not an investigation. Once you have the contact details, the work of writing to each scheme with your National Insurance number, dates of employment and previous names is yours.

It is available online, by phone on 0800 731 0175 between 10am and 3pm Monday to Friday, or by post to The Pension Service in Wolverhampton. You can also use it on someone else’s behalf with their permission.

The free service finds the letterbox. Writing the letter, and chasing the reply, is the part you are being quoted a percentage for.

2. What commercial tracers actually sell

Paid tracing firms do the step the free service will not: they contact schemes for you, chase them, and come back with a valuation. Some then offer to consolidate the pots, which is where a second layer of charges can appear.

Charging models vary and firms do not always lead with the number. The common shapes are a percentage of the value found, a flat fee per pot traced, or a free trace followed by a fee on consolidation or advice.

Nothing about that is improper, and for someone who genuinely cannot face the admin it may be money well spent. What matters is doing the sum before agreeing, because a percentage of a pension pot is a large number attached to a small amount of work.

The last column is the only honest way to judge a percentage fee. It converts the charge into what the firm is implicitly valuing your afternoon at. At 25% on a modest pot, the answer is several hundred pounds an hour, which almost nobody would accept if it were quoted that way round.

The flat fee row is different in kind, and it is worth asking whether a firm will quote one. A fixed fee does not grow with the size of the pot, and the work does not grow with the size of the pot either, so a flat fee is the pricing that actually matches the service.

And the bottom row matters. Doing nothing is not free. A small, forgotten pot sitting in an old scheme can be quietly reduced by annual charges over decades.

4. Doing it yourself, in order

  1. Write down every employer you have had, in order, with rough dates. Old payslips, P60s, bank statements showing salary, and your National Insurance record on GOV.UK all help.
  2. Find your National Insurance number and any previous names, because schemes will ask for both.
  3. Use the Pension Tracing Service for each employer to get current scheme contact details.
  4. Write to each scheme with your NI number, dates of employment, date of birth and previous addresses, asking whether you have a preserved benefit and what it is worth.
  5. Keep a simple log of who you have written to and when, because replies take weeks and arrive out of order.

There is a further development worth knowing about. Pensions dashboards will let people see their pensions in one place, which changes the balance of this decision for anyone whose pots are already findable. If your case is not urgent, that may be worth waiting for.

Worked example: Gareth in Bangor

Gareth is 54 and has worked for six employers. He knows about his current pension and the one before it. The other four are a blank.

The offer. A firm that found him through a search advert offers to trace everything for 25% of whatever it finds, with nothing to pay if it finds nothing.

What he did instead. He listed the four employers from old P60s, ran each through the free Pension Tracing Service, and wrote four letters on one evening. Three schemes replied within six weeks. Two held small preserved pots, of £3,100 and £5,900.

What the offer would have cost. 25% of £9,000 is £2,250.

What it cost him. An evening listing employers, four letters, and about two hours of chasing. Call it four hours in total, so the firm’s fee would have valued his time at £562 an hour.

The fourth scheme. Never replied, because the employer was dissolved and the scheme wound up decades ago. A paid tracer would have hit exactly the same wall, and charged nothing for that one, which is the part their pricing relies on.

5. Frequently asked

Is the Pension Tracing Service really free? Yes. It is a government service and there is no charge for the online service, the phone line or the postal route.

Will it find my State Pension? No. That is a separate thing entirely, and you check it with a State Pension forecast rather than a trace.

What if I cannot remember the employer? Your National Insurance record on GOV.UK shows the years you paid contributions, which can jog the memory. Old bank statements showing a salary credit name are often the best clue.

Should I consolidate everything into one pot? Not automatically. Older schemes sometimes carry guarantees or protected pension ages that are lost on transfer. Check what you would be giving up before moving anything.

Are paid tracers regulated? Tracing itself is not necessarily a regulated activity, but giving advice on transferring a pension is. Check the FCA register before letting anyone advise on moving money.

6. The honest verdict

Start with the free Pension Tracing Service, because it costs nothing and covers the only part of the job that is genuinely hard to do yourself, which is finding out who a 1998 scheme belongs to today. Then write the letters. It is an evening and some chasing, not a project.

Only consider paying someone if you cannot name your old employers at all, and then ask for a flat fee per pot rather than a percentage. On a £9,000 pot a 25% charge values your afternoon at several hundred pounds an hour, and no service that is essentially four letters is worth that.

Whatever you decide, do something. A forgotten pot is not preserved in amber. It is sitting in a scheme paying charges, and the only person who will ever come looking for it is you.
