The Stairs Wear Out Four Times Faster Than The Landing. Price Them That Way

Ruhul Amin · Published 14 September 2026 · 6 min read · Figures checked September 2026

Carpet is sold by the square metre, fitted on stairs by the step, and worn out on the third tread from the bottom. Those three facts pull in different directions, and they are why buying the same grade for the stairs as for the bedroom is usually the expensive choice.

The short version

  • Fitters charge per stair, not per square metre, typically 12 to 20 pounds a step. A straight 13 step flight is roughly 150 to 250 pounds of labour before any carpet.
  • Carpet itself runs about 6 to 20 pounds per square metre for basic, 20 to 45 for standard and 45 to 60 plus for premium wool.
  • Underlay is not optional on stairs and costs 3 to 10 pounds per square metre. It is the cheapest part of the job and the part that decides how long the carpet lasts.
  • Supplied and fitted, a standard straight staircase commonly lands at 450 to 1,200 pounds, with complex layouts and premium finishes pushing past 1,800.
  • The payback: on cost per stair per year, an 80 per cent wool twist at 40 pounds per square metre beats a budget polypropylene, because it lasts roughly twice as long on the one part of the house that gets every footstep.
  • Turns, winders and bullnose steps are charged extra and are where quotes diverge. Count them before you compare prices.

1. Why fitters price per step

A landing is a rectangle. A fitter rolls it out, trims it, stretches it onto gripper and moves on. A staircase is thirteen separate three dimensional shapes, each needing the carpet cut, folded around a nosing and fixed on two planes, mostly while kneeling on the piece below the one being fitted.

That is why the trade prices stairs at roughly 12 to 20 pounds a step rather than by area. On a straight thirteen step flight the labour alone comes to something like 150 to 250 pounds plus VAT, and that figure barely moves whether the carpet on it cost 10 pounds a metre or 60.

The consequence is the single most useful thing to understand about this job. A large share of the cost does not scale with the quality of what you buy. Choosing a cheaper carpet saves you money on part of the bill and none of the rest, so the saving is smaller in percentage terms than it looks, while the reduction in lifespan is not.

2. Grade matters more on stairs than anywhere else

Every footstep in a two storey house that goes upstairs goes over the same thirteen treads. Nothing else in the house concentrates traffic like that, which is why stair carpet is almost always the first floor covering in a home to look tired, and why it usually fails in a narrow band rather than evenly.

Three things decide how long it lasts, and only one of them is the carpet.

Fibre and construction. A wool rich twist, typically 80 per cent wool and 20 per cent synthetic, recovers from crushing better than a cheap loop or a low density polypropylene. On stairs, crush recovery matters more than stain resistance, because the failure mode is flattening at the nosing rather than a spill.

Underlay. At 3 to 10 pounds a square metre it is the cheapest line on the quote and it does more for lifespan than an extra ten pounds a metre on the carpet. A good underlay takes the impact that would otherwise be taken by the pile.

Fitting. Whether the carpet is fitted with a properly formed fold over each nosing, and whether the gripper is correctly set, decides whether the tread edges wear at the pace of the rest.

If the budget is tight, cut the carpet grade before you cut the underlay. Nobody has ever regretted good underlay.

4. Worked example: the Akinyemi household in Corby

A 1980s three bedroom semi. Straight flight, thirteen steps, one small winder at the top, two children and a dog.

Quote A, budget. Polypropylene at 10 pounds per square metre, 6 square metres, 60 pounds of carpet. Underlay 5 pounds per square metre, 30 pounds. Fitting at 16 pounds a step for 13 steps, 208 pounds, plus 25 pounds for the winder. Grippers and door bar 15 pounds. Total 338 pounds. Expected life on that traffic, about 4 years. 84.50 pounds a year.

Quote B, wool twist. Same fitting, same underlay, carpet at 40 pounds per square metre, so 240 pounds of carpet. Total 518 pounds. Expected life about 12 years. 43.17 pounds a year.

The premium is 180 pounds. Payback: at 41.33 pounds a year of lower running cost, it repays in 4.4 years, which is the same point at which the budget carpet would have needed replacing anyway.

The dog is not a footnote. Claws concentrate wear on the nosing, which is where cheap carpet fails first, so on that household the four year estimate is generous rather than pessimistic.

If the question is really whether to carpet the stairs at all, the same cost per year method applied to hard flooring is in carpet against laminate against LVT on cost per year. Stairs are the one place where the hard flooring answer changes, because a laminate staircase needs nosing profiles that push the fitted cost well above carpet.

Frequently asked

Why is my quote so much higher than the per step figure? Almost always turns, winders, half landings or bullnose bottom steps. Each of those takes as long as two or three straight treads. Count them, and ask the fitter to price straight steps and shaped steps separately so you can compare quotes honestly.

Can I fit stair carpet myself? It is the hardest domestic carpet fitting job there is, the tools are specialised, and a badly formed nosing fold wears through in a year. If you want to save labour, do the bedrooms yourself and pay for the stairs.

Should the stairs match the landing? They do not have to, and matching them locks you into replacing both at once even though the stairs wear four times faster. A deliberate change at the top step is a normal and sensible detail.

How much extra carpet do I need to order? Stairs waste more than rooms, because the pile has to run the same way down every tread. Expect the fitter to order the flight as one continuous length rather than as the sum of the treads. Ask what the ordered length is, not what the calculated area is.

The honest verdict

Buy the wool twist, not the budget synthetic and not the premium. On cost per stair per year it is the cheapest real option, and the extra 180 pounds over the budget quote repays itself in about four and a half years.

Spend properly on underlay whatever else you do. It is the smallest line on the quote and the biggest lever on lifespan.

Count your winders before you compare two prices, because that is where two quotes for the same staircase stop being for the same job.
