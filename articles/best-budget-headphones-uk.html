The best budget headphones in the UK are no longer the ones with the biggest battery number. Almost every set under £100 now claims noise cancelling and a long battery.

What separates them is how well the noise cancelling works, whether they suit your phone, and whether they suit your day. This guide walks through each one in order.

1. What does budget noise cancelling actually block?

Active noise cancelling, or ANC, makes a sound wave that opposes incoming noise. It works best on steady, low frequency sound.

It is much weaker on sudden, high pitched sound. That one fact explains most disappointed buyers.

NoiseBudget ANC under £100
Train and road rumbleStrong. This is what the technology is best at
Aircraft cabin droneStrong
Air conditioning and fan humStrong
Nearby conversationWeak. It reduces it, it does not remove it
A crying child, a barking dog, a keyboardVery weak. Passive isolation from the earcup does more than the ANC

If you are buying for an open plan office full of talking, ANC is not the answer. No amount of extra spend fully fixes it either.

Good passive isolation and something playing quietly does more. That is worth knowing before you spend a penny.

Independent testers also note that cheap ANC sets usually isolate slightly less than premium ones, especially on deep engine rumble. So the best budget noise cancelling headphones get close to premium sets, but not all the way.

Two useful distinctions. Hybrid or adaptive ANC systems generally perform better than basic designs. And the gap between the best and worst ANC at this price is very wide.

Every set at this price says noise cancelling. The gap between the best and worst of them is wider than the gap between the best of them and headphones costing three times as much.

2. How much battery life do you need?

Battery life used to be the reason to spend more. That is over.

Sony lists the WH-CH720N at up to 35 hours with noise cancelling on and up to 50 hours with it off. Anker lists the Soundcore Space Q45 at about 50 hours with ANC on and about 65 hours with it off, measured at 60% volume.

In practice, that is several weeks of commuting between charges. So stop using battery as a tiebreaker unless a model is unusually low.

Treat anything advertising under 25 hours in 2026 as a signal. The rest of the specification may also be dated.

Two things still matter. Quick charge is genuinely useful. Sony says three minutes on the WH-CH720N gives up to an hour of play, and Anker says five minutes on the Space Q45 gives about four hours.

The second is USB C rather than micro USB, so you are not carrying a second cable for one gadget.

3. Which Bluetooth codec matters for your phone?

A codec is how audio is squeezed to travel over Bluetooth. Which one you get depends on your phone as well as your headphones.

That is why the spec sheet alone can mislead you.

  • SBC. The baseline, on everything. Fine, not great.
  • AAC. What iPhones use. If you have an iPhone, this is your ceiling, and LDAC support on the headphones does nothing for you.
  • LDAC. Higher bitrate, Android only. A real improvement if your phone supports it and you stream at a lossless or high quality tier.
  • aptX and aptX Adaptive. Android and some Windows laptops. Lower latency, which matters for video and gaming.

One catch worth knowing. Anker says LDAC on the Space Q45 is switched off while multipoint is in use. Many budget sets make you choose between the two.

The honest note: on compressed streams through budget headphones on a noisy train, most people cannot reliably tell LDAC from AAC. Treat codec support as a tiebreaker, not as a reason to pay more.

4. Over ear or in ear: which should you buy?

This is the decision that actually decides whether you enjoy them. It is about your life rather than about audio.

Over earIn ear
Passive isolationBetter, the cup physically blocks soundDepends entirely on the tip seal
BatteryUsually 30 hours or more in the headphonesSeveral hours per bud, plus case charges
Long sessionsMore comfortable for most peopleCan ache after an hour or two
PortabilityBulky, needs bag spacePocketable
Glasses and warm weatherPressure on the arms, hot earsNo issue
Losing themDifficultEasy, and a single bud is costly to replace

The most underrated thing about in ear buds: the ear tips decide almost everything. A poor seal loses bass and ruins the noise cancelling.

Try every tip size in the box. If none seal properly, third party foam tips are a cheap fix that often rescues a set you were about to return.

5. Which four specs are worth checking?

  1. Multipoint pairing. Connected to your laptop and your phone at once, switching when a call comes in. Once you have used it you will not go back, and not every model at this price has it.
  2. A transparency or ambient mode. Lets outside sound in without taking the headphones off. Essential near roads and at shop counters.
  3. Physical buttons rather than touch controls. Touch panels misfire with gloves, in rain, and when you adjust the fit. It is a genuine daily annoyance.
  4. Replaceable ear pads on over ear models. Pads perish long before the electronics. If replacements are sold, the headphones last years longer.

Notice what is not on that list. Driver size, frequency response range and the number of microphones.

Those numbers are marketing at this price. They do not predict how anything sounds.

6. A worked choice for one household

The Hughes household in Bristol: three people, a £70 budget each, £210 in total.

Amir commutes 50 minutes each way by train, Android phone. That is 100 minutes a day, or 8 hours 20 minutes over a five day week. A set rated at 35 hours with ANC on covers four full weeks of that commute on one charge.

So battery does not decide his choice. Over ear, because train rumble is what budget ANC handles best. Multipoint matters because he takes work calls on a laptop. Priority: ANC quality, then multipoint.

Sophie runs and uses the gym, iPhone. In ear, and over ear would be the wrong buy at any price. She needs a proper seal, an IP water resistance rating and a secure fit.

LDAC is irrelevant on her iPhone, so she should not pay extra for it. Priority: fit and IP rating, then transparency mode for road safety.

Dan works from home with two young children. ANC will not solve his problem, because children are not steady low frequency noise.

Over ear with good passive isolation gets him further than any ANC claim. Priority: comfort for long wear, and realistic expectations.

The sums: stepping each of them up to a £200 pair would cost the household £600 instead of £210. That is £390 more, mostly for better ANC that only Amir would really notice.

The point: there is no single best set under £100. There is a best set for the noise you are trying to escape, and these three answers have almost nothing in common.

8. What are the traps in budget headphone marketing?

  • Battery figures measured with ANC off. Check whether the headline number is with noise cancelling on. On the two models above, the gap is 15 hours.
  • "Hi-Res Audio" badges on Bluetooth headphones. Largely meaningless over a wireless connection at this price.
  • Permanent sales. If it has been reduced from the same "was" price every week since spring, the sale price is simply the price.
  • Reviews of a different model. Marketplace listings sometimes carry reviews from an earlier version. Our guide to spotting fake Amazon reviews shows what to check.
  • No replaceable pads. Sealed pads mean the headphones are disposable once the pads perish.
  • Buying for a noise ANC cannot fix. The most common disappointment. It is a mismatch of expectations rather than a bad product.

The honest verdict

Under £100 is now a genuinely good place to buy headphones. Battery is solved, build quality is decent, and ANC is real if not magic.

The step up to £200 buys better ANC and better microphones. For most people it is not necessary.

Choose in this order. Over ear or in ear, based on how you will use them. Then ANC quality from independent testing rather than the box. Then multipoint if you take calls on a laptop.

And be honest about the noise you are escaping. If it is a train, budget ANC will delight you. If it is your colleagues talking, no set at any price will.

Frequently asked questions

Are budget noise cancelling headphones worth it?

Yes, for steady noise such as trains, buses, planes and fans. Budget ANC handles that well and lets you listen at a lower volume. It is not worth it if your main problem is voices or children, because ANC barely touches sudden, high pitched sound. Good passive isolation helps more there.

Is ANC bad for your ears?

No. It works by adding an opposing sound wave, not by pressure. Some people feel a slight sense of pressure, which is a known effect and not harmful. The real hearing risk is volume, and noise cancelling usually lets you listen more quietly, which is better for your ears over time.

Can I use budget headphones for work calls?

Yes, but check the microphone, because it is the weakest part of most budget headphones. Multipoint pairing matters most here, so the headphones stay linked to your laptop and phone at once. On some models, turning on multipoint switches off LDAC, so check which you value more.

How long should budget headphones last?

Several years is a reasonable hope with care. The battery and the ear pads usually wear out first, long before the electronics. Replaceable pads make a real difference, so check that they are sold before you buy. Keeping the headphones in their case also protects the hinges, which often break first.

When is the best time to buy headphones in the UK?

Big sales such as Black Friday on 27 November 2026 bring real discounts on some models. Check the price history first, because some "was" prices are inflated. Buying online also gives you 14 days to cancel after delivery, so you can test the noise cancelling on your real commute.
