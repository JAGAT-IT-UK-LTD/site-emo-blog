September is when the big seasonal employers open their books. Royal Mail's careers site is taking registrations for Christmas 2026 and last year it announced 20,000 roles on 29 September. If the job is on top of your existing one, the payslip will surprise you: HMRC taxes a second job at 20% from the first pound. The National Insurance rules, oddly, work in your favour. Here is the arithmetic before you sign up.

1. Who is hiring and what they paid last year

Royal Mail's Christmas careers page is live and says it plainly: "Want to be part of Christmas 2026? Register your interest today and we'll let you know when opportunities open in your area." The roles listed are Seasonal Mail Sorter, Parcel Delivery Driver, LGV and MGV Driver, and Customer Service Assistant, and recruitment is run through Angard Staffing, its in-house agency. No pay rates are printed on the page. Last year the formal announcement came on 29 September 2025: 20,000 roles, with 12,000 sorting positions in England, 2,000 in Scotland, 600 in Wales and 400 in Northern Ireland, on contracts from late October to early January.

Amazon's 2025 festive recruitment, published on About Amazon UK, was for more than 15,000 seasonal roles with pay starting at a minimum of £14.30 an hour, rising to £15.30 depending on location. Those are 2025 figures. At the time of writing Amazon had not published its 2026 seasonal announcement, and we have not guessed it.

Supermarkets, parcel carriers and high street retailers hire through October. Whatever they offer, the legal floor for anyone aged 21 or over is the National Living Wage of £12.71 an hour, in force since 1 April 2026. Most seasonal warehouse and sorting roles pay above it; most seasonal retail roles pay at or close to it.

2. The BR tax code: 20% from the first pound

This is the part that catches people. GOV.UK's tax code guide defines code BR as: all your income from this job or pension is taxed at the basic rate, usually used if you have more than one job or pension.

The logic is simple. Your personal allowance of £12,570 is attached to your main job through code 1257L. HMRC cannot give it to you twice, so the second job gets no allowance at all. Every pound you earn there is taxed at 20% from the first one, before National Insurance.

Two consequences. First, your seasonal payslip will show a much higher proportion of tax than your main one, and that is correct, not an error. Second, if your main job does not use the whole allowance, for example because you work part time and earn £9,000, you are entitled to have the spare £3,570 of allowance moved to the second job. HMRC does not do that automatically. You ring or use the personal tax account to ask for the allowance to be split. If you do not, you overpay and wait for a P800 the following summer; we explain how that letter works in the guide linked below.

If the Christmas job is your only job, none of this applies. You give the employer a starter checklist saying it is your only employment, you get code 1257L, and you pay no tax until your earnings for the year pass £12,570.

3. The National Insurance rule that helps

National Insurance works the opposite way to income tax. It is calculated per employer, per pay period, with no reference to your other jobs. The employee threshold is £242 a week, and above that the rate is 8%.

So a second job paying £230 a week attracts no employee National Insurance at all, even though your main job is paying plenty. A second job paying £300 a week attracts 8% on the £58 above the threshold, which is £4.64. Compare that with the £60 of income tax on the same £300 and you can see which deduction dominates.

This is one of the few places in the tax system where having two jobs is treated more generously than having one job paying the same total, and it is entirely legal. It is also why people on modest seasonal hours often find the NI line on the payslip is a few pence.

4. A worked example: Fatima in Oldham

Fatima works full time in Oldham on £24,000 a year and takes a 20 hour a week seasonal sorting job for eight weeks from late October, paid at the National Living Wage of £12.71.

Over eight weeks she grosses £2,033.60 and keeps £1,619.04. The 20% on every pound is the whole story: a fifth of the gross goes in tax, and the NI is under a pound a week. If she had been paid £14.30, Amazon's 2025 starting rate, the same hours gross £286 a week, tax is £57.20, NI is £3.52, and she keeps £225.28.

Had Fatima's main job been part time on £9,000, her unused allowance of £3,570 could have been moved to the seasonal job, wiping out the tax on it entirely. The only way that happens is if she asks HMRC.

5. Rights, benefits and the things to check before you accept

Agency status. Royal Mail seasonal roles come through Angard Staffing, so you are an agency worker. Under the Agency Workers Regulations you get the same pay and basic conditions as a directly employed colleague doing the same job after 12 weeks in the role, and access to facilities and vacancies from day one. Most Christmas contracts are shorter than 12 weeks, so plan on the agency rate throughout.

Holiday pay. All workers accrue 5.6 weeks of paid holiday a year pro rata from the first day, including agency temps. On a short contract it is usually paid as a top-up or at the end. Check the payslip line.

Universal Credit. Seasonal earnings count in the assessment period they are paid in. Above any work allowance, Universal Credit falls by 55p for every £1 of net earnings. Eight weeks of extra pay can mean two assessment periods with a much lower award and one with a higher one afterwards. It still leaves you better off, but budget for the timing.

Student loans. If you are repaying, deductions are made per employer when that job's pay crosses the weekly or monthly threshold for your plan. On 20 hours at the minimum wage it usually will not.

The starter checklist. Tick the right statement. Saying it is your only job when it is not gives you an allowance twice and a tax bill the following year.

6. The honest verdict

A second job at 20 hours on the minimum wage clears about £200 a week after tax. That is real money over eight weeks, and the BR code is why it is not £250. Go in knowing that and the payslip will not shock you.

If your main job is part time and does not use your full allowance, ask HMRC to split it. That is the one action that changes the number.

Register with Royal Mail now and watch for Amazon's 2026 announcement. There is nothing to buy here, and no agency should charge you a fee to find you work; that is illegal.

Frequently asked

Why is my Christmas job taxed at 20% on everything? Because it is your second job and is on tax code BR. Your personal allowance is already used by your main job, so the second one gets none. GOV.UK describes BR as all income from that job taxed at the basic rate.

Do I pay National Insurance on a second job? Only on earnings above £242 a week from that job, at 8%. National Insurance is calculated per employer, so a second job under the threshold attracts none regardless of your main job.

What did Royal Mail and Amazon pay last Christmas? Royal Mail does not publish seasonal rates on its careers page. Amazon's 2025 announcement stated £14.30 to £15.30 an hour for seasonal operations roles. The 2026 figures had not been published when we checked.

Do agency temps get holiday pay? Yes. All workers accrue 5.6 weeks a year pro rata from day one. After 12 weeks in the same role, agency workers are also entitled to the same pay and basic conditions as directly employed staff.
