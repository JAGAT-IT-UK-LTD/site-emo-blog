Every list of side hustles includes paid surveys, and almost none of them tells you the hourly rate. That is because the rate is the whole story. Only one major platform publishes a floor, and it is below the minimum wage by design. Here is what the sums look like, what a realistic month earns, how it is taxed, and the narrow set of people for whom it still makes sense.

1. The one published floor, and how it is measured

Prolific is not a survey site in the usual sense. It recruits members of the public for academic and commercial research studies, and it is the only large platform that publishes a minimum rate. Researchers must pay at least £6 an hour, which Prolific calls its absolute minimum, and it recommends at least £9 an hour. Both figures are on its researcher help pages and its participant help pages, which is where the numbers in this article come from.

The detail that matters is how the rate is measured. Prolific works out the effective hourly rate from the median time participants actually took to complete the study, not from the time the researcher estimated. A study advertised as ten minutes for £1.50, which is £9 an hour, becomes underpaying if the median participant takes fifteen. Prolific says it contacts researchers to top up underpaying studies, and participants can report them. No other mainstream site does this, and it is the reason Prolific is the exception in every honest comparison.

The other platforms, the ones with points and prize draws, do not publish an hourly figure. That is a choice. A points scheme lets a site advertise 500 points for a survey without ever saying that 500 points is 50p or that the survey takes twenty minutes. If a platform will not tell you the rate, assume it is below £6, because the one platform that does tell you sets its floor there.

2. The wage test every side hustle should pass

The National Living Wage for workers aged 21 and over is £12.71 an hour from 1 April 2026, with £10.85 for 18 to 20 year olds and £8 for under-18s and apprentices. Those are the rates an employer must pay. Survey work is not employment, so nothing obliges a platform to match them, and none does.

That gives a simple test. If an hour of your time could be sold to an employer for £12.71, then an hour spent on surveys at £6 to £9 is an hour sold at a discount of a quarter to a half. The test is not whether surveys pay something. It is whether the hour had a better buyer. A student with a shift available at £12.71 should take the shift. A parent at home during a nap, a commuter on a train, someone convalescing, someone whose evening would otherwise be spent on a phone anyway: these are hours that have no other buyer, and £9 for one of them is £9 more than nothing.

The test also explains why survey income never scales. Doubling your hours on a job doubles your pay. Doubling your hours on Prolific does not, because the studies you qualify for are limited by demographics and by how many researchers are recruiting that week. The platform's own participant guidance says as much: availability, not rate, is the constraint.

3. What a realistic month looks like

Take Prolific as the best case, because it is. Prolific pays in pounds, screens you into studies using a profile you fill in once, and cashes out to PayPal once your approved balance reaches £6, normally within 24 hours of the request. There is a cool-off period of 72 hours between cash-outs until you have made four, and then once a day.

Suppose you can find three hours of studies a week, which on Prolific is optimistic in a quiet month and easy in a busy one, and they average the recommended £9. Three hours times £9 is £27 a week, or about £117 a month on our arithmetic over a 4.33 week month. At the £6 floor the same hours are £78 a month. A year of it is £936 to £1,404.

Now the points sites. Because they do not publish rates we will not invent one, but two structural facts hold on nearly all of them. Screening questions, the first few minutes that decide whether you fit the survey's quota, are unpaid if you are rejected, and rejection is common. And points convert to vouchers, not cash, at a rate set by the site and usually with a minimum balance that takes weeks to reach. Both facts push the effective hourly rate below the headline, and neither is mentioned in the sign-up page.

4. A worked example in Middlesbrough

Leanne is 34, lives in Middlesbrough, and is at home with a two year old. She has roughly an hour a day when the child sleeps, which she currently spends on her phone. She signs up to Prolific and one points site.

In her first month she completes 14 Prolific studies, totalling 3 hours 50 minutes, for £36.40. That is £9.50 an hour on our arithmetic, a little above the recommended rate, which is typical for someone in a demographic researchers want. On the points site she spends about four hours, is screened out of eleven surveys, completes six, and ends the month with points worth £4.50 in vouchers that she cannot redeem until she has £10 of them. Her effective rate there is £1.13 an hour, which is why she deletes the app in month two.

Over a year on Prolific alone, at the same pace, Leanne earns about £437. It is real money and it has cost her nothing she was using. It is also less than she would earn in five weeks of a part-time job at the National Living Wage, and if a job with hours that fit becomes available, the arithmetic says take it. The survey money was worth having precisely because those hours had no other buyer.

One more figure from her year: her best month was £61 and her worst was £14. The variation was entirely about how many studies she was eligible for, not how hard she tried. Anyone budgeting survey income as a fixed line has misunderstood it.

5. How it is taxed

Money from surveys and research studies is trading income, the same as any other self-employed earnings. The trading allowance covers the first £1,000 of gross trading income in a tax year: if your total from all such sources is £1,000 or less, you do not have to tell HMRC or file a return, though you must keep records. Above £1,000 you register for Self Assessment and either deduct the £1,000 allowance or your actual expenses, whichever is better. We set out the details, including the announced but not yet legislated £3,000 reporting threshold, in our side hustle tax guide.

Leanne's £437 is inside the allowance, so she owes nothing and files nothing. Someone doing three hours a day across several platforms could cross £1,000, at which point the registration deadline is 5 October after the end of the tax year in question. Vouchers count as income at their face value; points do not count until converted.

Survey income does not affect Universal Credit until it is paid, and then it counts as earnings in the assessment period it lands in. For anyone on UC the taper is 55p in the pound above the work allowance, which is worth knowing before a £60 month arrives as a lump.

6. The honest verdict

Paid surveys pay below the minimum wage on purpose, and only Prolific tells you by how much: a £6 floor, a £9 recommendation, measured on real completion times.

Worth doing on Prolific with hours that have no other buyer: a nap, a commute, a wait. A realistic three hours a week is about £117 a month, and it will not scale because supply, not effort, is the limit.

Not worth doing with hours you could sell for £12.71, and not worth doing on any site that will not tell you the hourly rate. Keep it under the £1,000 trading allowance and it is tax free and paperwork free.

Frequently asked

How much does Prolific pay per hour? Researchers must pay at least £6 an hour and Prolific recommends at least £9. The rate is calculated on the median time participants actually take, and underpaying studies can be reported.

Do I pay tax on survey income? It is trading income. Up to £1,000 a year of gross trading income is covered by the trading allowance and does not need to be reported. Above that you register for Self Assessment.

Why do survey sites pay in points? Points let a site advertise a reward without stating an hourly rate or a cash value. Convert the points to pounds and divide by the time taken, including unpaid screen-outs, before valuing any site.

Can I make a living from paid surveys? No. Even the best-paying platform's recommended rate is below the National Living Wage, and the number of studies you qualify for is limited. Treat it as small, irregular extra money.
