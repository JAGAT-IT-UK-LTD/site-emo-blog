The Trading Allowance Triples To £3,000 From April 2027: What It Means For Your Side Hustle

Ruhul Amin · Published 15 November 2026 · 10 min read · Figures checked November 2026

From 6 April 2027, the first £3,000 a year of side hustle income is tax free and no longer needs reporting to HMRC, up from £1,000. Until then the old £1,000 rule still applies, so everything you sell or earn before 5 April 2027 is judged by it.

The short version

  • The trading allowance rises from £1,000 to £3,000 on 6 April 2027. It covers the 2027 to 2028 tax year onwards. HMRC published the measure on 28 October 2026, and it goes into the Finance Bill 2026 to 2027.
  • The Self Assessment reporting threshold for trading income rises to £3,000 at the same time. Gross side income of £3,000 or less a year will be tax free and need not be reported.
  • Nothing changes before then. Income you earn up to 5 April 2027 is still judged against £1,000.
  • It is still gross income, and still allowance or expenses, not both. Above £3,000 you deduct either £3,000 or your real costs, whichever is bigger.
  • The property allowance is not part of this. No Budget 2026 document changes it, so it stays at £1,000.
  • HMRC expects 400,000 people to pay less tax, by £380 on average, and 100,000 to stop reporting this income altogether.

1. What exactly changed at the Budget?

The Budget on 28 October 2026 tripled the trading allowance. HMRC's policy paper, published the same day, says the allowance goes from £1,000 to £3,000.

It takes effect from 6 April 2027, for the 2027 to 2028 tax year onwards. The law is made by the Finance Bill 2026 to 2027, which amends the Income Tax (Trading and Other Income) Act 2005.

This is bigger than the change many people were expecting. At the tax update in spring 2025, the government had only announced a rise in the Self Assessment reporting threshold to £3,000, by the end of this Parliament. Earlier guides on this site, including our side hustle tax rules guide, described that older plan. The Budget went further. It raises the tax free amount and the reporting line together, from the same date.

The allowance covers trading income and some miscellaneous income. GOV.UK lists three kinds:

  • self employment, such as selling things you make or buy to resell, freelancing, or delivery work
  • casual services, such as babysitting or gardening
  • hiring out personal equipment, such as power tools

Selling your own unwanted things is a different matter. HMRC has said plainly that if you are not trading and only sell unwanted items now and then, there is no tax due. The allowance only matters once you are trading.

What did not change? The property allowance. The overview of tax legislation for Budget 2026 lists the trading allowance increase and nothing on the property allowance. GOV.UK still shows it at £1,000, so income from renting out a driveway or a parking space keeps the old limit. Rent a Room is a separate scheme again.

HMRC's own estimate of the effect is useful. Up to 1.4 million people could benefit. About 400,000 are expected to see their tax bill fall, by £380 on average. About 100,000 will no longer need to report the income at all.

2. Do I still need to register for Self Assessment?

The test is your gross income, meaning the money coming in before any costs or fees come off. Profit does not decide it. It is also one allowance per person, across every trade you run, not one per activity.

Here is how the rule moves, using GOV.UK's current guidance and the Budget 2026 change:

Your gross trading income in the yearUp to 5 April 2027 (2026 to 2027)From 6 April 2027 (2027 to 2028)
£1,000 or lessTax free, usually no need to tell HMRCTax free, usually no need to tell HMRC
£1,001 to £3,000Register for Self Assessment, deduct £1,000 or your expensesTax free, usually no need to tell HMRC
Over £3,000Register, deduct £1,000 or your expensesRegister, deduct £3,000 or your expenses

So the people who stop needing to register are those whose side income sits between £1,000 and £3,000 a year. That is a lot of Etsy shops, weekend dog walkers and occasional freelancers.

There are exceptions, and they are the same as now. You cannot use the allowance at all in a year where you have trading income from:

  • a company you, or someone connected to you, owns or controls
  • a partnership where you or someone connected to you is a partner
  • your employer, or your spouse's or civil partner's employer

You also still need to register, even under the limit, if you want to claim a loss, pay voluntary Class 2 National Insurance, use Tax-Free Childcare based on your self employment, or claim Maternity Allowance based on it. GOV.UK lists all four.

Keep records either way. GOV.UK says you must keep a record of your income even when the allowance covers all of it. A spreadsheet of sales, platform statements and bank statements are enough.

One timing trap. Income from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027 is still the old year. If it tops £1,000, you must register by 5 October 2027 and file that return as normal. If you are already late for an earlier year, our guide to registering for Self Assessment covers what to do.

3. Should I claim the allowance or my expenses?

Once your gross income goes over the allowance, you choose one of two methods. GOV.UK is clear that you cannot do both.

  • Use the allowance. Take £3,000 off your gross income from April 2027, and pay tax on the rest.
  • Use your expenses. Take off your real allowable costs, and pay tax on the profit.

The rule of thumb is simple. If your real costs are under £3,000, the allowance wins from April 2027. If they are over £3,000, claim expenses. The table below shows three cases at the 20% basic rate. Every figure is our own arithmetic.

Gross incomeReal expensesTaxable with allowanceTaxable with expensesBetter choice and tax at 20%
£2,500£400Nil, fully covered£2,100Allowance, £0
£4,000£800£1,000£3,200Allowance, £200
£6,000£3,500£3,000£2,500Expenses, £500

Low cost services gain the most. A tutor, a proofreader or a virtual assistant often has very few costs. For them the new allowance takes £3,000 off the bill with no receipts to add up. Our guides to virtual assistant work and proofreading show what those jobs earn.

High cost trades gain less. A reseller who buys stock, or a driver with big fuel bills, may already claim more than £3,000 in costs. For them the allowance changes little, apart from the reporting line.

Two side effects are worth knowing. GOV.UK says that using the allowance lowers your income for the High Income Child Benefit Charge, student loan repayments and tax credits. It also says Universal Credit is not affected by the allowances, so a claimant still reports earnings in the usual way. 4. Worked example: the Adeyemis in Coventry

Grace and Femi, two side hustles, one household

Grace works part time on a salary of £22,000. She sells handmade earrings online. In a year her gross sales are £2,600, and her materials and fees come to £700.

Femi earns £31,000 in a warehouse and does food delivery in the evenings. His gross delivery income is £5,500, and his records show £2,400 of costs for fuel, phone and insurance.

Both salaries already use their £12,570 Personal Allowance, so side income is taxed at 20%. All figures below are our own arithmetic.

Under today's rules (2026 to 2027). Grace is over £1,000, so she must register. The allowance leaves £1,600 taxable, and her expenses would leave £1,900. She takes the allowance and pays £320.

Femi compares £4,500 taxable with the allowance against £3,100 with his expenses. He claims expenses and pays £620.

Under the new rules (2027 to 2028), same figures. Grace's £2,600 is under £3,000. She pays nothing and no longer needs to tell HMRC about it. That saves her £320 and a tax return.

Femi is still over £3,000, so he still files. But the allowance now leaves £2,500 taxable, against £3,100 with expenses. He switches to the allowance and pays £500, a saving of £120.

Together the household keeps £440 a year more, and one of them stops filing. Neither owes Class 4 National Insurance, because GOV.UK only charges it on profits above £12,570.

The lesson from Femi is that the best method can flip. A method that was right under the £1,000 rule may be wrong under the £3,000 rule. Check both every year, which takes five minutes with your totals. Our food delivery earnings guide shows the typical costs a rider or driver carries.

5. Will Vinted, eBay or Deliveroo still tell HMRC about me?

Yes. The digital platform reporting rules are separate from the allowance, and the Budget did not touch them.

Since 1 January 2024, platforms collect sellers' details and income and send them to HMRC each January for the year before. GOV.UK says a seller of goods is left out only if they make fewer than 30 sales and receive less than €2,000, about £1,700, in the calendar year.

Services are different. That exception covers goods, so a delivery rider or a freelancer on an app can be reported whatever they earn.

Being reported does not mean you owe tax. GOV.UK says the report does not automatically mean that, and the platform must send you a copy. Three things follow for the new allowance:

  • A report can show more than £1,700 while your trading income is still under £3,000. Both can be true, and you may owe nothing.
  • Platform reports run on calendar years, while the allowance runs on tax years from 6 April. Do not copy the platform total straight across.
  • The platform figure is after its fees. The allowance test uses gross income, so add the fees back when you check yourself against £3,000.

If HMRC writes to you about platform income, our guide to HMRC nudge letters explains what the letter does and does not mean.

6. Does this change Making Tax Digital?

No. Making Tax Digital for Income Tax has its own thresholds, and the Budget left them alone. GOV.UK sets them by qualifying income:

7. The honest verdict

This is a real gain for small side hustles, not a headline trick. From April 2027, anyone with gross side income of £3,000 or less a year pays nothing on it and can stop filing for it. Above that, most people with low costs save up to £400 a year at the basic rate, our own arithmetic on the extra £2,000 of allowance.

Do not act early. Income before 6 April 2027 still counts against £1,000, and returns for that year are due as normal. Do not try to shift sales from March into April either. The saving is small, and your records have to show when each sale really happened.

If your side income is heading past £3,000, keep proper records of costs anyway. You will need them to know which method wins each year. This guide is general information, not tax advice.

Frequently asked

When does the £3,000 trading allowance start? It starts on 6 April 2027 and applies to the 2027 to 2028 tax year onwards. HMRC published the measure on 28 October 2026, and it is being made law in the Finance Bill 2026 to 2027. Until 5 April 2027 the allowance stays at £1,000, so this year's income is judged by the old limit.

Is the £3,000 trading allowance based on profit or turnover? It is based on gross income, meaning everything coming in before costs or platform fees. If your gross income is £3,000 or less from April 2027, it is fully covered. Above that, you deduct either the £3,000 allowance or your actual expenses, but you cannot deduct both in the same year.

Has the property allowance gone up to £3,000 too? No. The Budget 2026 overview of tax legislation only raises the trading allowance. GOV.UK still shows the property allowance at £1,000 a year, and Rent a Room is a separate scheme. Income from letting land or property, such as a parking space, keeps the £1,000 limit.

Do I get £3,000 for each side hustle? No. GOV.UK says the allowance applies to income from one or more trades, so it is one allowance per person. Selling crafts, tutoring and delivery work all add together. A couple each get their own allowance, because it belongs to the individual rather than to the household.
