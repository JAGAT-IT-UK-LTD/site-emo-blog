Since October 2024 UK private sellers have paid nothing to sell on eBay. That has pulled thousands of side hustlers onto the platform, and some of them are not private sellers at all. eBay's business fee schedule was updated on 4 August 2026, and the difference between the two regimes on a single sale is now large enough to decide whether a resale side hustle is viable. Here is what each side pays, where the line sits, and why HMRC draws it in the same place.

The short version

  • UK private sellers pay no final value fee and no regulatory fee, get 300 free listings a month and pay 35p per listing beyond that. Motors are excluded. Source: eBay, fees for private sellers, last updated 1 October 2024.

  • Plus a per-order fee of 30p (orders up to £10) or 40p (over £10), plus a 0.35% regulatory operating fee.
  • Our arithmetic: a £60 clothing sale costs a business seller £7.75 on the item alone, £8.18 once £3.50 postage is included, and a private seller nothing.

1. What private sellers pay: nothing, and why

eBay's fees page for private sellers, last updated 1 October 2024, is unusually clear. It is free for UK-based private sellers to sell on eBay, excluding vehicles. You pay no final value fees and no regulatory operating fees when items sell. You get 300 free listings a month and pay 35p for each listing beyond that. Optional upgrades and international delivery carry fees; ordinary domestic selling does not.

The money did not vanish. It moved to the buyer. On listings from UK private sellers eBay charges the buyer a Buyer Protection fee, which its help page defines as a flat 10p per item plus 7% of the item price up to £20, 4% of any portion from £20 to £300, and 2% of any portion from £300 to £4,000. Nothing is charged above £4,000. The fee is included in the price the buyer sees, so a private seller listing an item at £60 will find buyers are shown roughly £63.10.

That matters for private sellers in one way: your item competes on the displayed price, so a business seller's £60 item shows at £60 and yours shows at £63.10. Your buyer is paying the fee that used to be yours. The saving is real but it is not invisible to the market.

2. What business sellers pay after 4 August 2026

The business fee page carries a "last updated 4 August 2026" stamp and three charges apply when an item sells.

Two details are easy to miss. The percentage is charged on the postage too, not just the item, so a £3.50 postage charge is fee bearing. And the per-order fee applies once per checkout, so a buyer taking three items in one order triggers it once. eBay's page notes a higher rate, up to 12.9% plus the per-order fee, for sellers rated "very high" for unresolved cases in a category.

Business sellers do not pay third party payment processing on top; eBay's single fee covers it. And the Buyer Protection fee is not charged to buyers on business listings, so the displayed price is the real price.

3. Where the line actually sits

eBay's rule is that you must register as a business if you sell items you have bought to resell, items you made yourself, or sell regularly in a way that constitutes trading. Clearing out your own wardrobe, the loft, or a relative's house is private selling however much it raises. Buying stock at a car boot to list on Sunday night is business selling however small it is.

eBay does not publish its detection thresholds and we will not guess them. What it says publicly is that accounts identified as trading are asked to upgrade, and the fee schedule changes from that point. There is no fine and nothing is backdated by eBay, but the free regime ends.

The honest framing for anyone running a resale side hustle: you are already a business seller under eBay's definition, and the choice is whether to register before eBay asks or after.

On the item alone, ignoring postage, the business fee is 11.9% of £60 plus 40p plus 0.35% of £60, which is £7.75. That is the headline figure. Including postage it is £8.18. Either way, roughly an eighth of the sale price goes to eBay as a business seller and nothing does as a private one. Across 40 similar sales a month, the difference is over £300.

If Connor sold the jacket because he no longer wears it, he is a private seller and the left column is correct. If he bought it for £25 at a charity shop to resell, he is trading, the right column applies, and his profit is £60 less £25 less £8.18 less the £3.50 he spends on postage, about £27 before tax.

5. The HMRC side of the same line

HMRC uses the same distinction and it does not care what eBay calls you. Selling your own possessions is not taxable income. Buying or making things to sell is trading, and trading income above the £1,000 trading allowance in a tax year must be declared through Self Assessment.

Since 1 January 2024 digital platforms have been required to collect seller information and report it to HMRC each January for sellers who make 30 or more sales or receive around £1,700 (EUR 2,000) in a year. The report covers the previous calendar year. It does not create a tax liability where none exists; a clear-out is still not taxable. But it means the question "are you trading" is now asked with data, and a resale side hustle running as a private account is visible on both sides.

We set out the trading allowance and the registration deadlines in the side hustle tax guide linked below. The short version: if you are a business seller on eBay's definition, you are very probably a trader on HMRC's, and the 5 October registration deadline for the 2025 to 2026 tax year is a month away.

6. The honest verdict

Selling your own things? eBay is genuinely free and you should use it. Just know the buyer is paying roughly 5 to 7% on top, so price against business sellers accordingly. There is nothing to buy here.

Buying to resell? You are a business seller already. Budget about 12.5% of each sale for eBay in the clothing category, less in electronics and books, and register with HMRC if profit will pass £1,000.

Do not rely on staying under eBay's radar. Its threshold is unpublished, HMRC receives the platform's data every January, and the fee difference is exactly the margin a small reseller runs on.

Frequently asked

Is it really free to sell on eBay as a private seller? Yes, for UK-based private sellers selling anything except vehicles, according to eBay's fees page last updated 1 October 2024. No final value fee, no regulatory fee, 300 free listings a month and 35p per extra listing.

What do business sellers pay? A category final value fee (for example 11.9% on clothing, 10.9% on books, 9.9% on mobile phones) on the total including postage, plus 30p or 40p per order, plus a 0.35% regulatory operating fee, per eBay's business fees page updated 4 August 2026.

How does eBay decide I am a business? Its rule is about what you sell: items bought to resell, items you made, or regular trading. It does not publish detection thresholds. If identified as trading you are asked to register as a business and business fees apply from then.

Does the buyer pay a fee on private listings? Yes. A Buyer Protection fee of 10p plus 7% up to £20, 4% from £20 to £300 and 2% from £300 to £4,000, included in the displayed price. It is not charged on business seller listings.
