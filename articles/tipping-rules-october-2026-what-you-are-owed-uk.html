Your employer has three weeks to argue about your tips, and so do you

A revised statutory code on tipping is out for consultation until the end of September, adding a duty to consult workers before a tips policy is set. Meanwhile the rights that arrived in 2024 are still barely used.

The short version

  • Since 1 October 2024 your employer must pass on all qualifying tips, with no deduction, under the Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023.
  • The payment deadline is fixed: the end of the month following the month the customer paid. GOV.UK's example is a tip left on 23 June, distributed by 31 July.
  • You can demand the numbers. One written request per three month period, covering up to three years.
  • The new bit: a revised code published 19 August 2026 adds a duty to consult workers before setting a tipping policy. Open for comment until 11:59pm on 29 September 2026, and the government timeline dated 25 September 2026 puts the change in force by the end of 2026.
  • The tribunal award limit rose to £5,366 on 6 April 2026, under SI 2026/310.

1. What the law already gives you

The Act and its statutory code took effect on Tuesday 1 October 2024 across England, Scotland and Wales. Two years on, the parts that would put money in a pocket are the least used.

Everything qualifying goes to staff. The code is explicit that the payment method makes no difference. What matters is whether the tip is received by the employer or subject to the employer's control.

There is a hard payment deadline. Under section 27G of the Employment Rights Act 1996, tips must be distributed at the latest by the end of the month following the month the customer paid them. GOV.UK spells it out: a tip left on 23 June must be distributed by 31 July.

That rule is a deadline on the calendar month, not a fixed number of days, and the difference is larger than it looks. A tip left on the 1st of a month can lawfully be held almost twice as long as one left on the 31st.

So a tip from early in the month is slow by design and the employer is not in breach. The date to put in a grievance is the last day of the following month, not four weeks after the shift. Getting that wrong is the most common reason a complaint that is right in substance gets a correct refusal.

There must be a written policy, under section 27I, wherever tips are paid at a site on more than an occasional and exceptional basis, covering how tips are accepted, allocated and distributed.

Agency workers are in. The code tells employers not to leave agency staff unaware of the policy because they missed the induction.

2. The change, and the 29 September deadline

On 19 August 2026 the Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade, under Kate Dearden MP and the Rt Hon Jonathan Reynolds MP, published a consultation on a revised statutory code. It closes at 11:59pm on 29 September 2026 and applies to England, Scotland and Wales. The substance is a new requirement, introduced by the Employment Rights Act 2025, for employers to consult workers at their place of business when developing or reviewing a tipping policy. It follows an earlier consultation between February and April 2026.

Two honest caveats. A duty to consult is not a duty to agree. And the government has published neither its response nor a final code, so nothing in the revised code is law today. The consultation is open to anyone.

There is now a date attached to it. The government's own implementation timeline, updated on 25 September 2026, lists strengthening tipping law under measures taking effect by the end of 2026. That is a stated intention rather than a commencement date in force, and this timeline has moved before, but it is the first public signal of when the revised code stops being a consultation. The practical read for anyone in hospitality: your employer will have to consult on its tipping policy within months rather than years, and the time to find out what your current policy actually says is before that starts, not after.

3. The record request almost nobody makes

Under section 27J an employer must keep a tipping record showing all qualifying tips received at the site and the amount allocated to each worker, for three years from the date the tip was paid.

You have a right to make a written request to see it. The limits are specific: one request per worker in any three month period, covering up to three years, and you must have worked there for the full period you are asking about. What comes back is the total qualifying tips the employer received at that site, and the amount allocated to you. That pairing is the point. You cannot argue about fairness without both numbers, and until you ask, you only have one.

Chloe, Newport. Front of house, eleven months in the same restaurant. Card tips are pooled and she is told the split is by hours. She requests the last six months. The record shows £14,800 of qualifying tips received at the site and £612 allocated to her. She worked 480 of roughly 5,200 paid team hours, which is 9.2 per cent, and 9.2 per cent of £14,800 is £1,362. The gap is £750. It may have a good explanation, a tronc she is not part of, or a kitchen weighting in the policy. But she can now ask about a specific £750 rather than a feeling, using a number the employer supplied.

4. What a tribunal can order

Use the internal process first and follow the Acas code on disciplinary and grievance procedures. If that fails, the Tipping Act gives a route to the employment tribunal, agency workers included.

A complaint can be about fair allocation, or about the policy and the records, or both. A tribunal finding it well founded can make a public declaration, order the employer to revise a previous allocation, make a non-binding recommendation, or order compensation. The detail that gets missed: compensation can be ordered for other workers at that site who never complained. Limits moved on 6 April 2026, when SI 2026/310 raised the awards under sections 27M(1) and 27O(2) from £5,135 to £5,366 each.

The verdict

The headline change, a duty to consult, is real but modest and is not law yet. The consultation closing on 29 September 2026 is your one cheap chance to say something about it.

The thing actually worth doing is older and duller: request the tipping record. It costs nothing, it is limited to once a quarter so there is no reason to wait, and it turns a suspicion into two numbers. Most people who feel short changed have never seen the total their site took. That is the number the law says you can have.

Frequently asked

Can my employer take an administration fee out of the tips? No. The Tipping Act requires qualifying tips to be passed on. Deductions of the old administration fee kind are what the Act was passed to stop.

Does this cover cash tips handed directly to me? The Act covers employer received tips, and worker received tips subject to the employer's control. A cash tip you keep, which the employer neither receives nor controls, generally sits outside it.

How do I make the record request? In writing, to your employer. Name the site and the period. You must have worked there for the whole period and can only ask once in any three months.
