Tipping law UK: what you are owed now, and the duty to consult that starts in January 2027

UK tipping law already says every qualifying tip goes to staff in full, by the end of the month after the customer paid. The extra duty to consult workers on the tips policy did not arrive in October 2026. GOV.UK now lists it for 1 January 2027, so the rights you can use today matter more.

The short version

  • Since 1 October 2024 your employer must pass on all qualifying tips in full, with no deduction, under the Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023.
  • The payment deadline is fixed: the end of the month after the month the customer paid. GOV.UK's example is a tip left on 23 June, paid out by 31 July.
  • You can ask for the numbers. One written request in any three month period, covering up to three years.
  • The October 2026 change did not happen. The duty to consult workers on the tips policy is now listed for 1 January 2027, in the GOV.UK timeline updated 5 November 2026.
  • The consultation on the revised code closed at 11:59pm on 29 September 2026. The government response is not out yet.
  • The tribunal award limit is £5,366 from 6 April 2026, under SI 2026/310.

1. What does tipping law in the UK already give you?

The Tipping Act and its statutory code of practice took effect on Tuesday 1 October 2024. They apply across England, Scotland and Wales.

Two years on, the parts that would put money in a pocket are still the least used. Here is what the tipping rules in the UK say today.

Every qualifying tip goes to staff. The code is clear that the payment method makes no difference. Card, app or cash, what matters is whether the employer receives the tip or controls it.

That means no administration fee, no card charge taken off, and no share kept back for the business.

There is a hard payment deadline. Under section 27G of the Employment Rights Act 1996, tips must be paid out by the end of the month after the month the customer paid.

GOV.UK spells it out. A tip left on 23 June must be paid out by 31 July at the latest.

The rule works on the calendar month, not a fixed number of days. The difference is larger than it looks.

A tip left on the 1st of a month can lawfully be held almost twice as long as one left on the last day.

So a tip from early in the month is slow by design, and the employer is not in breach.

The date to raise a grievance is the last day of the following month. It is not four weeks after the shift.

Getting that date wrong is the most common reason a complaint that is right in substance gets a correct refusal.

There must be a written tips policy. Section 27I says so wherever tips are paid at a site more than occasionally. It must cover how tips are accepted, allocated and paid out.

You are entitled to see it. If nobody has shown you one, ask.

Agency workers are covered. The code tells employers not to leave agency staff unaware of the policy just because they missed the induction.

Tips do not count towards the minimum wage. Your hourly pay must reach the legal rate before any tips. If you want to check that part of your pay, our guide to what your payslip should show at the minimum wage walks through it line by line.

2. What happened to the October 2026 tipping change?

When this guide first went up, the plan was a new duty from October 2026. That date passed with no change in force.

Here is the timeline, from GOV.UK.

  • February to April 2026: a first consultation on strengthening tipping law.
  • 19 August 2026: the government published a draft revised tipping code of practice for consultation.
  • 29 September 2026, 11:59pm: that consultation closed. The GOV.UK page now says the department is analysing the feedback.
  • 5 November 2026: the government's implementation timeline was updated. It lists strengthening tipping law under measures taking effect on 1 January 2027.

The change itself comes from section 14 of the Employment Rights Act 2025. It adds a duty for employers to consult before they write or review a tips policy.

Where there is a recognised trade union, the employer consults the union. Where there is not, it consults the workers at that place of business.

Two honest caveats. A duty to consult is not a duty to agree. Your employer must listen, but it can still decide the split.

And 1 January 2027 is a planned date. It still needs commencement regulations and approval of the revised code by Parliament. This timeline has moved once already, from October to the new year.

The practical read for anyone in hospitality is simple. Your employer will soon have to ask your view on the tipping policy.

The time to find out what the current policy says is now, before that conversation starts.

October 2026 did bring one change that matters here. From 1 October, GOV.UK says the time limit for most employment tribunal claims rose from three months to six. Our guide to the six month tribunal time limit explains how it works.

3. How do you ask for the tipping record?

Under section 27J, an employer must keep a tipping record. It shows all qualifying tips received at the site and the amount allocated to each worker.

The record must be kept for three years from the date the tip was paid.

You have a right to make a written request to see it. The limits are specific.

  • One request per worker in any three month period.
  • Up to three years of records in one request.
  • You must have worked there for the whole period you ask about.

What comes back is two numbers. The total qualifying tips the site received, and the amount allocated to you.

That pairing is the point. You cannot argue about fairness without both numbers, and until you ask, you only have one.

A worked example: Chloe in Newport

Chloe, Newport. She works front of house, eleven months in the same restaurant. Card tips are pooled, and she is told the split is by hours.

She asks in writing for the last six months of the tipping record.

The record shows £14,800 of qualifying tips received at the site. It shows £612 allocated to her.

She worked 480 of roughly 5,200 paid team hours. That is 9.2 per cent.

9.2 per cent of £14,800 is £1,362. The gap is £750.

It may have a good explanation, such as a tronc she is not part of, or a kitchen weighting in the policy.

But she can now ask about a specific £750 rather than a feeling, using a number the employer supplied.

Keep a copy of your request and the date you sent it. That date starts the clock on the three month limit for your next one.

4. What can a tribunal order?

Use the internal process first. Follow the Acas code on disciplinary and grievance procedures, and put your grievance in writing.

If that fails, the Tipping Act gives a route to the employment tribunal. Agency workers can use it too.

A complaint can be about fair allocation, about the policy and the records, or about both.

If the tribunal finds the complaint well founded, it can:

  • make a public declaration;
  • order the employer to revise a past allocation;
  • make a recommendation, which is not binding;
  • order compensation.

The detail most people miss: compensation can be ordered for other workers at the site who never complained.

The limits moved on 6 April 2026. SI 2026/310 raised the awards under sections 27M(1) and 27O(2) from £5,135 to £5,366 each.

For how those caps sit beside the other tribunal limits, see our guide to employment tribunal compensation limits.

The verdict

The headline change, a duty to consult, is real but modest. It missed its October 2026 date and is now planned for 1 January 2027.

The thing worth doing today is older and duller. Ask for the tipping record.

It costs nothing. It is limited to once a quarter, so there is no reason to wait. And it turns a suspicion into two numbers.

Most people who feel short changed have never seen the total their site took. That is the number tipping law in the UK says you can have.

Frequently asked

Can my employer take an administration fee out of the tips? No. Under UK tipping law, every qualifying tip the employer receives or controls must be passed on to workers in full. Card processing charges and administration fees taken from tips are exactly what the Act was passed to stop. If you see a deduction, ask for the written tips policy and the record.

Does tipping law cover cash tips handed directly to me? The Act covers tips the employer receives, and tips workers receive that the employer controls, such as cash that goes into a shared pot. A cash tip you keep, which the employer neither receives nor controls, generally sits outside it. The written tips policy should say how cash is handled at your site.

When does the new tipping consultation rule start? The GOV.UK implementation timeline, updated 5 November 2026, lists strengthening tipping law for 1 January 2027. From then, employers must consult a recognised union or their workers before writing or reviewing a tips policy. It still needs commencement regulations, so check the timeline again before relying on the exact date.

How do I make a tipping record request? Write to your employer and name the site and the period you want. You must have worked there for the whole of that period. You can make one request in any three month period, covering up to three years. Keep a copy, with the date, so you know when you can ask again.

Do tips count towards the minimum wage? No. Your basic pay must reach the National Minimum Wage or National Living Wage on its own. Tips, gratuities and service charges are paid on top and cannot be used to make up the legal rate. If your hourly pay only reaches the minimum once tips are added, your employer is underpaying you.
