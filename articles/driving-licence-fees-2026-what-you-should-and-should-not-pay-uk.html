Driving licence fees: three of the common ones are free, and one costs £7.50 more than it needs to

DVLA publishes every driving licence fee in one table, and a surprising number of them are nil. Changing your address is free. Changing your name is free. Renewing at 70 is free. Knowing which is which is worth more than it sounds, because copycat sites charge for all three.

The short version

  • Changing your address is free, online or by post. So is changing your name by post (GOV.UK, Driving licence fees, page updated 2 September 2026).
  • Renewing because you are 70 or over is free. So is renewing a short-term medical licence.
  • A photo renewal is £14 online, £17 by post, and £21.50 at a Post Office. Same licence, three prices.
  • A first provisional licence is £34 online or £43 by post, and a replacement for a lost, stolen or damaged licence is £20.
  • Your first full licence after passing your test is free, unless you want a different photo from your provisional, which costs £17.

1. The ones that cost nothing

Start with the free list, because it is longer than most drivers assume and because it is exactly the list that third-party websites charge for.

  • Changing your address: free, online or by post.
  • Changing your name: free, by post. There is no online route for a name change.
  • Renewing because you are 70 or over: free, online or by post.
  • Renewing a short-term medical driving licence: free.
  • Your first full driving licence after passing your test: free, by post, unless you want a different photo from the one on your provisional, which costs £17.
  • Adding, renewing or giving up bus or lorry entitlement: free, by post.
  • Exchanging a paper licence for a photocard at the same time as a change of address or name: free.
  • Exchanging a full Northern Ireland licence for a GB one: free.
  • Getting your licence back after it was revoked for not handing it over to DVLA or at court: free.

The short-term medical renewal is worth a line of its own. GOV.UK says you can renew online if you have diabetes, epilepsy, Parkinson's disease, a visual impairment, a sleep condition or a heart condition, and it is free. Somebody renewing every one or three years on medical grounds is doing this repeatedly, and paying for it would add up.

You are legally required to keep your address up to date, so the fact that it is free removes the only reason anyone might delay it.

2. Renewals, and the £7.50 Post Office premium

Photocard licences are valid for 10 years unless otherwise stated, and DVLA sends a reminder before the current one ends. Then there are three ways to do the same thing.

How you renewFee
Online£14
By post£17
At a Post Office£21.50

The Post Office route is £7.50 more than online for the identical outcome. It exists for people who want the photo taken and the form checked in person, which is a reasonable thing to pay for. It is not a reasonable thing to pay for by accident.

Changing just the photo follows the same pattern: £14 online, £17 by post. Changing the photo and address together is also £14 online. Changing the photo and name together is £17, by post only, because names cannot be changed online.

The useful trick in that table is the combination. If you have moved house and your photo is due, do them together: the address element is free and the photo element is the same £14 it would have cost alone. Doing them as two separate transactions does not cost more, but doing the photo first and the address later means two forms instead of one.

3. Getting your first licence

WhatOnlineBy post
First provisional licence, car, motorcycle or moped£34£43
First full licence after passing your testNot availableFree, or £17 to use a different photo
First full GB licence in exchange for a designated foreign licenceNot available£43

The provisional is £9 cheaper online, which is the largest online discount on the whole DVLA fee list in percentage terms.

The free full licence catches people out in the other direction. After you pass, the licence itself costs nothing, and the only reason to pay is vanity: £17 to avoid keeping the photo you had at 17. That is a genuine choice rather than a fee, and it is worth knowing it is optional.

For context on the rest of the journey, GOV.UK's learn to drive step by step puts the theory test at £23 and the driving test at £62 to £75. Those are DVSA test fees rather than licence fees, and they are separate from everything in the table above.

4. Owen in Neath: three transactions, one bill

Owen is 71 and lives in Neath. He has just moved from one side of town to the other, his photocard has expired, and he has misplaced the old card somewhere in the move.

He searches for how to renew and lands on a site offering to handle it for him.

What he actually owes DVLA:

TransactionFee
Renewing at 70 or overFree
Change of addressFree
TotalNil

The lost card is not a separate problem. He is renewing anyway, and the renewal produces a new card, so there is no need to pay the £20 replacement fee on top.

Change one fact and it looks completely different. If Owen were 45 rather than 71, the same set of jobs would be £14 online: the address change is still free, and the photo renewal is the only chargeable element. By post it would be £17, and at a Post Office £21.50.

Two practical points from the same GOV.UK page. For postal applications, cheques and postal orders are payable to DVLA, and damaged or altered cheques will not be accepted, which is a slow and avoidable way to lose a few weeks. And the address you post to depends on what you are applying for, so it is worth checking rather than reusing an old envelope.

Northern Ireland has a separate fee schedule through nidirect, so none of the figures above apply there.

5. Exchanges, and getting a licence back

Exchanging a paper licence for a photocard has a quirk worth exploiting. On its own it costs £20, online or by post. But if you do it at the same time as a change of address it is free, and at the same time as a change of name it is also free, by post. Anyone still holding a paper licence who is about to move house should do both in one go.

Exchanging a foreign licence depends on your history. A full European Community, European Economic Area or other designated foreign licence is exchanged free if you have previously held a GB licence, and costs £43 if you have not.

Getting your licence back after a disqualification has its own set of fees, all by post:

SituationFee
Disqualified for any reason except drink driving£65
Drink driving, medical exam with a DVLA doctor required£90
Drink driving, no medical exam required£65
Revoked under the New Drivers Act£50
Revoked for not handing the licence to DVLA or the courtFree

The New Drivers Act figure is the one worth knowing in advance. A driver in their first two years who reaches six penalty points has their licence revoked and goes back to provisional, and getting it back costs £50 on top of retaking both tests. That is a fee people discover at the worst possible moment.

On the copycat sites. Everything in this article is done directly on GOV.UK, and the free items are genuinely free there. Sites that charge a "processing" or "checking" fee for a driving licence transaction are not DVLA and are not required. Start every one of these jobs from gov.uk rather than from a search result, and read the address bar before you type a card number.

The verdict

Three of the things people most often expect to pay for cost nothing: changing your address, changing your name, and renewing at 70 or over. A first full licence after passing your test is free too. What does cost money is the ten year photo renewal, and there the only decision that matters is the route: £14 online against £21.50 at a Post Office, for the identical card. If you are moving house and still hold a paper licence, exchange it at the same time and the £20 exchange fee disappears. And do all of it on gov.uk, because the sites that charge for the free ones rank well and look official.

Frequently asked

Does it cost anything to change my address on my licence? No. GOV.UK lists a change of address as free, both online and by post. You are legally required to keep it up to date, so there is no reason to delay it.

Why is the Post Office more expensive? It is £21.50 there against £14 online for renewing an expired licence. You are paying for the in-person service, including having the photo taken and the application checked. The resulting licence is identical.

Do I have to pay for my first licence after passing my test? No. The first full driving licence after passing is free, by post. The only charge is £17 if you want to use a different photo from the one on your provisional, which is optional.

What does it cost to get my licence back after a ban? £65 for a disqualification other than drink driving, £65 for drink driving where no medical exam is needed, £90 for drink driving where a DVLA medical exam is required, and £50 if it was revoked under the New Drivers Act. All are postal applications.
