Get Paid to Use Less Electricity This Winter: What It Really Pays

Yes, you can be paid to cut or shift your electricity use at busy times this winter, and it is free to join if you have a smart meter. The bigger saving, for the right home, is a tariff that is cheaper when the grid is quiet.

1. How can I get paid to use less electricity?

The grid is under most strain on winter evenings, when everyone cooks, heats and switches on lights at once. Paying homes to use a little less at those moments can be cheaper than firing up an extra power station. That is the whole idea.

There are three ways the money reaches you:

  1. Events. Your supplier invites you to cut use for an hour or two. Use less than your normal, and you are paid per kWh saved. Most of these run through NESO's Demand Flexibility Service.
  2. Standing offers. A supplier gives you cheap electricity at fixed quiet times, such as British Gas on Sunday daytimes.
  3. Time of use tariffs. Your unit rate itself changes through the day, so moving use away from the peak saves money every single day.

All three need a smart meter that sends readings to your supplier. GOV.UK says you will not be charged for the installation. Ask your energy supplier to book one.

2. Is the Demand Flexibility Service running this winter?

Yes. NESO, the National Energy System Operator, says the Demand Flexibility Service moved from a winter only service to a year round one on 27 November 2024. So there is no separate "winter 2026 to 2027" launch to wait for. It is already running.

The service changed this year. Ofgem approved a new design on 25 March 2026, and NESO's control room could use it from 14 April 2026. NESO describes it as a "year-round merit based margin tool". In plain English, NESO buys flexibility when it needs it and when the price beats the alternatives. There is no fixed price and no promised number of events.

Two other changes matter for households:

  • You can now be paid to use more, too. NESO says participants can be rewarded for increasing use at certain times, usually when there is spare renewable power.
  • NESO pays providers, not you. In NESO's words, "Payments are made directly to Providers by NESO." Each supplier or aggregator decides how to reward its customers.

To take part, NESO says you must live in England, Scotland or Wales, have a working smart meter and sign up with a registered provider. If you miss an event there is usually no reward and no penalty, depending on your provider's rules.

3. What do Octopus, British Gas and OVO pay?

Each supplier wraps the idea differently. Here is what their own pages say today.

Octopus Saving Sessions

Octopus says most of its Power Down and Power Up sessions run as part of NESO's service, and it enrols you when you join. Your "normal" is worked out from your half hourly use over the last 10 weekdays, leaving out session days and bank holidays. Save against that baseline and you earn points.

The rate moves. In a press release on 24 June 2026, Octopus said that night's session offered 415 Octopoints, or 52p, per kWh. The session the day before offered 196 points per kWh, which is 24.5p on our arithmetic. Octopus says it has paid customers £5.9 million through Saving Sessions in total.

The fine print matters. Your meter must have sent 80% of its half hourly readings in the past 15 days. You must opt in before every session, or you earn nothing. Results reach your dashboard within 14 days, and points expire 3 years after you earn them.

British Gas PeakSave

This is the simplest. You pay as normal, then British Gas credits back half the cost of the electricity you used between 11am and 4pm on Sundays. Its own example is 5 kWh at 12.25p, which gives a 61p credit. A separate Super Sunday tariff runs from 9am to 5pm and is available all year round.

OVO Power Move

OVO rewards a habit rather than a single event. Use 85% or more of your electricity outside the weekday 4pm to 7pm peak and you collect points each month. Its page gives sign up bonuses of 100 and 150 points, but it does not give a pound value for a point, so we cannot price it.

Other suppliers and aggregators also take part in NESO's service. NESO's page links to its current list of registered providers.

4. How much does a household really earn?

This is where the headlines fall apart. NESO's last full winter report covers 27 November 2024 to 28 March 2025. These are its figures:

  • 1.98 million consumers signed up.
  • The service was used 44 times.
  • Homes and businesses cut or shifted 3,917.7 MWh.
  • £943,983 was paid out to registered providers.

Now our own arithmetic. £943,983 divided by 1.98 million consumers is about 48p each for the whole winter. And 3,917.7 MWh across 1.98 million consumers is about 2 kWh each. NESO paid roughly 24p for each kWh delivered.

Those are averages across everyone signed up, including people who rarely joined an event, so keen households will earn more than 48p. But it shows the scale. This is a few pounds a winter for most people, not a second income.

We could not find a full winter 2025 to 2026 summary from NESO, so 2024 to 2025 is the latest full picture.

5. Is a time of use tariff the bigger win?

For some homes, yes, because it pays out every day instead of on a handful of evenings.

Take Agile Octopus. Its prices change every half hour, tied to wholesale prices, and are set once a day for the next 24 hours. Octopus says prices are usually highest between 4pm and 7pm, never go above 100p per kWh, and can even drop below zero. Shift your big loads away from teatime and you pay less. Get caught cooking at the peak on a cold, still evening and you pay a lot more.

Cosy Octopus is built for homes with a heat pump or electric heating. Its cheapest bands are 4am to 7am, 1pm to 4pm and 10pm to midnight, which Octopus says are 51% cheaper than the day rate in your region. The 4pm to 7pm peak is 50% above it.

Compare that with the price cap. Ofgem's average capped electricity price from 1 October to 31 December 2026 is 26.32p per kWh, with a standing charge of 54.83p a day. A time of use tariff only beats that if most of your use sits in the cheap hours.

The homes that win big are those with a large load they can move: an electric car, a home battery or a heat pump. We ran those sums in our guide to a home EV charger and off peak tariff. A flat with a gas boiler and no car has far less to move.

6. Worked example: the Okafors in Swindon

Ada and Tunde Okafor live in a three bed semi in Swindon with two children. They have a working smart meter and gas central heating. This household and its habits are illustrative. The rates are the published ones above.

If they are with Octopus. They join Saving Sessions. During each session they hold the tumble dryer and dishwasher until afterwards, and cook in the air fryer, not the oven. Say that saves 1 kWh per session against their baseline, and they join 12 sessions this winter. Both numbers are our assumption, because Octopus does not promise any.

  • At 196 points per kWh: 12 × 196 = 2,352 points, which is £2.94.
  • At 415 points per kWh: 12 × 415 = 4,980 points, which is about £6.23.

If they are with British Gas. They add PeakSave Sundays and move the week's washing and a batch cook into Sunday 11am to 4pm. Say that is 5 kWh each Sunday. At the 26.32p cap average, half back is 13.16p per kWh. On our arithmetic that is about 66p a week, or about £34 over 52 Sundays.

The lesson is that a regular, easy habit beats occasional events. A weekly Sunday shift earns several times more than a winter of evening sessions. Neither pays for a new appliance, so the Okafors buy nothing for it. Our air fryer versus oven guide shows when that switch saves money on its own.

7. The honest verdict

Worth joining, not worth chasing. If you already have a smart meter and your supplier runs a scheme, sign up. It is free and you lose nothing by skipping an event.

Expect pounds, not hundreds. NESO's last full winter averages about 48p per signed up consumer, by our arithmetic. Keen savers will beat that, but not by a life changing amount.

Do not switch supplier for it. The size of your unit rate and standing charge matters far more than a few sessions. Check you are on a fair deal first, as in our guide to fixed tariffs.

Never go cold for it. Move the dishwasher, not the heating, especially if someone at home is old, very young or unwell.

The real money is a time of use tariff, but only with a big load to move, such as an electric car, battery or heat pump.

Frequently asked

Is the Demand Flexibility Service running in winter 2026? Yes. NESO made it a year round service on 27 November 2024, and an updated version went live on 14 April 2026. There is no separate winter launch. Sign up through a supplier or aggregator that takes part, and it will tell you when an event is on.

Do I need a smart meter to get paid to use less electricity? Yes. NESO requires a working smart meter so your use can be measured, and Octopus also needs half hourly readings. GOV.UK says you will not be charged for installation. Ask your supplier to book one if you do not have one yet.

How much is an Octopoint worth? Octopus says 800 Octopoints are worth £1, so 8 points make 1p. You can turn them into credit on your Octopus account. Saving Session rates change from session to session. In June 2026, one session offered 415 points per kWh, which Octopus put at 52p.

What happens if I miss a session or use more than normal? NESO says that if you miss an event there is usually no reward and no penalty, depending on your provider's rules. With Octopus you simply earn no points for that session. You are never charged extra for taking part.
