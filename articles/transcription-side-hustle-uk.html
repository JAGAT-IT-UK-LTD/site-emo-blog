Transcription jobs in the UK are paid per audio minute, and one audio hour takes a new typist about four hours. So you need roughly 85p a minute just to match the £12.71 National Living Wage. The big beginner platforms publish rates far below that, which is why this side hustle only pays once you specialise or sell direct.

The short version

  • You are paid per audio minute, not per minute of your time. A common rule of thumb is four hours of work for one audio hour when you are new.
  • At 4 to 1 you need 85p an audio minute to match the National Living Wage of £12.71. At 6 to 1, on bad audio, you need £1.28.
  • The beginner platforms pay well under that. TranscribeMe publishes $15 to $22 per audio hour. Scribie publishes $5 to $20. Rev publishes $0.40 to $1.10 per minute.
  • AI took the easy work. Rev sells machine transcription at $0.25 a minute against $1.99 for a human. That is about one eighth of the price.
  • What still pays is the work machines get wrong: legal, medical, technical and difficult audio, sold direct to UK clients.
  • Tax: the trading allowance is £1,000 until 5 April 2027, then £3,000.

1. How long does it take to transcribe one hour of audio?

Transcription is priced per audio minute. That means one minute of recording, not one minute of your time.

The gap between those two things is the whole economics of the job.

No official body measures it. The table below is the rule of thumb the trade works to, so treat it as a starting point and time yourself on a real file.

Your levelHours of work per audio hourWhat that means
Complete beginner5 to 6Constant rewinding, new to the tools
A few weeks in4The working assumption in this guide
Experienced, clean single speaker audio2.5 to 3Good touch typing, foot pedal, familiar subject
Poor audio, several speakers, heavy accents6 to 8This is where people lose money

Everything follows from that ratio. A job advertised at £1.20 a minute is £72 an audio hour. At 4 to 1 that is £18 an hour of your time.

Now suppose the recording turns out to be a noisy four person meeting. At 6 to 1 the same job pays £12 an hour, below the National Living Wage of £12.71.

So the most important habit in this work is listening to a sample before you accept a job. The rate does not tell you what you will earn. The audio quality does.

The rate is per audio minute. Your wage is per real minute. Nobody who has quoted you a price has any incentive to help you convert between the two.

2. How much do transcription jobs pay in the UK?

Start with the sum, because it works for any rate you are offered.

Your hourly pay = rate per audio minute x 60, divided by your ratio.

Here is that sum at the 4 to 1 ratio, measured against the National Living Wage for anyone aged 21 or over.

3. Has AI replaced transcription jobs?

It has replaced the easy ones. Older guides do not mention this, and it changes the advice.

Rev sells machine transcription at $0.25 per audio minute and claims accuracy of 95% or better. The same company charges $1.99 a minute for a human transcript and claims 99% or better.

That is about one eighth of the price. Rev's developer service is cheaper still, at $0.20 for a whole audio hour of English.

What that killed: plain typing of clean, single speaker, non technical audio for general clients. If a machine does it for an eighth of the price and the client does not need better, that work is not coming back.

What it did not kill, and in some cases made more valuable:

  • Regulated and sensitive content. Legal, medical, police, safeguarding. Here 95% is not a pass mark and confidentiality is in the contract.
  • Technical vocabulary. Engineering, pharmaceutical, financial. Machines mangle terms they have not seen, and a wrong drug name is not a typo.
  • Difficult audio. Several speakers talking over each other, strong regional accents, background noise, poor recordings.
  • Editing and checking machine output. This is now a real job. Scribie's published rate is for exactly this work.
  • Verbatim work. Every stutter, pause and false start captured, usually for research or legal use.

The strategic point: do not compete with a machine on speed and price. Compete on the things it gets wrong, and charge for that.

4. Is transcription worth it as a side hustle?

Measure it against the National Living Wage of £12.71, the same test we use for every side hustle you can start for under £100.

  • Beginner on a general platform: fails. TranscribeMe's published range works out at $3.75 to $5.50 an hour at 4 to 1. It is worse if the audio is poor.
  • Beginner selling direct at 85p to £1 a minute: passes narrowly. £12.75 to £15 an hour, before tax and unpaid admin.
  • Experienced at 3 to 1 on £1 a minute: passes well. £20 an hour.
  • Specialist in legal, medical or technical work: passes comfortably. This is where the job becomes worth doing.

The honest reading is this. General transcription is a marginal side hustle in 2026. Specialist transcription is a decent one.

The difference is a few months of building toward a niche on purpose, not taking whatever appears. If you like careful work with words, compare it with proofreading as a side hustle, where the professional body publishes suggested rates.

5. How do you get faster?

You cannot easily raise your rate in your first months. You can cut the hours a job takes, which has the same effect on your pay.

  • A foot pedal. Stopping and rewinding with your feet, not your hands, removes thousands of interruptions. Check the price and that it works with your software before you buy.
  • Proper transcription software. Adjustable playback speed, automatic rewind on pause, timestamp shortcuts. Free options exist and beat a media player and a document side by side.
  • Text expansion. Shortcuts for speaker names, recurring phrases and formatting. Ten minutes of setup pays back on the first job.
  • Touch typing. If you still look at the keys, that is the bottleneck. Free practice sites fix it in a few weeks, and it lifts everything else.
  • Start from a machine draft where the client allows it. Editing a good draft is usually faster than typing from nothing. Check the contract first. Some clients forbid uploading their audio to outside services, and for confidential material you must not.

6. A worked first three months

Jo, 44, Newcastle. Twelve hours a week. £48 spent on a foot pedal. This is an example to show the sums, with rates Jo agreed herself.

Month one. General work at 80p a minute. Her ratio is 5 to 1 because she is learning. She transcribes 9 audio hours across 45 working hours. £432 gross, £9.60 an hour. Below the wage floor, and the honest truth of month one.

Month two. The foot pedal arrives and she sets up text expansion. Her ratio improves to 4 to 1. Now 48 working hours produce 12 audio hours. £576, £12 an hour. Still just under.

Month three. She stops taking everything. She listens to a sample before accepting and turns down bad audio. She also approaches two local firms directly at £1.30 a minute.

Her ratio is now 3.5 to 1 on the work she has chosen. That is 13 audio hours in 45.5 working hours. £1,014, about £22.30 an hour.

What changed between month one and month three. Not her typing. She bought a pedal, stopped accepting bad audio, and went direct. Her rate per minute went up 62% and her hours per job went down 30%. Together that more than doubled her hourly pay.

Tax note. She passed £1,000 of gross trading income during month two. In the 2026 to 2027 tax year that means registering for Self Assessment by 5 October after the tax year ends.

From 6 April 2027 the trading allowance rises to £3,000, so the same first three months would fall inside it. Our guide to the £3,000 trading allowance from April 2027 explains what changes.

The honest verdict

General transcription is no longer a good side hustle. Machine services at $0.25 an audio minute have removed the floor. The beginner platforms publish pay that falls well below the UK wage floor once you apply the 4 to 1 ratio.

Specialist transcription is a different job and still pays properly. Legal, medical, technical and difficult audio all need a human. A direct client who needs it right will pay a rate that clears £20 an hour of your time.

If you want to try it, do four things. Buy a foot pedal. Always sample the audio before accepting. Aim to get your ratio under 4 to 1. Pick a niche within three months.

Without the niche you are competing with software on price. That is not a competition you can win.

Frequently asked

How much do transcriptionists earn in the UK? Employed roles average about £23,600 to £25,400 a year on Talent.com and Indeed, close to the wage floor. Freelance pay depends on your rate per audio minute and your speed. At a 4 to 1 ratio, £1 a minute works out at £15 for each hour you actually work.

Can I get transcription jobs in the UK with no experience? Yes. Platforms such as Rev, TranscribeMe, Scribie and GoTranscript take beginners after a test. They pay in dollars, and the published rates work out below the UK National Living Wage at beginner speed. Treat them as paid practice for a month or two, then look for direct clients.

Do I need a qualification or a foot pedal? No qualification is required. Medical and legal work have their own terms and layouts, and a course in one of those niches can help you charge more. A foot pedal is not essential, but it is the tool most likely to speed you up. Decent over ear headphones matter just as much.

Is Rev or TranscribeMe worth it? Only as a way to learn. Rev publishes $0.40 to $1.10 per audio minute and TranscribeMe $15 to $22 per audio hour. At four hours of work per audio hour, TranscribeMe pays $3.75 to $5.50 an hour. Its own page gives average monthly earnings of $250.

Do I pay tax on transcription income? Trading income up to £1,000 a year is covered by the trading allowance, which rises to £3,000 from 6 April 2027. Above it you register for Self Assessment and pay tax on your profit. The allowance counts gross income, before any expenses, across all your side work added together.
