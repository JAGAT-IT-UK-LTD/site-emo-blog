Windows 10 Was Supposed To Stop Getting Security Updates On 13 October. It Now Runs To 2027, But Only If You Enrol.

Anyone who dealt with the Windows 10 end of support last autumn was told the extension would last one year, to 13 October 2026. That date is five weeks away and it no longer applies. Microsoft's UK page now says consumer Extended Security Updates run until 12 October 2027. What has not changed is that nothing happens unless you enrol, and the free route requires a Microsoft account. Here is what changed, what it costs, and the honest case for and against a new machine.

1. What changed, and what did not

Start with what is fixed. Microsoft's UK Extended Security Updates page states that Windows 10 support has ended, on 14 October 2025. Since then, a Windows 10 PC that is not enrolled in ESU has received no security updates. That has not changed and will not.

What changed is the length of the safety net. When the consumer ESU programme was announced in June 2025 it was a one year bridge, ending 13 October 2026. That is the date on every enrolment confirmation screen from last autumn, and it is the date most people still have in their heads. Microsoft's page now says: "You can enroll in ESU any time until the programme ends on 12 October, 2027. If you're already enrolled, your coverage will automatically continue through that date, no action needed." Which? reported the extension on 26 June 2026, citing Microsoft's ESU page.

If you enrolled last year, you have gained a year without doing anything. If you did not, the door is open until October 2027, but an unenrolled device is unpatched in the meantime.

2. How enrolment works and what it costs

The route is on Microsoft's page. Go to Settings, then Update and Security, then Windows Update. If the device qualifies you will see an Enrol now link. The prerequisites are: Windows 10 version 22H2 Home, Pro, Pro Education or Workstations; the latest Windows update installed; and a Microsoft account that is an administrator on the device and is not a child account. If you sign in with a local account you will be prompted to sign in to, or create, a Microsoft account.

Whichever option you pick, the licence covers up to 10 devices signed into the same Microsoft account, and on each additional device you use Add device from the same Windows Update screen. Once one user on a PC enrols, everyone on that PC is covered. Microsoft's page also says consumer ESU is not offered on devices in kiosk mode, joined to a domain, or managed by mobile device management, which is its way of saying businesses pay separately.

The free option is the one most people should take, and the objection to it is legitimate: it requires a Microsoft account and a settings sync. If you have kept Windows 10 on a local account precisely to avoid that, ESU asks you to give it up. The alternative is £24.99 for a year, per Which?, which still requires the account for the purchase.

3. What an unsupported PC actually risks

This is worth stating plainly rather than dramatically. An unsupported operating system continues to work. What stops is the flow of fixes for newly discovered security holes. Attackers know which systems are no longer patched and target them, which is why Microsoft and Which? both say the same thing: keep the updates flowing or move off the system.

The practical risk is proportional to what the machine does. A Windows 10 PC used for online banking, email and shopping is a target worth patching. A machine used offline for a photo archive is a different calculation. Which?'s research suggests around 21 million people in the UK own and use a Windows 10 PC, so this is not an edge case.

Browsers, antivirus and Office builds also set their own Windows 10 end dates, and ESU does not extend those. That is usually what eventually forces the move.

4. A worked example: the Hughes family in Bangor

The Hughes family in Bangor have two Windows 10 machines: a 2017 desktop that fails the Windows 11 hardware check, and a 2020 laptop that passes it but has never been upgraded.

Last October they enrolled the desktop in ESU using the free settings backup and saw the confirmation screen say support ran to 13 October 2026. They did nothing with the laptop. Today the desktop is covered to 12 October 2027 automatically. The laptop has had no security updates for almost eleven months.

The right moves are different for each. The laptop should simply be upgraded to Windows 11, which is free and takes an hour: Settings, Update and Security, Windows Update. The desktop cannot be upgraded, so it stays on ESU until October 2027, and the family has a year to decide whether to replace it, put ChromeOS Flex or a Linux distribution on it, or retire it. Total cost so far: nothing. The 2027 decision is the one that costs money, and they now have twelve extra months to make it.

5. The honest case for and against a new PC

Against: if your PC passes the Windows 11 check, the upgrade is free and the hardware is fine. If it fails the check, ESU keeps it patched until October 2027 for nothing, and both ChromeOS Flex and Linux are free routes to a supported system on old hardware. There is no deadline in the next five weeks that requires anyone to spend money.

For: a machine that fails the Windows 11 check is by definition six or more years old, ESU now has a hard end in October 2027, and browsers and antivirus will drop Windows 10 on their own schedule. If you are going to replace it within two years anyway, replacing it during a sale rather than when it dies is cheaper.

If you do buy, Which?'s guidance for a Windows 11 machine that will last is at least 8GB of RAM (16GB for future proofing), at least a 256GB SSD, and an Intel Core 5 or i5 or AMD Ryzen 5 processor or better, avoiding Pentium and Celeron.

6. The honest verdict

If your PC can run Windows 11, upgrade it. It is free. Do not pay for ESU on a machine that qualifies.

If it cannot, enrol in ESU today using the free settings backup. You are covered to 12 October 2027 on up to 10 devices, and every unenrolled month is an unpatched month.

Do not buy anything because of the 13 October date. It no longer exists. Buy, if at all, on your own timetable before October 2027, and buy to Which?'s specification rather than to a discount.

Frequently asked

Does Windows 10 stop getting security updates on 13 October 2026? No. That was the original end of the consumer Extended Security Updates programme. Microsoft's UK page now states the programme ends on 12 October 2027, and existing enrolments continue automatically to that date.

Is Windows 10 ESU free? It is free if you back up your PC settings to a Microsoft account. Otherwise it costs 1,000 Microsoft Rewards points or a one-off payment Microsoft prices at USD 30 or local equivalent plus tax, reported by Which? as £24.99 in the UK.

Do I need a Microsoft account? Yes, for all three routes. The account must be an administrator on the device and cannot be a child account. Users on local accounts are prompted to sign in or create one during enrolment.

How many PCs does one ESU licence cover? Up to 10 devices signed in to the same Microsoft account, according to Microsoft's page. Use Add device on each one from Windows Update.
