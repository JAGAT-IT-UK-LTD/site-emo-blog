£100 Household Energy Payment: Who Gets It and When

Ruhul Amin · Published 15 November 2026 · 10 min read · Figures checked November 2026

If you live in England, pay council tax and your home is in band A, B, C or D, you should get a one off £100 Household Energy Payment from January 2027. Pay your council tax by Direct Debit and the government says you need to do nothing. That makes it the easiest £100 of the winter, and also the easiest one for a scammer to pretend to offer you.

The short version

  • It is called the Household Energy Payment, and it is £100. The government announced it in the Autumn Budget on 28 October 2026.
  • It is for households in council tax bands A to D in England. You must be liable for council tax on the home and live there as your main home. The government says up to 20 million households could be eligible.
  • Payments start from January 2027. If you pay council tax by Direct Debit, the money goes into your bank account and you do not need to apply.
  • If you pay another way, your council will invite you to apply. Nobody but your council should ever ask you to claim it.
  • It sits on top of other help. The £150 Warm Home Discount and £25 Cold Weather Payments are separate schemes with their own rules.

1. What exactly is the £100 payment?

The official name is the Household Energy Payment. The Department for Energy Security and Net Zero set it out on 28 October 2026, in its guide to what the Autumn Budget means for energy bills. The same page also calls it "the £100 rebate".

It is a flat £100 per eligible household. It is not a discount on your energy bill and it does not go through your energy supplier. It is run through council tax, which is why your band decides whether you get it.

The government says up to 20 million households could be eligible. It also says the payment would reach an estimated 83% of fuel stressed households in England, and 88% of households in the bottom three income deciles.

For the rest of the Budget, see our Autumn Budget 2026 guide.

2. Do I qualify, and who misses out?

The test on GOV.UK is short. You qualify if you are "liable for council tax on, and live in, a property in council tax bands A to D as their main home". The home must be in England.

Three groups are named as not eligible:

  • Second homes.
  • Empty properties.
  • Households where the person living there is not responsible for paying the council tax. That can include some lodgers and people in shared houses where the landlord pays.

There is good news for people who pay no council tax at all. The government says most households that are exempt from council tax are expected to be included. Full criteria will be published later, so a borderline case should wait for that detail rather than assume.

Not sure of your band? It is printed at the top of your council tax bill, or you can look it up on the GOV.UK council tax band checker. If you think your band is wrong, our guide to challenging your council tax band explains the risks before you ask.

What about Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland?

The £100 payment is England only. GOV.UK says the governments of Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland will receive additional funding and decide how to use it. So if you live there, any help will come from your own government's scheme, not this one.

What if I am in band E or above?

You will not get the £100. The government has set aside an extra £100 million for councils to give discretionary support to households who are not eligible. Each council sets its own criteria, so if you are in a higher band and struggling, ask your council what it is offering once the scheme opens.

3. How and when will I get it?

Payments start from January 2027. There is no single national payday. Each council pays its own residents, so the exact date will differ from one area to the next.

How it reaches you depends on how you pay council tax:

  1. Direct Debit payers. GOV.UK says you do not need to take any action, and the rebate goes directly into your bank account.
  2. Everyone else. Your council will invite you to apply. Bristol City Council, for example, says these households may be asked for payment details through a secure claims process.

Bristol also suggests that setting up a Direct Debit now could make things quicker when the scheme launches. That is a free step, not a requirement.

For now there is nothing to do and no form to fill in. Bristol's page says you do not need to contact the council about the payment at this stage. Councils will publish local details once they have the government's full guidance.

4. How do I spot a fake £100 text?

A one off £100 with a known start month is perfect bait. The government's own guide is blunt about it: "Nobody other than your council should get in touch asking you to claim the rebate."

It also says to give personal information only when you are certain your local council is the one contacting you, and to check with your council if you are unsure. Put together, that gives some simple rules:

  • If you pay by Direct Debit, you should never be asked to claim. A text or email asking you to "apply" or "confirm your bank details" is a red flag.
  • Energy suppliers, banks and the DWP do not run this payment. A message in their name about the £100 is not genuine.
  • Do not use a link in a message. Go to your council's website by typing its address yourself, or ring the number on your council tax bill.
  • Nobody needs a card number or PIN to pay you. A real payment only needs the account it goes into.

If you think you have already given details to a scam, call your bank at once on the number on the back of your card. Our guide to APP fraud reimbursement explains what you can claim back if money was taken.

5. Can I get it with the Warm Home Discount and Cold Weather Payment?

Yes. They are three different schemes, paid by three different bodies, and nothing on GOV.UK says one cancels another. The government's Budget guide itself says people on means tested benefits "may also be eligible for the £150 Warm Home Discount".

A few details matter here. For the Warm Home Discount in England and Wales, the qualifying date was 23 August 2026. Qualifying benefits include Universal Credit, Housing Benefit, income related Employment and Support Allowance and Pension Credit. Letters go out between late October 2026 and early January 2027. Our guide to who gets the Warm Home Discount automatically walks through the letter.

For the Cold Weather Payment, the 2026 to 2027 scheme runs from 1 November 2026 to 31 March 2027. A payment is triggered when the average temperature in your area is recorded or forecast at zero degrees or below for 7 days in a row. People in Scotland cannot get it, and may get a Winter Heating Payment instead. More in our Cold Weather Payment guide.

6. Worked example: Jean and Derek in Doncaster

Jean and Derek are retired and own a band B semi in Doncaster. They pay council tax by Direct Debit. They get Pension Credit, and Jean's name is on the electricity bill. This household is illustrative. The amounts are the official ones above.

  • Household Energy Payment: band B, England, main home, Direct Debit. They should receive £100 into their bank account from January 2027 without doing anything.
  • Warm Home Discount: on Pension Credit on 23 August 2026, with Jean's name on the bill. They should get £150 off their electricity bill by 31 March 2027.
  • Cold Weather Payment: Pension Credit usually qualifies. They get £25 for each 7 day cold spell in their area. How many spells there will be is unknowable today, so we count none.

By our own arithmetic, that is £100 plus £150, so £250 of certain help this winter, plus £25 for every cold spell.

How big is that? Ofgem's price cap from 1 October 2026 is £1,723 a year for a typical household using both gas and electricity. On our own arithmetic, £250 divided by £1,723 is about 14.5% of a typical year's bill. The £100 on its own is about 5.8%.

Now compare their neighbours, a band E household. They miss the £100, so their only route is to ask the council about the discretionary fund. One band apart, and the help is very different.

7. The honest verdict

This is real money with almost no effort, for most people in bands A to D in England. If you pay council tax by Direct Debit, the government says it simply arrives from January 2027.

It will not fix a winter bill. £100 is about 5.8% of the £1,723 typical annual bill under the current cap, by our arithmetic. Check whether you also qualify for the £150 Warm Home Discount and Cold Weather Payments.

The biggest risk is a fake claim message. Only your council should ever ask you to claim, and Direct Debit payers should not be asked at all.

Your one useful job today: check your band, and set up a council tax Direct Debit if you were planning to anyway.

Frequently asked

Do I need to apply for the £100 energy payment? Not if you pay council tax by Direct Debit and live in band A to D in England. GOV.UK says the rebate goes straight into your bank account. If you pay another way, your council will invite you to apply, so wait for that contact rather than searching for a form now.

When will the £100 Household Energy Payment be paid? From January 2027. Councils make the payments, so the exact date depends on where you live. Your council should publish its timetable once it has the government's detailed guidance. Until then, nothing needs doing, and Bristol City Council asks residents not to contact it about the payment yet.

Does the £100 payment apply in Scotland or Wales? No. The Household Energy Payment is for England only. GOV.UK says Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland will get additional funding and their own governments will decide how to use it. Watch your devolved government's announcements for any comparable support.

Will I get the £100 if I already get the Warm Home Discount? Yes, if you also meet the council tax test. The two schemes are separate. The Warm Home Discount is £150 off your electricity bill for people on certain means tested benefits. The £100 depends on your council tax band in England, not on benefits.

I rent a room and my landlord pays the council tax. Do I get it? Probably not. GOV.UK says households where the householder is not responsible for paying council tax are not eligible. The person liable for the council tax on that home is the one the payment is aimed at, if the home is in bands A to D.
