What does the Universal Credit Act 2025 say about the health element?

The Universal Credit health element is the extra monthly amount for people found to have limited capability for work and work-related activity, often shortened to LCWRA. The Universal Credit Act 2025 split it into two rates from 6 April 2026.

The Act is short. Four sections matter, and all four are now in force.

Section 2 changes the table of amounts in regulation 36 of the Universal Credit Regulations 2013. It adds a new row worth £217.26. That row covers a claimant with LCWRA "other than a pre-2026 claimant, a severe conditions criteria claimant or a claimant who is terminally ill".

The old row is relabelled. The higher amount now applies only to those three protected groups. Subsection (3) starts the change on 6 April 2026, for assessment periods beginning on or after that date.

Section 3 switches off the yearly uprating duty in section 150 of the Social Security Administration Act 1992. It covers the LCWRA and LCW elements for the reviews in the tax years ending 5 April 2026 to 5 April 2029. In plain words, the government does not have to raise them with prices until April 2030.

Section 1 works the other way. It makes the Secretary of State set the standard allowance for 2026-27 to 2029-30 at no less than last year's figure raised by CPI, then raised again by a fixed uplift.

Those uplifts are written into the Act: 2.3% for 2026-27, 3.1% for 2027-28, 4.0% for 2028-29 and 4.8% for 2029-30. This is the above-inflation rise offered in exchange for the cut.

Section 4 protects people on the higher rate. Their combined standard allowance and protected health element must keep pace with CPI each year to 2029-30. The Secretary of State must raise one or the other to make that true.

GOV.UK puts the 2026-27 figures simply. The standard allowance is £424.90 a month for a single person aged 25 or over. The health element is £429.80 for a severe condition "unlikely to change" or someone nearing the end of life, and £217.26 where the condition "may improve over time".

Who gets £429.80 and who gets £217.26?

GOV.UK's health condition guidance sets out who keeps the higher rate. The test is about dates and the kind of condition, not about how ill you feel.

One detail surprises people. If you told DWP about your condition before 6 April 2026, you are protected whatever the date of the LCWRA decision. A decision made in summer 2026 can still carry the higher rate.

The severe conditions test has four parts. GOV.UK says the condition must mean you cannot work, last your whole life, not get better, and be officially diagnosed by a health professional. The work capability assessment looks at the medical evidence to decide.

Couples get one health element, never two. If either partner qualifies for the higher amount, the household gets the higher amount.

Combined with the standard allowance, a single person aged 25 or over gets £854.70 a month in 2026-27 if protected. A new claimant gets £642.16. That is £212.54 a month apart, or £2,550.48 a year, before housing costs, children or the carer element.

A household with children has more riding on the claim. That includes whether your children qualify for free school meals on Universal Credit, which is a separate test with its own rules.

What happens to the Universal Credit health element in April 2027?

DWP published its proposed benefit and pension rates for 2027-28 on 11 November 2026. They show the two rates moving apart.

The lower rate stays at £217.26, exactly as section 3 allows. The protected rate rises to £441.41, an increase of £11.61 a month. The standard allowance for a single person aged 25 or over rises to £443.21, up £18.31 a month.

The LCW amount, paid only to some people with limited capability for work from before April 2017, also stays frozen at £158.76.

These are proposed rates. Parliament still has to approve the uprating order. The September 2026 CPI figure behind them is explained in why the September CPI figure sets your April 2027 benefits.

The lesson is simple. A new claimant's total still rises each April, because the standard allowance rises. The health part does not.

What does the rule not change?

It does not cut anyone's existing award. If you got LCWRA before 6 April 2026, GOV.UK says the change does not affect you. Section 4 protects the real value of your combined amount to 2030.

It does not touch Personal Independence Payment. PIP is a separate benefit with its own rules. The PIP changes in the original bill were removed before it became law. You can get PIP and either rate of the health element at the same time.

What has shifted for PIP is the review process. The gap between PIP reviews and what the Timms review has actually changed are the parts that reach existing PIP claimants.

It does not change the test. The work capability assessment and its descriptors are the same. What changed is the amount attached to the result.

It does not apply to legacy benefits. Employment and Support Allowance has its own support group rate. The Act is about Universal Credit.

It does not stop the standard allowance rising. Section 1 guarantees above-inflation rises for four years for every claimant, health element or not.

A worked example in Oldham

Tariq lives in Oldham. He is 34, single, rents privately, and has claimed Universal Credit since 2024. The housing element helps with his rent.

In July 2026 he reports a back condition that stops him working. He sends in his fit note and is referred for a work capability assessment. In October 2026 he is found to have limited capability for work and work-related activity.

He declared his condition after 6 April 2026. It is not judged lifelong and unchanging. So he is on the £217.26 row.

Leaving housing aside, his monthly award is £424.90 plus £217.26, which is £642.16. From April 2027, on DWP's proposed rates, it becomes £443.21 plus £217.26, which is £660.47.

His neighbour Sandra is 52. She reported a similar condition in November 2025 and was found to have LCWRA in March 2026. She is a pre-2026 claimant.

Sandra gets £424.90 plus £429.80, which is £854.70 now. From April 2027 that becomes £443.21 plus £441.41, which is £884.62.

Same town, same condition, same decision. The gap between them grows from £212.54 to £224.15 a month. Over 2027-28 that is £2,689.80. The only difference is the date on the form.

Tariq has one route upwards. If his condition gets worse and becomes lifelong and unchanging, he should report the change and send the medical evidence. A new assessment could move him to the higher row.

If his back pain started at work and his employer paid him while he was off, what Statutory Sick Pay pays also matters, because earnings count against Universal Credit.

What should you check, and where is free help?

If you were already on the health element, check your statement. It should show £429.80 for every assessment period since 6 April 2026, and £441.41 once the April 2027 rates start.

If it shows £217.26 and you told DWP about your condition before 6 April 2026, write in your journal. Ask for a mandatory reconsideration. That decision can be challenged.

If you are reporting a condition now, do not wait because of the lower rate. The element starts only once limited capability is established, so reporting later means it starts later. Upload your fit note.

If your condition is lifelong and will not get better, say so in the assessment. Send the medical evidence that shows it. The severe conditions criteria are the whole difference between £217.26 and £441.41 next year.

Free, named help exists. Citizens Advice handles Universal Credit queries and mandatory reconsiderations. Turn2us has a benefits calculator and helpline. Scope runs a disability helpline. GOV.UK carries the official figures.

Nobody needs to pay a "benefits consultant" to find out which row they are on.

If money is tight but you can put a little aside, Help to Save pays a 50% bonus to people on Universal Credit who have some earnings.

The honest verdict

If your health condition was on your claim before 6 April 2026, nothing has been cut. Your higher rate rises to a proposed £441.41 in April 2027, and the Act protects its real value to 2030. Check your statement.

If you report a condition now and it is not severe or terminal, the extra amount is £217.26 a month, frozen until 2030. That is the law, sections 2 and 3, and no adviser can change it.

The one lever is the severe conditions criteria. If your condition is lifelong and will not improve, make sure the assessment sees the evidence. Free help exists for exactly that.

Frequently asked questions about the Universal Credit health element

How much is the Universal Credit health element in 2026?

There are two rates in 2026-27. The higher rate is £429.80 a month, for people protected from before 6 April 2026, people with a severe lifelong condition and people nearing the end of life. The lower rate for new claims is £217.26 a month. DWP proposes £441.41 and £217.26 for 2027-28.

I was getting the health element before April 2026. Has mine gone down?

No. Section 2 of the Universal Credit Act 2025 keeps pre-2026 claimants on the higher row, and GOV.UK says the change does not affect you. Section 4 protects the real value of your combined standard allowance and health element to 2029-30. Your rate is proposed to rise to £441.41 in April 2027.

Can you get the Universal Credit health element and PIP?

Yes. PIP is a separate benefit and does not depend on your income. The PIP changes in the original bill were removed before it became law. You can receive PIP alongside either rate of the health element. They are assessed separately, so getting one does not guarantee the other.

Will £217.26 rise with inflation?

Not before April 2030. Section 3 switches off the yearly uprating duty for the LCWRA and LCW elements in the reviews up to 5 April 2029. DWP's proposed rates for 2027-28 confirm this, keeping the lower rate at £217.26 while the standard allowance and the protected rate both go up.

Is this benefits advice?

No. It is general information taken from the Universal Credit Act 2025, GOV.UK guidance and DWP's proposed rates for 2027-28. Your own claim depends on your dates, your condition and your household.
