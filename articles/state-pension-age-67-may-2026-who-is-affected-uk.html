State Pension Age 67: Born in 1960 or 1961? Here Is Your Exact Date and What the Wait Costs

Your State Pension age is 66 if you were born on or before 5 April 1960, and 67 if you were born on or after 6 March 1961. Everyone born in between gets 66 plus one to eleven months, and that phased rise to 67 has been under way since 6 May 2026. Each month of extra waiting is worth about £1,046 at the full rate, so it pays to know your exact date.

1. State Pension age if you were born in 1960 or 1961

Your State Pension age depends on your exact date of birth. Anyone born on or before 5 April 1960 reached it at 66. Anyone born a day later did not.

The Pensions Act 2014 brought the rise to 67 forward by eight years. Instead of one big jump, it adds a month at a time, by birth month. Section 26 of the Act sets out the bands:

The last column is our own arithmetic: your birthday, plus 66 years, plus the months in the middle column. Always confirm your own date with the GOV.UK State Pension age checker.

The Act also fixes three awkward month-end cases. Someone born on 31 July 1960 reaches State Pension age on 30 November 2026. Born on 31 December 1960, it is 30 September 2027. Born on 31 January 1961, it is 30 November 2027.

2. Who has already reached it, and who is next?

As of 9 November 2026, the first three bands have reached State Pension age. That is everyone born from 6 April 1960 to 5 July 1960.

The fourth band, born 6 July to 5 August 1960, reaches it right now. Their dates run from 6 November to 5 December 2026, with the 31 July birthday landing on 30 November.

The table shows a quiet side effect. Because each band waits one month longer than the band before, new pensioners arrive in alternate two-month windows. Nobody reached State Pension age between 6 April and 5 May 2026, and the same pattern repeats until 2028.

The State Pension is not paid automatically. You have to claim it, and the GOV.UK checker is the quickest way to see the date your claim can start.

3. How much does each month of waiting cost?

The full new State Pension for 2026 to 2027 is £241.30 a week. That is £12,547.60 a year, or £1,045.63 a month on our arithmetic.

From April 2027 it rises again. The government's Pension Tax Allowance explainer, published with the Budget on 28 October 2026, puts the full annual value for 2027 to 2028 at £13,036.40. That is £250.70 a week, or about £1,086 a month, a rise of 3.9% on our arithmetic.

Compared with someone born on 5 April 1960, who got the pension at 66:

Those figures assume the full rate. That needs 35 qualifying years of National Insurance under the new State Pension. Many people get less, and some get more through a protected payment from the old system.

Your own figure is on your State Pension forecast at GOV.UK. That is the number to multiply.

The State Pension is taxable income, so the net cost is lower for anyone who pays income tax. A new rule changes that picture from April 2027. The Pension Tax Allowance means people over State Pension age whose total income is at or below £13,036.40 will pay no income tax in 2027 to 2028. It tapers away up to £13,319 and runs until 5 April 2030.

4. What else moves with your State Pension age?

Your State Pension age is a reference point for several other things. They move with it rather than staying at 66.

  • Pension Credit. The qualifying age is State Pension age. If yours is 66 and 7 months, that is the earliest you can get Pension Credit. The GOV.UK checker shows both dates.
  • The older person's bus pass in England. Free bus travel starts at State Pension age. London, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland run their own schemes with their own ages.
  • National Insurance. You stop paying employee Class 1 National Insurance from State Pension age. A later date means more months of NI on your wages.
  • Winter Fuel Payment. This uses a fixed date of birth each winter. For winter 2026 to 2027 you must be born on or before 27 June 1960, with a qualifying week of 21 to 27 September 2026. That matches reaching 66 years and 3 months by the end of that week. People born from 28 June 1960 wait for next winter.

What does not move with it: access to workplace and personal pensions. The normal minimum pension age rises from 55 to 57 on 6 April 2028. That is a separate timetable and is not tied to State Pension age.

5. A worked example in Bedford

Kamal in Bedford was born on 20 October 1960. The table puts his State Pension age at 66 years and 7 months, so he reaches it on 20 May 2027.

His brother was born in March 1960. He got his State Pension on his 66th birthday in March 2026.

Kamal's forecast shows the full rate. Seven months at this year's £1,045.63 a month is about £7,319 he will not get, compared with someone born seven months earlier. The last few weeks of his wait fall after the April 2027 rise to £250.70, so the real gap is slightly bigger.

He works as a delivery driver on £27,000 a year and plans to carry on to 67. For those seven months he also keeps paying National Insurance on his wages.

Two practical points come out of his forecast. First, it shows a two-year gap in his NI record from 2009 to 2011. If he needs those years for the full rate, the forecast tells him whether voluntary contributions for recent years would change anything.

Second, if he keeps working past May 2027 and does not need the pension at once, he can defer it. GOV.UK says that adds 1% for every nine weeks, just under 5.8% a year. Deferring for 52 weeks adds £13.99 a week to the current full rate. Whether that pays off depends on how long he lives, and nobody can tell him that.

6. Is the State Pension age rising to 68 sooner?

Not yet. The rise to 68 is already in law for 2044 to 2046, under the Pensions Act 2007. It starts with people born on 6 April 1977, who reach 68 from 6 May 2044. Anyone born after 5 April 1978 has a State Pension age of 68.

Two earlier government reviews looked at bringing it forward, and neither led to a change. A third review launched on 21 July 2025. It has two parts: an independent report led by Dr Suzy Morrissey, and a Government Actuary's Department report on life expectancy.

The independent report's call for evidence closed. As of 9 November 2026 the GOV.UK review page shows no published report and no decision. Any change to 68 would need an Act of Parliament.

The Budget did change the triple lock, though not soon. The Budget document says it stays in place through April 2029. From April 2030 the earnings part is replaced by the rise needed to keep the State Pension at its April 2029 value relative to earnings.

So if you were born in 1960 or 1961, your date is fixed and is in the table above. If you were born in the late 1970s or later, 67 and then 68 are the law, and the review is where any change would show up first.

7. The honest verdict

If you were born between 6 April 1960 and 5 March 1961, find your line in the table and write the date down. The gap between neighbouring bands is a month, worth about £1,046 at this year's full rate.

Then check your State Pension forecast on GOV.UK. It gives your real weekly figure and shows any NI gaps while there is still time to fill them.

Do not plan around a change to 68. The review exists, it has not reported, and nothing before 2044 is in law.

Frequently asked

What is my State Pension age if I was born in 1960? If you were born on or before 5 April 1960, it is 66. If you were born from 6 April 1960 to 31 December 1960, it is 66 plus between 1 and 9 months, depending on your birth month. The table above gives each band, and the GOV.UK checker gives your exact date.

What is my State Pension age if I was born in 1961? Born 6 January to 5 February 1961, it is 66 years and 10 months. Born 6 February to 5 March 1961, it is 66 years and 11 months. Born on or after 6 March 1961, it is 67. Those born from 1 to 5 January 1961 are in the 66 years and 9 months band.

Does the rise to 67 change how much State Pension I get? No. The amount depends on your National Insurance record, not your State Pension age. The rise only changes the date your payments start. The full new State Pension is £241.30 a week now and £250.70 a week from April 2027, but your own figure is on your forecast.

Can I get Pension Credit at 66 if my State Pension age is later? No. The Pension Credit qualifying age is the same as your State Pension age, so it moves with your date. If your State Pension age is 66 and 8 months, that is also the earliest you can claim Pension Credit. The GOV.UK checker shows both dates together.

Has the State Pension age rise to 67 been scrapped or delayed? No. It is in the Pensions Act 2014 and has been happening since 6 May 2026. Nothing in the 28 October 2026 Budget changed it. The third State Pension age review is about the later rise to 68, and it has not reported.
