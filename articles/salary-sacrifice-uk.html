Salary Sacrifice UK: The Payslip Trick Most Employees Never Ask About

Ruhul Amin · Published 18 August 2026 · Updated 20 November 2026 · 9 min read · Figures checked November 2026
data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg' viewBox='0 0 100 100'%3E%3Crect width='100' height='100' fill='%230fae7e'/%3E%3Ctext x='50' y='63' font-family='Segoe UI,Arial' font-size='38' font-weight='700' fill='%23ffffff' text-anchor='middle'%3ERA%3C/text%3E%3C/svg%3E

Salary sacrifice in the UK lets you pay into a pension before income tax and National Insurance are taken, so the same contribution costs you less. In 2026/27 a basic rate taxpayer saves an extra 8% in National Insurance on every pound sacrificed. From 6 April 2029 that saving is capped at the first £2,000 a year, so here is how it works, the four traps, and what the cap means for you.

The short version

  • You agree to a lower salary, and your employer pays the difference into your pension. It comes off before income tax and National Insurance, so the same contribution costs you less take-home.
  • The National Insurance saving in 2026/27 is 8% for basic rate pay and 2% above £50,270. On £200 a month that is £192 a year for a basic rate taxpayer.
  • It is most valuable near a threshold, because it lowers the income assessed for tax. It can pull you back under £50,270 or £100,000, both frozen until 5 April 2031.
  • Your employer saves 15% National Insurance too, and some pass part of it into your pension. Always ask.
  • From 6 April 2029, only the first £2,000 a year of pension salary sacrifice stays free of National Insurance. Income tax relief is unchanged.
  • Four things to check first: mortgage affordability, statutory pay, the minimum wage floor of £12.71 an hour, and salary-linked benefits.

1. How does salary sacrifice work?

Normally your pension contribution comes out of pay that has already been taxed. Or tax relief is added back afterwards. Either way, National Insurance has usually already gone.

With salary sacrifice, you formally give up part of your contractual salary. Your employer pays that amount straight into your pension instead.

Because your gross salary is genuinely lower, both income tax and National Insurance are worked out on the smaller figure. The pension gets the same amount. Your take-home falls by less than the contribution.

That gap is the National Insurance you no longer pay, and it is the whole point. In 2026/27 employee National Insurance is 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270, and 2% above that.

It also lowers the headline salary on your payslip. That matters more now that pay bands are expected in job adverts.

It is not a loophole. It is a formal change to your employment contract that the tax system deliberately treats differently, and most large employers already have the scheme set up.

Worked example: Priya in Reading, £45,000

Priya earns £45,000 and wants to put an extra £200 a month into her pension. That is £2,400 a year. She is a basic rate taxpayer.

Without salary sacrifice: she pays £160 from her net pay. The pension scheme claims £40 of basic rate relief, so £200 goes in. Her take-home falls by £160 a month.

With salary sacrifice: her contractual salary drops by £200 a month. She saves £40 income tax (20%) and £16 National Insurance (8%). Her take-home falls by £144 a month, and the pension still gets £200.

Same pension. £16 a month smaller hit to her wages. Over a year that is £192. If her employer also passes on its own 15% saving of £30 a month, the pension gets £230 a month instead of £200.

The effect is bigger for anyone whose income sits just above £50,270 or £100,000, because the sacrifice can pull assessed income back below the line. The sharpest case is the High Income Child Benefit Charge, where a pension contribution can pull adjusted net income back under the threshold. We work that arithmetic through here. If you have a teenager who has just started sixth form, check that the claim did not quietly stop on 31 August.

2. Who does it suit, and who not?

It suits you if you are employed, your employer offers it, and you can manage on slightly less take-home. It suits you most if you are just above £50,270 or close to £100,000.

Both lines are frozen until 5 April 2031, so more people drift over them each year. Our guide to frozen tax thresholds explains why those two numbers matter so much.

It does not suit you if you are close to the National Minimum Wage, since sacrifice cannot take you below it. Or if you are applying for a mortgage soon. Or if you need every pound of take-home now, because pension money is locked until pension age.

It is not available if you are self-employed. There is no salary to sacrifice. Your pension contributions still get tax relief, but through a different route.

Higher rate taxpayers on a personal contribution must often claim the extra relief themselves. How to claim higher rate pension relief covers that. Salary sacrifice avoids the claim, because the relief is built in.

Providers are also allowed to say more than they used to. What your pension provider can now tell you changed under the FCA targeted support rules.

3. What should you check before you sign?

  1. Mortgage affordability. Lenders may assess your reduced contractual salary. If you are buying or remortgaging within a year, ask a broker first. A lower gross salary can cut how much you can borrow.
  2. Statutory pay. HMRC says salary sacrifice can reduce statutory pay, such as maternity or sick pay, and can end the entitlement if your average weekly earnings fall below the lower earnings limit. If any of those are foreseeable, get your employer's approach in writing.
  3. The minimum wage floor. HMRC is clear that sacrifice must not take your cash pay below the National Minimum Wage. The National Living Wage is £12.71 an hour for workers aged 21 and over from April 2026, rising to £13.21 from April 2027. Your payslip should show you are above it.
  4. Salary-linked benefits. Life cover, income protection and bonuses are sometimes based on the reduced salary. Good schemes use "notional" or pre-sacrifice salary. Ask which yours uses.

4. What is the 2029 salary sacrifice pension cap?

The government announced it at the Budget on 26 November 2025. From 6 April 2029, only the first £2,000 a year of pension contributions made through salary sacrifice will stay free of National Insurance.

Anything above £2,000 in a tax year will attract Class 1 National Insurance for both you and your employer. Income tax relief on the contributions is unchanged. Ordinary employer pension contributions, outside salary sacrifice, stay free of National Insurance.

GOV.UK still sets the plan out in these terms, and the coverage of the 28 October 2026 Budget we checked reports it kept, not changed. HMRC has said more guidance will follow before April 2029.

HMRC's own estimate is that the average extra employee National Insurance for people affected will be £84 in 2029/30, the first year.

What it means for Priya. Her £2,400 a year is £400 over the cap. At today's rates she would pay 8% on that £400, so £32 a year. Her employer would pay 15%, so £60. Her income tax saving does not change.

What it means for larger amounts. The cap narrows the benefit, but it does not abolish salary sacrifice. The income tax advantage stays in full, and so does the threshold effect at £50,270 and £100,000.

It is about two and a half years away, and policy can change again. It is not a reason to avoid setting this up now if it suits you today. A separate pension change lands sooner: unused pensions join the estate for inheritance tax from 6 April 2027.

5. What else can you salary sacrifice?

Pensions are where the numbers are largest, but they are not the only option. HMRC lists pension payments, employer pensions advice, workplace nurseries, cycle to work and some older childcare voucher schemes as benefits that keep their tax treatment under salary sacrifice.

The best known example outside pensions is a bike. Cycle to work has no HMRC cap on the value of the bike, whatever limit your employer scheme sets itself.

Salary sacrifice electric car schemes work differently. You still pay benefit in kind tax on the car, but at a low rate for a zero emission model.

Those rates rise by 2 points a year from 2028/29, so the deal gets less generous each year. Weigh the running costs too: electric against petrol cost per mile and the pay per mile charge from April 2028 both change the sums.

If a company car is part of your package, the way the tax is collected changes on 6 April 2027. Cars, vans, fuel and medical benefit move onto the payslip in real time.

Payroll giving runs on the same before-tax principle. We compare it with the alternative in payroll giving against Gift Aid on the same donation.

6. How do you ask your employer?

Most people never ask, because they assume it is complicated or not on offer. Two sentences to HR or payroll usually settles it:

"Do we operate salary sacrifice for pension contributions, and if so does the company pass on any of its National Insurance saving into the employee's pension?"

The second half is the part worth asking. Some employers keep the whole saving, some share it, and a few pass on all of it. It costs nothing to find out, and the answer can be worth hundreds a year.

Also ask whether the scheme uses notional salary for benefits and statutory pay. Ask how often you can change your contribution. And ask how the scheme will handle the £2,000 cap from April 2029.

7. What is it worth, in pounds?

For a basic rate taxpayer, salary sacrifice is worth about 8% of whatever you sacrifice. For most higher rate taxpayers it is worth 2%. That is the whole National Insurance advantage.

The large numbers people quote come from somewhere else, which is the thresholds. A salary sacrifice pension calculator will show the same pattern as the table below.

Take the same £2,400 a year, £200 a month, into the pension. The comparison is with a personal contribution under relief at source, which is how most workplace pensions handle it. Higher rate relief is assumed to be claimed in full.

Your salaryCost in take-home without sacrificeCost in take-home with sacrificeYou keep
£35,000£1,920£1,728£192
£45,000£1,920£1,728£192
£60,000£1,440£1,392£48
£104,000£960£912£48

Read the last two rows and the case looks thin. £48 a year is not a reason to vary your employment contract. Then add the two things the table leaves out.

First, the employer saving. Employer National Insurance is 15% above £5,000 a year. On a £2,400 sacrifice the employer saves £360 and pays nothing.

That is nearly twice the basic rate employee's saving, and seven and a half times the higher rate one. If your employer passes half of it into the pension, the deal is worth £372 a year, not £192. If it passes all of it, £552. That is why the question in section 6 matters more than the mechanics.

Worked example: Daniel in Stevenage, £104,000

Second, the thresholds. Daniel earns £104,000. Above £100,000 of adjusted net income, the Personal Allowance falls by £1 for every £2 over, reaching zero at £125,140.

So his marginal rate on that top £4,000 is 60% in income tax, plus 2% in National Insurance.

He sacrifices £4,000, which brings his assessed income to exactly £100,000. Income tax saved is 40% of the £4,000, which is £1,600. He also gets back £2,000 of Personal Allowance, worth 40%, so £800. That is £2,400 in total.

National Insurance saved is 2% of £4,000, which is £80. So £4,000 lands in his pension and his take-home falls by £1,520. Check the sum: 4,000 less 2,400 less 80 is 1,520.

From April 2029, £2,000 of his sacrifice is over the cap. At today's rates he would lose £40 of the National Insurance saving, and his employer £300. The £2,400 tax saving is untouched, so the cap barely dents his case.

And that may not be the biggest number for him. Tax-Free Childcare and the funded childcare hours in England both have an income limit that works as a cliff, not a taper. A household just over the line can lose the lot, while one just under keeps all of it.

If children in nursery are part of your picture, check Tax-Free Childcare against the funded hours before you decide. For some households that single effect is worth more than the tax and National Insurance put together.

Who this does not help. If you earn £35,000 and are nowhere near a threshold, the whole thing is worth £192 a year unless your employer shares its saving. That is real money and costs nothing to set up, but it is not a financial event.

If you are within a year of a mortgage application, the lower contractual salary can cost you more in borrowing power than the £192 is worth. And if you are paid at or near the National Living Wage of £12.71 an hour, sacrifice cannot take you below the floor. The honest answer may be that there is no meaningful amount available to you.

Frequently asked

Is salary sacrifice worth it? Usually yes, if your employer offers it and you can manage on a little less take-home. A basic rate taxpayer saves 8% National Insurance on top of tax relief, so £192 a year on £200 a month. It is worth much more near £50,270 or £100,000, or if your employer shares its own 15% saving.

What is the salary sacrifice pension change in 2029? From 6 April 2029, only the first £2,000 a year of pension contributions made through salary sacrifice will be free of National Insurance. Above that, both you and your employer pay Class 1 National Insurance in the usual way. Income tax relief on the contributions is not affected, and ordinary employer contributions stay free of National Insurance.

Does salary sacrifice reduce my State Pension? For most people, no. Your State Pension depends on how many qualifying years are on your National Insurance record, not the amount paid. It only becomes a concern if sacrifice takes your earnings below the lower earnings limit, so that the year might not count. Check your record on GOV.UK if your pay is low.

Can salary sacrifice take me below the minimum wage? No. HMRC says a salary sacrifice arrangement must not reduce your cash pay below the National Minimum Wage. From April 2026 the National Living Wage is £12.71 an hour for workers aged 21 and over, rising to £13.21 in April 2027. Employers must cap deductions so pay stays above the floor.

Can I change my mind? Usually yes, but only at set points, often once a year or after a life event such as a birth or a house move. That is because it is a formal change to your contract, not a payroll setting. Ask how often you can change it before you sign, and get the answer in writing.

Is it legal? Yes. It is a long-established arrangement that HMRC recognises, as long as the contract is genuinely varied.

The honest verdict

If you are employed, your employer offers it, and you can live on slightly less take-home, salary sacrifice is one of the few genuinely free improvements on a payslip. The same pension contribution simply costs you less.

Check the four traps first, especially if a mortgage or parental leave is coming. Ask whether the employer shares its National Insurance saving, because that question alone can be worth a few hundred pounds a year.

The 2029 cap trims the National Insurance saving on amounts over £2,000, but leaves the tax relief and the threshold effects alone. Set it up, then forget it. It is dull, and dull is usually where the reliable money is.
