Affiliate Marketing in the UK for Beginners: What It Really Pays in 2026

Published 21 July 2026 · Updated 14 November 2026 · 14 min read · Figures checked November 2026

Affiliate marketing in the UK for beginners is simple to start and slow to pay. You earn a commission when a reader buys through your link, and Amazon UK pays between 0% and 6% depending on the category. Most first years earn very little, so it suits people with two years to give and another income meanwhile.

The short version
  • UK brands spent £1.8bn on affiliate and partner marketing in 2025, and it drove £20.7bn in revenue, about 15 times the spend, according to the APMA. The channel is real. Your share of it is not guaranteed.
  • Amazon Associates UK pays 0% to 6% by category. Most physical goods sit at 3% to 5%. Video games, consoles and grocery are 1%. Fashion and luxury are the top rate at 6%.
  • The Amazon link only counts for 24 hours after the click, and a new account needs three qualifying sales within 180 days or the application can be withdrawn.
  • You must label it. The ASA says a generic "may earn commission" line is ambiguous, and the label must be clear before the reader engages, not buried at the bottom.
  • Over the £1,000 trading allowance, register for Self Assessment by 5 October after the tax year ends.

1. How does affiliate marketing actually pay you?

You send someone to a retailer with a tracked link. They buy. The retailer pays you a percentage or a flat fee.

That is the whole model. Its simplicity is exactly why it attracts so much nonsense.

Three details matter more than the headline commission rate.

The cookie window. Amazon UK counts a sale only within 24 hours of the click. If the reader adds the item to the basket in that window, it still counts when they buy within 89 days. Most networks give much longer windows, often around a month.

That difference is enormous. On Amazon, a reader who thinks it over for four days without adding to the basket earns you nothing. The detail is worth reading once, because it decides which reviews are worth writing: what an affiliate cookie window actually measures.

Returns and cancellations. Commission is reversed when a customer returns the item. Fashion sees the most returns, so its affiliates lose the most to clawbacks.

Your dashboard number in week one is not your payment in week eight.

Payment terms. Networks pay after a sale validates, which can be weeks or months after the purchase. A January sale can land in your bank in spring. The detail varies by network, and how affiliate network payment terms work in the UK sets out what to expect.

Plan cash flow accordingly. For a beginner that mostly means do not plan any.

3. How much traffic do you need to earn anything?

Here is the sum that gets left out of every course sales page.

Assume a strong buying guide turns about 2% of its readers into a purchase. That is an assumption for the sum, not a promise. Informational content converts far lower.

Say your review pulls 1,000 visits a month. At 2% that is 20 sales. At an average order of £60 and Amazon's 4% beauty or sports rate, that is £48 a month.

In the 3% "all other categories" band the same page earns £36. That is from an article that may take you six hours to write and many months to rank.

Ranking at all is getting harder for this kind of page. The December 2026 fair ranking rule for affiliate sites is the change to read before planning a year of them. Google's AI answers are the other pressure, covered in where affiliate traffic still comes from under AI Overviews.

Affiliate commission is not the only way to earn from an audience. What a newsletter sponsorship slot actually sells for is worth knowing early.

None of this is a reason to quit. It is the reason the model only works at scale, or on high value products, or both.

The other lever is what else that same traffic could earn. That is the comparison in affiliate commission against display ads on the same pages.

You need either fifty articles like that, or pages selling something with a large flat fee. Scale has a number attached to it, and how many articles a site really needs before it earns is larger than almost every course admits.

Your own site is a cost before it is ever a commission. What UK hosting really costs a beginner in year one and again at renewal is the bill to size up before writing fifty articles.

5. How do you start affiliate marketing in the UK?

The steps are short. The patience is the hard part.

  1. Pick one topic you know properly. You need things you have used, measured and can photograph. How to pick an affiliate niche in the UK walks through the choice.
  2. Get a site you own. A domain and a basic host are enough. Do not buy themes, courses or tools yet.
  3. Write ten genuinely useful pages first. Buying guides and comparisons with your own photos and an actual opinion.
  4. Apply to programmes once there is real content. Amazon then needs three qualifying sales within 180 days.
  5. Label every affiliate link properly from day one. It is easier than fixing a hundred pages later.
  6. Track your income and costs from the first sale. HMRC will want the record once you pass £1,000.

Expect the first sale to take longer than you hope. How long the first affiliate sale really takes sets honest expectations.

6. Which affiliate programmes suit beginners in the UK?

  • Amazon Associates UK. Easy to join, converts well, pays little, 24 hour cookie. Once you apply you have 180 days to refer three qualifying sales, or the application can be withdrawn. Amazon says it cannot reinstate a rejected account or ID.
  • Awin. One of the biggest networks in the UK. Thousands of British retailers, longer cookies and proper reporting. Some merchants approve instantly, most review your site. Awin asks for a small refundable deposit to verify your bank details, returned once you reach your first payment.
  • Impact and Partnerize. Where a lot of larger brands and software companies now sit.
  • Direct programmes. Many hosting companies, software firms and UK retailers run their own. Rates are often better because there is no middle layer.

The trade offs between the two biggest are set out in Amazon Associates against Awin for a UK site.

Apply once you have a genuine site with real content. Applying with three thin posts and no traffic is how you collect rejections.

7. What are the UK disclosure rules?

This is the part beginners get wrong, and it is not optional.

The ASA enforces the CAP Code, and the CMA enforces consumer protection law. Both treat an affiliate link as advertising.

The ASA's guidance on affiliate marketing says the content must be obviously identifiable as advertising before the reader engages with it. A disclaimer at the bottom of a post is unlikely to be enough.

In practice:

  • Where a whole post is about affiliate products, the ASA suggests a clear label such as "Ad" in the title.
  • Where only some links are affiliate links, mark those parts, for example with "(Ad)" beside them.
  • The ASA has ruled that a generic "may earn commission" disclaimer is ambiguous. It also rejected "*affiliate" and "#collab" as labels people may not understand.
  • On social, put "Ad" at the start of the post, in the first frame of a video, or say it at the beginning. Not the eleventh hashtag.
  • Amazon also requires the exact statement "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases" in its operating agreement.

The full detail, with examples, is in the UK affiliate disclosure rules explained.

The ASA cannot fine you directly. It names non compliant advertisers publicly and refers persistent offenders on.

The CMA is a different matter. Since 6 April 2025, under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, it can fine businesses directly for breaking consumer law, up to 10% of global turnover.

For a small site, the practical risk is also Google. Hidden or thin affiliate pages lose rankings, and that costs far more than a label ever would.

8. Do you pay tax on affiliate income?

Affiliate commission is trading income. It is taxable.

  • There is a £1,000 trading allowance. If your gross trading income in a tax year is under that, from all self employed sources combined, you generally do not need to declare it.
  • Over £1,000 you must register for Self Assessment by 5 October in the following tax year, then file a return.
  • The cash basis is now the standard way for sole traders to keep records. You record income when the money arrives, not when the sale happens. A March sale paid in May falls in the next tax year.
  • Payments in foreign currency need converting to pounds. Keep the record of the rate you used.
  • You can deduct genuine costs: hosting, domain, themes, stock images, a fair share of broadband. Not your whole laptop if you also use it for Netflix.

The step by step is in how to declare affiliate income to HMRC, and the wider picture is in the UK side hustle tax rules.

This is general information rather than tax advice. If the numbers get significant, an accountant costs less than getting it wrong.

9. Which mistakes kill beginner sites?

  1. Reviewing things you have never touched. Google's reviews guidance asks for evidence of first hand use: your own photos, measurements and comparisons. Rewriting the manufacturer blurb is the most common reason a review site never ranks.
  2. Chasing the highest commission instead of the best fit. A coffee blog pushing web hosting converts at nothing and damages trust.
  3. One network, one merchant. Amazon has cut its rates before. Build on a single programme and you have handed someone else the switch.
  4. Writing for the keyword rather than the buyer. A "best budget X UK 2026" page with no actual recommendation is a page nobody links to and nobody buys from.
  5. Giving up at month six. Month six is when it looks pointless. It is also roughly when a patient site starts to move.

10. Which myths cost people money?

"Affiliate marketing is passive income." It becomes semi passive after a couple of years and a lot of unpaid work. In year one it is a job you pay to do.

The word passive is doing enormous marketing work in that sentence.

"The average UK affiliate earns a full time salary." Salary style figures come from surveys of people who already call themselves affiliate marketers. That leaves out everyone who tried and earned nothing.

That is survivorship bias. Many beginners earn little or nothing in year one.

"You need a course." Most of what a course costing hundreds of pounds teaches is in the Awin and Google documentation for free.

What courses genuinely sell is structure and accountability. Decide whether that is worth the price to you, but do not confuse it with secret information.

The honest verdict

This helps you if you already write about something you know properly, you can commit two years, and you have income from elsewhere in the meantime. It is a genuinely good long term asset. UK brands put £1.8bn into this channel in 2025, and they need publishers.

This does not help you if you need money in the next six months, you do not enjoy writing, or you plan to build it on Amazon commissions alone. In those cases you could spend 400 hours to earn less than a fortnight of part time work would pay.

Nothing to buy here. Start on the cheapest hosting plan and a domain, and do not spend money on tools until something is earning. What an affiliate site really costs to run lists the bills worth paying.

Frequently asked

How long does affiliate marketing take to make money in the UK? Realistically, several months for the first commission and one to two years before it is meaningful. A new site has to earn Google's trust, and buying guides take time to rank. Anyone promising faster is usually selling a course. Treat year one as building the asset, and keep another income while it grows.

Can a beginner do affiliate marketing with no money? Almost. Joining Amazon Associates is free, and Awin asks only for a small refundable deposit. You can post on YouTube, Pinterest or a newsletter at no cost. A site you own needs a domain and basic hosting, which is the one bill worth paying. Do not buy courses or tools before you earn.

Do I need a limited company for affiliate income? No. Most beginners start as a sole trader, and under £1,000 a year the trading allowance means you may not need to tell HMRC at all. A company brings accounts, filing and fees. It is worth a look only once profits get much larger, ideally with an accountant's view first.

How much does Amazon pay affiliates in the UK? Amazon's UK rate card runs from 0% to 6%. Fashion, shoes, watches and luxury pay 6%, books and home goods 5%, beauty and sports 4%, and most other items 3%. Video games, consoles and grocery pay 1%. The cookie lasts 24 hours from the click, so timing matters.

Is affiliate marketing still worth it in 2026 with AI search? For generic information pages, less than before, because AI answers now cover many questions. Pages with first hand testing, real photographs and a clear opinion hold up better, because a model cannot invent the experience of using the thing. Build those, and build a name people search for.
