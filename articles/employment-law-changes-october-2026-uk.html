Two of the October Employment Law Changes You Have Read About Are Not Actually Happening

Ruhul Amin · Published 25 August 2026 · 6 min read · Figures checked August 2026

A large slice of the Employment Rights Act 2025 lands on 1 and 30 October 2026. But the tipping code was quietly withdrawn, and the tribunal deadline change is far narrower than the headlines suggest. Here is what is confirmed, what is not, and the single date that decides whether you get unfair dismissal protection in January.

The short version

  • Tribunal time limits do rise from three months to six on 1 October 2026, but only for a narrow list of claims. Breach of employment contract damages in England and Wales, part time and fixed term less favourable treatment, and Information and Consultation Regulations breaches. Unfair dismissal and discrimination are not in the confirmed October list.
  • The new tipping code was withdrawn by the government without explanation. It was due on 1 October 2026. The revised timetable now says tipping changes land "by the end of 2026", with no fixed date.
  • 30 October 2026 is the bigger date. Employers must take "all" reasonable steps to prevent sexual harassment, third party harassment protection returns, and you gain a right to be told you can join a union.
  • 1 January 2027 is when the money changes. The unfair dismissal qualifying period drops from two years to six months, and the compensation cap of £123,543 disappears entirely.
  • If you started your job on or after roughly 1 July 2026, you hit six months' service in time to be protected from 1 January 2027. Start much later and you wait.

1. The tribunal deadline change that is smaller than reported

The Employment Rights Act 2025 received Royal Assent on 18 December 2025 and is being switched on in stages. One of the most repeated lines in coverage is that from October 2026 you get six months instead of three to bring an employment tribunal claim.

That is true, but only for a short list. The draft regulations laid before Parliament extend the limit from three months to six months on 1 October 2026 for these claims only:

  • Damages for breach of a contract of employment, in England and Wales
  • Less favourable treatment of part time workers and fixed term employees
  • Breaches of the Information and Consultation of Employees Regulations

Scotland gets the breach of contract extension slightly later, on 9 November 2026.

Unfair dismissal and discrimination, the two claims most people actually bring, are not on that confirmed October list. A wider rollout is widely expected, and lawyers reading the piecemeal nature of these regulations think it is likely. But expected is not the same as in force.

If you are dismissed in October and you assume you now have six months, you may be assuming your way past a deadline that is still three months. Check the specific claim, not the headline.

The practical rule has not changed: for unfair dismissal and discrimination, work to three months less one day from the dismissal or the act complained of, and start early conciliation with Acas well before that. Treat any extra time as a bonus you have confirmed, not one you have read about.

2. The tipping code that vanished

If you work in hospitality, you will have seen headlines promising new tipping rules from 1 October 2026. The core protections are already law and have been since 1 October 2024. Employers must pass on 100% of tips with no deductions except those required by law such as PAYE and National Insurance, and must pay them out no later than the end of the month following the month the customer left the tip. The detail of who is owed what, and by when, is set out in the tipping rules as they stand in October 2026.

What was supposed to arrive this October was an updated statutory Code of Practice, plus a new duty on employers to consult workers before introducing or changing a written tipping policy, and to review that policy at least every three years.

The government withdrew the draft code without comment. Unite had called it flawed, arguing it required consultation but still left the final allocation decision with management. UKHospitality asked for urgent clarification. The 16 July 2026 timetable update now says tipping changes will take effect "by the end of 2026", which is not a date.

So the honest position for a waiter or bar worker reading this: nothing new lands for you on 1 October. Your existing right to 100% of tips is unchanged and already enforceable. If your employer is deducting from tips, that is already unlawful and you can raise it now.

3. What genuinely arrives on 30 October 2026

This is the substantial date, and it gets far less coverage than the tribunal story.

  • Sexual harassment. The duty on employers moves from taking "reasonable steps" to taking all reasonable steps to prevent it. That is a meaningfully higher bar.
  • Third party harassment. Employers become liable for failing to protect staff from harassment by customers, clients and contractors. For anyone in retail, hospitality or care, this is the change that matters most.
  • Union rights. Employers must tell workers they have the right to join a trade union. Union access rights and the recognition process are both strengthened.
  • Industrial action. Protection against detriment for taking industrial action is extended.
  • Sector pay. Adult social care gets a Fair Pay Agreement negotiating body in England, and public procurement gets a two tier code.

Note also that the Fair Work Agency was established on 7 April 2026 under chair Matthew Taylor CBE, but it has not yet been given a date to start exercising enforcement powers. A regulator that cannot enforce yet is worth knowing about before you rely on it.

4. January 2027, and the start date that decides everything

The change with real money attached is on 1 January 2027. Two things happen at once.

First, the qualifying period for ordinary unfair dismissal drops from two years to six months. Second, the cap on the compensatory award is removed. For dismissals from 6 April 2026 that cap is currently £123,543 or 52 weeks' gross pay, whichever is lower, up from £118,223 the year before. From 1 January 2027 there is no cap at all, and the regulations are clear that only employees whose actual termination date falls on or after that day get the uncapped regime.

Here is the part almost nobody spells out. The six month rule applies to people already employed on 1 January 2027, counting their existing service. So anyone who started a job from around 1 July 2026 onwards reaches six months' service by 1 January 2027 and is protected from day one of the new regime. Start in November 2026 and you are waiting until May 2027.

If you are weighing up a move, that is a real factor and one worth putting alongside the usual salary maths in our guide to switching careers in the UK.

Worked example: Nadia, hired in July

Nadia started a new operations role on 13 July 2026 on £38,000. Under today's rules she would need until 13 July 2028 to gain ordinary unfair dismissal protection.

Under the new rules, she has six months' service on 13 January 2027. She is not protected on 1 January, but she gets there twelve days later, instead of eighteen months later. That is roughly a year and a half of exposure removed.

Her colleague Tom started on 2 November 2026. Same employer, same job. He does not reach six months until 2 May 2027. Three and a half months of difference in start date, four months of difference in protection.

The cap point. On £38,000, 52 weeks' gross pay is well under £123,543, so the cap removal changes nothing for Nadia. It matters to high earners, where 52 weeks' pay exceeds the cap. Be honest about which group you are in before getting excited about uncapped awards.

Frequently asked

Do I now have six months to claim unfair dismissal? Not on the confirmed October regulations. Work to three months less one day and contact Acas early conciliation well inside that. If a wider extension is confirmed later, you lose nothing by having been early.

When do the zero hours guaranteed hours rules start? During 2027, not October 2026. The government consultation on how they will work closed on 25 August 2026. The current preference is a 12 week reference period, shift notice of somewhere between one and four weeks, and enforcement by the Fair Work Agency. The rules will cover agency workers too.

What is the minimum wage now? From 1 April 2026 it is £12.71 an hour for age 21 and over, £10.85 for 18 to 20, and £8.00 for 16 to 17 and apprentices. The Low Pay Commission's central estimate for April 2027 is £13.18, within a range of £13.02 to £13.34. That is a projection, not a decision.

Has statutory sick pay changed? Yes. From 6 April 2026 the lower earnings limit and the three waiting days were removed, so SSP is payable from day one regardless of how little you earn. The rate is £123.25 a week, or 80% of your pay if that is lower.

The honest verdict

Most of what you will read about "the October employment law changes" is written for employers, and two of the most repeated claims do not survive checking. The tribunal extension on 1 October is real but narrow. The tipping code was pulled and has no replacement date.

The changes that will actually affect an ordinary worker's money and security are the third party harassment protection on 30 October 2026, and the drop to a six month unfair dismissal qualifying period on 1 January 2027.

There is nothing to buy here and no product that helps. The only genuinely useful action is to know your own start date, and to keep working to the three month tribunal deadline until a wider extension is actually confirmed.
