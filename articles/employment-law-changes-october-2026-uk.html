Employment law changes in October 2026: what actually changed, and what moved to January

Ruhul Amin · Published 25 August 2026 · Updated 9 November 2026 · 8 min read · Figures checked November 2026

The employment law changes of October 2026 did happen: most tribunal claims now have six months instead of three, and tougher harassment duties began on 30 October. The tipping changes did not arrive and are now listed for 1 January 2027. Here is what is in force, what is still coming, and the start date that decides your unfair dismissal rights.

The short version

  • Most tribunal claims now have six months, not three. From 1 October 2026 the time limit doubled for claims where the relevant date is on or after that day. That includes unfair dismissal and discrimination. Scotland's breach of contract claims followed on 9 November 2026.
  • A correction. When we first wrote this in August, the draft list we had seen left out unfair dismissal and discrimination. The final regulations, SI 2026/954, cover them. We were wrong on that point and have fixed it.
  • Nothing new arrived on tipping. The revised tipping code was withdrawn from Parliament on 29 July 2026 and sent back to consultation. The government timeline now lists stronger tipping law for 1 January 2027.
  • 30 October 2026 brought the harassment changes. Employers must now take "all reasonable steps" to prevent sexual harassment, and must not permit third parties to harass their staff. Union access and recognition rules also changed.
  • 1 January 2027 is when the money changes. The unfair dismissal qualifying period drops from two years to six months, and the compensation cap of £123,543 goes. The duty to tell workers they can join a union also moved to that date.
  • If you started your job on or before about 1 July 2026, you have six months' service by 1 January 2027. Start later and you wait until your own six month point.

1. Which employment law changes happened in October 2026?

The employment law changes of October 2026 came in two waves, on 1 October and 30 October. Both were steps in switching on the Employment Rights Act 2025, which received Royal Assent on 18 December 2025.

The government's own implementation timeline, last updated on 5 November 2026, now lists them as done. Here is what it says took effect, and what it says is still to come.

2. Do I now have six months to bring a tribunal claim?

For most claims, yes. This is the change that turned out wider than early reports suggested, including ours.

The Employment Rights Act 2025 (Commencement No. 5 and Transitional Provisions) (Amendment) Regulations 2026, SI 2026/954, were made on 1 September 2026. They brought section 152 and Schedule 12 of the Act into force on 1 October 2026.

Section 152 is headed "Increase in time limits for making claims". The schedules in SI 2026/954 list unfair dismissal under the Employment Rights Act 1996 and complaints under section 120 of the Equality Act 2010, the main discrimination route.

Breach of contract claims needed a separate order. The Employment Tribunals Extension of Jurisdiction (England and Wales) (Amendment) Order 2026, SI 2026/759, changed "three" to "six" from 1 October 2026 in England and Wales.

Scotland's breach of contract claims moved on 9 November 2026. Acas confirms that date.

The catch is the start date. SI 2026/954 says the longer limit does not apply where the relevant date is before 1 October 2026. For unfair dismissal, the relevant date is the effective date of termination.

If you were dismissed on 30 September 2026, you still have three months less one day. One day earlier, half the time.

Acas gives two clean examples. Paid the wrong amount on 1 October 2026, and your deadline is 11:59pm on 31 March 2027. Paid wrongly on 30 September 2026, and it is 11:59pm on 29 December 2026.

The practical rule: find the date of the act you are complaining about, then check it against the Acas page. Start early conciliation well inside the limit. Acas says notifying it pauses the clock, but only if you notify within your time limit.

We have a fuller walk through of the deadline rules in our guide to the six month tribunal time limit from 1 October 2026.

3. What happened to the new tipping rules?

Nothing new landed in October. If you work in hospitality, that is the honest answer.

The core protections have been law since 1 October 2024 under the Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023. Employers must pass on all tips with no deductions except those required by law, such as tax.

They must pay them out no later than the end of the month after the month the customer left the tip. The detail of who is owed what is set out in the tipping rules as they stand in October 2026.

What was meant to follow was a revised statutory code of practice. It would add a duty on employers to consult workers before introducing or changing a written tipping policy.

GOV.UK shows that the revised code was laid before Parliament and then withdrawn on 29 July 2026, so that a new public consultation could be held. That consultation ran from 19 August to 29 September 2026. The government says it is analysing the feedback.

The 5 November 2026 timeline now lists "strengthening tipping law" for 1 January 2027. Until then, GOV.UK tells employers to keep following the existing code.

So for a waiter or bar worker: your right to all of your tips is unchanged and already enforceable. If your employer is deducting from tips, that is already unlawful and you can raise it now.

4. What arrived on 30 October 2026?

This was the substantial date, and it got far less coverage than the tribunal story. According to the government timeline, these took effect:

  • Sexual harassment. The duty on employers moved from taking reasonable steps to taking all reasonable steps to prevent it. That is a meaningfully higher bar.
  • Third party harassment. Employers must not permit third parties, such as customers, clients and contractors, to harass their employees. For anyone in retail, hospitality or care, this is the change that matters most.
  • A power for ministers to set out later the evidence based steps employers must take against sexual harassment.
  • Union rights. A stronger right of access for unions, reforms to recognition and derecognition, and new rights for union representatives.
  • Industrial action. Protection against detriment for taking industrial action was extended.

One item many guides placed on 30 October did not arrive then. The duty on employers to tell workers they have the right to join a trade union is now listed for 1 January 2027.

The Adult Social Care Negotiating Body for England is listed for "by January 2027", not October.

The Fair Work Agency, the new enforcement body chaired by Matthew Taylor, was set up on 7 April 2026. The timeline lists its establishment but gives no separate dates for each enforcement power, so check before relying on it for a specific complaint.

5. What changes on 1 January 2027, and does my start date matter?

The change with real money attached is on 1 January 2027. Two things happen at once.

First, the qualifying period for ordinary unfair dismissal drops from two years to six months, for dismissals from that date.

Second, the cap on the compensatory award is removed. For dismissals from 6 April 2026 that cap is £123,543 or 52 weeks' gross pay, whichever is lower, up from £118,223 the year before. That figure is set by SI 2026/310. From 1 January 2027 the timeline says awards will be uncapped.

Here is the part almost nobody spells out. The six month rule counts the service you already have. So anyone who started a job on or before about 1 July 2026 has six months' service by 1 January 2027 and is covered from the first day of the new regime.

Start after that and you are covered at your own six month point. Start in November 2026 and you wait until May 2027.

If you are weighing up a move, that is a real factor. Put it alongside the usual salary maths in our guide to switching careers in the UK, and the cap detail in employment tribunal compensation limits.

Worked example: Nadia, hired in July

Nadia started a new operations role on 13 July 2026 on £38,000. Under the old rules she would need until 13 July 2028 to gain ordinary unfair dismissal protection.

Under the new rules, she has six months' service on 13 January 2027. She is not protected on 1 January, but she gets there twelve days later, instead of eighteen months later. That is roughly a year and a half of exposure removed.

Her colleague Tom started on 2 November 2026. Same employer, same job. He does not reach six months until 2 May 2027. Their start dates are about three and a half months apart, and so are the dates they become protected.

The cap point. On £38,000, 52 weeks' gross pay is £38,000, well under £123,543. So the cap removal changes nothing for Nadia. It matters to high earners, where 52 weeks' pay goes above the cap. Be honest about which group you are in before getting excited about uncapped awards.

The deadline point. If Nadia were dismissed in February 2027, she would have six months less one day to claim, not three. That alone gives her far more room to try conciliation first.

The honest verdict

Most of what you will read about "the October employment law changes" is written for employers. When we first wrote this, we said two headlines were wrong. One of them was. The tipping change did not arrive in October and is now listed for January 2027.

The other, the tribunal time limit, turned out better for workers than the draft we had read. Most claims, including unfair dismissal and discrimination, now get six months where the problem happened on or after 1 October 2026.

The changes that will affect an ordinary worker's money and security most are the third party harassment protection from 30 October 2026, and the six month unfair dismissal qualifying period from 1 January 2027.

There is nothing to buy here and no product that helps. The useful actions are free: know your own start date, and know the date of anything you might need to complain about.

Frequently asked

Do I now have six months to claim unfair dismissal? Yes, if your effective date of termination was on or after 1 October 2026. SI 2026/954 brought the six month limit into force that day and lists unfair dismissal. If you were dismissed before 1 October 2026, you still have three months less one day. Notify Acas early, because conciliation only pauses the clock inside the limit.

When do the zero hours guaranteed hours rules start? There is no date yet. The government consultation on how guaranteed hours, shift notice and payment for cancelled shifts will work ran from 2 June to 25 August 2026. GOV.UK says it is analysing the responses. The implementation timeline says the timings will be updated after the consultation, so nothing changed in October.

What is the minimum wage now? From 1 April 2026 it is £12.71 an hour for age 21 and over, £10.85 for 18 to 20, and £8.00 for under 18s and apprentices. GOV.UK now also lists the April 2027 rates: £13.21 for 21 and over, £11.27 for 18 to 20, and £8.31 for under 18s and apprentices.

Has statutory sick pay changed? Yes. From 6 April 2026 the lower earnings limit and the waiting period were removed, according to the government timeline. GOV.UK gives the rate as £123.25 a week or 80% of average weekly earnings, whichever is lower. It is paid for up to 28 weeks, so low earners now get something from the start.

Did the October 2026 changes apply in Scotland and Wales? The Employment Rights Act changes apply across Great Britain. The one difference in October was timing for breach of contract claims. England and Wales moved to six months on 1 October 2026 under SI 2026/759. Scotland, which has its own order, moved on 9 November 2026, as Acas and the government timeline both confirm.
