Christmas Bank Holiday Pay in 2026: Do You Get Paid on 25 and 28 December?

Ruhul Amin · Published 23 November 2026 · 8 min read · Figures checked November 2026

Nothing in the law gives you the Christmas bank holidays as extra paid days. Your employer can count 25 December, 28 December and 1 January inside your 5.6 weeks, so one line in your contract decides whether you keep your full 28 days. Here is how to check it, and what part time workers should watch.

The short version

  • The dates: Christmas Day is Friday 25 December 2026, Boxing Day moves to Monday 28 December, and New Year's Day is Friday 1 January 2027, in England and Wales.
  • No automatic extra day. GOV.UK says bank holidays do not have to be given as paid leave. Your contract decides.
  • Statutory holiday is 5.6 weeks, capped at 28 days. An employer can count bank holidays inside it.
  • Part time workers get the same 5.6 weeks, so 3 days a week means at least 16.8 days, bank holidays included.
  • No pay instead. Acas says a worker cannot be paid in lieu of bank holidays, except for unused holiday when leaving.
  • Check two lines in your contract: "28 days including bank holidays" or "plus bank holidays", and any enhanced rate for working them.

1. Which days are bank holidays this Christmas?

In England and Wales, GOV.UK lists three bank holidays between Christmas and New Year 2027.

Friday 25 December 2026 is Christmas Day. Monday 28 December 2026 is Boxing Day, as a substitute day, because 26 December falls on a Saturday. Friday 1 January 2027 is New Year's Day.

Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own lists. Check GOV.UK bank holidays if you work there.

GOV.UK counts eight bank holidays in England and Wales in 2026, and eight again in 2027. That number matters later, when we work out what your allowance is really worth.

If you also want to know when money lands over the break, see which benefits are paid early over Christmas 2026.

2. Does the law say you get them off, or paid extra?

Many people assume it does. It does not.

GOV.UK says: "Bank or public holidays do not have to be given as paid leave." It adds that an employer can choose to include bank holidays as part of a worker's statutory annual leave.

Acas goes further on the practical side. It says whether a worker has to work on bank holidays is up to their employer. Shops, hotels, care homes and restaurants are more likely to be open.

Pay for working a bank holiday is also a contract matter. The sources we checked do not give a legal right to time and a half. Some employers pay it. Check your contract or staff handbook.

What the law does give you is statutory holiday. GOV.UK says workers are legally entitled to 5.6 weeks' paid holiday a year. It limits the statutory entitlement to 28 days.

For a five day week, 5.6 weeks is 28 days. That is also the ceiling, so working six or seven days a week does not push it higher.

3. What does "including bank holidays" cost you?

This is where the money is. An employer is allowed to count bank holidays as part of your 28 days.

Acas says an employer can include bank holidays in the statutory minimum of 5.6 weeks. If a bank holiday falls on a usual working day and the workplace shuts, the employer can require you to take it as holiday. That must be in your contract, or you must be given notice.

Here is our own arithmetic for a full time worker on a five day week. We use the GOV.UK figure of eight bank holidays in England and Wales.

The gap is eight days. For a worker on £15 an hour for 7.5 hours a day, that is 8 x 7.5 x £15 = £900 of paid time off, as our own sum.

One rule helps. Acas says working a bank holiday does not reduce your entitlement. You must still get your full 5.6 weeks.

4. Why do part time workers lose out unevenly?

Part time workers get the same 5.6 weeks, scaled to their week. GOV.UK gives an example. A worker on three days a week gets at least 16.8 days, because 5.6 x 3 = 16.8.

Acas says the rules are the same for full time and part time staff. It also warns about the catch. If bank holidays are counted inside your entitlement, the days you usually work decide how many you use.

If a bank holiday falls on a day you do not usually work, Acas says the employer cannot make you take it as holiday. So the same 16.8 days can go further or less far.

Here is our own count for 2027, using the dates GOV.UK lists. Only the Mondays and Tuesday matter for a worker who does Monday to Wednesday.

5. Which wording in your contract means what? A quick table

"28 days including bank holidays"Bank holidays come out of your 28 days
"28 days plus bank holidays"Bank holidays are extra, up to 36 days off
"Bank holidays are paid at time and a half"An enhanced rate for working them, set by the contract
"You may be required to work bank holidays"The employer can roster you on, and the day counts toward your holiday
Silent on bank holidaysAsk HR in writing. Past practice may matter

If the contract is silent, ask in writing. A written answer is worth far more than a verbal one if the question comes up later.

Can you swap? Acas says a worker can ask to work the bank holiday and take another day off instead. The employer does not have to agree.

6. Worked example: two jobs, one Christmas

Maya works full time in a Leeds warehouse. Her contract says 28 days including bank holidays. The site shuts from 25 December to 1 January.

Her count. 25 December and 28 December are two of the eight bank holidays in 2026, and 1 January is one of the eight in 2027. Each one that falls on a working day comes out of her 28, which leaves 20 days to book once all eight are used. The other working days in the shutdown, 29, 30 and 31 December, come out of the 20 if her employer requires it, and the contract or notice allows that.

Daniel works Monday to Wednesday at a Bristol café. He has 16.8 days a year. On Monday 28 December the café is open, and he is rostered on.

His position. The day is a working day for him, so it can be counted in his 16.8. If he works it, his entitlement is not reduced. Whether he gets extra pay is in his contract.

What both should do: check the line on bank holidays and the holiday year start date, and ask in writing before booking time off for the week.

Both are legal. Their Christmases cost them very different amounts of annual leave.

If you take on short term work over the break, our guide to Christmas temp jobs and the tax code on a second job shows how the pay is taxed. If you are leaving soon, notice periods are worth reading first.

Acas says a worker cannot be paid instead of bank holidays. The exception is unused holiday when someone leaves a job. So a "pay out" offer in place of the day is not a standard right.

The honest verdict

The law guarantees your 5.6 weeks, not three particular days at Christmas. Whether 25 December, 28 December and 1 January are extra or come out of your allowance depends on one sentence in your contract.

If your contract says "including", these three days come out of your 28. Plan your leave for the rest of the year around that, not around 28.

If it says "plus", you are better off by up to eight days a year, and you should make sure your employer is honouring it.

Not sure? Ask HR in writing this week, before anyone books leave. It costs nothing, and it can be worth hundreds of pounds.

Frequently asked

Do I get paid for bank holidays if I do not work them? Not automatically. GOV.UK says bank holidays do not have to be given as paid leave. If your employer counts them as part of your 5.6 weeks, you are paid holiday for that day. If it does not, your contract decides whether you are paid or unpaid.

Can my boss make me work on Christmas Day? Acas says whether a worker has to work bank holidays is up to their employer, and some sectors are more likely to be open. Check whether your contract requires it. Working a bank holiday does not reduce your statutory holiday.

Can my employer make me take 28 December as holiday? Yes, in some cases. Acas says that if the workplace shuts on a usual working day, the employer can require you to take it as holiday. That must be in your contract, or you must be given notice. They cannot do it for a day you do not normally work.

Do part time workers get bank holidays pro rata? They get the same 5.6 weeks, scaled to their week. GOV.UK gives 16.8 days for three days a week. Acas says the rules are the same for part time staff, but the days you work decide how many bank holidays fall on your working week.

Can I be paid instead of taking a bank holiday? No, not as a right. Acas says a worker cannot be paid in lieu of bank holidays. The exception is unused holiday entitlement when you leave a job, which is paid out.
