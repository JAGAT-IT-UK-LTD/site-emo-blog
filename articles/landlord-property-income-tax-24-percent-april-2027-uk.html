Landlord Tax From April 2027: What the 24% Property Income Rate Costs a Typical Let

From 6 April 2027, rental profit in England, Wales and Northern Ireland is due to be taxed at 24%, 44% or 49%, four points above today's rates. In our worked examples a basic rate landlord pays £208 more a year and a higher rate landlord £240 more, because the mortgage interest credit rises too.

The short version

  • From 6 April 2027, rental profit is due to be taxed at 24%, 44% and 49% instead of 20%, 40% and 45%. HMRC published the plan on 29 October 2026, after the Budget.
  • It is not law yet. The Finance Act 2026 set 22%, 42% and 47% for 2027 to 2028. The higher rates need the Finance Bill 2026 to 2027.
  • The mortgage interest credit rises with it. By law the credit is given at the property basic rate, so it moves from 20% to 24% if the new rate passes.
  • A basic rate landlord in our example pays £208 more a year, and a higher rate landlord £240 more (our own arithmetic, shown below).
  • It covers England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Scotland and Wales have been given powers to set their own property rates.
  • Making Tax Digital catches landlords with over £30,000 of gross rent and self employed income from April 2027.

1. What exactly changes in April 2027?

Today, rental profit is taxed like wages: 20%, 40% or 45%, depending on your band. From the 2027 to 2028 tax year, property income gets its own set of rates.

Those rates have already changed once. The Finance Act 2026 fixed them at 22%, 42% and 47%. Then, at Budget 2026, the government raised them by two more points. HMRC's policy paper of 29 October 2026 sets out the new figures.

Band2026 to 2027 (now)Finance Act 2026, for 2027 to 2028Budget 2026 plan, for 2027 to 2028
Basic20%22%24%
Higher40%42%44%
Additional45%47%49%

HMRC says legislation will be introduced in the Finance Bill 2026 to 2027 to set the higher rates. Until that passes, the 22%, 42% and 47% in the Finance Act 2026 are what the law says. Plan on the 24% figures, because that is the government's stated intention.

The bands themselves do not move. HMRC's rates annex keeps the Personal Allowance at £12,570 and the basic rate band at £37,700 in both years. Our guide to frozen tax thresholds explains why that matters.

Where you live matters too. HMRC says the new rates apply to individuals in England, Wales and Northern Ireland. It adds that the Scottish and Welsh governments have been given the power to set property income rates in line with their existing Income Tax powers. If you are a Scottish or Welsh taxpayer, watch for their own announcement.

There is one more change in the order of tax. HMRC's technical note says property income will be taxed after wages, trading and other income, but before savings and dividends. Your Personal Allowance is used against your wages first. In practice, rent sits on top of your salary.

HMRC expects about 5% of taxpayers to pay an average of £225 more in 2027 to 2028, and 2.4 million landlords to be affected by 2031 to 2032.

2. What happens to my mortgage interest credit?

Since April 2020, landlords cannot deduct mortgage interest from rental profit on homes they let. This is the rule people call Section 24. Instead, you get a tax credit worth the basic rate on your interest. GOV.UK says relief on all residential finance costs is restricted to the basic rate of Income Tax.

The Finance Act 2026 changed the wording of the law that sets that credit. Section 274AA of the Income Tax (Trading and Other Income) Act 2005 now gives the credit at the property basic rate, for 2027 to 2028 and later years.

So the credit follows the property basic rate. If Parliament passes 24%, the credit becomes 24%. If it stays at the Finance Act figure, it is 22%. Either way it rises from today's 20%. The 24% credit is our reading of the two documents together. HMRC's 29 October paper does not state it in words.

This softens the blow for a basic rate landlord, but not for a higher rate one. A higher rate landlord pays 44% on the profit before interest, and gets back only 24% on the interest. That gap was 20 points before, and it stays 20 points after.

3. How much more will a typical let cost?

Here are two landlords, each with one mortgaged flat. Their rents and costs are our assumptions for illustration. Every tax figure is our own arithmetic using GOV.UK rates and the 24% credit.

Karen in Coventry, and David in Leeds

Karen earns £30,000 as a teaching assistant. Her flat lets for £1,000 a month, or £12,000 a year. Agent fees, insurance and repairs cost her £2,000, so her profit before interest is £10,000. Her mortgage interest is £4,800.

After her Personal Allowance, her salary uses £17,430 of the basic rate band. The £10,000 of rent fits in the rest, so all of it is basic rate.

Now: £10,000 at 20% is £2,000, less a credit of £4,800 at 20%, which is £960. She pays £1,040.

From April 2027: £10,000 at 24% is £2,400, less £4,800 at 24%, which is £1,152. She pays £1,248, which is £208 more.

David earns £60,000 as an engineer. His flat lets for £1,200 a month, or £14,400 a year. His costs are £2,400, so his profit before interest is £12,000. His mortgage interest is £6,000.

His salary already fills the basic rate band, so all his rent is taxed at the higher rate.

Now: £12,000 at 40% is £4,800, less £6,000 at 20%, which is £1,200. He pays £3,600.

From April 2027: £12,000 at 44% is £5,280, less £6,000 at 24%, which is £1,440. He pays £3,840, which is £240 more.

David's real cash profit, after interest, is £6,000. His tax takes 60% of it now, and 64% from April 2027.

Tax on the flatNow (20% / 40%)Finance Act 2026 (22% / 42%)Budget 2026 plan (24% / 44%)
Karen, basic rate£1,040£1,144£1,248
David, higher rate£3,600£3,720£3,840

The pattern is simple. For both landlords the rise works out at 4 points on profit after interest: 4% of £5,200 for Karen and 4% of £6,000 for David. The real sting for David is not the rise. It is that he already pays 60%.

If your rent pushes you over £100,000 of income, you also start to lose your Personal Allowance. GOV.UK says it falls by £1 for every £2 above that line.

4. Does the £1,000 property allowance help?

Only for small or mortgage free lets. The property allowance lets you take £1,000 off your rental income instead of claiming your real costs. You cannot do both.

GOV.UK also says you cannot use the allowance if you claim the tax credit for residential finance costs. So any landlord with a mortgage has to choose. For Karen and David, real costs of £2,000 and £2,400 plus the interest credit beat £1,000 easily.

Where it does help is a let with low costs and no loan, such as a garage or a parking space. If your gross rent is £1,000 or less, the allowance covers all of it. If you let a room in your own home, the separate Rent a Room scheme applies instead. Our guide to taking in a lodger covers that.

5. Would a limited company be cheaper?

Companies do not pay Income Tax on rent. They pay Corporation Tax on profit, and they can deduct mortgage interest in full. HMRC's rates annex shows Corporation Tax at 19% on profits up to £50,000 and 25% above £250,000, with marginal relief between, for both 2026 and 2027.

Taking money out is taxed again, as dividends. GOV.UK gives the 2026 to 2027 dividend rates as 10.75% basic and 35.75% higher, after a £500 allowance. Here is the same let held in a company, with all profit paid out. This is our own arithmetic.

Karen, basic rateDavid, higher rate
Company profit after interest£5,200£6,000
Corporation Tax at 19%£988£1,140
Dividend tax after £500 allowance£399.04£1,558.70
Total tax in a company£1,387.04£2,698.70
Total tax personally, April 2027£1,248£3,840

For Karen, a company costs more. For David, it saves about £1,141 a year on paper. But the paper is not the whole story.

Moving an existing let into a company is a sale. GOV.UK says you may have to pay Capital Gains Tax when you sell a buy to let property, reported within 60 days. The company's purchase also falls under Stamp Duty Land Tax, which GOV.UK lists under special rates for companies. Then add company accounts, filing and usually dearer mortgages.

For one flat, those costs can swallow the saving for years. Our guide to sole trader versus limited company covers the running costs, and our dividend tax guide covers the second layer of tax. Get advice from a tax adviser before you move a property.

6. When does Making Tax Digital catch me?

A landlord with two flats bringing in £1,300 a month each has £31,200 of gross rent, and is in from April 2027. 7. The honest verdict

For a typical one flat landlord, the April 2027 rise costs a few hundred pounds a year, not thousands. Karen pays £208 more and David £240 more in our examples. That is unwelcome, but it is not a reason to sell on its own.

The bigger number is the one that already exists. A higher rate landlord with a mortgage hands about 60% of their cash profit to HMRC today, and that becomes 64%. If your let only works because prices rise, run your own sums before April 2027.

Do not rush into a limited company to dodge four points. For a basic rate landlord it is usually worse, and for anyone with an existing let the move itself can cost more than years of savings. The useful jobs now are simpler: check your Making Tax Digital date, keep your records digital, and watch the Finance Bill for the final rate.

Frequently asked

What will landlords pay in tax from April 2027? HMRC's plan is 24% for basic rate, 44% for higher rate and 49% for additional rate on property income in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, from 6 April 2027. The Finance Act 2026 currently sets 22%, 42% and 47%, so the higher figures still need the Finance Bill 2026 to 2027.

Will mortgage interest relief go up to 24%? The law now says the credit is given at the property basic rate from 2027 to 2028. That rate is 22% under the Finance Act 2026, and 24% if the Budget 2026 plan is passed. So the credit rises from 20% either way, following whichever basic rate becomes law.

Does the 24% landlord tax apply in Scotland? HMRC says the new property rates apply to individuals in England, Wales and Northern Ireland. It also says the Scottish and Welsh governments have been given the power to set property income rates under their existing Income Tax powers. Scottish taxpayers should watch for the Scottish Government's own decision.

Is it worth putting a buy to let into a limited company? Rarely for one existing let. In our example a higher rate landlord saves about £1,141 a year on paper, but a basic rate landlord pays more. Moving a property is a sale, which can bring Capital Gains Tax and Stamp Duty Land Tax. Take advice before you act.
