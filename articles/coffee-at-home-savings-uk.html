How Much Can You Save Making Coffee at Home? About £860 a Year

Ruhul Amin · Published 26 August 2026 · Updated 25 November 2026 · 9 min read · Figures checked November 2026

Making coffee at home saves about £860 to £910 a year if it replaces one £4 shop coffee each working day. A flat white costs roughly 35p in your kitchen with a single shot and 55p with a double, milk and electricity included. The kit that pays back fastest costs about £41. Here are the sums, with no lecture at the end.

1. How much does a daily shop coffee cost a year?

Finder puts the average price of a coffee in the UK at £3.97 in 2026. So £4 is a fair round number for the habit.

The big chains do not publish drink prices on their websites. We checked Costa, Greggs, Starbucks and Pret in November 2026 and found menus with no prices. Your own receipt is the best source, and London will be dearer than most places.

Take one £4 coffee on each of your working days, say 250 days a year:

HabitPer weekPer year
One £4 coffee, working days only£20£1,000
One £4 coffee, every day£28£1,460
Two £4 coffees, working days£40£2,000
One £2 coffee, working days£10£500
That number surprises people because £4 never feels like a decision. It feels like nothing, 250 times a year.

2. How much does it cost to make a coffee at home?

About 35p for a flat white with a single shot, and about 55p with a double. Here is where that comes from, using Tesco prices checked in November 2026.

A 250g bag of Lavazza Rossa beans was £6.25. That bag makes about 30 single shots, which is 21p of coffee per shot. Tesco's own House Blend, at £3.75 for 227g, brings it down to about 14p.

Milk is cheaper than people guess. Four pints of semi skimmed was £1.75, so the 150ml in a flat white costs about 12p.

ComponentSingle shotDouble shot
Beans, 250g bag at £6.25 making about 30 shots21p42p
Milk, 150ml12p12p
Electricity, see belowabout 1pabout 1p
Totalabout 34pabout 55p

So the same drink is around £4 outside and between 35p and 55p at home.

On 250 working days that is £1,000 against £85 to £138. The gap is roughly £860 to £910 a year.

And that is before you count the sandwich you buy because you were already standing in the queue.

3. How much electricity does a cup of coffee use?

Very little. Most articles ignore the running cost. It is a small thing, so here it is done honestly.

Electricity is capped at 26.32p per kWh until 31 December 2026 on a typical direct debit tariff. On 25 November Ofgem confirmed it rises to 32.69p from 1 January 2027.

Heating one 250ml mug of water from cold to boiling takes about 0.028 kWh, allowing for losses. That is 0.7p now and 0.9p from January.

A full litre takes about 0.1 kWh. That is 2.6p now and 3.3p from January. So filling the kettle for one mug costs nearly four times what you need to spend.

A bean to cup machine rated at 1,400W, running for the ninety seconds it takes to brew, uses about 0.035 kWh. That is roughly 1p a cup. A machine left on standby all day adds more than the brewing does.

If the kettle goes on that often, the next question is the tap. Whether a boiling water tap beats a kettle on running cost turns on how many times a day you fill it.

The practical points: boil only what you need, and switch a bean to cup machine off at the wall. Neither will change your life. 4. Is buying a coffee machine worth it?

Only if the machine is not silly money. Here are four routes, cheapest first. The payback is measured against a £4 daily habit on working days, with a home cup costing 40p, so £3.60 saved a day.

Cafetiere. Makes good black coffee, uses no electricity beyond the kettle, and has nothing to break.

Moka pot or AeroPress. Both make something close to espresso strength. Add a handheld milk frother and you have a flat white that is close to the shop version. The total is under £50.

Bean to cup. Real espresso, grinder built in, one button. Payback takes over three months at a coffee a day. The graveyard of unused coffee machines exists for a reason, so buy this after a few months of the cheap route, not before.

Pods. Nespresso's own Original capsules cost 39p to 59p each, and most sit between 45p and 53p. Compatible pods at Tesco ran from about 26p to 46p. That is more than a 21p shot of beans, every single cup.

The machine is discounted because the refills are not. It is the same business model as printer ink.

5. What ruins the saving?

Buying £11 speciality beans every week undoes much of the exercise. Speciality coffee is a hobby, and a good one, but it is not a saving.

If the goal is money, a £6 supermarket bag is the right choice. A £3.75 own brand bag is better still.

Watch the milk too. Oatly was £2.20 a litre at Tesco, so the 150ml in a flat white costs 33p. That is nearly three times the cost of dairy milk. Tesco's own oat drink at £1.45 a litre narrows the gap.

And be honest about what you are buying outside. For a lot of people the £4 is not the coffee. It is the ten minutes out of the building.

A flask does not replace that. Pretending otherwise is why these plans collapse in week three. Our no spend challenge guide has more on habits that survive.

6. Worked example: a realistic year

Not the fantasy version

Sam in Leeds buys one £4 coffee on each of 250 working days. That is £1,000 a year.

The change: an AeroPress at £39.99 and a £1 frother, so £41 once.

One kept treat: one bought coffee a week for 50 weeks, £200. Total bans fail, and this is the version people stick to.

The other 200 drinks are made at home. Sam likes a double shot, so each one costs 55p. 200 x 55p = £110 for beans, milk and electricity.

Year one total: £351. Against £1,000. Saved: £649, in a year that included buying the kit.

Year two: £310, because the kit is already paid for. Saved: £690, and it repeats every year after that.

The version with no treat costs 250 x 55p = £137.50 a year and saves about £860. It is also the one most people abandon by March. £649 you keep beats £860 you do not.

7. Where should the saved money go?

£650 a year sitting in a current account earns nothing. Money that stays where it was will quietly get spent on something else.

Move it on the day you would have spent it, or on payday, into somewhere separate.

In rough order of what it is worth:

  1. Against a credit card balance that charges interest. Clearing that debt beats any savings rate, as our cashback card guide explains.
  2. Into an easy access savings account or an ISA. Small, real, and truly passive.
  3. As starting capital. Most of the side hustles on this site need under £100 to test properly. Our £12.71 test guide shows which ones clear minimum wage.

The honest verdict

The gap is real and it is large. Roughly £4 outside against 35p to 55p at home, which is about £860 to £910 a year on working days alone.

Spend about £41 on an AeroPress and a frother and it pays for itself in 12 working days.

But do not go to zero. Keep one bought coffee a week, move the difference somewhere separate on payday, and buy the £6 supermarket beans, not the £11 speciality bag. That version saves around £650 a year and survives contact with February, which is the only test that matters.

And if the £4 is really about ten minutes out of the office, not the drink, this article does not solve your problem. Take the ten minutes.

Frequently asked

How much does it cost to make a coffee at home? About 34p for a flat white with a single shot and 55p with a double, using £6.25 supermarket beans. Milk adds about 12p and electricity about 1p. Black coffee from a cafetiere costs roughly 21p to 42p, depending on how strong you make it.

Is buying a coffee machine worth it? A cheap one, yes. An AeroPress and frother at about £41 pays for itself in 12 working days against a £4 daily habit. A £243.80 bean to cup machine takes over three months. Buy the expensive machine only after you have made coffee at home for six months.

How much is a Nespresso pod per cup? Nespresso's own Original capsules cost 39p to 59p each in November 2026, with most between 45p and 53p. Compatible pods at Tesco cost about 26p to 46p. A single shot from a £6.25 bag of beans costs about 21p, so pods roughly double the coffee cost.

What is the cheapest way to make coffee at home? Instant. A 200g jar of Tesco Classic at £2.40 works out near 2p a cup at a 2g teaspoon, and a branded jar about 6p. If you like it, the sums cannot be beaten. For a flat white, own brand beans in a cafetiere or AeroPress come next.

Could I turn good coffee into income? Possibly, though the licensing and food hygiene rules are the barrier, not the coffee. A mobile or home based coffee business needs registering with the council before it trades. Our coffee side hustle guide covers what it takes and what it costs to start.
