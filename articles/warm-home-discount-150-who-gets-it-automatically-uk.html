Warm Home Discount 2026/27: who gets the £150 automatically, and the letter you must not ignore

1. What is the Warm Home Discount, and what is it not?

The Warm Home Discount is a one off £150 discount off your electricity bill. If you are eligible, your electricity supplier takes it off your bill.

If the same supplier gives you gas and electricity, GOV.UK says you may be able to have it on your gas bill instead. You have to ask your supplier for that.

Three things it is not, and each one catches people out.

It is not a cash payment. The money does not arrive in your bank account. It comes off what you owe your supplier. If you are in credit, your credit goes up instead.

It is not a cut to your direct debit. Your monthly payment is set on your projected yearly use. A one off £150 credit does not change it on its own.

Many people get the discount, see no change in what leaves their account, and assume it never arrived. Check the bill or the app, not the bank statement.

It is not the Winter Fuel Payment. That is a separate scheme, paid to you rather than credited to a bill. GOV.UK confirms the Warm Home Discount does not affect your Winter Fuel Payment or your Cold Weather Payment.

2. Who is eligible for the Warm Home Discount 2026 to 2027?

In England and Wales, the rules for this winter are simpler than before. You may be eligible if all three of these applied on 23 August 2026:

  • your energy supplier is part of the scheme
  • you or your partner get a qualifying means tested benefit
  • your name, or your partner's, is on the electricity bill

The qualifying benefits are Universal Credit, Housing Benefit, income related Employment and Support Allowance and Pension Credit. Both parts of Pension Credit count, the Guarantee element and Savings Credit.

The government's eligibility statement explains why it is simpler. From this scheme year, the old two core groups were merged into one. The high cost to heat test was removed the year before.

In plain terms, being on one of those four benefits on the right day, with your name on the bill, is what counts. How expensive your home is to heat no longer decides it.

The participating suppliers include British Gas, EDF, E.ON Next, Octopus Energy, OVO, ScottishPower, So Energy, Utilita, Utility Warehouse and others on the GOV.UK list.

3. Why the 23 August qualifying date matters

Eligibility is judged on a single qualifying date. For winter 2026 to 2027 that date was 23 August 2026.

The government sets the date as close as possible to its data matching run. That keeps changes in people's circumstances to a minimum between the snapshot and the awards.

The practical result is blunt. Whether you get £150 this winter was settled by your circumstances on one day in late August.

Starting a benefit claim now, or switching supplier now, does not change the answer for this scheme year. It may well change it for next winter.

So the useful question in November is not "how do I qualify". It is "was I on a qualifying benefit on 23 August, and has my letter come".

4. Do you have to apply, or is it automatic?

In England and Wales, you usually do not apply. The government matches benefit records against supplier customer records.

Where the match works, your supplier is told to apply the discount and you are told by letter. No form and no phone call. GOV.UK says that letter will confirm you do not need to do anything.

The eligibility statement says that, through data matching, the majority of eligible households get the rebate automatically. That is also why searching for an application form is usually wasted effort.

Letters go out between late October 2026 and early January 2027. Your supplier must then apply the discount by 31 March 2027.

Scotland runs on different rules, set by Scottish ministers. Section 7 below explains them.

5. The letter you must not ignore, and the 26 February deadline

Here is the one place where doing nothing costs you money.

Sometimes data matching cannot tell whether a household is eligible. A common case is a benefit claimant who cannot be matched to a participating supplier.

That person gets a letter telling them to call the Warm Home Discount helpline to confirm their details. GOV.UK says you must call by 26 February 2027.

That letter is not junk mail. It is the only way an eligible household the computer could not place gets its £150. If it goes in the recycling, the discount does not happen.

Mismatches happen for ordinary reasons. The energy account is in a partner's name. The names on the benefit record and the bill differ slightly. Or the household moved recently.

If you think you are eligible and no letter has come by early January 2027, ring the helpline on 0800 030 9322, Monday to Friday, 8am to 6pm. Relay UK users dial 18001 then 0800 229 4325.

6. A worked example: what £150 does to a real bill

Ade lives in Coventry on a standard variable tariff with E.ON Next. He was getting Universal Credit on 23 August 2026, and his name is on the electricity bill.

He ticks all three boxes, so he should get a letter by early January 2027 confirming he need do nothing.

Set it against the price cap. Ofgem's cap for 1 October to 31 December 2026 is £1,723 a year for a typical dual fuel household paying by direct debit. That was up £60 a year from £1,663.

So the £150 discount is two and a half times the whole yearly cap rise. Against the typical yearly bill of £1,723, £150 is about 8.7%.

That is real money. It is larger than most of the energy tips written about far more often.

What Ade will actually see is a £150 credit on his electricity account by 31 March 2027, with his direct debit unchanged. If he wants lower monthly payments instead of credit building up, he must ask E.ON Next to recalculate his direct debit.

One honest limit. If Ade's circumstances on 23 August 2026 had not met the rules, nothing in this guide would change that for this winter. The useful step then is to check the benefits that drive eligibility, which are usually worth far more than £150.

The next cap, for 1 January to 31 March 2027, is due from Ofgem by 25 November 2026. The £150 stays the same whatever it does.

7. How it works in each nation

This is not one scheme. GOV.UK splits its guide by nation, and the difference is whether the £150 arrives without you doing anything. Checked on GOV.UK on 22 November 2026.

Park or mobile homePaid by a separate routeApply for the Park Homes Warm Home Discount

Scotland in detail. You get it automatically if you or your partner get Pension Credit. You also get it automatically with a disability extra in Universal Credit, or a disability or pensioner premium in income related ESA.

The same applies if you have a child under 5 living with you and are unemployed on Universal Credit, or get income related ESA. Everyone else in Scotland is in the broader group and must apply to their supplier, who may set extra rules. GOV.UK says to check even if you got it last year.

Four things worth knowing before you chase it. A prepayment or pay as you go meter does not stop you qualifying, and your supplier will say how it arrives, often as a top up voucher. If one supplier bills you for both fuels, you may be able to have it taken off gas instead. If your supplier stops trading, Ofgem appoints a new one and you may still be eligible. And it does not reduce your Cold Weather Payment or Winter Fuel Payment.

Who this does not help. Anyone in Northern Ireland. Anyone whose electricity account is in another person's name, such as a lodger or an adult child paying a share of the bill. People living permanently in a care home, and most caravans, are outside the main scheme too.

Frequently asked

Who is eligible for the Warm Home Discount 2026 to 2027? In England and Wales, you may be eligible if on 23 August 2026 you or your partner got Universal Credit, Housing Benefit, income related ESA or Pension Credit. Your name or your partner's must be on the electricity bill, and your supplier must be in the scheme.

Do I need to apply for the Warm Home Discount? In England and Wales, usually not. Data matching finds most eligible households and your letter will say you need do nothing. If the letter asks for more information, call the helpline by 26 February 2027. In Scotland, the broader group must apply to their supplier.

Is the Warm Home Discount paid into my bank account? No. It is a £150 discount your supplier applies to your electricity bill, by 31 March 2027 this winter. If your account is in credit, the credit goes up. On a prepayment meter, your supplier will tell you how it arrives, often as a voucher.

Can I get the Warm Home Discount on Universal Credit? Yes, in England and Wales. Universal Credit is one of the four qualifying benefits for 2026 to 2027, so getting it on 23 August 2026 counts, as long as your name or your partner's is on the electricity bill. In Scotland, only some Universal Credit claimants get it automatically.

What if I have not had a letter yet? Letters go out between late October 2026 and early January 2027, so wait until then. If nothing has come by early January and you think you qualify, ring the Warm Home Discount Scheme on 0800 030 9322 before 26 February 2027.
