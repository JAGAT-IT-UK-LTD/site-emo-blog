Up to GBP 9,800 Is Available When a Partner Dies, and the Clock Starts on the Day of the Death

The short version

  • Higher rate: a one-off GBP 3,500 plus 18 monthly payments of GBP 350. Claimed in full that is GBP 9,800.
  • Lower rate: a one-off GBP 2,500 plus 18 monthly payments of GBP 100, so GBP 4,300 in full.
  • Both rates are unchanged for 2026 to 2027.
  • Claim within 3 months of the death to get everything. That is the only window that pays the lump sum and all 18 monthly payments.
  • Between 3 and 12 months you keep the lump sum but lose some monthly payments.
  • Between 12 and 21 months you lose the lump sum entirely and get only some monthly payments. On the higher rate that is GBP 3,500 gone.
  • After 21 months you usually get nothing at all.
  • If you were living together as if married, you get the higher rate, not the lower one. Cohabiting partners have been able to claim since 9 February 2023.
  • It does not affect your other benefits for a year after the first payment, but you must tell your benefits office.

1. The two rates, and which one you get

Bereavement Support Payment replaced the old widowed parent allowances and bereavement payment. It is not means tested, it does not care what you have in savings, and it does not depend on whether you work.

There are two rates, and which you get depends on your circumstances at the time your partner died.

The higher rate applies if you were married or in a registered civil partnership and one of these was true when your partner died: you were getting Child Benefit for a child living with you, you had been told by the Child Benefit Office you were entitled to it for a child living with you, or you were pregnant. The most you can get is a one-off GBP 3,500 plus 18 monthly payments of GBP 350.

The lower rate applies to everyone else who was married or in a civil partnership. The most you can get is a one-off GBP 2,500 plus 18 monthly payments of GBP 100.

Do the arithmetic, because the gap is larger than the headline suggests. The higher rate in full is GBP 3,500 plus 18 times GBP 350, which is GBP 6,300, giving GBP 9,800. The lower rate in full is GBP 2,500 plus GBP 1,800, giving GBP 4,300. The difference is GBP 5,500, and it turns on whether there was a child or a pregnancy.

Both rates are unchanged for 2026 to 2027.

2. The four claim windows, and what each pays

Almost every write-up of this benefit says you must claim within three months. That is not quite right, and the full picture matters, because a lot of people are outside three months by the time anybody mentions the benefit exists.

There are four windows, measured from the date of the death.

Within 3 months. You get the one-off payment and all 18 monthly payments. This is the only window that pays the full amount.

Over 3 months but under 12. You still get the one-off payment, but only some of the monthly payments. The lump sum survives. Some of the monthly money does not.

Over 12 months but under 21. You cannot get the one-off payment at all, though you can still get some monthly payments. On the higher rate, crossing the twelve month mark costs you GBP 3,500 in a single step.

Over 21 months. You usually get nothing.

There is one further reducer worth knowing: if you reach State Pension age within 18 months of your partner death, you may get fewer monthly payments.

The twelve month mark is not a taper. It is a step, and on the higher rate the step is three and a half thousand pounds. Nothing on a death certificate or a funeral invoice tells you it is coming.

3. Cohabiting partners, and the rate that surprises people

Until 9 February 2023 this benefit was closed to unmarried couples entirely. Since then, partners who were living together as if they were married can claim.

The detail people get wrong is which rate. It is easy to assume that a cohabiting partner would be treated as a lesser case and pushed onto the lower rate. GOV.UK says the opposite: if you were living together as if you were married, the most you can get is a one-off GBP 3,500 plus 18 monthly payments of GBP 350. That is the higher rate.

The conditions attached to a cohabiting claim are set out on the eligibility page rather than the payment page, so read that one before you assume you do or do not qualify. But if you have been putting this off because you were not married and assumed the answer was no, the answer has been yes for over three years.

4. What it does to your other benefits

This is the part that stops people claiming, and the rule is more generous than the fear.

Bereavement Support Payment will not affect your other benefits for a year after your first payment. Not reduced, not tapered, ignored.

After that year, money you have left over from the first payment could affect what you get if you renew or make a new claim for another benefit, because by then it is just savings like any other. So the lump sum is protected for twelve months and then counts as capital.

You must tell your benefits office, for example your local Jobcentre Plus, when you start getting it. That is an obligation, not an option, and it is easy to forget in the weeks after a death.

Worked example: two widows in Dumfries

Two women are widowed within a week of each other. Both were married, both were getting Child Benefit for a child at home, so both qualify for the higher rate. Identical entitlement on paper.

Morag is told about the benefit by the registrar when she registers the death. She claims in week three. Inside the three month window, so she receives the full GBP 3,500 plus all 18 monthly payments of GBP 350, which is GBP 6,300. Total GBP 9,800.

Elspeth is not told by anyone. She finds out from a leaflet at a support group fourteen months later. She is past twelve months, so the one-off payment is gone. She receives no GBP 3,500, and only some of the monthly payments.

The cost of the delay: at minimum GBP 3,500, the entire lump sum, plus the monthly payments that lapsed before she claimed. Same marriage, same child, same grief, same entitlement on the day of the death.

What would have changed it: one phone call to 0800 151 2012, or fifteen minutes on the online form, in the first three months. She needed her National Insurance number, her bank details, the date of the death and her husband National Insurance number. That is the entire list.

If you are reading this past the three month mark: claim anyway, today. Between three and twelve months you still get the lump sum. Between twelve and twenty-one you still get some monthly payments. The only window that pays nothing is after twenty-one months, and even then it is worth a call rather than an assumption.

5. The honest verdict

This is money that is not means tested, ignores your savings, and is protected from affecting your other benefits for a full year. There is no good reason not to claim it, and no test you can fail on grounds of being too well off.

The problem is purely one of timing and information. The benefit is worth up to GBP 9,800 and the full amount is only available for three months, starting on the worst day of your life, when nobody is thinking about DWP forms. The twelve month cliff then removes GBP 3,500 in one step from people who were simply never told.

So the single most useful thing anyone can do with this article is not read it for themselves. It is remember it for somebody else. If you know a household that has lost a partner in the last few months, the useful sentence is: have you claimed Bereavement Support Payment, and do you know there is a three month clock.

Where this genuinely does not help: if your partner died more than 21 months ago, you usually cannot get any payments, and no amount of appealing changes the schedule. Say so plainly. In that case Turn2us and Marie Curie are the right free places to check what else you may be entitled to instead.

Frequently asked

Is it affected by my savings or my earnings? No. It is not means tested and does not depend on your income or capital.

Do I have to have been married? No, not since 9 February 2023. Partners who were living together as if married can claim, and GOV.UK puts them on the higher rate. Check the eligibility page for the conditions.

What if my partner died abroad, or I live abroad? Call the International Pension Centre rather than the usual helpline. Northern Ireland also has its own route to apply.

How do I actually claim? Online, by phone on 0800 151 2012, or by post on form BSP1. You need your National Insurance number, your bank details, the date of death and your partner National Insurance number.
