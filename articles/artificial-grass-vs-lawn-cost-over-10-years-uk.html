Save Money

Artificial grass is sold as a saving, and there is almost nothing there to save

Ruhul Amin · Published 9 November 2026 · 6 min read · Figures checked November 2026

Artificial grass against a real lawn is not close on cost. A lawn costs roughly £10 to £80 a year to run at current electricity and water rates, so a fitted artificial lawn almost never pays for itself before it wears out. The pitch is always no mowing, no watering, no feed, so here are the sums behind it.

The short version

  • A cordless mower costs about £1.42 a year to charge at 26.32p per kWh, on 30 cuts a year. A corded one is around £8.42.
  • Most UK lawns are never watered at all, and if you did water heavily it is about £40.95 a year at £4.0945 a cubic metre on a metered supply.
  • Price a lawn generously and it costs about £80 a year to run, including feed and seed. Price it realistically and it is closer to £10.
  • Fitted artificial grass costs about £50 to £90 a square metre across most of the UK, according to the Fantastic Services 2026 cost guide, so £2,500 to £4,500 for 50 square metres.
  • A £3,000 install divided by £80 a year is a payback of 37.5 years, against a surface the same guide says lasts 10 to 20 years.
  • So do not buy artificial grass to save money. Buy it because you want a hard wearing surface you never have to cut, which is a real reason and a different one.

1. What a real lawn actually costs to run

This is the number the whole argument rests on, and almost nobody puts a figure to it, so here it is at rates we can name and date.

Mowing. Our stated assumption is 30 cuts a year, which is roughly weekly from April to October with a few skipped. A cordless mower with a 36V 5Ah battery holds about 0.18 kWh per charge. Thirty charges is 5.4 kWh, and at 26.32p per kWh, the Ofgem cap electricity rate for 1 October to 31 December 2026, that is £1.42 a year. A 1,600W corded mower running 40 minutes each time uses about 32 kWh a year, which is £8.42. Ofgem also notes there is no VAT on electricity between 1 October 2026 and 31 March 2027.

Watering. Here is the part the sales pitch depends on and it is simply not true of most British gardens. The overwhelming majority of UK lawns are never watered, because it rains, and a lawn that browns off in August greens up again in September without help. If you do water, and you put on 10 cubic metres across a summer, that is £40.95 at Severn Trent's 2026/27 metered rate of £4.0945 per cubic metre. On an unmetered bill it is £0.

That rate is £2.5503 for water supply plus used water at £1.6255, which Severn Trent bills on 95 per cent of the water supplied. Garden water soaks into the ground rather than reaching the sewer, but the 95 per cent assumption already allows for normal garden watering, so you pay it anyway unless you can prove larger losses by measurement.

Feed, seed and moss killer. Our stated assumption is £30 a year for someone who takes it seriously, and nothing at all for someone who does not. There is no published national figure for this and we are not going to invent one, so treat it as the placeholder it is and put your own spending in.

Add those up and a keenly tended, metered, watered lawn costs about £80 a year. A normal one, cut with a cordless mower and otherwise left alone, costs about £10 a year. Those two numbers are the denominator of every payback calculation below, and the fact that they are small is the entire finding.

2. Artificial grass vs a real lawn: the payback at each price

Installed prices depend on area, ground preparation, edging and whether the old lawn has to be dug out and taken away, so your own quote is the only exact figure. As a guide, the Fantastic Services cost guide, which says its figures reflect 2026 market rates, puts fitted artificial grass at £50 to £90 a square metre across most of the UK and £65 to £110 in London and the South East.

On its figures, a 20 square metre lawn costs £1,000 to £1,800 and a 50 square metre lawn £2,500 to £4,500 outside London. Other trade guides give different numbers, so treat these as a range, not a price.

The saving column is the running cost of the lawn you are replacing, because that is all artificial grass removes. The payback is install cost divided by that annual saving, at the rates named above, checked again on 9 November 2026.

Look at the right hand column against every other column. Fantastic Services says artificial grass lasts 10 to 20 years overall, with budget grades at about 5 to 8 years, mid range at 10 to 15 and premium at 15 to 20.

Only one cell on this table comes in under 20 years: a £1,500 job against an unusually expensive £80 a year lawn, at 18.8 years. Even that only works if a £1,500 job buys a premium grade that lasts to the very top of the range, and on the guide's own rates £1,500 covers only about 17 to 30 square metres. That is not a marginal call that could tip the other way with a slightly better quote. It is an argument that does not work at any normal price.

And the limited life is a further cost rather than a neutral fact, because at the end of it you are paying to remove and dispose of a plastic surface that cannot be composted, and then paying again for whatever replaces it. A lawn at the end of twenty years is a lawn.

3. The reasons that are actually good

None of the above means artificial grass is a bad purchase. It means it is not a financial one, and the industry sells it as a financial one because the real reasons are harder to put in an advert.

A surface that survives a dog or three children. A small back garden with a large dog does not have a lawn, it has mud from November to March. No amount of seed fixes that, because the problem is traffic rather than grass. If the honest alternative is mud, artificial grass is solving a real problem and the payback question does not arise.

You physically cannot mow. Mowing is awkward, noisy work involving a heavy machine and a trailing cable or a battery. For someone with limited mobility, the choice is often not lawn against artificial grass but artificial grass against paying somebody to cut it. Gardeners' rates vary a lot, so take our stated assumption of £20 a cut and put your own price in. At £20 a cut, 30 cuts a year is £600, and a £3,000 install pays back in 5 years. That is the one version of this calculation that works, and it works because you are replacing labour rather than electricity.

A shaded strip where grass will not grow. North facing, under a tree, between two fences. Grass fails there every year and you reseed it every year. Replacing something that does not work is not the same decision as replacing something that does.

There is also a cost on the other side of the ledger that the running cost table cannot hold, and it is worth naming even though we cannot price it. An artificial surface still needs brushing to keep the pile standing up, still collects leaves and moss through a damp British autumn, and still needs washing down if a dog uses it. Low maintenance is not no maintenance, and the jobs it removes are seasonal while some of the jobs it creates are not. Anyone selling it on the basis that you will never touch the garden again is describing a product that does not exist.

What is not on that list is drought or hosepipe bans, which get used as a selling point and should not be. A lawn that goes brown in a dry August is not dying, it is dormant, and it does not cost you anything to let it happen.

One planning point for front gardens. GOV.UK's guidance on the permeable surfacing of front gardens, published in 2009, says that in a front garden, covering more than 5 square metres with a surface that does not let water through needs planning permission. A permeable surface, or one that drains onto a lawn or border, does not. The guidance does not mention artificial grass, so if you plan to lay it in a front garden on a solid base, ask your council first. Back gardens are not covered by that rule.

4. A worked example

Marcus, Chelmsford. Metered supply, back garden of about 60 square metres, two young children, a cordless mower he bought in 2022, and a quote of £2,800 for artificial grass including lifting the old turf. That is a keen price: at £50 to £90 a square metre, the Fantastic Services rates put 60 square metres at £3,000 to £5,400.

He prices his lawn honestly. He never waters it, so that is £0. He cuts it about 30 times a year at roughly £1.42 of electricity. He buys a bag of feed most springs, call it £18. His lawn costs him £19.42 a year.

So the payback is £2,800 divided by £19.42, which is 144 years. Even the longest life in the trade guide is 20. There is no arithmetic that rescues this as an investment, and the salesman's line about never buying weedkiller again was worth about eighteen pounds.

He buys it anyway, and that is a reasonable decision. The garden is north facing, the section by the fence has been mud for three winters, and two children under six use it every day regardless of the weather. He is buying a usable garden in February, not a saving, and once he stops pretending it is a saving he negotiates harder on the quote, because he is no longer telling himself it will come back.

That last part is the practical value of running the numbers even when they say no. A buyer who believes a purchase pays for itself accepts the first price. A buyer who knows it does not haggles.

The verdict

Do not buy artificial grass to save money. A normal British lawn costs around £10 to £80 a year to run at the rates named here, so even a modest install takes decades to come back, and trade guides say the surface lasts 10 to 20 years.

Buy it if you have mud instead of grass, if you cannot mow, or if you are currently paying somebody to cut it, which is the only version where the sums actually work. Then negotiate on the price, because you now know the saving is not going to rescue a bad quote.

Frequently asked

How much does it cost to run a lawn mower? On our stated assumption of 30 cuts a year, a cordless mower with a 36V 5Ah battery uses about 5.4 kWh a year, which is £1.42 at the 26.32p Ofgem cap rate for 1 October to 31 December 2026. A 1,600W corded mower used for 40 minutes a cut is about £8.42.

Does artificial grass save water? Only if you were watering, and most UK lawns are never watered. If you did put 10 cubic metres on a lawn over a summer on a metered supply, that is £40.95 at Severn Trent's 2026/27 metered rates. On an unmetered bill it saves nothing at all.

How much does artificial grass cost to install? The Fantastic Services 2026 cost guide puts it at about £50 to £90 a square metre fitted across most of the UK, and £65 to £110 in London and the South East. That makes a 20 square metre lawn roughly £1,000 to £1,800. Ground preparation and removing old turf move the price, so get several quotes.

How long does artificial grass last? The Fantastic Services 2026 cost guide says 10 to 20 years overall: about 5 to 8 years for budget grades, 10 to 15 for mid range and 15 to 20 for premium. Heavy use can shorten it. Warranties vary by product, so check the one on the grass you are quoted for.

Is there any case where it pays for itself? Yes, one: if you currently pay somebody to cut the grass. On our assumed £20 a cut and 30 cuts a year that is £600, and a £3,000 install pays back in five years. The saving is labour, not energy or water.
