YouTube Earnings UK: What a Channel Really Pays, and Why the Bar Doubles in February 2027

Ruhul Amin · Published 26 August 2026 · Updated 26 November 2026 · 11 min read · Figures checked November 2026

Most UK channels earn nothing from YouTube until they reach 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours, and then they keep 55% of the ad money on long videos. From 1 February 2027 new channels need 8,000 watch hours instead. Here is what views are really worth, what you must hit first, and the honest hourly rate once you count the editing.

  • Tax: past £1,000 of gross trading income you must register for Self Assessment. From 6 April 2027 that allowance and the reporting threshold rise to £3,000.

1. How many subscribers and watch hours do you need?

To run ads and earn a share of them, you must be accepted into the YouTube Partner Programme. YouTube's eligibility page sets two routes, and both need 1,000 subscribers.

RouteThreshold todayThe catch
Long videos1,000 subscribers + 4,000 public watch hours in 12 monthsThe watch hours, not the subscribers, are what stop people
Shorts1,000 subscribers + 10 million Shorts views in 90 daysTen million. In ninety days. Read that twice
Fan funding and Shopping only500 subscribers + 3,000 watch hours, or 3 million Shorts views in 90 daysMemberships and Super Thanks, but no ad share

4,000 hours is 240,000 minutes of people actually watching. Not clicking and leaving. Watching.

The detail that catches people out: Shorts watch time in the Shorts feed does not count toward the watch hour threshold. Post both, and only your long videos build that number.

Plenty of people spend a year making Shorts, gain subscribers, and find they are no closer to monetising the normal way.

You must also be at least 18, or have a guardian over 18 who handles payments through AdSense for YouTube, and follow YouTube's monetisation policies. YouTube also reviews your channel before it accepts you.

2. What changes on 1 February 2027?

This is the biggest change to the programme in years. YouTube announced it on 10 August 2026 in its creator blog, and the details are on its changes page.

New channels need twice as much. From 1 February 2027, new applicants need 1,000 subscribers plus 8,000 watch hours in the past 365 days, or 20 million Shorts views in the past 90 days.

Channels already in the programme keep their place. YouTube says the new entry thresholds do not affect existing partners.

But existing partners must accept new terms. Review and accept them in YouTube Studio by 31 January 2027. Miss that, and you stop earning from the linked features on 1 February. You stay in the programme and can accept later.

Shorts pool earnings get a monthly bar. From 1 February 2027, a channel needs 10 million Shorts views over the past 90 days to earn from the Shorts pool that month. Fall below it and your long video earnings carry on.

Small channels must stay active. You count as active with any one of: 1,000 watch hours in 365 days, 1 million Shorts views in 90 days, or two long videos or five Shorts every 90 days.

The fan funding tier stays at 500 subscribers. So a small channel can still earn from memberships and Super Thanks before it reaches the ad share.

The practical point for anyone starting now: if you are close to 4,000 hours, getting in before February matters. After that, the same channel needs twice the watch time.

3. How much does YouTube pay per 1,000 views in the UK?

The number that matters is RPM, revenue per 1,000 views. YouTube's own definition says RPM is calculated after YouTube's revenue share. It also counts memberships, Premium, Super Chat and Super Stickers, not only ads.

Do not confuse it with CPM. That is what advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions, before YouTube's share. It is the bigger number, and the one people quote to make things sound better.

The split itself is public. YouTube says creators keep 55% on long videos and 45% on Shorts. On Shorts, the money first goes into a pool, part of it pays for music licensing, and your 45% applies to your slice of that pool, as its Shorts page explains.

What YouTube does not publish is an average RPM. It varies by subject, by where your viewers live, and by season.

So the table below is not a YouTube figure. It is the set of rough RPMs we use in our own sums, to show how subject changes the answer. Your real number is on the Revenue tab of YouTube Analytics once you are accepted.

5. Where does YouTube money really come from?

Ask any UK creator who earns a living, and ads will usually be the smaller part.

  1. Sponsorships. Usually the best paid work. Rates are agreed deal by deal, and a small channel with engaged viewers in a clear niche can be worth more to a brand than a big general one. YouTube also says it plans brand deal incentives for smaller Shorts channels.
  2. Affiliate links. They work quietly in the background. A tech or money channel linking products it really uses can out earn its own ad revenue. The rates are in our Amazon commission rates guide. Disclosure is not optional: the ASA wants it clear and upfront, so a spoken recommendation needs a spoken disclosure.
  3. Your own product. Courses, templates, printables, a service. The audience is already there, and you keep almost everything.
  4. Memberships and Super Thanks. Small for most channels, meaningful for ones with a loyal community. These open at 500 subscribers.

Brand work for other people's channels is another route. Our guide to what UGC creators get paid by brands covers it.

The pattern is the same across every side hustle on this site. Platform payouts are the entry level. The real money is in what you build on top of the audience.

6. Which subjects pay the most?

Two filters, and you need both.

Filter one: is the viewer close to spending money? Personal finance, software, business tools, home improvement and expensive kit all draw higher paying adverts. That is the RPM table above.

Filter two: could you still film it in month eighteen? Almost every channel that fails does so because the person got bored, not because the algorithm buried them.

Eighteen months is roughly when ad revenue starts to be worth having. Anything you would drop before then earns nothing at all.

The overlap, a subject with commercial value that you would happily talk about for free, is where a channel works. Picking a high RPM subject you find dull is the most common costly mistake. You quit at month seven having earned £40.

7. Worked example: two channels, same effort

Callum in Bolton and Priya in Leeds both start in January. Both publish twelve videos a month.

The RPMs, sponsor fee and affiliate income below are our assumptions to show the shape of the sums. They are not YouTube figures.

Callum: retro gaming. He reaches the Partner Programme in month nine, which is fast. By month fourteen he gets 50,000 views a month at an RPM of £2. YouTube revenue £100 a month. Sponsors in this niche are mostly small, and affiliate sales are thin because his viewers buy second hand. Realistic total after eighteen months: about £130 a month.

Priya: UK small business finance. She grows more slowly and only reaches the programme in month thirteen. If that falls after 1 February 2027, she needs 8,000 watch hours, not 4,000, which could push her back several months more.

By month eighteen she also gets 50,000 views a month, at an RPM of £6. YouTube revenue £300 a month. Add one sponsored slot a month from a software firm at an assumed £400, and affiliate links to accounting tools at about £150. Realistic total: about £850 a month.

Same videos published. Same hours. Six and a half times the income. The difference was never the effort. It was who wanted to reach the audience.

Run the hourly test. Priya's £850 over 48 hours is about £17.70 an hour, above the £12.71 minimum. Callum's £130 is about £2.70 an hour.

The honest caveat: if Callum has no interest in small business finance, Priya's channel does not exist for him. He would have quit in month five. Enjoying it is not a soft point. It is what gets you to month eighteen.

8. Do you pay tax on YouTube earnings in the UK?

Yes. HMRC treats YouTube income as trading income, the same as any other self employed work.

The £1,000 rule. Once your gross trading income from YouTube and any other side work passes the £1,000 trading allowance in a tax year, you must register for Self Assessment. That is gross income, not profit, and it is the mistake people make most.

The deadline. Register by 5 October after the end of the tax year in which you passed £1,000. Our guide to registering for Self Assessment walks through it.

The change in April 2027. The Budget raised the trading allowance from £1,000 to £3,000 from 6 April 2027, for the 2027 to 2028 tax year. HMRC's policy paper says the Self Assessment reporting threshold rises to £3,000 too. Our £3,000 trading allowance guide has the detail.

Expenses or the allowance, not both. Keep receipts for the camera, microphone, lighting, editing software and props. They cut the profit you pay tax on. If you claim the trading allowance, you cannot also deduct expenses, so once your kit spending is high, deducting properly wins.

The US tax form. YouTube asks every creator for US tax information, because some of your viewers are in the US. Google's tax page says that with the form in, withholding on US viewer earnings is between 0% and 30%, depending on whether your country has a tax treaty with the US.

Leave it out, and an individual can face 24% backup withholding on total worldwide earnings, not just the US part. The UK has a tax treaty with the US, so fill the form in through AdSense for YouTube when asked.

Should you start?

Start if you want to build an audience and can accept earning nothing for the first year. That is the honest entry fee, and the asset you end up with is one nobody can take away.

Start soon if you are serious. From 1 February 2027 the watch hour bar for new channels doubles, so every month of head start now counts twice.

Do not start if you want money within three months. For that, our remote jobs guide pays this month rather than next year, and the £12.71 test shows which side hustles clear minimum wage from the first hour.

If you do stick with it, pick the overlap: a subject advertisers pay for that you would talk about anyway. Then treat ad revenue as the least valuable thing the channel gives you, because for almost every working creator, it is.

Frequently asked

How much does YouTube pay for 1,000 views in the UK? There is no fixed rate, and YouTube publishes no average. Your RPM depends on subject, viewer country and season, and is already after YouTube's share. In our sums we use about £2 for gaming and £6 for finance. Check your real figure on the Revenue tab in YouTube Analytics.

How many subscribers do you need to get paid on YouTube? For ad money, 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours in 12 months, or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. From 1 February 2027 new channels need 8,000 hours or 20 million Shorts views. Memberships and Super Thanks open earlier, at 500 subscribers with 3,000 watch hours or 3 million Shorts views.

Do YouTube Shorts pay anything? Yes, but far less per view than long videos. Shorts ad money goes into a shared pool, part pays for music, and you keep 45% of your slice. From 1 February 2027 you also need 10 million Shorts views in 90 days to earn from the pool that month. Treat Shorts as a way to grow.

Do I have to pay tax on YouTube money in the UK? Yes, once your gross trading income passes £1,000 in a tax year you must register for Self Assessment by 5 October after that year ends. From 6 April 2027 the allowance and reporting threshold rise to £3,000. You can deduct kit and software costs instead of using the allowance.

How long until monetisation, realistically? For people publishing steadily, often between six months and two years, and many never get there. Twelve videos a month for a year is the sort of effort that does. From February 2027 the watch hour target for new channels doubles to 8,000, so it may take longer.
