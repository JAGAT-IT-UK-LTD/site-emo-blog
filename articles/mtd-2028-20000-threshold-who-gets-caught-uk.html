MTD 2028 threshold: who the £20,000 rule catches, and the year that counts

1. What is the Making Tax Digital 2028 threshold?

The Making Tax Digital 2028 threshold is £20,000 of qualifying income. Cross it in the 2026 to 2027 tax year and you must use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax from 6 April 2028.

HMRC's policy paper is titled "Reduction of the mandation threshold from £30,000 to £20,000 from April 2028". The substance is exactly that.

Being mandated means three things. You keep digital records. You send a short summary to HMRC every quarter through compatible software. You file your end of year return through that software too.

Here is the part that makes it urgent. HMRC does not test your income in 2028. It tests the 2026 to 2027 tax year.

That year began on 6 April 2026 and ends on 5 April 2027. It is running as you read this.

So the income that decides your 2028 position is the income you are earning today. There is no later year to adjust in. The measurement is already under way.

HMRC reads the figure from your 2026 to 2027 Self Assessment return, which is due by 31 January 2028. If you are over the line, it writes to you before the April it applies from.

2. Did the November 2026 Budget change the 2028 step?

No, not on the evidence published so far. The Budget on 28 October 2026 changed the penalties, not the thresholds.

The Budget document says people joining in April 2027 will get no penalty points for late submission of their first four quarterly updates for the 2027 to 2028 tax year. HMRC updated its sign up and penalty guidance to say the same on 19 November 2026.

The same Budget paragraph adds that the government is deferring the rollout to several taxpayer groups until further notice. It does not name those groups in the main document.

What we can say for certain is what HMRC's own guidance shows on 22 November 2026. The "find out if and when" page still lists £20,000 for the 2026 to 2027 tax year, from 6 April 2028.

HMRC's exemption page, updated 12 November 2026, also still says you are automatically exempt if your qualifying income is £20,000 or less. And HMRC's press release of 5 October 2026 says the threshold "will decrease further to £20,000 from April 2028".

So plan on the 2028 step happening. If HMRC later names it as one of the deferred groups, this guide will say so.

3. Is the £20,000 turnover or profit?

Turnover. Qualifying income is gross income from self employment and property, before you deduct expenses. It is not your taxable profit.

HMRC's own press release puts it plainly. Turnover includes gross income "before any tax allowances or expenses are deducted". So the trading allowance does not lower the figure either.

This trips up more people than any other part of the rule. A business with high costs and thin margins can be well over £20,000 while its profit is a few thousand pounds. The threshold does not care about the margin.

The second trap is aggregation. If you have more than one self employment or property source, HMRC adds them together. Two small streams that each look harmless can combine into a mandated total.

The third trap is a part year. If you became a sole trader part way through the year, HMRC annualises your income. Its own example: trade for six months and it will double your income to find your qualifying figure.

If your income comes from property, HMRC says you must annualise it yourself.

4. Who gets caught at £20,000 that was safe at £30,000?

HMRC's impact note for the 2028 step says lowering the threshold brings approximately 970,000 additional individuals into scope.

They join the earlier waves. HMRC's estimate was around 780,000 people over £50,000 from April 2026. In October 2026 it said around 1,077,000 more sole traders and landlords will need to join from 6 April 2027.

£20,000 of turnover is not a large business. In practice it describes things like:

  • a single let property at around £1,700 a month in rent
  • a part time online shop turning over a few hundred pounds a week
  • a trade done at weekends alongside a full time PAYE job
  • a freelance sideline that has grown quietly over a couple of years

Many people in that group do not think of themselves as running a business. They file one tax return a year and otherwise forget about HMRC. Quarterly reporting is a different rhythm entirely.

Who is outside it? Employment income, pensions, dividends from your own company, savings interest and your share of a partnership profit do not count towards the test. Partnerships themselves are not in yet. HMRC says it will set out their timeline later.

5. A worked example: a side hustle that crosses the line

Ewan works full time for a logistics firm in Dundee and is taxed through PAYE. On the side he sells refurbished bikes. He also lets a small studio flat he owns, which counts as ordinary property income.

His figures for the 2026 to 2027 tax year are shaping up like this.

  • Bike sales, gross takings: £14,800
  • Property income, gross: £7,300

Qualifying income: £14,800 plus £7,300, which is £22,100.

That is over £20,000. On these figures Ewan is heading for Making Tax Digital from 6 April 2028.

His salary does not count, even though it is far larger and sits on the same tax return. The test looks only at self employment and property income.

His profit on the bikes, after parts, listing fees and postage, is a fraction of £14,800. That does not matter either. The test is on the £22,100.

What Ewan should notice is that he is only £2,100 over the line. If bike sales come in a little lower this year, he may fall under it. That is a real possibility, and a reason to work out his actual figure rather than assume.

Now change one detail. Say Ewan owns the flat jointly with his sister, half each. HMRC counts only his share of the rent, so £3,650. His total becomes £18,450, and he is under the line.

One more route out. HMRC sets out exemptions, including for people who are digitally excluded. That exemption has to be applied for, and you still file a normal Self Assessment return afterwards.

6. What should you do now, and what can wait?

Worth doing now: add up your gross self employment and property income for the tax year so far. Project it to 5 April 2027. That one number tells you whether 2028 applies to you.

It takes ten minutes with your bank statements. It is the only thing that settles the question.

Worth doing if you are over the line: start keeping records digitally at a natural break, such as the start of a tax year. HMRC lists compatible software, and there are free and low cost options on it. Building the habit before anything is at stake is far easier than against a deadline.

Not worth doing: buying software now for a 2028 duty. The market, the prices and HMRC's compatible list will all change before then.

Also not worth doing: panicking. Quarterly updates are summaries sent from software, not four tax bills. Your payment dates of 31 January and 31 July do not change. Your tax rates and allowable expenses do not change.

And if your gross self employment and property income is nowhere near £20,000, this rule does not apply to you. HMRC calls that an automatic exemption. You do not need to contact anyone.

7. The full MTD timetable at a glance

The threshold is never measured in the year it bites. HMRC reads a return for a tax year that has already finished, then writes to you before the April it applies from. Read down the first column to find the year your income is being counted in. Dates are from HMRC's own guidance, checked again on 22 November 2026.

So the year being measured for the £20,000 threshold is the one you are trading in today. It is the only one of the three you can still do anything about.

The honest part. HMRC says it will write to you once a return shows you are over the line. The same guidance says it is still your responsibility to check and sign up if no letter arrives. A letter that never turns up is not a defence.

Two smaller traps sit behind it. You cannot sign up unless you have filed a return in the last two years. And if your accounts run to 31 March, your start date is 1 April rather than 6 April.

Frequently asked

Which tax year decides whether I need MTD in 2028? The 2026 to 2027 tax year, which runs from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. HMRC reads your qualifying income from that year's Self Assessment return, due by 31 January 2028. If it is over £20,000, you must use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax from 6 April 2028.

Does my salary count towards the £20,000? No. Only gross income from self employment and property counts. HMRC lists employment income, State Pension, private pensions, dividends and a partnership profit share as not counting. So a full time employee with a small side business is tested on the side business and any rent alone.

Is the £20,000 MTD threshold profit or turnover? Turnover. Qualifying income is your gross takings before expenses or tax allowances are taken off. A business with £22,000 of takings and £18,000 of costs has only £4,000 profit, but it is still over the threshold and would be mandated.

What happens if I earn under £20,000? HMRC says you are automatically exempt if your qualifying income is £20,000 or less. You do not need to apply or contact HMRC. You simply keep filing a normal Self Assessment return each year, with the same deadlines and the same rules as now.

Will I have to pay tax four times a year? No. Quarterly updates are short summaries of income and expenses sent from software, not tax bills. Your payment deadlines of 31 January and 31 July stay the same, and you still file one final return for the year through the software.
