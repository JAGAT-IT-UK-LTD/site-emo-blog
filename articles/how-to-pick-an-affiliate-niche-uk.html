The Affiliate Niche Question Is Really a Maths Question. Here Is the Maths

Most advice on choosing an affiliate niche tells you to follow your passion, or to find something nobody else is covering. Neither of those things is a number. The Amazon UK commission rate card is a number, and once you put it next to a realistic basket size it settles most of the argument before you write a word.

The short version

  • The rate you earn is fixed by category, not by how good your writing is. Amazon's UK Advertising Fee Schedule, read on 11 September 2026, pays 5.0% on Home, Home Improvement, Furniture, Kitchen and Dining, Books, Automotive and Power and Hand Tools.
  • Electronics is a 2.5% niche. Appliances, Mobile Electronics and Fire TV devices all sit at 2.5%. Groceries, Amazon Fresh, Video Games and Video Game Consoles pay 1.0%.
  • Some categories pay nothing at all. Gift Cards, Android Apps, Kindle Unlimited and Wine are on 0.0%. A review of a gift card earns you precisely zero.
  • Everything not listed pays 3.0%. That is the default you land on if you have not checked.
  • Rate times basket is the only number that matters. To clear £200 a month at 2.5% you need £8,000 of tracked sales. At 5.0% you need £4,000. Same effort, half the traffic.

1. Start with the rate card, not the passion

Affiliate commission is not negotiated and it is not earned. For the Amazon UK programme it is set by a published table, and the table is the same for a site with a hundred visitors a month and a site with a million. That makes it the single most useful thing to look at before you commit two years of writing to a subject.

Here is what the Amazon.co.uk Advertising Fee Schedule paid on 11 September 2026, read directly from the signed-in Associates Central page:

6.0% Amazon Fashion Private Brands, Clothing and Accessories, Luxury, Luxury Stores Beauty, Luxury Stores Fashion, Shoes, Handbags and Wallets, Watches.

5.0% Amazon Instant Video, Audible Audiobooks, Automotive, Books, Digital Music, Furniture, Handmade, Home, Home Improvement, Jewellery, Kindle Books, Kitchen and Dining, Music, Power and Hand Tools.

4.0% Beauty, Luggage, Personal Care Appliances, Sports and Fitness.

2.5% Appliances, Fire TV Devices, Mobile Electronics.

1.0% Amazon Fresh, Grocery, Pantry, Video Games, Video Game Consoles.

0.0% Android Apps, Gift Cards, Kindle Unlimited, Wine, Coach products.

3.0% All other categories.

Read that list again with an eye for which niches the internet most often tells beginners to start in. Tech reviews. Gaming. Gadgets. Those are the 2.5% and 1.0% rows. Two cautions: the schedule changes, and it changed materially in April 2026, and these are Amazon's rates only.

2. Rate times basket is the only number that matters

A percentage on its own tells you nothing. What you actually earn is the rate multiplied by the value of what people buy after clicking, and those two things are often inversely related in exactly the wrong way.

Work backwards from a target instead of forwards from a hope. Suppose you want the site to clear £200 a month. That is a modest, realistic first milestone rather than a fantasy.

At 1.0% you need £20,000 of tracked sales a month.

At 2.5% you need £8,000.

At 3.0% you need £6,667.

At 5.0% you need £4,000.

At 6.0% you need £3,333.

The spread between the top and bottom rows is a factor of six. No amount of better writing closes a gap of six times. You close it by choosing a different category on day one, or you do not close it.

Nobody writes six times better than anybody else. But the rate card differs by six times, and it is free to read.

Now add basket size. A £700 sofa in Furniture at 5.0% pays £35 from a single decision. A £60 game at 1.0% pays 60p. High rate and high basket together is the combination worth hunting, and in the UK schedule that means Furniture, Home, Home Improvement, and the watches and fashion rows at 6.0%.

3. The three niches the rate card quietly rules out

Consumer electronics and phones. Mobile Electronics sits at 2.5%. It is also the most competitively written category in the language, and the one where readers are most likely to click your review and then buy from a retailer you do not earn from. You are taking the hardest possible competition for a below-average rate.

Gaming. Video Games and Video Game Consoles pay 1.0%. A £500 console generates £5. An enormous audience of people who love the subject, paying the lowest rate on the card, is the classic passion trap. If you want to write about games, do it because you want to, and monetise it some other way.

Anything gift card shaped. Gift Cards and Android Apps are on 0.0%. Not low. Zero. Any page whose natural conclusion is "buy them a gift card" earns nothing from the Amazon programme, no matter how many people read it.

If a niche you love falls in one of these rows, that is not a reason to abandon it. It is a reason to know, before you start, that Amazon will not be the thing that pays for it.

4. Worked example: Nadia in Luton picks between two niches

Nadia works in a school office in Luton and wants to start a site in her evenings. She has narrowed it to two things she genuinely knows about: budget gaming setups, and small kitchen appliances for people in flats.

She assumes, optimistically but not absurdly, that a page which ranks will send about 60 clicks a month to Amazon and that 4% of those clicks end in a purchase. That is 2.4 orders a month per page.

Gaming route. Average order value roughly £180, mostly games and a console every so often. 2.4 orders times £180 = £432 of tracked sales. At 1.0%, that is £4.32 a month per page.

Kitchen route. Average order value roughly £95, because a stand mixer or air fryer pulls the average up. 2.4 orders times £95 = £228 of tracked sales. At 5.0%, that is £11.40 a month per page.

The gaming pages move nearly twice as much money through Amazon and pay Nadia less than half as much. To reach £200 a month she would need about 46 gaming pages or about 18 kitchen pages. At one properly researched page a week, that is the difference between eleven months and four.

The maths did not tell Nadia what to write about. It told her what each choice costs in evenings, which is the currency she is actually spending.

The honest verdict

Run the rate card first. It takes ten minutes, it is free, and it eliminates more bad options than any amount of keyword research will. When you do reach the keyword stage, the free keyword research tools worth using will cover it without a subscription. If two niches are equally interesting to you and one pays double, that is not a close call.

Then apply the filter the rate card cannot: can you answer a real buying question honestly, from experience, including the times the answer is "do not buy this". If you cannot, the rate will not save you, because the pages will read like everyone else's and will not earn the click in the first place.

And be clear with yourself about the timescale. Even on the good side of the rate card, the worked example above needs roughly eighteen genuinely useful pages before it pays for a weekly shop. Anybody promising faster is selling you something.

Frequently asked

Should I just pick the 6.0% categories then? Not automatically. Clothing and shoes pay the top rate but have high return rates, and a returned item removes the commission. Watches and Luxury pay 6.0% on high baskets and are worth a serious look. Fashion generally is a harder business than the rate suggests.

Do other affiliate networks pay better than Amazon? Often, yes, and sometimes very much so. Amazon's advantage is conversion rate, not commission rate: people already have an account and a saved card. A 20% commission on a programme nobody buys from beats nothing, but it also earns nothing. Test both rather than assuming.

What about a niche with no products to sell at all? Then Amazon is the wrong monetisation and you should say so to yourself early. Sites about money, law or process often earn better from display advertising or their own product than from affiliate links, because there is nothing natural to buy.
