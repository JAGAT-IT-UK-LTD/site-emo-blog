1. Why is buying a laptop in the UK different in 2026?

On 14 October 2025, Microsoft ended support for Windows 10. The machines did not stop working.

They simply stopped getting free security fixes. Over time that turns an ordinary laptop into an unpatched one holding your email, banking and documents.

Microsoft offered consumer Extended Security Updates (ESU) to bridge the gap. It first said the programme would last one year, to 13 October 2026.

That has changed. Microsoft's UK page now says you can enrol any time until the programme ends on 12 October 2027.

There are three ways in. It is free if you sync your PC settings to a Microsoft account. You can also redeem 1,000 Microsoft Rewards points, or pay a one off $30 in local currency plus tax.

So a Windows 10 laptop has a longer runway than first promised. But it still has a hard stop, and only if you enrol. Our guide to Windows 10 support to October 2027 explains the enrolment steps.

The practical result is a busy secondhand market. Offices and schools have replaced fleets of Windows 10 machines, and many now sit on resale sites at tempting prices.

Some are real bargains. Some can never run Windows 11 at all. Telling them apart takes five minutes.

In 2026 the most important laptop specification is not the processor. It is whether the machine can run a supported operating system.

2. What is the five minute check that saves you £500?

Before you buy any used or refurbished Windows laptop, confirm it can run Windows 11. Microsoft's UK specifications page lists the minimum:

  • TPM 2.0, the Trusted Platform Module security chip
  • UEFI firmware, Secure Boot capable
  • A compatible 64 bit processor with two or more cores at 1GHz or faster, broadly Intel 8th generation or newer, or AMD Ryzen 2000 series or newer
  • 4GB RAM and 64GB storage as the bare minimum, though you should aim far higher
  • A display of at least 720p and more than 9 inches

The processor is the part that rules most old machines out. Microsoft keeps a list of supported chips, and many from before 2018 are not on it.

If a listing does not state the exact processor model, ask. If the seller will not say, that is your answer.

There are unofficial ways to install Windows 11 on unsupported hardware. They exist and sometimes work, but updates can stop without warning.

For a main machine that holds your bank details, that is not a risk worth taking to save a few pounds.

4. What is pure marketing?

Huge storage on a slow drive. A 1TB mechanical hard drive sounds generous and feels dreadful. A 256GB SSD beats it every day.

Touchscreens on cheap laptops. They add cost, weight and glare. Many people barely use them.

Gaming branding on a budget machine. Red keys and angular vents do not make a slow laptop fast. Real gaming laptops cost real money.

"Up to" battery figures. Makers measure battery life in ideal conditions. Expect noticeably less with a bright screen, video calls and many tabs.

Extended warranties sold at the till. You already have legal rights in the UK. Read section six before paying for a policy that may repeat them.

Paid antivirus bundles. Windows 11 includes built in protection. Our guide on whether you need paid antivirus covers when extra protection is worth it.

5. Is a refurbished laptop worth it in the UK?

For most people on a fixed budget, yes. A refurbished business laptop often gives better specifications than a new machine at the same price.

A three year old business laptop is also usually built better than a cheap new consumer model.

The catch in 2026 is age. Older refurbished stock may carry 8GB of RAM and older SSDs, and some falls the wrong side of the Windows 11 line.

The bargain is real, but only if you check the model rather than the price. The same logic applies to phones, as our look at refurbished iPhones versus new shows.

6. What are your UK consumer rights when a laptop goes wrong?

Buying from a business, including a refurbisher, gives you protection under the Consumer Rights Act 2015. Goods must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose and as described.

  1. Within 30 days you can reject a faulty item and get a full refund.
  2. Within six months, the seller must repair or replace a faulty item unless it can prove the fault was not there at sale. The burden is on them, not you.
  3. Buying online adds a 14 day right to cancel from delivery, even if nothing is wrong. You then have another 14 days to send it back.

Buy from a private person on a marketplace and almost none of this applies.

That is the real difference between a cheap listing and a slightly dearer refurbisher. It is usually worth the gap.

Worked example: Priya needs a laptop for an online course

Priya lives in Coventry and has a budget of £400. She needs a laptop for coursework, video calls and a lot of browser tabs.

The new machines she sees in her budget mostly have 8GB of RAM and a small drive. They would work, but feel tired within a year or two.

Instead she looks at refurbished business laptops from a UK refurbisher. She sets four rules: 16GB of RAM, a 512GB SSD, a Full HD screen and a 12 month warranty.

She finds one at £380 with an 11th generation Intel processor. That generation is on Microsoft's Windows 11 list, so it passes the check.

She also reads the returns policy. Because she buys online from a business, she has 14 days to cancel and 30 days to reject a fault.

Her sums are simple. £400 budget minus £380 leaves £20, enough for a laptop stand from the list below.

The one compromise is the battery, which the seller says holds about 85% of its original charge. For a machine that mostly sits on a desk, that is a fair trade.

7. What should you buy at each budget?

Under £300. Refurbished only, and expect compromises. Put an SSD and a Windows 11 capable processor above everything else.

A Chromebook is a real alternative if your work is all in a browser.

£300 to £600. The sweet spot for refurbished business machines: 16GB RAM, a 512GB SSD, a decent screen and a real warranty.

£600 to £1,000. New becomes sensible. Expect 16GB, a fast SSD, a good screen and a battery that lasts a working day.

Over £1,000. You are buying build quality, screen, weight and battery life rather than raw speed. Only worth it if you carry it daily or do heavy creative work.

8. The honest verdict

For most UK buyers in 2026, a refurbished business laptop from a proper retailer is the best value on the market. You get more specification per pound, a warranty and full consumer rights.

But only after one check. Confirm the processor supports Windows 11, insist on 16GB of RAM and an SSD, and ignore most of the marketing.

Do that and £400 buys a machine that should still feel quick in three years. Skip it and £400 may buy something you replace well before then.

Frequently asked

What laptop should I buy in the UK on a budget? For most people, a refurbished business laptop from a UK retailer with 16GB of RAM, a 512GB SSD and a Full HD screen. Check the processor is on Microsoft's Windows 11 list before paying. If your work lives in a browser, a Chromebook is a cheaper and simpler alternative.

Is it safe to keep using Windows 10? Only if you enrol in Extended Security Updates, which now run to 12 October 2027. Enrolment is free if you sync your PC settings, or costs 1,000 Rewards points or a one off $30 payment. Without it, your laptop gets no security fixes, and the risk grows every month.

How much RAM do I need in a laptop? Which? says 8GB suits most light users and 16GB suits people who run many programs or tabs. We suggest 16GB as the 2026 baseline, because memory is often soldered in and cannot be upgraded later. Treat 4GB as too little for Windows 11 in daily use.

Is it worth buying a refurbished laptop? Usually yes, if you buy from a business rather than a private seller. You keep your full rights under the Consumer Rights Act, including 30 days to reject a fault. Check the processor generation, RAM, SSD and battery health, and make sure there is a written warranty before you pay.

Is a Mac worth it on a budget? Macs hold their value and last a long time, but the entry price is high. Which? suggests a MacBook suits budgets above roughly £800. If your budget is tighter, a refurbished Windows laptop usually gives more specification per pound, as long as it passes the Windows 11 check.
