Switch and Save on Household Bills in the UK: Where the Money Is Now

Ruhul Amin · Updated 26 November 2026 · 10 min read · Figures checked November 2026

The quickest way to save on UK household bills is not switching supplier. It is paying energy by Direct Debit, worth £162 a year from 1 January 2027, then cancelling forgotten subscriptions and haggling at contract end. Insurance loyalty pricing was banned in 2022, so much of the old switching advice is out of date. Here is where the money is now, and the order to go after it.

The short version

  • The energy price cap rises 26% on 1 January 2027. Ofgem set the typical Direct Debit bill at £2,166 a year, up from £1,723.
  • How you pay is the biggest free win. From January, paying on receipt of a bill costs £2,328 for typical use, which is £162 a year more than Direct Debit for the same energy.
  • Insurance price walking has been banned since 1 January 2022. Your home or motor renewal price can be no higher than an equivalent new customer would pay. Shopping around still wins, for a different reason.
  • Broadband and mobile changed too. Contracts taken from 17 January 2025 must state any future rise in pounds and pence, not as inflation plus a percentage.
  • Providers must warn you 10 to 40 days before a contract ends, with their best deals. Most people delete that text.
  • The order matters. Payment method, then contract end dates, then the subscription audit, then haggle, then switch. Most people do it backwards.

1. What did the regulators actually change?

Almost every article about switching opens with the same line. Companies quietly raise prices for loyal customers, so loyalty is punished.

That was accurate for years. In two of the biggest categories it is now largely wrong, and repeating it sends people looking for money in the wrong place.

Banking is the exception that still pays a flat cash sum for moving, and the amounts are published rather than negotiated: what the current account switch bonuses are worth now.

What changedWhenWhat it means for you
FCA ban on price walking in home and motor insurance1 January 2022Your renewal price must be no higher than what an equivalent new customer would be quoted through the same channel
Ofcom end of contract notifications15 February 2020Your provider must tell you 10 to 40 days before your contract ends, what you will pay after, and its best deals
Ofcom rules on mid contract price risesContracts from 17 January 2025Any rise written into the contract must be set out in pounds and pence at the point of sale, not as CPI plus a percentage

Read the first row carefully, because people get it wrong in both directions. The ban stops insurers charging you more because you renewed.

It does not stop a different insurer being cheaper. It does not stop your premium rising because claims costs across the market went up. Those are different things.

The rule did not make renewal the best price. It removed one specific trick. Shopping around still wins, and now it wins for a more honest reason: insurers genuinely disagree about how risky you are.

2. Where does the loyalty penalty still cost you?

So where is the money now? Here it is, ranked by how reliably it is there.

  1. Broadband and mobile, after the contract ends. This is the clearest remaining case. Contracts end and the price steps up to a standard rate nobody would sign up for. Ofcom makes providers warn you, but the warning arrives as a text in a busy week, and most people never act on it.
  2. Energy, if you sit on the default variable tariff. The price cap limits that tariff, it does not make it cheap. Many households never leave it, so check whether you are on a fixed or a default tariff.
  3. Insurance add ons and excess levels, rather than the headline premium. Since the headline is regulated, this is where the variation lives now: legal cover, breakdown cover, key cover, and an excess that quietly rose.
  4. Anything on auto renewal that you forgot exists. Not a loyalty penalty exactly, but the same money. Citizens Advice found people spent £688 million on unused subscriptions in a year, more than double the £306 million it found at the end of 2022. Our no spend challenge guide covers the audit that finds yours, and the new subscription cancellation rules will make leaving easier.

3. How much can you save on energy without switching?

The largest free saving on UK household bills is not a switch at all. It is how you pay.

Ofgem announced on 25 November 2026 that the price cap rises 26% from 1 January to 31 March 2027. Wholesale gas costs drove it. Same energy, three different prices:

How you payOctober to December 2026January to March 2027Against Direct Debit from January
Prepayment meter£1,678£2,108£58 less
Monthly Direct Debit£1,723£2,166Baseline
Paying on receipt of a bill£1,861£2,328£162 more

These are Ofgem's annual figures for a typical home using 2,500 kWh of electricity and 9,500 kWh of gas. Your own bill depends on your use and region.

If you pay on receipt of a bill, moving to monthly Direct Debit is worth about £162 a year at the January rates. It costs one phone call. No comparison site, no switch, no exit fee, no risk.

It is the single highest value line in this article. It gets little coverage because it is not a switch and nobody earns commission on it.

The same logic applies to water, where a WaterSure or social tariff can cut the bill by far more than any change of payment method.

Two honest caveats. Direct Debit means a fixed monthly amount that can build a credit balance, and you can ask for that back. And prepayment being cheapest on the capped rates is not a reason to move to it deliberately, because it carries other drawbacks.

What else changes in January? The electricity unit rate on Direct Debit rises from 26.32p to 32.69p per kWh, and gas from 7.97p to 10.96p. Ofgem notes that VAT has been removed from electricity from 1 October 2026 to 30 June 2027, and around 20 million households will get £100 towards their energy bill from January.

Should you fix? With the cap rising this much, a fixed deal is worth pricing now. Compare unit rates and standing charges against your own annual kWh, not headline annual figures against each other, and check the exit fee. Full detail is in our price cap guide, and the free changes that beat any switch are in our energy bills guide.

4. Does haggling still work, and what do you say?

You often do not have to leave. Retentions teams have real authority to discount, and the call is shorter than people expect.

Before you call, have two things: a competitor's actual quote with the price written down, and your own contract end date. Without those you are asking for a favour. With them you are presenting a decision.

If the sticking point is the handset rather than the airtime, a refurbished phone on a SIM only deal usually beats whatever they offer to match.

What to actually say

"My contract ends on the 14th. I have a quote from [provider] at £[x] a month for [equivalent service]. I would rather not go through the hassle of moving. Can you match it?"

Then stop talking. The silence is the negotiation. The most common mistake is filling it by softening your own position.

If the first answer is no, one more line: "Can you put me through to retentions or cancellations?" The first person you speak to often cannot approve a discount. The department whose job is to stop you leaving can.

Be genuinely willing to leave. If you are not, you will accept the first small offer, and they can tell.

5. What order should you do it in?

Most people start by comparing, which is step four, and give up before finishing. This order gets the free money first.

  1. Fix your payment methods. Energy on Direct Debit rather than on receipt of a bill. This is free and immediate. Check separately whether you get the £150 Warm Home Discount automatically, because the government says around six million households will get it this winter.
  2. List every contract end date in one place, with a calendar reminder six weeks before each. This habit does more than any comparison site, because switching only works at the right moment.
  3. Audit recurring payments for things you no longer use. One evening, and it repeats every year.
  4. Compare before every renewal. Never let anything auto renew unexamined.
  5. Haggle with the quote in your hand. Ask them to match or beat it.
  6. Switch if they will not. The receiving provider usually handles it.

6. Worked example: one afternoon, one household

The Okonkwo household, three bedroom semi in Leicester

Energy. They pay quarterly on receipt of a bill because that is how it was set up years ago. Moving to monthly Direct Debit: about £162 a year at the January 2027 cap levels for typical use, one phone call, no switch.

The other annual energy line in this household is the boiler service. People cancel it first when money is tight, yet the quoted price for the same visit varies more than most tariffs do, so check what a boiler service should cost and what the engineer has to do for it before dropping the cover plan.

Broadband. The contract ended fourteen months ago and the price stepped up. A competitor quotes £6 a month less for the same speed. They call, ask for retentions, and are matched: £72 a year.

Mobile, two handsets. Both out of contract on plans that still include the cost of phones paid off long ago. Moving both to SIM only is a substantial monthly saving. The exact figure depends on what they were on, which is why we are not inventing one. Our smartphone guide shows why buying the handset separately usually beats a bundled contract.

Recurring payments. One evening with the banking app finds three forgotten subscriptions at £38.98 a month, which is £467.76 a year, including a magazine unread for fourteen months.

One recurring payment worth questioning rather than cancelling blindly is the TV licence: whether you legally need one turns on live TV and iPlayer, not on owning a set.

Insurance. They compare before renewing and find a cheaper insurer. The saving is real, but note the reason: not because their old insurer was punishing loyalty, which is now banned, but because insurers price the same house differently. The same is true of the car: the ABI's Q2 2026 tracker is built on price paid, not price quoted, and our car insurance prices guide shows why an individual renewal can sit a long way from the average.

The total from three lines: £162 plus £72 plus £467.76 is £701.76 a year, before mobile and insurance.

The pattern worth noticing: the two largest items, the payment method and the forgotten subscriptions, involved no switching at all. The housekeeping beat the switching. The discounts nobody claims belong in the same group, and the 25 per cent single person council tax discount is often worth more than any tariff move, for the cost of one form.

7. What are the traps when switching?

  • Introductory prices that step up. Always check the price after the offer ends, not the headline. Multiply the post offer price by twelve and compare on that.
  • Auto renewal. Designed to keep you paying more. Diary every renewal date so it never catches you.
  • Comparing on price alone. Cheaper insurance with a £750 excess and no legal cover is not the same product. Compare like for like, or you have not saved anything, you have removed protection.
  • Exit fees. Check before you move mid contract. A £60 saving with a £75 exit fee is a loss.
  • Cancelling a direct debit without cancelling the contract. The contract carries on, you still owe the money, and it can end up with debt collectors. Cancel with the company first, always.
  • Switching for the sake of activity. If the numbers do not clearly favour it, staying is a decision too.

The honest verdict

Switching still pays better per hour than almost anything else you will do this year, but the reasons have moved. The insurance loyalty penalty was regulated away in 2022, and broadband price rises had to be stated in pounds and pence from January 2025, so the easy story is out of date.

What is left is unglamorous and reliable. Get your energy off paying on receipt, which is worth £162 a year at January's cap for one phone call. Diary every contract end date, audit the subscriptions you forgot, and compare before every renewal with a real quote in your hand.

Broadband is now the easiest of these to move, because the one touch switch process puts the whole change in the hands of the provider you are joining. One bill that is not a switch at all: if every other adult in your home is disregarded for council tax, the 25 per cent discount applies without you living alone, which is the part most people miss.

Do those things once and you will find several hundred pounds. Then do the diary part every year, because that is the only bit that has to repeat.

If anyone in the household is on a qualifying benefit, a broadband social tariff is usually cheaper than anything you will find by switching on price alone. A bigger change is coming to the phone line itself, covered in the 31 January 2027 landline switch off and what to do before it.

Frequently asked

How can I save money on household bills in the UK? Start with the free steps. Put energy on Direct Debit, which is £162 a year cheaper than paying on receipt at January 2027 cap levels. Cancel unused subscriptions. Then list contract end dates and haggle with a competitor's quote before switching. Check benefits based help such as the Warm Home Discount and social tariffs.

If price walking is banned, why did my insurance still go up? Because the ban stops you being charged more than an equivalent new customer, not because premiums are frozen. Claims costs, repair costs and your own circumstances all move the price. Compare anyway before renewing, because insurers disagree about risk and the same home or car can be priced very differently.

Can my broadband provider still raise the price mid contract? For contracts taken from 17 January 2025, any rise must have been set out in pounds and pence before you signed. Older contracts may still be on inflation linked terms, so check what you agreed. Our telecoms charter guide and broadband price rises: the true cost cover the detail.

Is it worth switching energy supplier before January 2027? Only against your own use. Take your annual kWh from a recent bill, price it at the January cap rates and at the fixed deal's rates, and compare totals. The cap figure is for a typical home that may look nothing like yours. A smart meter can open up cheaper time of use tariffs.

Will switching hurt my credit score? Switching energy or broadband usually involves a soft check or none, and insurance quotes use soft searches, which lenders do not see. Applying for credit, such as a phone on finance, is different. If you are about to apply for a mortgage, keep credit applications to a minimum in the months before.
