The DWP Bank Account Power Is Real, and It Reaches Far Fewer People Than the Headlines Say

Ruhul Amin · Published 10 September 2026 · 6 min read · Figures checked September 2026

Direct deduction orders came into force on 24 June 2026. They let DWP instruct your bank to hand over money owed, without going to court first. They also cannot touch you at all if you are on benefits or in PAYE employment, which is most people who owe DWP money.

The short version

  • The powers are already live. The Public Authorities (Fraud, Error and Recovery) Act 2025 sections 94 to 97, 100 and 104 and Schedules 5 and 6 came into force on 24 June 2026 under SI 2026/601. Section 99, on recovering costs, follows on 26 October 2026.
  • They only apply if you are neither on benefits nor in PAYE employment. If you are, DWP already deducts from those, and this power is not used.
  • It is a last resort. DWP's own factsheet says it is for cases where repeated attempts to agree an affordable, sustainable repayment plan have failed.
  • Deductions must not cause hardship in meeting essential living expenses for you, your household or anyone financially dependent on you. Affordability and vulnerability checks come first.
  • Your bank can charge you an administration fee, capped in legislation. Agreeing a repayment plan before an order is enforced avoids it.
  • There is a driving licence power too, for debts over £1,000 where someone persistently evades payment. Only a court can order it, and it is suspended while you keep to the terms.

1. What changed, and exactly when

Until this year, if you owed DWP money and were no longer claiming benefits and no longer in PAYE work, DWP could not take it from your bank without a court order. In England and Wales it could apply for a third party debt order, but those only allow a lump sum, which is often not affordable.

The Public Authorities (Fraud, Error and Recovery) Act 2025 changed that. The commencement regulations, SI 2026/601, were made at 2.06pm on 8 June 2026 and signed by Andrew Western, Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State at DWP. They brought sections 94, 95, 96, 97, 100 and 104, and Schedules 5 and 6, into force on 24 June 2026. Section 99, which lets the Secretary of State recover the costs of recovery, comes into force on 26 October 2026.

In plain terms, section 95 inserts a new section 80C and Schedule 3ZA into the Social Security Administration Act 1992, which is the machinery for direct deduction orders. Section 96 adds the driving disqualification route, and section 97 requires the code of practice DWP has published. The design deliberately mirrors powers the Child Maintenance Service has had for years, so the operational model is not new territory.

2. Who this can and cannot reach

This is the part most coverage skips, and it is the part that decides whether you need to care.

The new powers apply to people who owe DWP money and are not on benefit and not in PAYE employment. DWP says this explicitly for both the deduction orders and the driving route. If you receive Universal Credit, or you are on a payroll, DWP already recovers through those channels and this is not the mechanism used.

The scale gives a sense of the target group. As at January 2024 there were 885,000 debtors off benefit and not in repayment. DWP's debt stock at March 2024 stood at £9.4 billion. The OBR estimated at Budget 2024 that these powers would recover an extra £565 million.

DWP's own volume estimate is modest. Once fully rolled out it expects annual bank statement requests in the tens of thousands up to around 110,000, and deduction order requests likely fewer than 20,000. Against 885,000 people in the target group, that is a small fraction.

If you are claiming a benefit or on a payroll, this power is not aimed at you. That single sentence is missing from most of what has been written about it.

3. The limits and the safeguards

Before an order is made, DWP must obtain and consider bank statements for the account covering a period of at least three months. It must give notice to the bank, to you, and, if it is a joint account, to the other account holders.

The other protections DWP has set out are these.

  • Last resort only. Recovery from benefits or PAYE earnings must not be possible, and attempts to agree an affordable plan must have failed.
  • Affordability and vulnerability checks before any deduction, so that it does not cause undue hardship in meeting essential living expenses such as food and accommodation, for you, your household or your dependants.
  • A cap on regular deductions, set in legislation, to prevent excessive amounts.
  • The right to make representations before any money moves. You are told what is proposed first.
  • A right of appeal to the First-tier Tribunal.
  • A right to apply to vary the order at any time if your circumstances change.
  • Independent complaint routes, including the Independent Case Examiner and the Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman.

One cost is easy to miss. Where a bank is instructed to make deductions, it may charge you an administration fee, capped in legislation. DWP notes you avoid that fee entirely by agreeing an affordable repayment plan before an order is enforced.

4. The driving licence power

This applies only where someone repeatedly and persistently evades repayment and the balance is more than £1,000, and again only to people not on benefit and not in PAYE employment.

DWP cannot do it alone. It has to apply to the court, and the court must first be satisfied that the debtor had the means to repay and did not, without a reasonable excuse. It must also consider whether there is an essential need to drive, including for work or caring responsibilities. Even then, the disqualification is always suspended while the person keeps to the court's repayment terms. If they stop without good reason it can take effect for up to two years, and it ends early once the debt is repaid.

Worked example: Dean Falconer in Coventry

Dean claimed Universal Credit for eight months in 2023 after a redundancy, was overpaid because a change of circumstances was recorded late, and now owes DWP £1,840. He is self-employed as a mobile mechanic, so he is off benefit and not on any payroll. He has ignored three letters.

Where he sits: squarely inside the group these powers exist for, not because of the amount but because neither existing recovery route reaches him.

If he keeps ignoring it: DWP would need at least three months of his bank statements, would have to notify him and his bank, and would run affordability and vulnerability checks. He could make representations, then appeal to the First-tier Tribunal. His bank could add its capped fee on top.

If he picks up the phone: he negotiates a figure he can meet. No order, no bank fee, no tribunal, and enforcement stops while he keeps to it. Because his balance is above £1,000, engaging also takes the driving licence route off the table, which for a mobile mechanic is not a small thing.

The arithmetic: £1,840 over 24 months is about £77 a month, agreed voluntarily. The same debt under an order is the same £1,840 plus a bank fee, decided for him rather than by him.

5. The honest verdict

This is a genuine expansion of state power over private bank accounts and it deserves scrutiny. It is also not what much of the coverage implied. It cannot be used against people on benefits or in PAYE work, cannot be used before DWP has tried to agree a plan, cannot leave someone unable to pay for food or rent, and the driving element needs a judge rather than a civil servant.

If you owe DWP money and you are off benefit and not on a payroll, the advice is unglamorous and unchanged: contact them and agree something you can genuinely afford. You keep control of the amount, you avoid the bank's fee, and you keep every option open. If you owe DWP nothing, this changes nothing for you at all, whatever a headline says.

Frequently asked

Can DWP just empty my account without telling me? No. It must obtain and consider at least three months of bank statements, give notice to you and to the bank, run affordability checks, and let you make representations before deductions start.

What about a joint account? The other account holders must be given notice as well. Joint accounts are dealt with expressly in Schedule 3ZA.

Does this apply to HMRC tax debt or council tax? No. This is DWP debt under the Social Security Administration Act 1992. HMRC and councils have their own separate powers.

Where can I read the actual rules? The Act is Public Authorities (Fraud, Error and Recovery) Act 2025, chapter 28. The commencement dates are in SI 2026/601. DWP's code of practice on direct deduction and driving disqualification orders is on GOV.UK. All three are linked below.
