Mesothelioma Compensation for Family Members Is Now Equal: What Changed on 31 October 2026

Ruhul Amin · Published 13 September 2026 · Updated 11 November 2026 · 7 min read · Figures checked November 2026

Yes, it happened. Since 31 October 2026, mesothelioma compensation for family members under the two government dust disease schemes is paid at the full rate, the same as the sufferer would have got. Before, a family received about £7,000 less on average if the person died before claiming. If someone you love has died of an asbestos disease, this guide shows who qualifies, how much is at stake and how to claim within the 12 month limit.

1. What happened to mesothelioma compensation for family members on 31 October 2026?

The change went through. Two sets of regulations came into force on 31 October 2026, and DWP announced it that day.

Bereaved families now receive the full award, the same rate the sufferer would have had. The reduced dependant rate has gone for new cases.

When this guide was first written in September, the rules were still draft and needed Parliament's approval. That condition has now been met. Both instruments are published on legislation.gov.uk as made law.

  • The Pneumoconiosis etc. (Workers' Compensation) (Payment of Claims) (Amendment) (No. 2) Regulations 2026, SI 2026 No. 1146, for the 1979 Act scheme.
  • The Mesothelioma Lump Sum Payments (Conditions and Amounts) (Amendment) (No. 2) Regulations 2026, SI 2026 No. 1148, for the 2008 scheme.

Both say they come into force on 31 October 2026. Both extend to England and Wales and Scotland.

2. Which schemes pay asbestos compensation for families?

Most people never hear of these schemes until they need one. The illness often appears decades after the work that caused it.

Workers who develop asbestos and dust related diseases, such as mesothelioma and pneumoconiosis, can claim a lump sum from the government. The condition is that they have not already got compensation from their employer.

The employer is often long gone. As DWP puts it, these diseases take years to develop, "meaning the employer responsible has usually gone out of business by the time someone is diagnosed".

You cannot sue a company that was dissolved in 1994. For many families, this is not the backup route. It is the only route.

Two schemes do the work:

  • the Pneumoconiosis etc. (Workers' Compensation) Act 1979 scheme, which covers several dust diseases
  • the 2008 Diffuse Mesothelioma Scheme, which covers mesothelioma, including people exposed outside work

Both pay lump sums to sufferers, or to their dependants after a death. Neither is the same as the separate insurer funded Diffuse Mesothelioma Payment Scheme, which is for people who can find no employer or insurer to pay.

3. How big was the gap in dust disease compensation for dependants?

Under the old rules, if the sufferer died before claiming, the family got a reduced dependant rate.

Average award to a sufferer in 2025: about £17,700.

Average award to a dependant in 2025: about £10,700.

The shortfall: about £7,000.

Same disease, same working life, same scheme. The only thing that changed the figure was whether the claim came before or after the death.

In mesothelioma, the time from diagnosis to death is often short. So the timing was out of the family's hands.

The government described families as "penalised twice, first by their loss, then by an unfair system". Campaigners and parliamentarians had raised it for over a decade.

In 2025, 2,730 sufferers received an award and 300 awards went to dependants at the lower rate. DWP now expects around 300 families a year to benefit.

4. Who gets the new full rate, and who does not?

This is the part that matters most for a family with a claim in progress. The two schemes draw the line slightly differently.

The 2008 mesothelioma scheme. The higher dependant rate applies only where the person with mesothelioma died on or after 31 October 2026. A death on 30 October falls under the old rates.

The 1979 Act scheme. The new rules apply where a person first meets the conditions for a payment on or after 31 October 2026. If entitlement arose earlier, the old rules still apply.

DWP's own summary says the change applies to new dependant claims made from 31 October 2026 and is not retrospective. If your case sits close to the date, ask the scheme team which rules apply before you assume.

The changes also simplify the sums. They remove old top up payments and references to Pneumoconiosis Medical Boards, which stopped operating years ago. They add a clear "relevant date" for award calculations.

5. Worked example: the Trevithick family in Redruth

John Trevithick worked in construction in Cornwall from the mid 1970s. He is diagnosed with mesothelioma in 2026, aged 61. The firm that employed him was wound up long ago. The 2008 scheme is his only route.

If his claim is paid while he is alive, the 2026 tariff at 61 is £50,567. It goes to him, and his wife Eileen may later inherit it through his estate.

If he had died before 31 October 2026 without claiming, Eileen would have received the dependant rate of £20,954. That is £29,613 less.

If he dies on or after 31 October 2026, Eileen now receives the full £50,567. The gap in this household closes completely.

What Eileen must do: claim within 12 months of John's death, with a copy of the death certificate.

The caveat: these are tariff figures for one age, not a promise. Awards vary with age and, under the 1979 Act, with the disability assessed. Younger claimants generally receive more.

The 1979 Act shows the same pattern. DWP's example is a 60 year old with a 31 to 40 per cent disablement assessment, at the 2026 to 2027 rate.

2008 mesothelioma scheme£56,656£24,526£56,656
1979 Act, 31 to 40% disablement£34,062£21,011£34,062

6. Why does the £7,000 average understate it?

The £7,000 figure is an average across all awards. Averages hide what matters here.

The schemes pay on a sliding scale by age. Under the old rules, the dependant scale fell away much faster than the sufferer scale. At 60 the gap was not £7,000. It was £32,130.

The figures below are the 2008 scheme tariffs from 1 April 2026, after a 3.8 per cent uprating. The dependant column is the old reduced rate. From 31 October 2026 it no longer applies to new deaths.

Three things stand out. The old dependant scale stopped moving at 67, so every older death got the same £10,387.

The loss was worst in the middle of the scale. At 60, a dependant got about 43 per cent of the sufferer rate.

And the £7,000 average only makes sense because most claims come from the oldest bands. There, the two columns were already close.

So for a family where the person died in their fifties or sixties, the change is worth tens of thousands of pounds. That is why it is worth knowing about now.

7. How does a family member claim?

GOV.UK is clear on the deadline. A dependant must claim within 12 months of the death.

Call the Industrial Injuries Disablement Benefit helpline on 0800 279 2322 to check eligibility. The claim form for lung diseases, PWC1, is on GOV.UK. Send a copy of the death certificate with it.

If you need certificate copies, our guide to death certificate copies and what they cost sets out the fees.

A lump sum may also affect the estate and other help. Our guides to Bereavement Support Payment and its three month rule and what probate really costs are worth reading next.

Free specialist help is available. Mesothelioma UK and the asbestos support groups know these schemes far better than a general adviser.

8. What does this not do?

Being clear about the limits matters here. Many readers are in the hardest weeks of their lives and cannot afford false hope.

It does not create a new right to compensation. The eligibility rules are unchanged. If you did not qualify before, you still do not.

It does not replace a claim against an employer. If the employer or its insurer still exists, a civil claim is a separate and usually larger route. That is a question for a solicitor.

It does not backdate. DWP says it is not retrospective. Awards already paid at the old dependant rate will not be topped up.

Northern Ireland is separate. Its schemes are run by the Department for Communities. DWP said on 31 October that it is "expected to introduce similar changes in due course".

Legislation.gov.uk does list Northern Ireland amendment regulations for both schemes in 2026, with a 31 October 2026 start. We have not confirmed the rates they set, so check with the Department for Communities before relying on them.

The honest verdict

This was a decade old unfairness with no defenders, and it has now been fixed. A widow no longer gets thousands less because the illness moved faster than the paperwork.

The practical reach is narrower than the headline. It creates no new right and does not backdate. If a relative died before 31 October 2026 and the award was paid at the old rate, this does not reach you.

Where it matters is for deaths from 31 October 2026 onwards. If that is your family, claim within 12 months and check the award uses the full rate. For anyone who died in their fifties or sixties, the difference can be tens of thousands of pounds.

Frequently asked

Can family members claim mesothelioma compensation in the UK? Yes. A dependant can claim under the 1979 Act or the 2008 mesothelioma scheme if the person died without claiming and no employer paid compensation. From 31 October 2026, new dependant claims get the same rate the sufferer would have received. The claim must be made within 12 months of the death.

My relative's claim was paid years ago at the lower rate. Will we get the difference? No. DWP says the change is not retrospective. It applies to new dependant claims from 31 October 2026. For the 2008 scheme, the higher rate only applies where the person died on or after that date. Awards already paid at the old dependant rate will not be topped up.

How much is mesothelioma compensation for a family member? It depends on age. Under the 2008 scheme, a dependant of someone who died at 60 now receives £56,656, up from £24,526. At 77 or over it is £18,733. Under the 1979 Act, the disablement assessment also matters. In 2025 the average award to a sufferer was about £17,700.

Does this apply in Scotland and Northern Ireland? The new rules cover England, Wales and Scotland. Northern Ireland runs its own schemes through the Department for Communities, which DWP said was expected to make similar changes. Northern Ireland amendment regulations are listed for 2026, but check the current rates with the Department for Communities before relying on them.

Who counts as a dependant? The 1979 Act sets out who counts, and GOV.UK does not list it in full on its dependants page. The definition is detailed, so do not guess. Call the Industrial Injuries Disablement Benefit helpline on 0800 279 2322, or ask a support group, before deciding whether to claim.
