The Government Charges £92 to Register a Power of Attorney. Everything Else You Are Quoted Is Somebody Else's Fee

Ruhul Amin · Published 13 September 2026 · 7 min read · Figures checked September 2026

There is one compulsory cost in setting up a lasting power of attorney and it is published, fixed and the same for everybody. Almost every quote you will be given is that number with something added on top. Knowing which part is which is worth a few hundred pounds.

The short version

  • £92 to register one LPA. That is the Office of the Public Guardian fee and it is the only compulsory cost.
  • £184 for both types. There are two, a personal welfare LPA and a property and affairs LPA, and each is registered separately at £92.
  • A reduction if you earn under £12,000, and an exemption if you are on certain benefits including Income Support. You apply for it with a separate form.

1. The fee, and what it does and does not buy

Applying to register a lasting power of attorney with the Office of the Public Guardian costs £92. If you want both a personal welfare LPA and a property and affairs LPA, it costs £184 in total. Those are the published figures on GOV.UK and they are the same for everyone.

Two things follow from that, and both are worth being clear about.

First, the £92 is a registration fee, not a drafting fee. Making the LPA and registering it are separate steps. You can make one yourself, on the government's own online service, and then pay £92 to register it. In that case £92 is the entire cost.

Second, every larger number you are quoted is somebody's charge on top. A solicitor drafting an LPA for you is doing real work and may be worth paying, particularly where there is a business, a blended family, property abroad or a likely dispute. But the £92 does not go to them and does not go away if you use them. If a quote does not separate the OPG fee from the professional fee, ask for it in writing split out.

You can pay by credit card, debit card or cheque. Cheques go to "Office of the Public Guardian" with your name on the back, sent with the forms to their Birmingham PO Box.

2. The reduction and exemption almost nobody claims

This is the part that is genuinely underused, and it is two sentences on the GOV.UK page.

You can apply for a reduction if you earn less than £12,000.

You might also be able to apply for an exemption if you are on certain benefits, such as Income Support.

Neither is automatic. You download and fill in a separate application form, and that form carries the detail on eligibility. The threshold to remember is £12,000 of income, which is low enough that a lot of people assume it will not apply to them and high enough that for pensioners on a small private income, part-time workers and people who have stopped work to care for someone, it very often does.

It is worth saying what this is not. It is not means-testing on savings in the way benefit rules usually work, and the page does not describe it that way. It is framed on earnings and on being in receipt of certain benefits. If you are close to the line, fill the form in and let them decide rather than deciding for them.

On £184 for a couple registering one LPA each, a successful exemption is the difference between a real cost and no cost at all, for the price of one form.

3. The timetable, which matters more than the money

An LPA that is not registered does not work. That sounds obvious and it is the single most common expensive misunderstanding in this whole subject, because the gap between signing and being able to use it is not short.

It takes 8 to 10 weeks to register an LPA if there are no mistakes in the application. That is GOV.UK's own figure and the qualifier at the end is doing work.

Before you even start that clock, there is another one. Before registering, you send an LP3 form to everyone you listed as a person to notify. They then have 3 weeks to raise concerns with the OPG. If you use the online service it creates and fills in the LP3 forms for you.

So the realistic end to end timetable is the notification period plus 8 to 10 weeks, assuming nothing is wrong. Call it three months from finishing the form to holding something usable.

Why that matters: people usually think about an LPA at the moment it becomes urgent, and at that moment three months is not available. The whole point of the document is that it has to exist before it is needed. An LPA sitting registered in a drawer for fifteen years is the document working correctly.

You can register it yourself if you are able to make your own decisions. Your attorney can also register it for you, and you will be told if they do, and you can object to the registration.

4. Worked example: the Okonkwo family in Luton

Grace is 71 and lives in Luton. Her state pension and a small works pension come to about £11,400 a year. Her son Emeka wants to be named on both types of LPA so that he can help with the bank and, if it ever comes to it, with care decisions.

The headline cost: two LPAs at £92 each is £184.

The cost after the reduction: Grace earns under £12,000, so she can apply for a fee reduction. She fills in the OPG fee form and sends it with the applications. That single form is the difference between £184 and something less, and on the published rules she is squarely inside the threshold.

What a high street quote would have been: if she had gone to a solicitor and been quoted a single all-in figure, the £184 would have been inside it and the reduction would very likely never have been mentioned, because the reduction is hers to claim and not the firm's job to chase.

The timetable: she posts the LP3 forms to her two people to notify in mid September. They have 3 weeks. She applies to register at the start of October. On the published 8 to 10 weeks, registration completes somewhere between early December and Christmas. If she had waited until there was a reason to need it, that is three months of not having it.

The number that actually matters here is not £184. It is the three months. The fee is a one-off that most households can find. The delay is the thing that cannot be bought out of.

5. The £46 mistake, and how to not make it

GOV.UK puts it carefully: depending on the type of mistake, OPG may let you correct it and apply again within 3 months for £46.

Read the hedges. May let you. Depending on the type of mistake. Within 3 months. So the half price second attempt is a discretion, not a right, and it has a deadline. A mistake that falls outside it means paying the full £92 again, and restarting the 8 to 10 weeks.

The practical defence is dull and effective. Use the government's own online service to create the form rather than a printed template, because it checks as you go and generates the LP3 notifications for you. Then get someone else to read the signed form before it goes in the post, specifically checking names, dates and signature order, which is where these applications usually die.

Two more things that are easy to get wrong. If you made your LPA on an older paper form, you register with form LP2 only if it was LPA114 or LPA117 before 1 January 2016, or LP PA or LP PW before 1 April 2011. Otherwise you have to make a new one. And you must send the original LPA form with the fee, or a certified copy with a covering letter explaining why you do not have the original.

The honest verdict

As government fees go, £92 for something this consequential is not unreasonable, and the fact that you can do the whole thing yourself for exactly that is the part most people do not realise. For a straightforward situation, one person, one set of savings, no business and no family argument brewing, the online service plus £92 is genuinely all this needs to cost.

Pay a solicitor when the situation is not straightforward, and be honest with yourself about which one you are in. A business, property abroad, a second marriage with children from a first, or an attorney you suspect somebody will challenge, are all good reasons to pay for advice. Wanting the form filled in correctly is not, because the government's own service does that.

The two things to act on today are the ones that cost nothing. Check the £12,000 earnings threshold, because the reduction is claimed by application and nobody will offer it to you. And start earlier than feels necessary, because three weeks of notification plus 8 to 10 weeks of registration is a long time to discover you needed this last month.

Frequently asked

Do I need both types of LPA? They cover different things. One is personal welfare, one is property and affairs, and they are registered separately at £92 each. Plenty of people register only the property and affairs one, and that is a decision about what you want covered rather than about the fee.

Can I really do it without a solicitor? Yes. GOV.UK provides an online service to make the LPA, and you can apply to register it yourself if you are able to make your own decisions. Whether you should depends on how complicated your circumstances are.

How long before I can use it? Registration takes 8 to 10 weeks with no mistakes, and before that your people to notify get 3 weeks to object. An unregistered LPA cannot be used.

What if I get it wrong? OPG may allow a corrected reapplication within 3 months for £46, depending on the type of mistake. It is a discretion rather than a guarantee, so it is worth having a second person check the form before it goes.
