Advisory Fuel Rates September 2026: Your Company Car Rate, and the 8p Electric Gap

The advisory fuel rates from 1 September 2026 are 14p to 27p a mile for petrol, 15p to 22p for diesel, and 7p or 15p for an electric car. Four bands moved, and the electric rates did not. These figures hold until HMRC's next review on 1 December 2026, and they matter most if you drive an electric company car and cannot charge at home.

1. What are the advisory fuel rates from 1 September 2026?

HMRC publishes advisory fuel rates every quarter. The set below applies from 1 September 2026. It is taken from HMRC's advisory fuel rates page on GOV.UK, last updated on 21 August 2026, and checked again in November 2026.

Four bands moved on 1 September, and three of them moved down. Petrol over 2000cc rose a penny to 27p.

Diesel between 1601cc and 2000cc fell from 17p to 16p. Diesel over 2000cc fell from 23p to 22p. LPG over 2000cc fell from 21p to 20p.

If your employer set the rate in June and has not looked since, the picture is mixed. The driver of a 2.0 diesel is paid a penny a mile more than the approved amount. The driver of a 2.5 petrol is paid a penny a mile less.

How HMRC works the numbers out

None of this is a judgement call. HMRC uses a pump price for each fuel: 159.9p a litre for petrol, 179.2p for diesel and 95.0p for LPG.

Each price is divided by a mean miles per gallon for the engine band, taken from manufacturer figures. The result is rounded to the nearest whole penny.

The electric rates run on 3.59 miles per kilowatt hour. Electricity is priced at 24.47p per kWh at home and 54.00p per kWh on a public charger.

That is the whole reason the public rate is more than double the home rate. It is also why a driver who charges away from home, but is paid the 7p home rate, loses money on every business mile.

Petrol. 1400cc or less, 14p. 1401cc to 2000cc, 17p. Over 2000cc, 27p.

Diesel. 1600cc or less, 15p. 1601cc to 2000cc, 16p. Over 2000cc, 22p.

LPG. 1400cc or less, 11p. 1401cc to 2000cc, 13p. Over 2000cc, 20p.

Fully electric. Home charger 7p. Public charger 15p.

Two quirks are worth knowing. Diesel beats petrol at the small end, 15p against 14p, then loses at the top, 22p against 27p.

Hybrids are treated as either petrol or diesel cars. There is no hybrid column to look for.

2. What are advisory fuel rates actually for?

This is where most of the confusion lives. Advisory fuel rates do two jobs, and neither is the job people assume.

  • Reimbursing business fuel in a company car. Your employer can pay you this much per business mile to cover fuel, with no tax or National Insurance to pay on it.
  • Repaying private fuel. If your employer pays for all your fuel, you can use these rates to pay back the private miles. That avoids the car fuel benefit charge, which is expensive and all or nothing.

Advisory fuel rates against the 55p mileage rate

Advisory fuel rates are not the rate for using your own car. That is the approved mileage allowance payment.

For the 2026 to 2027 tax year it is 55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles, up from 45p, and 25p after that. Our guide to the HMRC mileage rate rise to 55p covers that change in full.

The two numbers are far apart for a reason. The 55p rate covers the whole cost of running a car, including insurance, servicing and wear. An advisory fuel rate covers fuel and nothing else.

If you are paid 17p a mile for using your own car, someone has used the wrong table. If your employer pays less than 55p, you may be able to claim tax relief on the difference. Our guide to claiming mileage relief with a P87 shows how.

One more detail matters for anyone with an electric company car. On HMRC's own wording, electricity is not a fuel for car fuel benefit purposes.

3. Who loses on the 7p and 15p electric split?

Splitting the electric rate by where you charge is sensible. The costs genuinely differ by that much. It also creates the clearest unfairness on the page.

The 7p home rate assumes a domestic electricity price. The 15p public rate assumes public charging prices, which are much higher per kWh.

If you have a driveway and a home charger, 7p is close to reality. If you live in a flat or a terrace with no off street parking, you cannot reach the cheaper option.

Your employer's policy may still only offer 7p, because that is the number someone put in the expenses system.

That is an 8p per mile gap. It falls on people whose housing is the only reason they are on the wrong side of it.

If you are weighing up a charger of your own, see what an EV home charger costs to install. For the wider running cost picture, our guide to electric car against petrol cost per mile in 2026 sets the numbers side by side.

4. Worked example: Tom in Swindon

Tom, Swindon. Fully electric company car, 7,000 business miles a year. He rents a terrace with no off street parking, so every charge is a public one.

Reimbursed at the home rate of 7p. 7,000 at 7p is £490.

Reimbursed at the public rate of 15p. 7,000 at 15p is £1,050.

The gap is £560 a year, out of his own pocket, for business miles he drove for his employer.

What fixes it. Not a claim form. His employer applying the correct rate for how he actually charges. HMRC publishes both figures because both situations exist. An expenses policy that only knows about 7p is ignoring half the guidance.

The same sum works for any band. A driver of a 2.0 diesel doing 7,000 business miles gets £1,120 at 16p. At the old June rate of 17p, it was £1,190.

5. Can you use a higher rate than the table?

Yes. The advisory rates are a safe harbour, not a cap.

HMRC's guidance says an employer can pay a higher rate where the actual cost per mile is higher and can be shown. That does not create a taxable profit.

That matters in three situations:

  • Public charging. Covered above, and the cleanest case, because the receipts exist.
  • Genuinely thirsty vehicles. A large engine working hard, towing or fully loaded, can beat the banded assumption.
  • Pump prices moving mid quarter. The rates are set four times a year and fuel prices are not. There are stretches where the published figure lags the forecourt.

The condition is evidence. Keep the charging or fuel receipts and a mileage log.

Without those, a higher rate is only a claim. Any excess over the advisory rate is then taxable pay.

6. When do the advisory fuel rates change next?

HMRC reviews the rates on 1 March, 1 June, 1 September and 1 December. The next review is 1 December 2026.

We will not guess the December figures. HMRC usually posts the new table on GOV.UK shortly before the date it applies.

After any change, you can keep using the previous rates for up to one month from the date the new ones apply. So the September rates can still be used during December.

Pump prices feed straight into the petrol and diesel bands. Our note on the fuel duty rise that was moved to July 2027 explains one reason pump prices have not jumped this autumn.

The honest verdict

Advisory fuel rates are boring and small. They are still worth ten minutes once a quarter, because the amounts add up quietly and nobody is checking them for you.

Two things are worth acting on from this set. If you drive your own car and are paid an advisory fuel rate, that is the wrong table, and the right number is 55p.

If you drive an electric company car and cannot charge at home, the public rate of 15p exists for you. Being paid 7p is not a rule. It is an expenses policy that has only read half the guidance.

Both are fixed by a short conversation with payroll, not by a claim to HMRC.

Frequently asked

Do advisory fuel rates apply to my own car? No. If you use your own car for business, the figure is the approved mileage allowance payment. For the 2026 to 2027 tax year that is 55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles, then 25p a mile after that. Advisory fuel rates apply only to company cars, for fuel and nothing else.

What is the advisory fuel rate for electric cars? From 1 September 2026 it is 7p a mile if you charge at home and 15p a mile if you charge on a public charger. Both figures are unchanged from June. HMRC bases them on 24.47p per kWh at home and 54.00p per kWh in public, at 3.59 miles per kWh.

What about a hybrid? HMRC treats hybrid cars as either petrol or diesel cars for advisory fuel rate purposes. Use the petrol or diesel band that matches the engine and its size. There is no separate hybrid rate, and a plug in hybrid cannot use the electric rates, even if you charge it at home most nights.

How often do advisory fuel rates change? Every quarter, on 1 March, 1 June, 1 September and 1 December. This set applies from 1 September 2026, and the next review is 1 December 2026. You can use the previous rates for up to one month after new ones start. Between reviews, the figures can lag real pump prices.

Can my employer pay less than the advisory rate? Yes, and some do. The advisory rate is the amount that can be paid without a tax charge, not a minimum. If you are paid less than the real cost of business fuel in a company car, that is a matter for your employer's expenses policy, not something HMRC will refund to you.
