Food Delivery Driver Earnings in the UK: What £17.18 an Hour Really Pays

Food delivery driver earnings in the UK come to about £8.50 an hour by car in our worked example, after fuel, insurance and waiting time. That is well under Deliveroo's £17.18 car floor, because the floor only covers time on an order. Here is the same job costed properly, with Uber Eats, Just Eat and the HMRC mileage rule that now favours the car.

The short version

  • The pay floor is real but narrow. For 2026/27, Deliveroo and the GMB union agreed minimum fees per hour of estimated order time: £17.18 by car, £14.40 by scooter, £12.99 by eBike and £12.88 by bike. It runs from accepting an order to marking it delivered. Waiting for the next offer is not in it.
  • Our worked example lands at £8.50 an hour by car, across 12 hours a week at peak only, after fuel, insurance and running costs. That is below the £12.71 National Living Wage for 2026/27.
  • Off peak it falls to about £4.02 an hour. The biggest driver of your real pay is not the app. It is the share of your logged in time you spend on an order.
  • Uber Eats and Just Eat publish no hourly floor. Both pay per job. Uber shows the fee before you accept. Just Eat's own courier site says you earn for the deliveries you complete, paid weekly, as an independent contractor.
  • Hire and reward insurance is not optional. Just Eat's courier site says car couriers need it, and that standard personal insurance may not give the cover you need.
  • The tax relief favours the car. From 6 April 2026 the HMRC flat rate for cars rose from 45p to 55p a mile for the first 10,000 miles. Motorcycles stayed at 24p. Bicycles are not on the list.
  • A bike earned almost the same as a car in our figures, £8.52 against £8.50 an hour, with no fuel bill and no motor insurance to buy.

What does the Deliveroo pay floor actually guarantee?

Deliveroo is unusually open about rider pay, so it is worth quoting precisely. Under its agreement with the GMB union, the 2026/27 minimum fees per hour, inclusive of vehicle costs, are:

  • Car: £17.18
  • Scooter: £14.40
  • eBike: £12.99
  • Bike: £12.88

These took effect on 1 April 2026. At the car rate, a 20 minute order works out at £5.73 at least.

Now the qualifier that changes everything. The floor applies to the time Deliveroo estimates it will take you to complete an order, from the moment you accept it to the moment you mark it delivered.

It is a floor on engaged time, not on session time. Log in at 5pm, get your first offer at 5:22pm, and those 22 minutes sit outside the guarantee entirely.

Deliveroo does not hide this. But many "how much do Deliveroo drivers make in the UK" answers treat £17.18 as an hourly wage, and it is not one.

The number that matters to you is simple. Fees earned, divided by the hours you could not do anything else.

Tips sit on top. Deliveroo's rider help pages say riders receive 100% of tips, paid with the weekly fees.

How much do Uber Eats and Just Eat drivers make in the UK?

Nobody can tell you an honest hourly figure for these two, because neither platform publishes one. Most numbers you see online come from salary sites or insurers, built on surveys and job adverts. We do not repeat them here, because we cannot trace them to the platforms.

Uber Eats. Uber says the fee for each delivery is shown before you choose to accept, decline or ignore a request, and you receive it plus any tips from the customer. Uber also says earnings structures differ by city and its information does not guarantee earnings. We found no published UK engaged time floor for Uber Eats couriers like Deliveroo's.

Just Eat. Just Eat's own courier site says you earn money for the deliveries you complete, paid into your bank account each week. Couriers accept their status as independent contractors. The site gives no pay rate.

That status is being challenged. Law firm Leigh Day is running a group claim asking for Just Eat couriers to be treated as workers, which would bring minimum wage and holiday pay rights. No judgment is reported on its claim page as of November 2026.

So Deliveroo is used through this guide because its published numbers can be checked. The cost side below applies to all three apps the same way.

Parcels run on a different model again. You book a fixed block and the pay is set before you start, which is why what an Amazon Flex block really pays is worth comparing against a food shift.

Which costs are never in the advert?

Fuel. In the week of 23 November 2026, average UK unleaded was 161.96p a litre on the government's weekly road fuel price series.

Urban delivery driving is short hop, cold engine, stop start work. We assume 32mpg for a small petrol car, which is our assumption, not a published figure. That works out at roughly 23.0p of fuel per mile.

Hire and reward insurance. This is the one that catches people out. Once you carry food for payment, an ordinary social, domestic and pleasure policy may not cover the use you are making of the car.

Just Eat's courier site says car couriers need "the correct Hire and Reward insurance". Driving without cover for the use in question is an offence under the Road Traffic Act 1988, and a claim during a delivery on the wrong policy can be declined.

Premiums depend heavily on age, postcode and history, so get your own quotes. Our example below uses £1,200 a year, which is an assumption to show the maths. Pay as you go cover charged by the hour also exists and suits occasional work.

Spread across a 12 hour week for 48 weeks, a £1,200 policy is £25 a week before you deliver anything. It is a fixed cost, so the fewer hours you work, the worse your hourly rate gets.

Everything else. Tyres, brakes, servicing, a phone charger, mobile data, parking. Small on their own, real in total. We use £8 a week below.

Worked example: Tomasz in Luton, 12 hours a week

Tomasz has a full time job and wants an evening top up. He has a 2016 petrol hatchback, pays £1,200 a year for hire and reward cover and works 12 hours a week.

We run him twice. The only thing that really moves the answer is how much of his time he spends on an order.

Scenario A, peak only. Friday and Saturday 6pm to 10pm plus Sunday evening. Demand is high, so 80% of his logged in time is engaged. He covers about 130 miles.

  • Engaged time 9.6 hours × £17.18 = £164.93
  • Fuel: 130 miles × 23.0p, less £29.91
  • Insurance: £1,200 ÷ 48 weeks, less £25.00
  • Running costs, less £8.00
  • Net £102.02 for 12 hours, which is £8.50 an hour

Scenario B, weekday afternoons. The same 12 hours in quieter periods. Engaged time drops to 50% and he covers 95 miles.

  • Engaged time 6.0 hours × £17.18 = £103.08
  • Fuel £21.86, insurance £25.00 and running costs £8.00 come off
  • Net £48.22 for 12 hours, which is £4.02 an hour

Same person, same car, same app, same hours. Choosing the wrong hours of the week costs him more than half his money.

Tips are on top of both figures. They are welcome, but they are not a plan.

A neighbour on a pedal bike made £8.52 an hour. The car made £8.50. The car's higher fee is almost exactly eaten by the cost of being a car.

That bike run used 70% engaged time: 8.4 hours × £12.88 = £108.19, less about £6 a week for tyres, lights and upkeep. That leaves £102.19, or £8.52 an hour, with no fuel bill and no insurance premium.

Over a year of 48 such weeks, Tomasz's peak plan nets about £4,897 before tax. That is roughly £408 a month averaged over twelve months, for 12 hours a week.

Both hourly figures sit below the £12.71 National Living Wage for workers aged 21 and over from April 2026. It rises to £13.21 in April 2027, which widens the gap.

The scenarios are ours, built on Deliveroo's published floor and public cost data. Your engaged share is the number to test, and you can measure it in a week.

Which mileage rule should a delivery driver use?

This is the part few guides cover, and it changed in April 2026.

HMRC's simplified expenses flat rates for the 2026/27 tax year are:

  • Cars and goods vehicles, first 10,000 business miles: 55p, up from 45p before 6 April 2026
  • Cars and goods vehicles after that: 25p, unchanged
  • Motorcycles: 24p, unchanged
  • Bicycles: not on the list, so no flat rate exists

Take Tomasz's peak year: 130 miles a week over 48 weeks is 6,240 business miles. As a car he can deduct £3,432.

Riding the same routes on a moped he could deduct £1,497.60. That is £1,934.40 less relief for the same journeys.

For someone whose profit sits in the basic rate band, that is worth roughly £503 a year. That counts 20% income tax and 6% Class 4 National Insurance, the 2026/27 rate on profits between £12,570 and £50,270. On a pedal bike he must track actual costs instead, which for most riders comes to very little.

Two warnings. First, mileage relief cuts taxable profit. It is not cash back, and if your total income is under the £12,570 personal allowance it is worth nothing to you.

Second, the flat rate is used instead of the actual costs of buying and running the vehicle, such as insurance, fuel, servicing and repairs. Once you use flat rates for a vehicle, you must keep using them for as long as you use it for the business.

So which is better? At 23.0p a mile of fuel, a £1,200 premium and 10p a mile for servicing and tyres, claiming actual costs beats the 55p flat rate below about 5,450 business miles a year. Above that, the flat rate wins.

That crossover is our calculation, and it moves with your own premium. GOV.UK has a simplified expenses checker to run it on your figures before you file.

Do food delivery drivers have to tell HMRC?

Nothing new is being taxed, but much more is being seen. Food delivery counts as a personal service under the digital platform reporting rules, which started on 1 January 2024.

Platforms send HMRC a yearly report of what you earned. Income from 1 January to 31 December 2024 was reported by 31 January 2025, and every year since works the same way.

The £1,000 trading allowance applies for 2026/27 and covers all your trading income together, not each app separately. Go over it and you must register for Self Assessment, by 5 October 2027 for this tax year.

From 6 April 2027 the allowance triples to £3,000, as confirmed in the Budget policy paper. Our guide to the £3,000 trading allowance explains who that helps. For the wider rules, see our side hustle tax rules guide.

One more check now applies to gig work. Platforms have to confirm you have the right to work in the UK, which we cover in right to work checks for gig workers.

Who does food delivery genuinely work for?

Being straight about it: as a way to reliably beat minimum wage, food delivery by car does not do it in our figures. But three situations still make sense.

You already own a bike and live in a dense city centre. No fuel, no premium, short hops, high engaged time. This is the version that works, and the version the adverts show least.

You need money this week and cannot wait for a payroll cycle. Deliveroo and Just Eat both pay weekly, and starting is quick. That speed has value an hourly comparison does not capture.

You want strictly bounded hours around something else. Three evenings and nothing more, no notice, no rota. Few jobs flex like that.

The honest verdict

The published pay floor is accurate, and Deliveroo deserves credit for putting it in writing. It just measures something narrower than the thing you care about.

Engaged time is not session time, and the gap between them is where your hourly rate goes. Uber Eats and Just Eat publish no floor at all, so the same gap applies with less to anchor it.

By car, at peak, in our example, this is about £8.50 an hour before tax. Off peak it is closer to £4. Neither reaches the £12.71 minimum wage, and no app strategy closes a gap that large.

If you have a bike and a city centre, the maths works. If you would be buying a car or a hire and reward policy just to do this, the numbers say do not.

Before you commit, work one 4 hour peak session. Write down your fees and your logged in minutes, and divide. Everything above is our arithmetic. That number is yours.

Frequently asked

How much do Deliveroo drivers make in the UK? Deliveroo guarantees at least £17.18 an hour by car for estimated time on an order, not for waiting. In our worked example a Luton driver working 12 peak hours a week nets about £8.50 an hour after fuel and insurance, or roughly £408 a month. Quiet hours can halve that.

How much do Uber Eats drivers make in the UK? Uber does not publish a UK hourly figure or a minimum. It shows the fee for each delivery before you accept, and you keep it plus any customer tips. Uber says earnings differ by city. Your real hourly rate depends on how busy your area is and on your vehicle costs, so track a week of fees against hours logged in.

How much do Just Eat drivers make in the UK? Just Eat pays per completed delivery, weekly, to self employed couriers, and its courier site gives no rate. A group legal claim is asking for couriers to be treated as workers with minimum wage rights, with no judgment reported yet. Until then, your pay is fees per job minus your own costs.

Do I need special insurance for occasional delivery? Yes. The need comes from the use, not how often you do it. One paid delivery on a social, domestic and pleasure policy may be a delivery driven without cover for that use. Pay as you go hire and reward cover, charged by the hour, exists for irregular work and can cost less than an annual policy.

Are delivery riders employees or workers? Usually neither. In November 2023 the Supreme Court held that Deliveroo riders were not in an employment relationship for collective bargaining, largely because of their right to send a substitute. Uber private hire drivers were found to be workers in 2021. Status depends on the facts, so one platform's ruling does not settle another's.
