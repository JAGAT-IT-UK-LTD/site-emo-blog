Twitch's own help pages now put Affiliate at 25 followers, four hours and four streaming days. The short version

  • Affiliate now needs four things: 25 followers, 4 hours streamed, 4 different streaming days, and an average of 3 viewers on 4 different days. All within the same rolling 30 day window.
  • The widely quoted 50 followers, 500 minutes and 7 days are no longer what Twitch's help page says. Check the Achievements section of your own dashboard rather than an article.

1. What Affiliate actually requires now

Twitch's help article on joining the Affiliate Program lists four requirements, and it lists them as a simple set:

  • Reach 25 followers
  • Stream for 4 hours
  • Stream on 4 different days
  • Reach an average of 3 viewers, on 4 different days

Search for these requirements and you will overwhelmingly be told 50 followers, 500 total minutes broadcast and 7 unique broadcast days. That was the bar for years, and a great deal of otherwise good advice still repeats it. Twitch's current page does not.

The practical difference is large. Four hours is a quarter of the old 500 minute requirement expressed as hours, four days rather than seven, and half the followers. If you looked at the old numbers, decided it was out of reach and stopped, it is worth looking again.

Three details that are easy to get wrong, all stated by Twitch.

All four must land in the same 30 day window. Twitch points out that your Creator Dashboard shows all time stats while the Achievements section shows the last 30 days, which is why the two disagree. Only the Achievements view is the one that counts.

The 3 viewer requirement is gentler than it looks. It means an average of 3 concurrent viewers, not 3 views in total, and Twitch says explicitly that days below 3 average viewers do not count against you. Its own example: stream 10 days out of 30, have 3 or more average viewers on 4 of them, and you have met it. The other 6 days are simply ignored.

Two factor authentication is required before you can begin Affiliate onboarding, for Affiliates and Partners alike. Set it up before you qualify rather than discovering it on the day.

Once eligible, the invite is automatic. Twitch says it typically arrives by email and notification within 24 to 48 hours, and in some cases up to 72. You do not need the email to start: onboarding is available in the Creator Dashboard.

If you are 13 to 17 you may join with parental or guardian consent and supervision, and Twitch publishes a separate guide for parents and educators on monetisation and minors.

Affiliate unlocks subscriptions, Bits, ad revenue, Channel Points, and VOD storage of 14 days instead of the standard 7.

2. What Partner requires, and why it is a different sport

Read the two sets of criteria next to each other and the gap is startling.

Twitch's Partner criteria: complete the Path to Partner achievement, and for the past two consecutive months prior to your application, have at least 6 individual streams that meet a minimum of 75 average viewers per 30 day period. Content must conform to the Community Guidelines, Terms of Service and DMCA Guidelines, and Twitch says it also considers suspension history.

Affiliate asks for an average of 3 viewers. Partner asks for 75. That is 25 times the audience, sustained over two months, not once. Anyone describing Partner as the next rung on the ladder has not put the two numbers side by side.

Two structural differences matter as much as the number.

Affiliate is automatic, Partner is an application. Completing Path to Partner unlocks a button to apply. Twitch states plainly that applying does not guarantee Partnership. It also says there are circumstances where it may accept applications outside the criteria, such as exceptional viewership growth or an established audience on another platform.

Some of your viewers do not count. Twitch says viewership from front page promotions or embeds does not count towards the viewership calculation. If a good chunk of your traffic arrives through an embed on someone else's site, your qualifying average is lower than your dashboard average.

What Partner adds: up to 60 emote slots, VOD storage of 60 days against the standard 7, custom chat and Bit badges, a verified badge, guaranteed video transcodes rather than transcodes when capacity allows, broadcast delay, priority support answering in 1 to 2 business days, the ability to create a Twitch Team, and two one time username benefits, a name change and a name revert, through Partner Help.

A Partner Manager is by invitation only, reviewed periodically on channel size, language and country. Twitch is also unusually candid about the role: a Partner Manager is not responsible for growing your channel.

Guaranteed transcodes deserve a line of their own, because it is the benefit most likely to affect your numbers. Without them, viewers on a weak connection cannot drop to a lower quality and simply leave. That is a growth benefit awarded to channels that have already grown.

3. The number Twitch will not print

Both pages describe how you earn. Neither states what share you keep.

On Bits, Twitch is specific: participating Partners receive a share of the revenue equal to 1 cent per Bit used to Cheer for them. On ads, both pages say only that you earn a share of the revenue. On subscriptions, the Partner page lists Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3 and the free Prime subscription, notes that revenue received from a subscription may vary depending on the country the subscriber lives in, and stops there.

The one place a subscription percentage appears is the Plus Program, and it is framed as an upgrade. Twitch says the Plus Program offers two levels of enhanced subscription net revenue share rates for qualifying Affiliates and Partners: Level 1 at 60/40 and Level 2 at 70/30, earned on recurring monthly subscriptions and gift subs.

Two things follow from that sentence and both are worth knowing.

The enhanced splits are open to Affiliates, not just Partners. Twitch's own wording says qualifying Affiliates and Partners. The near universal assumption that a better split is a Partner privilege is not what the page says.

Calling 60/40 enhanced tells you the baseline is below it without telling you what it is. The figure usually reported elsewhere is an even split, and if that is right, moving to Level 1 is a 20% pay rise on the same audience and Level 2 a 40% pay rise. We are not stating the baseline as fact, because Twitch does not state it on these pages, and a money site should not print a number it cannot source. Check your own payouts.

Note also the word net. The share is described as a net revenue share, so it applies after whatever Twitch deducts first, and the local price variation means a Tier 1 sub from one country is not worth the same to you as a Tier 1 sub from another. If your audience is spread internationally, your per subscriber revenue will not match anyone else's.

4. Worked example: Nadia in Dundee, 14 weeks to Affiliate

Nadia streams craft projects from Dundee two evenings a week, about two and a half hours each. She has 11 followers after a month and had read that she needed 50 followers, 500 minutes and 7 days, and had privately written it off.

Against the current requirements she is much closer than she thought.

Hours: two streams a week at 2.5 hours is 5 hours a week. The requirement is 4 hours in 30 days. She clears it in the first week of any month.

Days: 8 or 9 streaming days in a 30 day window, against a requirement of 4. Cleared comfortably.

Average of 3 viewers on 4 different days: this is her real constraint. She averages 2 on a quiet night and 4 or 5 when she posts in a craft forum beforehand. Because days below 3 do not count against her, she does not need every stream to hit it. She needs 4 good nights in a 30 day window, which at 8 streams a month means half her streams, not all of them.

Followers: 11 of 25. At roughly 1 new follower per stream she reaches 25 in about 14 more streams, or seven weeks. Under the old 50 follower bar it would have been about 39 more streams, or five months.

So her plan is not to stream more. It is to post in the forum before every stream, because the viewer average is the binding constraint and followers follow from viewers. She also enables two factor authentication now so onboarding is not held up later.

What Affiliate is worth to her. Honestly, at first, very little. Subscriptions, Bits and ad revenue on an audience of five people is pocket change, and it would be dishonest to imply otherwise. What it actually unlocks is the ability to accept money at all, and 14 days of VOD storage rather than 7. The money becomes real somewhere in the hundreds of regular viewers, not the tens.

Partner is not on her horizon and should not be. Six streams a month averaging 75 viewers, two months running, is a different channel from the one she has. That is not discouraging, it is just the correct size of the gap, and knowing it saves her from measuring herself against the wrong target for two years.

Whatever she earns is taxable. Gross income over the £1,000 trading allowance in a tax year means telling HMRC, and Twitch requires a tax interview during onboarding, which is a step people routinely get stuck on.

The honest verdict

If you last checked the Affiliate requirements more than a year ago, check again. Twitch's own page now says 25 followers, 4 hours, 4 days and an average of 3 viewers on 4 of them, and the internet is still confidently quoting numbers roughly twice that.

Treat Affiliate as the administrative step it is. It lets you accept money and adds a week of VOD storage. It does not make you money on its own, and pretending otherwise is how people end up disappointed by a milestone they worked months for.

Partner is not the next step, it is a different scale: 75 average viewers across 6 streams for two consecutive months, applied for rather than granted, with front page and embed viewers excluded from the count. Knowing that stops you measuring against the wrong target.

And before you plan an income, find your own numbers. Twitch publishes 1 cent per Bit and describes 60/40 and 70/30 as enhanced subscription rates, but does not print the standard split, says subscription revenue varies by the subscriber's country, and calls the share net. Your payout statements are the only reliable source for what you actually keep.

Frequently asked

Is it 25 followers or 50? Twitch's current help article on joining the Affiliate Program lists 25 followers, 4 hours streamed, 4 different days and an average of 3 viewers. A great many guides still say 50 and 500 minutes. Check the Achievements section of your own Creator Dashboard, which shows your progress in the last 30 days.

Do I have to hit 3 average viewers on every stream? No. Twitch says days below 3 average viewers do not count against you. You need 4 days in the window that reach the average, and the rest are ignored.

Can an Affiliate get the better subscription split? Twitch describes the Plus Program as offering enhanced subscription net revenue share rates, Level 1 at 60/40 and Level 2 at 70/30, for qualifying Affiliates and Partners. So it is not restricted to Partners, though qualifying conditions apply.

What happens to my subscribers if I leave the Affiliate programme? Twitch says you lose access to monetisation tools, existing subscriptions expire and subscribers are not charged again, but you keep revenue already earned and it is paid out on the normal schedule. You can rejoin later, and your achievements typically remain completed.
