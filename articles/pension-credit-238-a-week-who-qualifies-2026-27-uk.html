The £238 Line: Who Gets Pension Credit in 2026/27, Why a Full State Pension Is £3.30 Too Much, and What the Claim Unlocks

Pension Credit is the benefit most often described as unclaimed, and the reason is not complicated: the rule is a single line, £238 a week for one person, and most people do not know which side of it they sit. This is the 2026/27 rule as GOV.UK states it, the additions that move the line for people with a disability or caring role, the savings rule that catches people with £15,000 in the bank, and the list of other help that a successful claim switches on, which for many households is worth more than the credit itself.

The short version

  • Guarantee Credit tops your weekly income up to £238 if you are single and £363.25 if you have a partner. Source: GOV.UK, Pension Credit: what you'll get, read 5 September 2026. You must have reached State Pension age (66, rising to 67 from May 2026 by birth date) and live in England, Scotland or Wales.
  • A full new State Pension is £241.30 a week in 2026/27, £3.30 above the line, so a single person on the full amount with no other income gets nothing, unless an extra amount applies.
  • The extra amounts move the line. Severe disability: £86.05 a week if you get Attendance Allowance, PIP daily living, DLA middle or higher care, or the Scottish equivalents. There are also extra amounts for carers and for some housing costs.
  • Savings: the first £10,000 is ignored. Every £500 above that counts as £1 a week of income, so £15,000 of savings adds £10 a week to your assessed income.
  • Backdating is 3 months. You can apply up to 4 months before State Pension age and at any time after, but the claim only reaches back 3 months. Every month you wait is a month lost.
  • Our verdict: if your weekly income is under about £250 single or £375 as a couple, or you get a disability benefit, claim. Even £1 a week of Guarantee Credit unlocks the Warm Home Discount, council tax help and, over 75, a free TV licence.

1. The £238 line, and what counts as income

GOV.UK's page "Pension Credit: what you'll get" says the benefit "tops up your weekly income to £238 if you're single" and "your joint weekly income to £363.25 if you have a partner". That top-up is Guarantee Credit. If your income is £200 a week, you get £38. If it is £237, you get £1, and the £1 matters, as section 4 explains. A partner means someone you live with as a couple, and for a couple both must usually have reached State Pension age.

Income includes the State Pension, any other pension, earnings, and most benefits including Carer's Allowance. GOV.UK lists what does not count, and the list is longer than people expect: Attendance Allowance, Personal Independence Payment, Disability Living Allowance, Housing Benefit, Council Tax Reduction, Child Benefit and the Christmas Bonus are all ignored. A pension you could claim but have deferred is counted as if you were taking it, so deferring the State Pension to build it up does not get you under the line.

The full new State Pension in 2026/27 is £241.30 a week. That is £3.30 over £238. So a single person who reached State Pension age after April 2016 with a complete National Insurance record and nothing else coming in does not qualify on income alone. The people who do are those with gaps in their record, those on the old basic State Pension with no earnings-related top-up, and anyone whose circumstances trigger an extra amount.

2. The additions that move the line

The extra amounts are the part the leaflets skip and the part that turns a "no" into a "yes". If you get Attendance Allowance, the daily living component of PIP, the middle or highest rate of the DLA care component, Armed Forces Independence Payment, or the Scottish Adult Disability Payment or Pension Age Disability Payment, GOV.UK says you could get an extra £86.05 a week for severe disability. In practice that lifts the single person's line from £238 to £324.05, so the same person on a full £241.30 State Pension now qualifies for £82.75 a week, over £4,300 a year, and the Attendance Allowance itself is not counted as income against it. If you are not claiming it yet, start with the two Attendance Allowance rates and who gets each.

There is a separate extra amount if you are entitled to Carer's Allowance, even if it is not paid because your State Pension overlaps it, and extra amounts for certain housing costs such as ground rent and service charges. The exact carer figure is on the GOV.UK page; the point is that anyone caring for a disabled partner or relative should not assume the £238 line is theirs. Savings Credit, the other half of the benefit, is closed to anyone who reached State Pension age on or after 6 April 2016, and pays at most £17.96 single or £20.10 for a couple to those who qualified before then.

3. The savings rule

Pension Credit has no savings cut-off, which surprises people who assume £16,000 ends everything as it does for Universal Credit. Instead GOV.UK's rule is that the first £10,000 of savings and investments is ignored, and every £500 over £10,000 counts as £1 a week of income. £11,000 counts as £2 a week; £15,000 counts as £10; £20,000 counts as £20. A single person with £215 a week of actual income and £20,000 in the bank is assessed at £235 and still gets £3 a week, plus everything that £3 unlocks. The savings themselves are not spent down; only the notional income is counted.

4. What a successful claim unlocks

This is why the £1 a week matters. Guarantee Credit is a passport. With it, in England and Wales, you are in the core group for the Warm Home Discount, £150 off the electricity bill each winter with no application. Over 75, it makes the TV licence free, worth £180 a year. It gives maximum Council Tax Reduction in most councils, which can be the whole bill, and Housing Benefit for renters. It brings free NHS dental treatment, help with glasses and travel to hospital, and a Cold Weather Payment in any qualifying cold spell. What it no longer gates is the Winter Fuel Payment, which since 2025 goes to pensioners with individual income up to £35,000 whether or not they claim Pension Credit; our Winter Fuel guide covers that separately.

Claiming is by phone on 0800 99 1234, online if you have already claimed your State Pension, or by post. GOV.UK says you can apply up to 4 months before State Pension age and that a later claim can only be backdated 3 months, so up to 3 months of arrears arrive in the first payment and anything earlier is lost.

5. A worked example in Blackburn

Margaret, 74, lives alone in Blackburn. She reached State Pension age in 2018 with gaps in her record and gets £196.40 a week of State Pension, plus £22 a week from a small works pension: £218.40. She has £13,000 in savings. She has never claimed because she "has savings".

Her savings over £10,000 are £3,000, six lots of £500, so £6 a week of notional income. Assessed income £224.40. Guarantee Credit tops that up to £238, so she gets £13.60 a week, £707 a year. Then the passport: Warm Home Discount £150, and her council's maximum Council Tax Reduction on a band B bill of about £1,600, which in her council would take it to nil. Next year, at 75, the TV licence goes too. Her £13.60 a week claim is worth roughly £2,450 a year in total, and a claim made in September rather than December keeps three months of it, about £177 of credit, that a later claim would lose. If Margaret's arthritis worsens and she is awarded Attendance Allowance, the severe disability addition lifts her line to £324.05 and her Guarantee Credit to about £99.65 a week on top of the Attendance Allowance itself. An award often opens other doors too, including what a Blue Badge application actually takes.

6. The honest verdict

The rule is one line, £238 a week single or £363.25 as a couple, and a full new State Pension sits £3.30 above it, which is why so many people assume they are out. The additions for disability and caring, the £10,000 savings disregard and the passported help all pull the real threshold higher than the headline.

If your income is anywhere near the line, or you get Attendance Allowance or PIP, claim now rather than after the winter. The credit backdates only three months, and the Warm Home Discount, council tax help and TV licence that come with it are worth more than the credit for most of the people who qualify. Guarantee Credit is also one of the qualifying benefits for help with a funeral, which matters because what a UK funeral actually costs runs to four figures before any extras.

Frequently asked

What is the Pension Credit income limit in 2026/27? There is no hard limit. Guarantee Credit tops income up to £238 a week single or £363.25 for a couple, and the line rises by £86.05 with the severe disability addition and by further amounts for carers and some housing costs. Source: GOV.UK, read 5 September 2026.

I get the full State Pension. Can I still claim? On income alone, no: £241.30 is £3.30 over the line. If you get Attendance Allowance, PIP daily living or equivalent, the severe disability addition means you probably can, and the claim is worth over £4,000 a year.

Do savings stop me getting Pension Credit? No. The first £10,000 is ignored and each £500 above counts as £1 a week of income. There is no upper cut-off, unlike Universal Credit's £16,000.

How far back can a claim be paid? 3 months. You can apply up to 4 months before State Pension age and at any time after, but arrears only reach back 3 months from the date of claim.
