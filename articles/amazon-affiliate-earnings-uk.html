The numbers to put on your wall

Nearly every article about affiliate income is written by someone selling a course about affiliate income. This one is not, so here are the measurements that actually describe the odds.

96.55%of all web pages get zero Google traffic. Ahrefs, across 14 billion pages.

Note what is not in that table. "95% of affiliate marketers fail" is quoted constantly and traces back to nothing. It is a number people repeat because it sounds authoritative. The figures above are real measurements, and they are worse than the invented one in some ways and better in others.

What the maths actually requires

Let us take the UK rate card and work backwards from a target rather than forwards from hope.

Say you want £500 a month. You are writing in a 5% category, so kitchen, automotive, tools, home or books. Your average qualifying basket is £120, which is realistic if your content is build-guide shaped rather than single-product shaped.

  • £120 basket at 5% equals £6 per conversion.
  • £500 divided by £6 equals 84 conversions a month.
  • At a 4% click-to-order rate, that needs 2,100 clicks to Amazon a month.
  • At a 5% click-through from your page to Amazon, which is a reasonable rate for genuinely useful content, that needs 42,000 monthly page views.

Forty-two thousand monthly page views for £500 a month. That is the honest shape of it. If your niche is in the 3% catch-all instead, the same £500 needs roughly 70,000 page views.

Now set that against the baseline table. Forty-two thousand monthly views is not an unreachable number, but it is a number that puts a site well inside the top few percent of everything published, and the average page that gets there took years.

A realistic first year

Roughly what happens if you publish consistently and do nothing wrong:

PeriodWhat actually happens
Months 1 to 3You publish ten to twenty articles. Almost nothing is indexed. Traffic is you, checking. Income is zero. This phase makes most people quit and it is completely normal.
Months 3 to 6Pages start appearing in Search Console with impressions at position 40 to 70. The first few clicks arrive. If you are going to clear the three-sale threshold, it usually happens here, and it is often from an article you did not expect.
Months 6 to 12A handful of pages find positions in the top twenty. Income is realistically £0 to £80 a month. Your first actual payment lands, if the £25 threshold has been cleared.
Year 2This is where sites that survive start compounding, because page age is one of the strongest ranking correlates there is. It is also where most people have already stopped.

If someone shows you a screenshot of £4,000 in month three, the plausible explanations are an existing audience, an existing site, paid traffic, or a screenshot from a different business. Not a new site from nothing.

Three hurdles before any money moves

  1. Three qualifying sales within 180 days. Miss it and the application is withdrawn and the Associates ID cannot be reinstated. Your own purchases do not count, and asking family to buy through your link is a fast route to a permanent ban.
  2. The £25 payment threshold. Bank transfer at £25, cheque at £50. Below that, the balance simply rolls forward.
  3. The 60 day payment delay. Commission accrues only after the order ships, and payment lands roughly 60 days after the end of the earning month. January's money arrives in late March.

Stack those and the gap between starting and holding actual money is six months in the good case.

When it is worth it, and when it is not

It is probably worth it if: you already have an audience somewhere, or genuine expertise in a specific category, or you enjoy the writing enough that a year of no income does not break you, or you are treating it as one stream inside a wider business rather than the whole thing.

It is probably not worth it if: you need income within six months, or you are planning to publish AI-drafted comparison pages at volume, which is now an explicit breach of Amazon's agreement as well as an SEO dead end, or the plan depends on a niche you have no interest in.

There is one genuinely encouraging finding worth setting against all of the above. AI Overview coverage in the first half of 2026 ran at 88% for healthcare, 83% for education and 82% for B2B tech, but only around 4% for e-commerce, down from 29%. Google restricted AI Overviews on transactional queries to protect its own ad revenue. Purchase-intent content is now structurally one of the safest positions left in search, and that is exactly where affiliate content lives. It is a far better argument for this business model than anything in the usual pitch.

The income stack, in build order

Amazon should not be the whole business, and the 2026 reports of commission cuts to large publishers are the clearest argument for that. A sensible order: The published rate changes are set out in what actually changed in the April 2026 Associates rate card, and eBay Partner Network pays more than Amazon in several categories on its own published card.

  1. Amazon Associates. Fastest to first pound, easiest to get approved for, most fragile. Treat it as the starter, not the plan.
  2. Higher-paying networks for the same clicks. Awin, Skimlinks and direct brand programmes frequently pay several times Amazon's rate for the same product category, with longer cookies. Where the same click is worth more elsewhere, send it elsewhere.
  3. Bounties. £8 for a full-price Audible membership in the UK is more than most product sales you will make. Overlooked by nearly everyone.
  4. Display advertising. Requires real scale and tier-one traffic to be worth the page-speed cost.
  5. An email list and your own product. The only streams you actually own. Everything above this line can be switched off by someone else.

Questions people ask

Can I do this without a website? Yes, on YouTube or Pinterest, and the Influencer Programme lets approved creators place shoppable videos on Amazon's own product pages, which means you do not have to generate the traffic. The catch is that onsite commissions run at roughly a third of offsite rates, and since April 2026 they only apply to the same product variant you linked. The two programmes pay the same published rates and differ mainly in the storefront and the eligibility test: the full comparison is here.

How many articles before it works? There is no threshold number. Amazon's own application review uses roughly ten posts as a working bar. Ranking is a function of depth and age, not count, and thirty good pages on one topic beat two hundred thin ones on many.

Do I need to register as self-employed? If affiliate income is trading income above the £1,000 trading allowance in a tax year, yes, you need to tell HMRC. Below that it generally does not need reporting. This is general information rather than tax advice, and it is worth checking your own position.

Is it too late to start? The measured odds are worse than they were five years ago and the AI Overview data says purchase-intent content is the least damaged part of search. Both are true. What has genuinely changed is that thin content no longer works at all, which raises the bar and thins the competition at the same time.

The verdict

Roughly 42,000 monthly page views for £500 a month in a 5% category, six months before the first payment clears, and a 1.74% chance of any given new page reaching the top ten in a year. That is the honest arithmetic. It is a real business for people who like the work and can wait, and a bad plan for anyone who needs income this year.
