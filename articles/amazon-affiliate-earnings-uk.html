How Much Do Amazon Affiliates Make in the UK? The Honest 2026 Numbers

Published 22 August 2026 · Updated 14 November 2026 · 10 min read · Figures checked November 2026

Most UK Amazon affiliates earn little or nothing in their first year. On Amazon's own UK rates, £500 a month in a 5% category takes roughly 42,000 page views a month. The first payment usually lands about six months after you start, if it lands at all.

The short version
  • 96.55% of web pages get zero Google traffic. That is an Ahrefs measurement across about 14 billion pages, not a motivational statistic.
  • Only 1.74% of newly published pages reach the top ten within a year. The average number one page is five years old.
  • A page with no backlinks has roughly a 1 in 6,671 chance of passing 1,000 monthly search visits.
  • £500 a month at Amazon's 5% UK rate needs about 42,000 monthly page views on our assumptions. At the 3% catch all, about 70,000.
  • Three qualifying sales within 180 days, a £25 threshold and a 60 day delay mean your first payment is realistically six months out.
  • The "95% of affiliates fail" line has no traceable source. The real numbers are bad enough without it.

What do the real traffic numbers say?

Nearly every article about Amazon affiliate earnings is written by someone selling a course about affiliate income. This one is not.

So here are the measurements that actually describe the odds, each from the study that produced it.

FigureWhat it measures
96.55%of all pages in the Ahrefs index get zero traffic from Google. Measured across about 14 billion pages.
1.74%of newly published pages rank in the top ten within a year.
5 yearsaverage age of a number one ranking page. 72.9% of top ten pages are more than three years old.
1 in 6,671pages with no backlinks that get more than 1,000 monthly search visits.
33% fallfall in Google search referrals to more than 2,500 publisher sites, November 2024 to November 2025, from Chartbeat data in the Reuters Institute 2026 report.
60% fallfall in search referrals over two years for small publishers with 1,000 to 10,000 daily page views, against 22% for the largest, from Chartbeat data reported by Axios.

Sources: Ahrefs studies of December 2023 and May 2025, the Reuters Institute 2026 trends report and Axios, March 2026, all linked below. The losses were regressive. Small sites bore them worst.

Note what is not in that table. "95% of affiliate marketers fail" is quoted constantly and traces back to nothing.

It is repeated because it sounds authoritative. The figures above are real measurements, worse than the invented one in some ways and better in others.

How much traffic does £500 a month need?

Take Amazon's UK rate card and work backwards from a target, not forwards from hope.

Say you want £500 a month. You write in a 5% category, so kitchen, automotive, tools, home or books.

Your average qualifying basket is £120. That is realistic if your content is shaped like a build guide rather than a single product review.

  • £120 basket at 5% equals £6 per order.
  • £500 divided by £6 equals 84 orders a month.
  • If 4% of clicks to Amazon become orders, that needs 2,100 clicks to Amazon a month.
  • If 5% of your readers click through to Amazon, a reasonable rate for genuinely useful content, that needs 42,000 monthly page views.

Forty two thousand monthly page views for £500 a month. That is the honest shape of it.

The 4% and 5% are our working assumptions, not Amazon figures. Your own Associates reports will show your real rates within a few months.

If your niche sits in the 3% catch all instead, the same £120 basket pays £3.60. The same £500 then needs roughly 70,000 page views.

Now set that against the table above. Forty two thousand monthly views is not unreachable. It does put a site well inside the top few percent of everything published, and the average page that gets there took years.

What does a small UK site actually earn?

Take a worked example. Gareth and Nia Hughes in Cardiff run a site about home DIY tools, which sits in Amazon's 5% band.

Two years in, the site has 60 articles and 9,000 page views a month. Here is their month, on the same assumptions as above.

StepWorkingResult
Readers who click to Amazon9,000 × 5%450 clicks
Clicks that become orders450 × 4%18 orders
Average basketDrill, bits, clamps£120
Commission per order£120 × 5%£6.00
Product commission18 × £6.00£108.00
Less returns, say one order£6.00 back£102.00

So £102 a month, from two years of evenings. Paid about 60 days after each month ends.

That is real money, and more than most new sites ever make. It is not a wage.

Over a year it comes to about £1,224. That is just above the £1,000 trading allowance, so the Hughes household would need to register for Self Assessment and tell HMRC.

The way to declare it is set out in how to declare affiliate income to HMRC.

The household, traffic and basket are illustrative. The 5% rate and the £1,000 allowance are from Amazon and GOV.UK, checked 14 November 2026.

What does a realistic first year look like?

Roughly what happens if you publish consistently and do nothing wrong:

PeriodWhat actually happens
Months 1 to 3You publish ten to twenty articles. Almost nothing is indexed. Traffic is you, checking. Income is zero. This phase makes most people quit, and it is completely normal.
Months 3 to 6Pages start appearing in Search Console with impressions at position 40 to 70. The first few clicks arrive. If you are going to clear the three sale test, it usually happens here, often from an article you did not expect.
Months 6 to 12A handful of pages reach the top twenty. Income is realistically £0 to £80 a month. Your first actual payment lands, if the £25 threshold has been cleared.
Year 2This is where surviving sites start compounding, because page age is one of the strongest ranking signals there is. It is also where most people have already stopped.

This timeline is our judgement from the ranking data above, not a promise. Your niche and your competition will move it.

If someone shows you a screenshot of £4,000 in month three, the likely explanations are an existing audience, an existing site, paid traffic, or a screenshot from a different business. Not a new site from nothing.

What must happen before Amazon pays you?

  1. Three qualifying sales within 180 days. Amazon only reviews your application once you have driven three qualified sales in your first 180 days. Personal orders do not count, and asking family to buy through your link is a fast route to a closed account.
  2. The £25 payment threshold. £25 by bank transfer or Amazon gift certificate. Cheques are only for people outside the UK, at £50. Below the threshold, the balance rolls forward.
  3. The 60 day payment delay. Commission accrues only after the order ships. Payment lands roughly 60 days after the end of the earning month, so January's money arrives around the end of March.

Stack those three and the gap between starting and holding real money is about six months, in the good case.

Is Amazon affiliate marketing worth it in the UK?

It is probably worth it if you already have an audience somewhere, or genuine expertise in one category.

It also suits you if you enjoy the writing enough that a year of no income does not break you, or you treat it as one stream inside a wider business.

It is probably not worth it if you need income within six months, or the plan depends on a niche you have no interest in.

It is also a poor bet if you plan to publish machine drafted comparison pages at volume. Since 14 April 2026, Amazon's agreement defines original content as needing commentary, analysis or transformation that adds value. Thin pages also fail in search.

There is one finding worth setting against the gloom. BrightEdge tracked thousands of ecommerce keywords in autumn 2025.

AI Overviews briefly spiked to 26% of those searches on 18 September, then fell back to about 9% by October. Queries containing buy and deals were stripped out entirely.

So pure shopping searches still mostly show ordinary results. Comparison and "best" searches are more exposed, and that is where much affiliate content lives.

The fuller picture is in AI Overviews and affiliate traffic. It is a reason for cautious hope, not a guarantee.

What should you add to Amazon, and in what order?

Amazon should not be the whole business. The May 2026 reports, led by Adweek, that Amazon cut commissions by up to 50% for some publishers are the clearest argument for that.

The published changes are set out in what actually changed in the April 2026 Associates agreement. Also, eBay Partner Network pays more than Amazon in several categories on its own published card.

A sensible build order:

  1. Amazon Associates. Fastest to a first pound, easiest to join, most fragile. Treat it as the starter, not the plan.
  2. Higher paying networks for the same clicks. Awin, Skimlinks and direct brand programmes often pay more than Amazon for the same category, with longer cookies. Where the same click is worth more elsewhere, send it there.
  3. Bounties. Amazon's UK Audible page pays £8 per full price membership as standard, and £20 from 1 October to 16 December 2026. That beats most product sales you will make.
  4. Display advertising. Needs real scale and UK or US traffic to be worth the page speed cost. Our affiliate against display ads comparison runs the numbers.
  5. An email list and your own product. The only streams you actually own. Everything above this line can be switched off by someone else.

Frequently asked questions

How much do Amazon affiliates make in the UK? Most make little or nothing in year one. On Amazon's UK rates, a site in the 5% band with a £120 average basket needs about 42,000 monthly page views for £500 a month. A small site with 9,000 views might make around £100. There is no official average earnings figure from Amazon.

Can I do this without a website? Yes, on YouTube or Pinterest. The Influencer Programme also lets approved creators place shoppable videos on Amazon's own pages. The catch is a lower onsite card, 1.5% catch all against 3% offsite, and since April 2026 it pays only on the same product variant you linked. See the full comparison.

How many articles before it works? There is no magic number. Ranking depends on depth, links and age far more than count, and the Ahrefs data says the average top page is five years old. Thirty strong pages on one topic will usually beat two hundred thin ones spread across many. Pick one niche and go deep.

Do I need to register as self employed? If your trading income, including affiliate commission, is above the £1,000 trading allowance in a tax year, you must register for Self Assessment, usually by 5 October after that tax year. Below £1,000 you generally do not need to tell HMRC. This is general information, not tax advice.

Is it too late to start? The odds are worse than five years ago, and small publishers lost 60% of search referrals in two years. But pure shopping searches still mostly show ordinary results. Thin content no longer works, which raises the bar and thins the competition at the same time. It is late for shortcuts, not for depth.

The verdict

Roughly 42,000 monthly page views for £500 a month in a 5% category, about six months before the first payment clears, and a 1.74% chance of any new page reaching the top ten within a year. That is the honest arithmetic. It is a real business for people who like the work and can wait, and a bad plan for anyone who needs income this year.
