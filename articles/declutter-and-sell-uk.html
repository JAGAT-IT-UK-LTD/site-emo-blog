Declutter and Sell Your Stuff in the UK: Fees, Tax and Where to Sell

Published 21 July 2026 · Updated 14 November 2026 · 11 min read · Figures checked November 2026

You can declutter and sell your stuff in the UK without paying tax, because selling your own used belongings is not trading. Platforms like Vinted and eBay do report sellers to HMRC after 30 sales or about £1,700 in a calendar year, but a report is not a tax bill. For most people the real costs are postage and time, not fees.

The short version
  • Vinted charges sellers nothing. The buyer pays the Vinted fee at checkout, which Vinted says is usually 3% to 8% of the price plus £0.30 to £0.80. It does not come out of your payout.
  • eBay private sellers pay no final value fee on up to 300 free listings a month, then 35p a listing. Cars and other motors listings are excluded. Business sellers pay final value fees of 2% to 14.9%, plus a per order fee and VAT.
  • Facebook Marketplace is free for local collection. Shipped sales carry a selling fee, so check the figure shown before you list.
  • Platforms report you to HMRC once you make 30 or more sales, or receive 2,000 euros (about £1,700) or more, in a calendar year. That is a reporting rule, not a tax rule. A nudge letter about online selling is not a tax bill either.
  • Personal items are rarely taxed. HMRC says you are unlikely to pay tax selling things like the contents of a loft or garage. Tax arrives when you trade, or sell one item for £6,000 or more.
  • The £1,000 trading allowance only matters if you buy or make things to sell. Postage and your own time decide your profit far more than fees do.

1. Do you pay tax when you declutter and sell your stuff?

This causes more confusion than anything else, largely because of headlines about HMRC and online platforms. Here is the actual position, from HMRC's own guidance.

Selling your own used possessions is not trading. Clearing out your wardrobe, your loft and your children's outgrown things is not income. You are selling things you already owned, usually for less than you paid.

HMRC puts it in one plain sentence. You are "unlikely to pay tax if you sell personal items from your home, like contents of a loft or garage."

Buying things in order to resell them is trading. So is making things to sell. That is where the £1,000 trading allowance applies. If your trading income is more than £1,000 in a tax year, you must tell HMRC and register for Self Assessment by 5 October after that tax year ends.

What the platform reporting rules actually say

Since 1 January 2024, digital platforms such as Vinted, eBay and Etsy collect seller details and report them to HMRC. Your details will not be reported if, in a calendar year, you make fewer than 30 sales of goods and receive less than 2,000 euros, about £1,700, for them.

Both halves have to be true to stay out of the report. Cross either one and you are in it. The platform sends the report to HMRC by 31 January after the calendar year, and sends you a copy.

Two details matter more than the threshold. First, the platform reports on the calendar year, 1 January to 31 December, split into quarters. Your tax is worked out on the tax year, 6 April to 5 April. The number on the platform statement is not the number for a tax return, so do not copy it across.

Second, the report shows what you received less the platform's fees. It says nothing about whether you were trading. Being named in a report does not mean you owe anything.

HMRC now holds more than one year of platform data and has started writing to sellers. What a nudge letter actually means, and how to answer it, is covered in its own guide. If you were selling your own belongings, that is the whole answer.

The one real exception

Capital Gains Tax can apply if you sell a single personal possession for £6,000 or more and make a gain. That covers jewellery, art, antiques and some collectables. It does not cover a used sofa.

2. Where should you sell unwanted items in the UK?

Each platform has its own crowd. Matching the item to the right audience matters more than any fee difference.

ItemBest place to sellWhy
Clothes, shoes, bagsVintedNo seller fees, the audience is there, buyer pays the fee
Electronics, tools, collectableseBayBuyers search eBay for these, and private selling has no final value fee
Furniture, white goods, anything bulkyFacebook MarketplaceLocal collection, no postage, no fee
Books, CDs, DVDs, gamesZiffit or WeBuyBooksPennies each, but you scan barcodes and they collect
Phones and laptopsTrade in sites, or eBayTrade in is fast and pays less. eBay pays more and takes longer

The most common mistake is listing a heavy item for postage. A £25 chest of drawers can cost more than that to send. It goes on Facebook Marketplace for collection, or it does not go at all.

4. What is worth listing and what is not?

Be honest about your hourly rate. Every listing needs photos, a description, answers to questions, packing and a trip to post it. Ten minutes an item soon adds up.

Decluttering is a one off by nature, because the cupboard eventually empties. If you want those hours to keep paying afterwards, the two honest comparisons are what stock photo sites actually pay per download and whether selling AI prompts and templates is a real market or just a loud one.

Resale valueWorth listing on its own?
Under £8No. Bundle it or donate it
£8 to £20Only if it posts cheaply and sells fast
£20 to £60Yes, this is the sweet spot
Over £60Yes, and take proper photographs

That table is our rule of thumb, not a platform figure. Adjust it to how much your own time is worth.

Bundling is the answer to the low value pile. Ten baby vests might sell for £12 as a bundle and not at all one by one. Same listing time, far more money.

Things that often sell better than people expect: branded trainers even when worn, Lego, cast iron cookware, power tools, vintage Pyrex, board games, unopened printer cartridges, curtains, and chargers in bundles.

Things that rarely sell: mass market paperbacks, DVDs, old televisions, mattresses, and anything with a safety label removed. Donate or recycle those and stop spending time on them.

If the unsellable pile needs shifting in one go, a skip and a man with a van price up very differently. For a single household clearout the van usually wins.

Saima in Bolton clears one bedroom and a wardrobe over a weekend. She sells only things she already owned.

Total£554£14£540

She spends about 14 hours across the weekend and the following fortnight, including packing and post office trips. That is roughly £38 an hour, tax free, because none of it is trading income.

Now check her against the HMRC reporting rule. She made 29 sales on Vinted and eBay combined, but each platform counts only its own sales. Vinted saw 26 sales and £230, eBay saw 3 sales and £235. Neither platform reaches 30 sales or 2,000 euros, so neither reports her.

Even if one did, she would owe nothing. These were her own belongings, and no single item came near £6,000.

The interesting line is the books. 63 items for £19 is 30p each. It is only worth doing because Ziffit scans and collects, so it costs almost no time. Listing them one by one would have been days of work for the same money.

6. How do you write listings that sell?

  • Natural light, plain background. A window and a white duvet beat any lighting kit.
  • Photograph the flaws. The bobbling, the scuff, the missing button. Buyers who see the flaw and buy anyway do not open disputes. Buyers who discover it later do.
  • Put the brand and size in the title. That is what people search. "Nike Air Max 90 UK 8 white" beats "lovely trainers".
  • Measure it. Chest, waist and length in centimetres. Sizing varies wildly, and measurements prevent most returns.
  • List when people browse. Evenings and weekends tend to be busiest, so new listings get seen.
  • Relist rather than cut the price, at first. A fresh listing gets a second look in the feed.

7. How do you stop postage eating the profit?

With seller fees close to zero, postage is now the main thing between you and your money.

  • Know your parcel sizes. A Royal Mail Large Letter is much cheaper than a Small Parcel. A jumper squashed flat into a large letter saves money every time.
  • Use a courier comparison site for heavier parcels. A booked drop off is often cheaper than the counter price.
  • Use the platform's own labels. On Vinted the buyer pays postage through the checkout, so you only print and drop.
  • Reuse packaging. Buyers do not care, and it is free.
  • Always get proof of postage. It is free and it is your whole defence in a not received claim.
  • Weigh before you list. Guessing the weight wrong can turn a £12 sale into a £3 one.

A cheap kitchen scale and a tape measure are the only equipment worth buying, and you probably own both.

8. Which myths cost people money?

"HMRC will tax my Vinted sales." Not if you are selling your own used things. Platforms report sellers at 30 sales or about £1,700, but reporting is not taxation. Trading is taxed. Decluttering is not.

"eBay takes a huge cut." It used to. For UK private sellers it now charges no final value fee on up to 300 free listings a month. A lot of advice online has not caught up. The full split, and the point at which you count as a business seller, is in our eBay private versus business seller fees guide.

"A letter from HMRC means I owe tax." It does not. A nudge letter asks you to check. Our guide to what an HMRC nudge letter does not mean walks through the reply.

"I will get more if I wait for the right buyer." Sometimes true for rare items. Usually it means the item sits in a bag for a year and sells for less. Price it to move.

The honest verdict

This is worth doing if you have a real backlog of unused things. A few hundred pounds from a proper household clearout is realistic, as Saima's £540 shows. It is tax free, and with near zero fees you keep almost all of it.

This is not a side hustle. It is a one off, because you run out of clutter.

Frequently asked

Do I need to tell HMRC if I sell my old things online? Not for selling your own belongings, however many items. If you buy or make things to sell and your trading income passes £1,000 in a tax year, register for Self Assessment by 5 October after that tax year. Selling one item for £6,000 or more can bring Capital Gains Tax. This is general information, not tax advice.

How many items can you sell on Vinted before HMRC knows? Vinted reports you to HMRC once you make 30 or more sales, or receive 2,000 euros (about £1,700) or more, in a calendar year. The report goes by 31 January and you get a copy. Being reported is not the same as owing tax, especially for personal items.

What is the best site to sell unwanted items in the UK? It depends on the item. Vinted suits clothes, because sellers pay no fee. eBay suits electronics and collectables, with no final value fee for private sellers. Facebook Marketplace suits bulky furniture for local collection. Book and media buyers suit large piles of cheap discs and paperbacks.

What if a buyer says the item never arrived? Proof of postage is your defence, and it is free to ask for at the counter or with a tracked label. Without it you will usually lose the claim. Keep the receipt until the buyer confirms, and use the platform's own label where you can.

What should I do with what does not sell? Try a charity shop or a local Buy Nothing group. A second relisting round rarely works for cheap items. Keeping the leftovers has a cost too, even if it is not financial. Clear them out the same week and the space is the real win.
