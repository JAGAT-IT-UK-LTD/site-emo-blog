Energy price cap £2,166 from 1 January: what the 26% rise costs you

Ofgem announced on 25 November 2026 that the energy price cap rises 26% to £2,166 a year from 1 January 2027, up £443. Gas does most of the damage, but every household on a standard variable tariff pays more, and the £100 payment only reaches some.

The short version

  • The cap is £2,166 a year for a typical dual fuel household paying by Direct Debit, for 1 January to 31 March 2027. It was £1,723.
  • That is £443 more, or 26%. Ofgem says electricity bills rise 19% and gas bills 33% compared with October.
  • Gas unit rate: 10.96p per kWh, up from 7.97p. Electricity: 32.69p, up from 26.32p. Standing charges barely move.

  • The £100 government payment is for council tax bands A to D in England only, paid from January 2027. It covers a fraction of the rise.

1. What did Ofgem announce on 25 November?

Ofgem confirmed the energy price cap for 1 January to 31 March 2027. For a typical household using gas and electricity and paying by Direct Debit, it is £2,166 a year. The level for October to December was £1,723.

That is a rise of £443, or 26%. Ofgem's own release splits it by fuel. The electricity part of a typical bill is up 19%. The gas part is up 33% compared with October.

This is a far bigger jump than the 4% rise we covered in our £1,723 guide, and it is the one most households will feel through the coldest months.

Two things matter before you do any sums. First, the £2,166 is not a limit on your bill. Ofgem says plainly that the cap "does not limit the cost of your total bill". It limits the price per unit and the daily standing charge. Use more energy and you pay more.

Second, it is a rate for a three month window. Ofgem resets it every three months, so this is the rate until 31 March, not a fixed price for the year.

2. What are the new rates?

Here are the UK average figures for Direct Debit customers, taken from Ofgem's unit rates page. Your own region will differ, because rates vary by region, payment method and meter type.

Rate1 Oct to 31 Dec 20261 Jan to 31 Mar 2027Change
Electricity unit rate26.32p per kWh32.69p per kWhUp 6.37p
Electricity standing charge54.83p a day54.77p a dayDown 0.06p
Gas unit rate7.97p per kWh10.96p per kWhUp 2.99p
Gas standing charge29.68p a day29.65p a dayDown 0.03p

Our own arithmetic on those figures: the electricity unit rate is up about 24% and the gas unit rate is up about 37.5%. The standing charges are essentially flat, so the whole rise lands on the energy you use.

That matters if you were thinking about a low standing charge tariff. The standing charge is not where this increase sits.

One more detail from Ofgem. There is no VAT on electricity from 1 October 2026 to 30 June 2027, and VAT on gas is included in the gas rate. Both periods in the table sit inside that window, so the comparison above is fair.

3. What does it cost a real household?

The £2,166 is for a typical home. Few homes are typical, so here are three households. These are our own sums, not Ofgem's. We used the UK average Direct Debit rates above, the usage we state, and 365 days of standing charges. We assumed the January rates stay in force for a full year, which they will not, so read the result as the size of the gap, not a forecast of your annual bill.

Household (assumed yearly use)At October ratesAt January ratesDifference

Priya, one bed flat with gas: 2,000 kWh electricity, 6,000 kWh gas£1,313£1,620£306 a year, about £26 a month
The Okafors, three bed semi: 2,500 kWh electricity, 10,000 kWh gas£1,763£2,221£458 a year, about £38 a month

Here is the Okafor sum in full. Electricity: 2,500 kWh at 32.69p is £817.25. Gas: 10,000 kWh at 10.96p is £1,096.00. Standing charges: 365 days at 84.42p (54.77p plus 29.65p) is £308.13. The total is £2,221.38.

At October rates the same usage comes to £1,763.46, so the rise is £457.92. That is close to the £443 headline, because the Okafors use a little more than a typical home.

Notice who is hit hardest in pounds. The household that burns the most gas carries the biggest increase, because gas went up the most in percentage terms. Dan, with no gas at all, still pays £204 more.

If the Okafors live in England in a council tax band A to D home, the £100 payment brings their net rise to about £358. That is our arithmetic, and it assumes they qualify.

4. Who is not affected, and who gets the £100?

The cap only protects people on standard variable tariffs. Ofgem says it does not cover fixed tariffs, business energy contracts, heat networks or heating oil.

Ofgem says about 20 million households are on standard variable tariffs and about 10 million are on fixed tariffs. If you are on a fix, this announcement does not change your price while the deal lasts. Our guide to whether you are in the wrong third explains how to check.

The government support is narrower than the headline suggests. GOV.UK says:

  • £100 goes to households in council tax bands A to D in England, paid from January 2027.
  • Direct Debit council tax payers need to do nothing, and the money goes into their bank account. Others will be invited by their council to apply.
  • Second homes and empty properties are not eligible.
  • £100 million is set aside for councils to help households outside bands A to D, on criteria each council sets.
  • Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland get extra funding and decide how to use it.

GOV.UK also warns that only your council should contact you about claiming. Treat any text or email asking for bank details as a scam. Our £100 payment guide has the full eligibility rules.

The Warm Home Discount of up to £150 is a separate scheme for some people on means tested benefits. Read our Warm Home Discount guide to check if you qualify.

5. What is worth doing before 1 January?

You cannot change the cap. You can change how much of it you pay. These are the steps with the clearest payoff.

Switch to Direct Debit if you pay on receipt of a bill. Ofgem says this could save the average customer about £160 a year. It is the biggest saving on the list and costs nothing.

Take a meter reading on 31 December. The new rates start on 1 January. A dated reading stops a supplier estimating the split between old and new rates. Photograph the meter.

Compare any fix against the cap rates, not the headline. Put the fixed gas rate beside 10.96p and the electricity rate beside 32.69p, then compare standing charges. Ofgem's advice is to consider whether the predictability of a fixed tariff suits you. Check for exit fees before you sign. Our fix now or wait guide sets out the trade off.

Cut the use, not just the price. Gas is now 10.96p per kWh, so each unit saved is worth more than it was in October. Our guide to cutting energy bills ranks the measures by saving.

Tell your supplier early if you are struggling. Ofgem urges anyone struggling to afford their bills to reach out to their supplier, and says it has written to suppliers and is increasing monitoring over the winter. You may be offered a payment plan or a hardship route. Do not wait until you are in arrears.

6. The honest verdict

This is a large rise and there is no trick that removes it. A typical home pays £443 more a year at the new rate, and a gas heated home with above average use pays more than that.

The £100 payment helps if you are in England in bands A to D, but it covers less than a quarter of the typical rise (our arithmetic: £100 against £443). It does nothing for a larger home in band E or above unless your council's discretionary fund applies.

The actions that pay are small and dull. Pay by Direct Debit, record your meter reading on 31 December, and check any fixed deal against the real rates. Do not panic fix just because the number is big. A fix that is dearer than the cap and has an exit fee is a poor trade if the cap later falls, because you are locked in.

If you are already on a fixed deal, there is nothing to do today. Diarise the end date and check the market a few weeks before it.

Frequently asked

When does the new price cap start? The new cap applies from 1 January 2027 and runs to 31 March 2027. Ofgem announced it on 25 November 2026. It affects customers on standard variable tariffs. If you are on a fixed tariff, your rates stay as agreed until the fix ends.

How much more will I pay a month? For a typical dual fuel household paying by Direct Debit, the cap rises by £443 a year, which is about £37 a month on our arithmetic. Your figure depends on your use and region. A gas heated family home will usually pay more than a small flat.

Does the price cap rise affect me on a fixed tariff? Not while your deal lasts. Ofgem says about 10 million households are on fixed tariffs and are not affected by the cap change. Check your end date and what happens when it arrives.

Do I get the £100 energy payment? If you live in England and are responsible for council tax on your main home in bands A to D, you should. Payments start in January 2027. Direct Debit council tax payers need to do nothing. Others will be invited by their council to apply.
