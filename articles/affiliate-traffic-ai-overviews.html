AI Overviews Ate Search, but Not Shopping: Where Affiliate Traffic Still Comes From

The most important finding, and it is good news

The story everyone tells about 2025 and 2026 is that AI Overviews destroyed publisher traffic. That is broadly true, and it hit small sites hardest: blogs doing 1,000 to 10,000 daily views lost around 60% of search traffic, against 22% for the largest publishers. Google has since handed publishers their own controls over how that content is reused, and what opting out of AI Overviews actually costs you in traffic is the decision sitting behind the headline.

But the damage is not evenly spread, and the distribution is the useful part.

Google deliberately pulled AI Overviews back on transactional queries, because those are the queries carrying its own shopping ads. Whatever you think of the motive, the consequence for anyone writing purchase-intent content is direct.

"How do I lower my cholesterol" is being eaten alive. "Best cordless vacuum for pet hair under £250" is comparatively protected. Commercial-intent content is now structurally one of the safest positions left in search, and it happens to be exactly where affiliate content sits. That is a considerably better argument for this business model than anything in the usual guru pitch.

The finding that inverts beginner advice

Pew measured which queries actually trigger AI Overviews across 68,879 real searches:

  • 8% of one or two word searches
  • 53% of searches with ten or more words
  • 60% of question-format queries

Now hold that next to the standard advice given to every new site owner: target long-tail question keywords, because they are winnable.

Long-tail question keywords are the queries most likely to trigger an AI Overview and lose the click. Combine it with Amsive's finding that keywords ranking outside the top three lose 27% of click-through under an overview, and a new site chasing long-tail questions is optimising straight into the worst-affected intersection of the whole search landscape.

This does not mean abandon long-tail. It means the shape of the winning long-tail query changed. "How do I clean a cast iron pan" is a question with a settled answer, and Google will answer it. "Cast iron pan for an induction hob under £60" is a decision with constraints, and the answer is a shortlist of products Google would rather sell ads against than summarise.

Four things that still earn clicks

  1. Branded queries. The only measured positive under AI Overviews, at +18.7% click-through. Building a name people search for by name is now a traffic-defence mechanism rather than vanity.
  2. Transactional, bottom-funnel, non-branded queries. "X vs Y", "best X under £200", "X for a specific constraint". This is the protected band.
  3. Top three positions. The damage is markedly worse below position three, which means depth on fewer topics beats breadth across many.
  4. Content an AI cannot restate. Your own test data, hands-on photographs, specific fit and compatibility answers, current availability. If the answer already exists inside the summary, the click does not happen. That is the entire filter.

Off-site mentions beat backlinks by roughly three to one. Being talked about by name, on forums, YouTube, podcasts, roundups, Reddit and trade press, does more for AI visibility than link building does. A mention with no link attached is worth having, which is not something anyone would have said three years ago.

Academic work on generative engine optimisation found the highest-impact page-level tactics were citing sources, at +24.9%, using precise technical terms at +23.1%, and an authoritative tone at +21.8%. Keyword stuffing was the worst performer of everything tested and actively counterproductive.

Correlation is not causation, and Ahrefs' own framing acknowledges that. But the practical instruction that falls out of it is the same either way: be specific, cite where your numbers came from, and get mentioned by name in places people actually read.

Ignore llms.txt

You will see advice to add an llms.txt file to your site. Do not spend time on it.

Google's Gary Illyes has confirmed Google does not support it and is not planning to, and John Mueller compared it to the discredited keywords meta tag. One monitoring study of more than 500 million AI bot events found only a few hundred requests to that file at all. The crawlers fetch HTML.

The actual actionable item is duller and more important: make sure AI crawlers are not blocked in your robots.txt. Google also states plainly that there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and no special optimisations necessary.

Google's own spec for a review page

Google publishes a document called "Write high quality reviews". Every bullet in it is a direct requirement, and a page that hits all of them is very hard for an AI-generated competitor to match. Use it as a checklist.

Prove experience:

  • Demonstrate you are knowledgeable about the category.
  • Provide evidence of your own experience: visuals, audio or links.
  • Share quantitative measurements.
  • Discuss benefits and drawbacks based on your own original research.
  • Describe how the product evolved from previous models.

Serve the decision:

  • Explain what sets it apart from competitors.
  • Cover comparable things, or explain which is best for which use.
  • Identify the key decision factors, with supporting evidence.
  • Include links to other useful resources.
  • Consider including links to multiple sellers. Google explicitly wants you to link beyond Amazon, which is worth noticing, because it is the opposite of what most affiliate advice tells you.

On structured data, the position in 2026 is: Product schema is alive and worth using on specific-product pages. Review and AggregateRating are alive, but you cannot aggregate ratings from other websites, so use your own editorial rating with a real named author and never scraped Amazon stars. ItemList is alive and valid for ranked lists. Article with Person produces no rich result but is still worth having for authorship signalling. FAQ markup remains valid, but the FAQ rich result itself was retired in May 2026, so do not expect a visual payoff from it.

What to actually do

  1. Write for decisions with constraints, not for questions with answers. Budget, compatibility, use case, comparison.
  2. Go deep on one category rather than wide across five. The click-through penalty below position three makes breadth expensive.
  3. Put something in every page that only you could have written. A photograph, a measurement, a failure you had.
  4. Cite your sources visibly. It is the single highest-impact page-level tactic measured, and it happens to be honest.
  5. Chase mentions, not just links. Being named on a forum thread or in a video is worth more to AI visibility than a directory backlink.
  6. Build a brand name worth searching for. Branded queries are the only intent gaining click-through.

Questions people ask

Is SEO dead? No, but the middle of the funnel is badly damaged and informational content in the heavily covered industries is in real trouble. Commercial-intent content is comparatively intact.

Should I stop targeting long-tail keywords? Target long-tail with purchase intent and constraints. Avoid long-tail that is a factual question with a settled answer, because that is precisely what an overview answers for free.

Does getting cited in an AI Overview send traffic? Barely. Pew measured 1% of users clicking a link inside the summary. Citation is a brand-visibility play, not a traffic play, and it is worth being clear-eyed about that.

How reliable are these numbers? They come from four independent studies with disclosed sample sizes, which is unusually good for this subject. They are also snapshots of a fast-moving system, so treat the direction as solid and the decimals as provisional.

The verdict

Search got worse for publishers and it got worse unevenly. If you write about what to buy, you are sitting in the narrow band Google chose not to summarise. The winning move is depth on a narrow category, pages that contain something an AI cannot restate, visible sources, and a brand name people type on purpose.
