AI Overviews Traffic Impact: Where Affiliate Clicks Still Come From in 2026

Published 22 August 2026 · Updated 14 November 2026 · 12 min read · Figures checked November 2026

AI Overviews now take a large share of the clicks that used to reach affiliate pages. Ahrefs found the top result gets about 58% fewer clicks when an overview appears. Shopping searches were spared in 2025, but by August 2026 Semrush tracked overviews on 88% of the commercial keywords it watched, so the old safe harbour has mostly gone.

The short version
  • When an AI Overview appears, position one gets about 58% fewer clicks, on Ahrefs' December 2025 data. Its April 2025 study had put the loss at 34.5%.
  • This guide first said shopping searches were protected. That is no longer true. Semrush found AI Overviews on 88% of tracked commercial keywords in August 2026, up from 45.75% in September 2025.
  • Pew found people clicked a normal result on 8% of searches with an AI summary, against 15% without one. Only 1% clicked a link inside the summary.
  • Branded searches are the one type Amsive measured gaining clicks under an overview, at about +18.7%.
  • Being mentioned by name across the web correlates with AI visibility about three times as strongly as backlink count.

Are shopping searches still safe from AI Overviews?

When this guide was first published in August 2026, the headline was good news. Shopping searches seemed to have escaped AI Overviews, while health and education searches were swamped.

The newer data says that shelter has mostly closed.

The health side is as bad as it looked. BrightEdge tracked AI Overview coverage on healthcare searches rising from 72% in May 2024 to 88% in December 2025. Finance rose from 6% to 21% over the same time.

The shopping side has changed. Semrush tracked a fixed panel of 400 commercial keywords in banking, ecommerce and software, on US desktop data, every month from September 2025 to August 2026.

Share of keywords that showed an AI Overview. Semrush study published 21 October 2026. US desktop data, so UK results may differ.

That last row explains where the old "around 4%" figure for shopping came from. Price searches in ecommerce really were nearly untouched a year ago. They are not now.

The "best X for Y" row is the one that matters most to an affiliate site. It is the exact shape of a buying guide, and almost every one of them now shows an overview.

So the honest update is plain. Purchase intent content is no longer a protected position in search. It is simply the position where the reader still has a reason to click, because they want to compare, check a price and buy.

Google also gives publishers some control over how their content is reused. What opting out of AI Overviews actually costs you in traffic is the decision sitting behind this headline.

The Ahrefs rows are the ones to read twice. The same team, using the same method, found the loss at position one grew from about a third to more than half in under a year.

Ahrefs also found the loss shrinks further down the page. Its table shows about 50.8% at position two and 19.4% at position ten. Lower positions had fewer clicks to lose in the first place.

The Amsive row contains the only positive number anyone has measured. Branded searches gained clicks. Hold on to that, because it shapes everything below.

Why does long tail keyword advice backfire?

Pew measured which searches actually trigger AI summaries across its 68,879 searches.

  • 8% of one or two word searches
  • 53% of searches with ten or more words
  • 60% of searches phrased as a question
  • 18% of all searches overall

Now hold that next to the standard advice for every new site owner: target long tail question keywords, because they are winnable.

Long tail questions are the searches most likely to show an AI summary and lose the click. A new site chasing them is aiming at the most affected part of search.

This does not mean abandon long tail. It means the shape of a winning long tail search has changed.

"How do I clean a cast iron pan" is a question with a settled answer. Google will answer it, and nobody needs to visit you.

"Cast iron pan for an induction hob under £60" is a decision with constraints. The overview may appear, but the reader still wants to see the pans, check prices and buy. Your page has to be the place that helps them do that.

Now say Priya builds a name people search for. A reader types "Priya's pan guide cast iron" instead of the generic phrase. Amsive found branded searches gained clicks under an overview, rather than losing them.

The lesson is not that her site is finished. It is that the same ranking is now worth less, and the gap has to be made up by brand, depth and more than one income stream.

What still earns affiliate clicks?

  1. Branded searches. The only measured gain under AI Overviews, at about +18.7% in Amsive's data. A name people search for is now a defence, not vanity.
  2. Decisions with constraints. "X vs Y", "best X under £200", "X for a specific need". Overviews now appear here too, but the reader still has to choose and buy, and that needs a page.
  3. Top three positions. Ahrefs' figures show every position loses clicks, so depth on fewer topics beats breadth across many.
  4. Content an AI cannot restate. Your own test data, hands on photographs, specific fit and compatibility answers, current availability. If the answer already sits inside the summary, the click does not happen.
  5. Traffic you own. An email list does not depend on a search box. What an email list costs an affiliate site sets out the sums.

Older pages can often be lifted rather than replaced. Refreshing affiliate content against writing new posts weighs the two.

How do you get cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini?

Ahrefs compared AI Overview brand visibility across 75,000 brands in May 2025. The result reorders most of what people were told about link building.

Mentions beat backlinks by roughly three to one. Being talked about by name on forums, YouTube, podcasts, roundups and trade press goes with more AI visibility than link building does.

A mention with no link attached is worth having. Few people would have said that three years ago.

Ahrefs also found 26% of brands had no mentions in AI Overviews at all. The top quarter of brands by web mentions averaged more than ten times the mentions of the next quarter down.

The study covers Google's AI Overviews. ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini work differently, so treat the pattern as a strong hint rather than a rule for all of them.

Academic work points the same way. The generative engine optimisation paper by Aggarwal and colleagues, presented at KDD 2024, found page changes could raise visibility in AI answers by up to 40%. It also found the best tactic varies by subject.

Correlation is not causation, and Ahrefs says so itself. The practical instruction is the same either way: be specific, cite where your numbers came from, and get mentioned by name where people actually read.

Do you need an llms.txt file?

You will see advice to add an llms.txt file to your site. For Google, do not spend time on it.

Google's own AI features page now says you do not need to create machine readable files, AI text files or special markup to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode. It says there are no additional requirements and no special optimisations necessary.

Gary Illyes of Google said in July 2025 that Google does not support llms.txt and is not planning to. John Mueller compared it to the keywords meta tag that search engines have ignored for years.

The useful job is duller and more important. Make sure Googlebot is not blocked in your robots.txt. Google says robots.txt is the control site owners use to manage how Search crawls them.

What does Google want on a review page?

Google publishes a page called "Write high quality reviews". Every point in it is a direct instruction. A page that meets all of them is very hard for an AI written competitor to match, so use it as a checklist.

Prove experience:

  • Show you know the category well.
  • Give evidence of your own experience: your own visuals, audio or links.
  • Share measurements across the things that matter.
  • Discuss benefits and drawbacks from your own original research.
  • Describe how the product improved on earlier models.

Serve the decision:

  • Explain what sets it apart from competitors.
  • Cover comparable products, or say which suits which use.
  • Focus on the key decision factors, with your evidence.
  • Back any "best overall" or "best for" pick with first hand evidence.
  • Link to other useful resources, yours or other people's.
  • Consider linking to multiple sellers so readers can buy from the shop they prefer. Google wants you to link beyond Amazon, which is the opposite of what most affiliate advice says.

On structured data, Product markup is still worth using on single product pages. Review markup is still valid, but use your own editorial rating with a real named author, never scraped Amazon stars.

The FAQ rich result has gone. Google's documentation says it stopped appearing in Search from 7 May 2026, and the documentation was removed on 15 June 2026. FAQ markup does no harm, but do not expect a visual payoff from it.

Google's ranking updates hit thin review pages hardest. What the 2026 core update did to affiliate sites shows which pages fell.

What should an affiliate site do now?

  1. Write for decisions with constraints, not for questions with answers. Budget, compatibility, use case, comparison.
  2. Go deep on one category rather than wide across five. Every position loses clicks under an overview, so thin breadth is expensive.
  3. Put something on every page that only you could have written. A photograph, a measurement, a failure you had.
  4. Cite your sources visibly. It helps readers trust you, and it is honest.
  5. Chase mentions, not just links. Being named on a forum thread or in a video goes with more AI visibility than a directory backlink.
  6. Build a brand name worth searching for. Branded searches are the only kind measured gaining clicks.
  7. Link to more than one seller and build an email list. Do not let one search box and one retailer hold all your income.

Frequently asked

How much traffic do AI Overviews take from websites? It depends on position and search type. Ahrefs found the top result gets about 58% fewer clicks when an overview appears, on December 2025 data. Pew found people clicked a normal result on 8% of searches with an AI summary, against 15% without. Branded searches were the one type Amsive measured gaining clicks.

Do AI Overviews appear on shopping searches? Increasingly, yes. Semrush tracked 400 commercial keywords on US desktop data and found AI Overviews on 88% of them by August 2026, up from 45.75% a year earlier. Ecommerce keywords reached 85.11%, and "best X for Y" searches 96.12%. UK figures may differ, but the direction is clear.

Should I stop targeting long tail keywords? No, but choose them differently. Pew found 60% of question searches showed an AI summary. Target long tail searches that carry a buying decision and constraints, such as budget or compatibility. Avoid factual questions with one settled answer, because that is exactly what an overview answers for free.

Does being cited in an AI Overview send traffic? Barely. Pew measured only 1% of visits where someone clicked a link inside the summary. Being cited builds your name, which matters because branded searches gained clicks in Amsive's data. Treat citation as brand building, not a traffic source, and plan your income around it accordingly.

How reliable are these AI Overview numbers? They come from several independent studies with stated sample sizes, which is unusually good for this subject. Most use US data, and the system changes fast. Ahrefs' own estimate moved from 34.5% to 58% in under a year. Trust the direction, and treat the decimals as provisional.

The verdict

Search got worse for publishers, and in 2026 it got worse for shopping content too. The idea that Google would leave buying guides alone has not survived the data.

That does not make affiliate sites pointless. It makes thin ones pointless. The winning move is depth on a narrow category, pages with something an AI cannot restate, visible sources, links to more than one seller, and a brand name people type on purpose.
