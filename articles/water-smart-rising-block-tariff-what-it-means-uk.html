The water tariff that is cheaper until it suddenly is not, and where the line sits

Ruhul Amin · Published 9 November 2026 · 6 min read · Figures checked November 2026

Most water bills charge one rate however much you use. A rising block tariff charges three, and the top one is more than twice the standard rate. Severn Trent publishes the numbers for its Water Smart trial in full, so we ran them, and the point where it flips from a good deal to an expensive one is sharper than you would expect.

The short version

  • Three rates instead of one. On Severn Trent's published Water Smart trial rates, water and used water add up to £1.5078 a cubic metre up to 5 cubic metres a month, £4.1758 from 5 to 10, and £10.4397 above 10. Used water is billed on 95 per cent of supply, so each cubic metre through the meter costs about £1.48, £4.09 and £10.24.
  • The top block is about seven times the bottom one and about two and a half times the standard tariff's flat rate.
  • Our calculated crossover is about 12.5 cubic metres a month, roughly 150 a year. Below that the trial tariff is cheaper. Above it, it is dearer, fast.
  • At 8 cubic metres a month it saves about £172 a year. At 15 it costs about £182 a year more. Same household, same tariff, opposite answer.
  • A 400 litre a day toilet leak is 12.17 cubic metres a month on its own, which would push almost any household over the line without anyone using more water.

1. What the two tariffs actually charge

Severn Trent publishes its full household scheme of charges, which is why we use it as the worked example throughout this series. These figures come from its metered charges page and its full household scheme of charges for 2026/2027, checked on 9 November 2026, and cover 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027.

On the standard metered tariff in zones 1 to 8, water supply is £2.5503 per cubic metre with a standing charge of £51.83 a year, and used water discharged directly or indirectly to a public sewer is £1.6255 per cubic metre with a fixed charge of £39.16 a year. There is also a highway drainage charge of £19.68 a year.

One detail changes every sum below. Severn Trent's scheme of charges says it assumes 95 per cent of the water supplied returns to the sewer, and bills used water on that volume. So one more cubic metre through the meter costs £2.5503 plus 95 per cent of £1.6255, which is £4.0945, however much you already use.

On the Water Smart tariff trial, the structure changes completely. The water standing charge drops to £40.33 a year, the used water standing charge stays at £39.16 and highway drainage stays at £19.68. The volumetric charges become monthly blocks:

  • Basic block, 0 to 5 cubic metres a month: water £1.0201, used water £0.4877, so £1.5078 combined.
  • Standard block, 5 to 10 cubic metres a month: water £2.5503, used water £1.6255, so £4.1758 combined, which is exactly the standard tariff rate.
  • Additional block, over 10 cubic metres a month: water £6.3758, used water £4.0639, so £10.4397 combined.

The used water blocks apply to the used water volume, which is 95 per cent of supply, so used water only reaches its own 10 cubic metre line when about 10.5 cubic metres has gone through the meter. Put together, a cubic metre through the meter costs about £1.48 in the basic block, £4.09 in the standard block and £10.24 in the additional block.

That middle block being identical to the flat rate is the clue to the whole design. The tariff is built so that a moderate user pays about the same, a light user pays much less, and a heavy user pays a great deal more. Surface water drainage is charged separately on both tariffs and is left out of everything below, so it does not distort the comparison.

2. Where the crossover sits

Because the blocks are monthly and cumulative, only the volume above each threshold is charged at the higher rate. That makes the arithmetic slightly fiddly and it is why nobody publishes the answer, so here it is.

Surface water drainage is excluded from both sides.

The shape is worth understanding rather than memorising. At exactly 5 cubic metres a month the saving is £168.17 a year, a touch lower, because used water at 95 per cent of supply has not quite filled its cheap block. From about 5.3 to 10 cubic metres the saving is flat at £171.58, because the middle blocks match the standard rates exactly and the standing charge gap of £11.50 is fixed.

Above 10 the saving erodes. First at £3.8255 a cubic metre, the gap between the top and standard water rates, until supply reaches about 10.5. Then at about £6.14 a cubic metre, once used water crosses its own line too. That erosion is quick: it takes only about 2.5 cubic metres a month above the threshold to wipe out the entire £171.58.

3. What this actually means for a household

Twelve and a half cubic metres a month is 12,500 litres, or about 411 litres a day. For a household of one or two that is a long way off. For a family of four or five with a garden, a couple of teenagers and a paddling pool in July, it is not.

The seasonality is the part that catches people, because the blocks are monthly rather than annual. A household averaging 9 cubic metres a month across the year is comfortably inside the safe zone on paper, but if July and August come in at 16 while February comes in at 5, those two summer months are charged at the top rate and the cheap winter months cannot claw it back. A monthly block tariff punishes a spiky usage pattern even when the annual total is modest.

Then there is the failure mode this series has already covered. Severn Trent's own figure for a bad toilet leak is up to 400 litres a day, which is 12.17 cubic metres a month. On its own, without anybody in the house using a drop more water, that leak takes a typical household from the middle block deep into the top one, where every cubic metre costs about £10.24 instead of about £4.09. A leak that costs about £600 a year on the standard tariff costs very much more than that here.

It is worth being clear about who this tariff is designed to help and who it is designed to discourage, because the two are not symmetrical. A single person or a couple in a flat will almost never approach 10 cubic metres a month and will bank the whole saving for changing nothing at all. A large family in a house with a garden has far less room to manoeuvre, because most of their consumption is people rather than habits, and you cannot decide to have fewer showers in the house than you have people in it. A rising block tariff rewards small households and charges large ones, and whether that is fair depends on a view about water that sits well outside an article on tariffs.

Two honest caveats. This is a trial tariff, which means it is not offered to everyone and the terms can change. Severn Trent says it is by invitation, for smart metered customers across Birmingham, Coventry, Warwickshire, Leicestershire and parts of Shropshire, for up to 3,000 households from 2025, lasting at least a year. Customers on its Big Difference scheme or WaterSure are left out, and anyone on the trial can switch back to the standard tariff. And we have used Severn Trent throughout because it publishes the full scheme openly. Other companies are moving in the same direction at different speeds and with different block sizes, and the method here transfers even though the numbers will not.

4. A worked example

Pauline, Coventry. Four in the house, two of them teenagers, a metered supply and a vegetable patch she waters most evenings from May to August.

She looks at twelve months of meter readings rather than the annual total, which is the right instinct. Her average is 9.2 cubic metres a month. On that number alone Water Smart looks like a straightforward £171.58 a year saving, because 9.2 sits inside the middle block.

The monthly detail says something different. November through March she is between 6 and 8. June, July and August are 14, 16 and 13. In those three months her supply is over the 10 cubic metre line by 4, 6 and 3, which is 13 cubic metres charged at the top water rate, a gap of £3.8255 each over the standard rate. Her used water, billed at 95 per cent of supply, is over its own line by 3.3, 5.2 and 2.35, which is 10.85 cubic metres at a gap of £2.4384. That is £49.73 plus £26.46, so £76.19 of extra cost concentrated in one summer.

She is still ahead overall, because £171.58 less £76.19 leaves about £95 a year, and that is a real saving for changing nothing. But it is little more than half what the annual average suggested, and if next summer is hotter it goes to nearly nothing.

What she does with that is the useful part. She moves the vegetable patch onto stored rainwater for the three peak months, which is the one use of a water butt where the arithmetic genuinely works, because it is displacing water costing about £10.24 a cubic metre through the meter rather than about £4.09. Displacing even 6 cubic metres across the summer at the top rate is about £61.42, which is a different proposition entirely from the same butt on a flat tariff.

The verdict

If you are offered a rising block water tariff and you stay under 10 cubic metres in your heaviest month, take it, because on Severn Trent's published 2026/27 rates it is worth roughly £171 a year and you can switch back if it does not suit you. Between 10 and about 12.5 it still wins, but by less each cubic metre.

Judge it on your worst month, not your annual average, because the blocks reset monthly and one hot summer can erase most of the gain. And before you sign up to anything, check your toilet, because a single leak is enough on its own to move you into the most expensive block.

Frequently asked

What is a rising block water tariff? A tariff with more than one volumetric rate, where the price per cubic metre increases as monthly consumption passes set thresholds. On Severn Trent's Water Smart trial the published water and used water rates add up to £1.5078, then £4.1758, then £10.4397 per cubic metre.

Is the trial tariff always cheaper? No. On our arithmetic using the published rates it is cheaper up to about 12.5 cubic metres a month and dearer above that, and the crossover is sharp because the top block is about two and a half times the standard flat rate.

Do the blocks reset every month or every year? Every month. Severn Trent describes the basic allowance as 60,000 litres a year but works it out as 5,000 litres a month, starting again each month. That is why a household with a modest annual total can still be charged at the top rate during a hot summer.

Can I choose a rising block tariff? Not yet at Severn Trent. Water Smart is an invitation only trial for smart metered customers in parts of its region, and Severn Trent says it may offer it more widely if the trial goes well. Other companies run their own trials with different block sizes, so check directly with yours.
