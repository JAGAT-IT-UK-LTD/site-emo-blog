1. Why did the obvious AI money disappear?

The instinct is reasonable. AI writes fast, so sell writing. The problem is that everybody had the same instinct, including your prospective client.

The work AI does easily is generic blog posts, simple logos, basic data entry and transcription clean up. A business owner with a free chatbot can produce an adequate version in under two minutes.

They will not pay you much for it, because they know what it took. Prices for that kind of work only go one way.

This is the part the "make money with AI" videos skip. AI did not create a market for selling AI output. It destroyed the market for anything AI can do unsupervised.

If a client can get most of your service for free in under a minute, you do not have a business. You have a demonstration.

The money moved one level up, from producing output to making the tools work inside a business. Most small firms know AI exists, have tried it once, and have no idea how to make it reliable.

3. How can you make money with AI in the UK? Four routes

Route A: the small business AI audit and setup

Find one local business drowning in repeated admin: quotes, invoices, enquiry replies, social posts. Spend a day mapping what they repeat.

Then build three or four templates and processes that use AI properly. Write them down so the staff can follow them without you.

If that is the side hustle you want, the sums are worked through in what it costs to start teaching AI tools to local businesses.

Charge £400 to £800 for the first one while you have no track record. Two of those is your £1,000. Realistic timeline: 4 to 8 weeks, most of it spent finding the first client, not doing the work.

Route B: an AI assisted service in a field you already know

If you already have a skill, such as bookkeeping, recruitment, property or teaching, sell that skill delivered faster with AI. Do not sell AI itself.

Your knowledge of the field is the moat. AI just raises your output. There is a worked version of this route in a 30 day AI assisted freelance writing plan built for under 50 pounds.

Timeline: 3 to 6 weeks. This is the fastest route if you have any professional background.

Route C: fixing what AI got wrong

A growing, unglamorous market. Businesses published AI content that is generic, inaccurate or hurting their search visibility. Now they need a human to audit and repair it.

The same goes for AI generated code and spreadsheets nobody understands. Timeline: 4 to 8 weeks. It is easier to sell than it sounds, because the client already knows they have a problem.

Route D: productised templates or tooling

Build one genuinely useful thing, such as a prompt system, a spreadsheet or a small automation, and sell it again and again. Lower price, no client calls.

But it needs an audience, which is the slow part. Timeline: 3 to 6 months to £1,000. Be honest with yourself about this one.

Worked example: Nadia in Bristol earns her first £1,000

Nadia works full time in an office in Bristol and has no design or coding background. She picks Route A and works evenings and weekends.

Weeks 1 to 2. She practises on her own repeated tasks: an enquiry reply system, a quote template, a weekly report. No client yet, but she now has a real before and after to show.

Week 3. She approaches five local businesses she already buys from. Not a cold email blast, five people who know her face. One says yes to a paid trial.

Weeks 4 to 5. A dog groomer, buried in enquiry messages. She builds a reply system, a booking confirmation flow and a simple monthly social plan, then writes a two page guide. £450 invoiced.

Weeks 6 to 7. The groomer refers a nearby beautician. Same work, faster the second time, and she charges more. £600 invoiced.

Total: £1,050 in seven weeks. Roughly 40 hours of work, including the unpaid learning and selling. That is about £26 an hour.

For comparison, the National Living Wage is £12.71 an hour for workers aged 21 and over. Nadia earned about double that, and her third client, priced properly, is where it starts to look like a real rate.

The tax. Her trading income of £1,050 is over £1,000 in 2026 to 2027, so she must register for Self Assessment by 5 October 2027.

She can deduct the £1,000 trading allowance instead of her actual costs. That leaves £50 taxable. Her salary already uses her personal allowance, so she pays 20% on it: £10 of income tax.

Had the same £1,050 come in after 6 April 2027, it would sit under the new £3,000 trading allowance.

4. How do you get a first client with no portfolio?

  1. Solve it for yourself or your employer first. A written before and after from a real business, even your own workplace, beats any certificate.
  2. Start with people who already know you. The gym, the barber, the takeaway, the accountant. Knowing you removes most of the risk in their mind.
  3. Lead with their problem, not the technology. "You answer the same five questions all day" lands. "I do AI automation" does not.
  4. Offer a small paid pilot, not a free trial. Free work attracts people who will never pay. Try £150 for one process, then more if it works.
  5. Leave them something they own. A written process document turns a one off job into a referral.

5. What should you charge, and how do you say it?

Price the outcome, not your hours. "Two hours of my time" invites haggling.

"This saves you around six hours a week, and the setup is £600" is a business case. The owner can weigh it against their own time.

Starting out, sit at the bottom of the range and move up every second client. Going from £400 to £600 to £800 across three jobs is a sensible climb.

One rule: never discount to win the first client. Reduce the scope instead. A cheap client sets a cheap anchor and tells everyone they refer what you cost.

6. Do you pay tax on AI side hustle income?

Yes, once it passes the trading allowance. For 2026 to 2027 that allowance is £1,000 of gross trading income.

Above £1,000 you must register for Self Assessment. The deadline to register is 5 October after the end of the tax year in which you started.

From 6 April 2027, the government is raising the trading allowance to £3,000. It also raises the Self Assessment reporting threshold for trading income to £3,000.

Full detail is in our side hustle tax guide. If HMRC writes to you about platform income, read what an HMRC nudge letter does and does not mean.

Set aside a share of every payment from day one, rather than finding the bill in January.

Honesty with clients. Tell them AI is part of your process. Most do not mind, because they are hiring your judgement. Hiding it and being found out ends the relationship and the referrals.

Data and responsibility. If you handle customer data, the ICO's guidance on AI and data protection applies to you. Check everything you produce, because you are professionally responsible for it, not the tool.

Once the work grows, look at professional indemnity insurance for freelancers. A setup that sends a wrong quote to a customer is exactly the claim it covers.

7. Which AI money ideas should you avoid?

Courses promising £10,000 a month. The person selling the course found a more reliable business than the one they teach: selling the course.

Mass produced AI content sites. Search engines have got better at spotting bulk low value pages. It is a lot of work for a coin flip.

Anything that needs you to pay upfront for the privilege of working. Real clients pay you. When it runs the other way, it is a scam.

Reselling raw AI output as your own service. Not only because you should tell your client, but because it is commercially fragile. That price only goes down.

Frequently asked

Can you really make money with AI in the UK?

Yes, but rarely by selling AI output. The paid work is in setting AI up inside a business and being accountable for it. UK contracts citing generative AI advertised a median of £563 a day in late 2026. A beginner should expect far less at first, and four to ten weeks to a first £1,000.

How can I make money with AI with no experience?

Start with a skill or a workplace you already know. Use AI to fix one repeated task there, write down what you did, and offer the same fix to a local business you already use. A small paid pilot of around £150 is easier to say yes to than a big project.

Do I need to be technical?

No. The most sellable version is understanding how a business repeats itself, plus clear written instructions people can follow. Most small firms want reliable replies, quotes and reports, not code. If you can map a process and explain it plainly, you can do the job without any programming.

Do I have to tell HMRC about AI side hustle income?

Yes, if your gross trading income passes £1,000 in the 2026 to 2027 tax year. You then register for Self Assessment by 5 October after the tax year ends. From 6 April 2027 the trading allowance rises to £3,000, so smaller side incomes will fall under it.

Can I do this alongside a full time job?

Yes, and most people should start that way. Two clients at four to six hours each is an evenings and weekends workload. Check your employment contract for any limit on outside work, and remember your salary already uses your personal allowance, so side profit is taxed from the first pound above any allowance.

The honest verdict

A first £1,000 from AI in the UK is realistic. It is not fast, it is not passive, and it will not come from selling AI generated text.

It comes from picking one repeated problem in one real business, solving it properly, writing it down, and charging £400 to £800 for the outcome. Do that twice and the £1,000 is done.

Do it five times and you have a rate, referrals and something that compounds. The skill that pays is not prompting. It is understanding a business well enough to know what is worth automating, and being accountable for the result.
