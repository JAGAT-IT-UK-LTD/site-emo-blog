Web Hosting UK for Beginners: Real Prices After Year One

Published 22 August 2026 · Updated 20 November 2026 · Figures checked November 2026 · 10 min read

Web hosting in the UK for a beginner costs about £1 to £3 a month at first, then usually £6 to £14 a month on renewal for an entry shared plan. The renewal price is the real price, so compare hosts on that. Shared hosting is enough for almost every first site.

This matters because the headline deal is built to hide the second bill. Read the renewal line before you hand over a card.

The short version
  • The advertised price is an introductory rate. Hostinger's Single plan starts at £1.49 a month and renews at £7.99. IONOS Plus starts at £1 and renews at £9 plus VAT.
  • Long first terms lock in that gap. A 48-month deal is paid up front, so it only pays if you keep the site that long.
  • Shared hosting is fine for a new site. You do not need a VPS, and you rarely need managed WordPress.
  • Register your domain somewhere separate from your host. It makes leaving possible.
  • Free SSL, regular backups and a 30 day refund window are baseline. Paying extra for any of them is a signal to look elsewhere.

Why does web hosting cost more after year one?

Almost every mainstream web hosting company in the UK runs the same model. A very low headline price for the first term, then a much higher one on renewal.

The renewal is automatic, on a card you already gave them.

The gap is not small. When checked in November 2026, Hostinger's UK prices showed the Single plan at £1.49 a month, renewing at £7.99. That is more than five times the starting rate.

IONOS UK showed its Plus plan at £1 a month for 12 months, then £9 a month. Both IONOS figures exclude VAT at 20%.

Nothing about the service changes. You are simply now on the real price.

Three things follow from this:

  1. Find the renewal price before you buy. It is usually in the terms, the FAQ, or a footnote under the pricing table. If you cannot find it in two minutes, that is deliberate.
  2. Multiply the renewal price by twelve and compare hosts on that number, not on the introductory one. The ranking often changes completely.
  3. Set a calendar reminder for a month before renewal. That is when you either negotiate, downgrade, or move.

Check whether a price includes VAT. Some UK hosts quote without it, which makes them look cheaper than rivals that include it.

The long first term is the same trick in another shape. A 36 or 48 month deal locks the low rate for longer.

That is good value if you will still want the site in four years. Many first sites do not last that long.

Paying four years up front for a project you abandon in eight months is a common way to lose money on hosting.

Nine things to check before you buy

  1. The renewal price. Written down, in the terms, not in the marketing.
  2. Refund window. Thirty days is common. Anything shorter is a warning.
  3. Free SSL certificate, automatically renewing. Charging for this in 2026 is indefensible.
  4. Backups included, how often, and how far back they go. Weekly is not daily. Also check whether restoring from backup costs extra.
  5. Where the servers are. UK or EU servers help latency for UK visitors, and may matter for how you handle personal data.
  6. Bandwidth and storage limits, and what happens when you exceed them. "Unlimited" always has a fair use clause.
  7. PHP version and how you change it. Old PHP is a security problem and a common cause of plugin breakage.
  8. Staging environment. Being able to test a change before it hits the live site saves you eventually.
  9. How you leave. Can you export a full backup yourself, without asking support? If not, you are a hostage.

Why should the domain go somewhere else?

Hosts offer a free domain with a hosting plan and it feels like a gift. It is a retention mechanism.

If your host also controls your domain, a dispute, an outage or a price rise is much harder to escape. Moving needs their cooperation on both the site and the name.

A separate registrar costs a few pounds a year. It lets you point the name anywhere, at once, without asking anyone.

Two related habits. Turn on auto renew for the domain, because a lapsed domain can be picked up by someone else.

And keep the registrar contact email on an address that is not on the domain itself. If the domain expires and your email was on it, you cannot receive the recovery message.

Is free web hosting any good?

Free hosting is a reasonable choice in exactly two situations: a static site, or a temporary experiment.

Static sites, such as a blog built with a static site generator, a portfolio or documentation, can be hosted free on a custom domain with SSL.

That infrastructure is often better than budget shared hosting. If your site does not need a database, this is the correct answer and it stays free.

Free WordPress style hosting is a different proposition. It typically comes with ads you do not control and a subdomain rather than your own name.

Expect limits on plugins too, and no simple export. Fine for learning. Not fine for anything you want to keep.

How do you move host without breaking anything?

Migration sounds frightening and mostly is not, provided you do it in this order:

  1. Take your own full backup first, files and database, and download it. Do not rely on either host's copy.
  2. Set up the site at the new host and test it there before changing anything public. Most hosts provide a temporary URL or a hosts file method for this.
  3. Lower the DNS TTL a day in advance so the switch propagates quickly.
  4. Point the domain at the new host. Keep the old account running for at least a week.
  5. Check the SSL certificate reissued, forms send, and email still works. Email is the thing people forget, particularly if it was bundled with the old host.
  6. Only then cancel, and ask about a pro rata refund. Some hosts give one.

Many hosts will migrate a site for you free as part of signing up. Take that offer, but still take your own backup first.

Frequently asked

What is the cheapest web hosting in the UK? On first term price, entry shared plans start around £1 to £1.50 a month. In November 2026, IONOS Plus was £1 a month plus VAT for a year and Hostinger Single was £1.49 over 48 months. The cheapest over several years depends on renewal, so compare the renewal figure.

Is cheap web hosting bad? No. Cheap shared hosting is fine for a site with modest traffic. What is bad is cheap hosting with no backups, no SSL, an unfindable renewal price and a support queue measured in days. Check those four things and a low price is simply a low price, not a warning sign.

Do I need a UK host for a UK website? Not strictly. Server location has some effect on speed and can matter for how you handle personal data. A good host with a content delivery network abroad will usually beat a poor host down the road. UK or EU servers are a sensible default for a UK audience, nothing more.

How much traffic can shared hosting take? More than a new site will produce. Most sites that seem to outgrow shared hosting have really been slowed by a badly configured plugin or an uncached page, not by visitor numbers. Fix caching and plugins first. Upgrade to a VPS only when real traffic keeps the server busy.

Should I pay for a four year plan to lock the low price? Only if you are confident about the project. Hostinger's 48 month Single plan costs £71.52 up front, which beats several years of renewals. But that money is gone if you stop in month six, once the 30 day refund window has passed. A one year term suits uncertain projects.

The verdict

Buy shared hosting from a host whose renewal price you have actually read. Choose the term length by how long you expect to keep the site, renewal included.

Register the domain somewhere else, and put a reminder in your calendar a month before it renews. If your site is static, host it free and spend the money on something that matters.

Everything else in hosting marketing is designed to make a simple decision feel complicated.
