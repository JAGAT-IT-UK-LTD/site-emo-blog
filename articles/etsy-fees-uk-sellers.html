Etsy Fees UK 2026: What You Really Pay on Every Sale

Published 22 August 2026 · Updated 22 November 2026 · 10 min read · Figures checked November 2026

Etsy fees for UK sellers add up to about 15% of a typical order once VAT on the fees is counted. That is a 6.5% transaction fee, 4% plus 20p for payment processing, a 0.48% regulatory operating fee and a small listing fee. The fee also lands on your postage, which is why so many makers earn less per hour than they think.

The short version
  • Up to six charges can land on one sale: listing, transaction, payment processing, the regulatory operating fee, VAT on the fees, and sometimes Offsite Ads.
  • On a typical UK order, expect Etsy fees to take about 15% before materials, postage or your time.
  • The 6.5% transaction fee applies to your postage charge too. That is the one that catches almost everybody.
  • The UK Regulatory Operating Fee rose from 0.32% to 0.48% on 22 June 2026.
  • Offsite Ads cost 15%, or 12% once your shop passes $10,000 in a year, and above that line you cannot switch them off.
  • Selling more than £1,000 a year means telling HMRC today. From 6 April 2027 the trading allowance rises to £3,000.

What are the Etsy fees for UK sellers in 2026?

Etsy charges a fee to list, a fee when you sell, and a fee to process the payment. On top sit a small regulatory fee and VAT.

Here is every line a UK seller can meet, using Etsy Payments.

FeeRateWhat to know
Listing fee$0.20, about £0.15Billed in US dollars and converted. Charged when you list, again every four months if it auto renews, and again each time an item sells. Multi quantity listings renew per sale.
Transaction fee6.5%Charged on the full order total including postage and gift wrap, not just the item price.
Payment processing4% + £0.20 for UK sellersCharged on the total the buyer pays. Rates differ for other countries.

The headline number is 6.5%. The real number is closer to 15%, because the other lines stack on top.

What does a £25 sale really leave you?

Meet Priya, who makes soy candles at her kitchen table in Leicester.

She sells one for £20 with £5 postage. The buyer pays £25.

This example applies VAT to every Etsy fee as a cautious worst case. Your Payment account shows exactly which fees carried VAT.

Now the uncomfortable bit. If that candle took Priya an hour to make, photograph, list and pack, she earned £11.18 for the hour.

That is before tax, with no holiday pay and no pension. It is below the National Living Wage of £12.71 an hour for workers aged 21 and over from April 2026.

If the sale came through Offsite Ads at 15%, another £3.75 goes, plus VAT of 75p on that fee. Priya is then left with £6.68 for the hour.

This is not an argument against Etsy. It is an argument for knowing the number before you set your price.

Does Etsy charge fees on postage?

Yes. This is the single most common mistake UK Etsy sellers make.

The 6.5% transaction fee is charged on your postage as well as the item price. Charge £5 postage and you pay 32.5p of fee on the postage alone.

Then add VAT on that fee, the payment processing percentage, and the regulatory fee. Postage is never fee free on Etsy.

Two things follow.

  • Free postage is not free. Folding postage into the item price does not avoid the fee, because the fee applies to whatever the buyer pays. What it changes is how the listing looks in search, and Etsy favours free postage listings, so it can still be worth doing.
  • Overcharging for postage costs you twice. You pay fees on the inflated amount, and buyers compare postage across shops.

Weigh and measure your packed items properly. A Royal Mail large letter costs far less than a small parcel.

The difference is often a few millimetres of packaging. An hour spent here saves money on every order you will ever send.

Can you turn off Etsy Offsite Ads?

Only while your shop is small. Etsy advertises listings on Google, social media and other sites.

If a buyer clicks one of those adverts and buys from you within 30 days, you pay a percentage of that order.

  • Below $10,000 of sales in the last 12 months: taking part is optional and the rate is 15%. You can switch it off in your shop settings.
  • Once you pass $10,000: taking part is mandatory for good, though the rate drops to 12%.
  • The fee is capped at $100 per order, which limits the damage on a large sale.

Whether it is good value depends on whether those buyers would have found you anyway.

For a distinctive product that people search for by name, you may be paying for traffic you already had. For a generic product in a crowded category, it can bring sales you would not have made.

If buyers search for your shop name, that name is worth protecting. Registering a UK trademark costs less than most sellers assume.

Either way, price as though every sale might carry the fee. A shop that only makes money when Offsite Ads does not fire is not a profitable shop.

How should you price to cover Etsy fees?

A method that survives real life:

  1. Materials at true cost, including wastage and the bulk buys you forget to count.
  2. Your time at a real hourly rate. Pick a number you would accept from an employer. Include making, listing, photographing, packing and answering messages.
  3. Add packaging, per order.
  4. Add 25% to cover Etsy's cut. Not 15%, because you need room for Offsite Ads and returns.
  5. Then check the market. If your number is far above similar listings, change the product or the buyer you aim at. Do not accept a wage below the minimum.

Two moves genuinely shift the numbers.

Raise the average order value with bundles and add ons. Most fees are a percentage, but your packing time is not.

Cut making time. It is the largest hidden cost in almost every handmade shop.

Do you pay tax and VAT on Etsy sales?

  • The trading allowance covers your first £1,000 of trading income in the 2026 to 2027 tax year. Above that, register for Self Assessment. It counts sales, not profit.
  • It rises to £3,000 from 6 April 2027, alongside a higher Self Assessment reporting threshold, according to HMRC's policy paper. Small shops under that line will have less paperwork from next tax year.
  • Etsy reports to HMRC. Under the digital platform reporting rules, marketplaces pass seller details and sales totals to HMRC once you pass 30 sales or about £1,700 in a year. Assuming a small shop is invisible is out of date.
  • VAT on Etsy's fees is charged to you as a UK seller. If you are VAT registered you can normally reclaim it.
  • Your own VAT threshold is separate. You must register once your taxable turnover passes £90,000 in any 12 months. Above it you charge VAT on sales, which changes your pricing entirely.

If HMRC has already written to you about online sales, read what to do with an HMRC letter to online sellers. Our wider guide to side hustle tax rules covers expenses and the allowance in more depth.

General information, not tax advice. Once your shop turns over real money, an accountant is cheaper than the mistakes.

Is selling on Etsy still worth it in the UK?

The honest case for Etsy is traffic. Millions of buyers arrive already looking for handmade, vintage or personalised things.

Building that audience yourself takes years. For that, about 15% is not an outrageous price.

The honest case against is control. Fees keep rising, Offsite Ads become compulsory above a threshold, search changes without warning, and the customer belongs to Etsy rather than to you.

The answer most successful UK sellers land on: use Etsy for discovery and build your own channel alongside it.

Put a card in every parcel. Collect email addresses lawfully. A direct channel that costs you 3% instead of 15% changes the economics of the whole business, and it is the only asset here that you own.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Etsy take from a £10 sale in the UK?

On a £10 order with no postage and no Offsite Ads, Etsy takes about £1.74, or 17%. That is 65p transaction fee, 60p processing, 5p regulatory fee and about 15p listing fee, which is £1.45, plus 29p VAT on those. Small orders lose a bigger share, because the 20p processing charge and the listing fee are flat.

Do I pay a listing fee if the item does not sell?

Yes. A listing costs about 15p to publish and the same again every four months if renewal is automatic. A shop with two hundred unsold listings is paying for all of them. Switch off auto renew on slow sellers, or remove items that have not sold in a year.

Is Etsy Ads worth it for a small UK shop?

Sometimes, and it is easy to lose money. Start with a very small daily budget and run it for at least a month. Judge it on sales you can trace to the ads, not on views. If you cannot clearly see it paying for itself, switch it off.

Can I avoid the Etsy payment processing fee?

Not in any meaningful way. Etsy Payments is required for UK shops, so the 4% plus 20p applies to every order. The only lever you have is order size: bigger baskets spread the flat 20p across more money.

What happens if a buyer opens a case?

Etsy can refund the buyer from your funds. Keep proof of postage on everything, because without it you will usually lose a non delivery claim. Tracked postage costs more, so weigh that against the value of the item.

The verdict

Assume Etsy takes about 15% of everything, including your postage, and price from there. Weigh your parcels properly, treat Offsite Ads as a cost that will arrive, and start a direct audience from your first order. Etsy is a good shop window and an expensive landlord. Treating it as both is how sellers stay profitable.
