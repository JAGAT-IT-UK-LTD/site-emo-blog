Capital Gains Tax on Shares and Crypto: The 31 December 2026 Deadline

Ruhul Amin · Published 25 November 2026 · 8 min read · Figures checked November 2026

If you sold shares, crypto or other assets at a profit in the 2025 to 2026 tax year, HMRC's real time service wants the report by 31 December 2026 and the money by 31 January 2027. Most small sellers owe nothing, but you may still have to report.

The short version

  • The tax-free allowance is £3,000 a year for 2026 to 2027. You only pay Capital Gains Tax (CGT) on total gains above it, after losses.
  • The rates are 18% and 24%. A basic rate taxpayer pays 18% on the part of a gain that fits inside the £37,700 basic rate band, and 24% on the rest. Higher and additional rate taxpayers pay 24%.
  • For a gain made in 2025 to 2026, GOV.UK says report by 31 December 2026 and pay by 31 January 2027 if you use the real time CGT service.
  • You may have to report even with no tax to pay. If your gains are under the allowance but you sold assets for more than £50,000 in total, they go on a tax return.
  • Shares in an ISA, Premium Bonds and gilts are outside CGT. The ISA limit is £20,000 a year.
  • Missing a Self Assessment return deadline starts at a £100 penalty, and unpaid tax gets a 5% charge at 30 days.

1. What CGT is charged on

CGT is tax on the profit you make when you dispose of something that has gone up in value. It is the gain that is taxed, not the amount you receive.

GOV.UK gives a simple example. A painting bought for £5,000 and sold for £25,000 produces a gain of £20,000. Selling, giving away, swapping or being paid compensation for an asset all count as a disposal.

GOV.UK lists what you pay CGT on, and what you do not:

Usually chargedNot charged
Shares that are not in an ISA or PEPAnything held in an ISA or PEP
CryptoassetsUK government gilts and Premium Bonds
Property that is not your main homeBetting, lottery or pools winnings
Most personal possessions worth £6,000 or more, apart from your carGifts to a husband, wife, civil partner or charity, usually
Business assetsYour main home, in most cases

Dividends and savings interest are a different tax. Dividends are covered in our dividend tax guide, and interest in our savings interest tax guide. Crypto has its own wrinkles, which our crypto reporting guide and crypto basics go through.

2. The allowance and the two rates

GOV.UK gives these figures for 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027:

Who you areRate on gains above the allowance
Basic rate taxpayer, gain inside the basic rate band18%
Basic rate taxpayer, part of a gain above the band24%
Higher or additional rate taxpayer24%
Trustees and personal representatives24%
Annual tax-free allowance£3,000 (£1,500 for trusts)

The basic rate band for 2026 to 2027 is £37,700. The rest of the band, after your other taxable income, is the room your gain can use at 18%. It is a bigger deal than it sounds, because the gain is added on top of your income to decide which rate applies.

A £50,270 income line is where higher rate starts, as our frozen thresholds guide explains. The Personal Allowance is £12,570.

Losses help. GOV.UK says you take losses off gains made in the same tax year first. If the total is still above the allowance, you can use unused losses from earlier years. You must claim a loss within four years of the end of the tax year you made it, on your tax return.

3. The dates that matter now

This is the part with a clock on it. For assets other than UK residential property, GOV.UK gives two ways to report:

  1. Self Assessment tax return. You report in the tax year after you sold the asset, and HMRC tells you what you owe.
  2. The real time CGT service. It is open for assets sold in 2025 to 2026 or 2026 to 2027. You must be a UK resident, and you attach your calculations.

GOV.UK's deadlines are to report by 31 December in the tax year after the gain, and pay by 31 January. Its own example: for a gain in 2025 to 2026, report by 31 December 2026 and pay by 31 January 2027.

If you already file Self Assessment, you must also put the sale on your return. The return and payment for 2025 to 2026 are both due by 11:59pm on 31 January 2027, and the date to file online if you want the bill taken through your tax code is 30 December 2026. Our guide to what is due on 31 January 2027 covers the rest of that bill.

Below the allowance, reporting is still needed in one case. GOV.UK says that if your total sale proceeds were more than £50,000, you must report on your tax return even if the gain is under the allowance. It is the sale price that counts, not the profit.

The penalties are real. For a late Self Assessment return, GOV.UK lists an initial £100 penalty, then £10 a day after 3 months, capped at £900. Late tax carries 5% of the unpaid amount at 30 days, 6 months and 12 months, plus interest.

4. Worked example: three sellers

Sam in Bristol, Daniel in Reading and Aisha in Leeds

All three sold shares held outside an ISA in the 2026 to 2027 tax year. Every sum below is our own arithmetic from GOV.UK's rates, and it assumes England and no other gains.

Sam earns £30,000 and made a gain of £15,000. After the £12,570 Personal Allowance his taxable income is £17,430. That leaves £20,270 of the £37,700 band.

The taxable gain is £15,000 less £3,000, which is £12,000. It fits inside the band, so the tax is 18% of £12,000: £2,160.

Had Sam's gain been £30,000, the taxable gain would be £27,000. £20,270 is taxed at 18%, which is £3,648.60, and £6,730 at 24%, which is £1,615.20. The bill is £5,263.80.

Daniel earns £60,000, so his basic band is already used up. His gain is £15,000, the taxable part is £12,000, and every pound is at 24%. The tax is £2,880.

Aisha earns £28,000, made a gain of £15,000 on one holding and a loss of £4,000 on another, in the same year. Net gain: £11,000. After the £3,000 allowance, £8,000 is taxable, all at 18%. Her tax is £1,440.

Sam and Daniel made the same profit, and the difference in tax is £720. The rate depends on your income, not only the size of the gain.

If any of them had sold less than £50,000 of assets in total and made less than £3,000 of gain, there would be no tax and nothing to report. If they sold more than £50,000, the sale goes on a return anyway.

5. Legal ways to owe less

  1. Use the allowance every year. The £3,000 does not roll over. Spreading sales across tax years lets you use it more than once.
  2. Use an ISA. Gains on shares in an ISA are not subject to CGT. The limit is £20,000 a year, and our cash ISA changes guide covers what is moving in 2027.
  3. Transfer to a spouse or civil partner. GOV.UK says you usually do not pay tax when you give shares to a husband, wife, civil partner or charity. Each person then has their own allowance and band.
  4. Claim your losses. A loss is only usable if you report it, within four years.
  5. Watch the 30 day matching rule. Under section 106A of the Taxation of Chargeable Gains Act 1992, if you buy the same securities within 30 days after selling, the sale is matched with that purchase first. Selling to book a loss, then buying straight back, does not work the way people expect.

The 30 day rule is why moving shares into an ISA by selling and rebuying needs care. Our other tax guides, such as savings tax from April 2027 and landlord property income tax, cover the neighbouring changes.

6. The honest verdict

If you sold shares or crypto last tax year and your total gain was under £3,000, you very likely owe nothing. Check the £50,000 proceeds rule before you relax.

If the gain was above £3,000, report it by 31 December 2026 through the real time service, or on your return by 31 January 2027. Set the tax aside now. It is 18% or 24% of the gain over the allowance.

For the future, put new investing in an ISA first. This guide is general information, not tax advice, and complicated cases such as share matching across many trades are worth an accountant.

Frequently asked

How much can you make before paying Capital Gains Tax? For 2026 to 2027 the tax-free allowance is £3,000 of total gains. You pay CGT only on gains above that, after taking off allowable losses. Gains inside an ISA do not count towards it at all.

What is the deadline to report Capital Gains Tax on shares? For a gain made in 2025 to 2026, GOV.UK says report through the real time service by 31 December 2026 and pay by 31 January 2027. If you file Self Assessment, the return is also due by 31 January 2027.

Do I pay Capital Gains Tax on crypto? Yes, GOV.UK lists cryptoassets among the things you pay CGT on. The same £3,000 allowance and the 18% and 24% rates apply. Swapping one crypto for another counts as a disposal, because GOV.UK includes swapping an asset.

Do I have to report a gain below the allowance? Usually not, but there is an exception. GOV.UK says that if you sold assets for more than £50,000 in total, you must report on your tax return even when the gain is under the £3,000 allowance.
