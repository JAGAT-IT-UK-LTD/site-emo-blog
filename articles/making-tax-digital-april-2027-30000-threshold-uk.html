Making Tax Digital from April 2027: the £30,000 threshold is turnover, not profit, and about a million people are caught

From 6 April 2027, Making Tax Digital for Income Tax applies to sole traders and landlords whose qualifying income was over £30,000 in the 2025 to 2026 tax year. The test is on income before expenses, not profit, and that is where people get caught. HMRC now expects around 1,077,000 more people to join.

The short version

  • From 6 April 2027, sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £30,000 in the 2025 to 2026 tax year must use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax.
  • HMRC now says around 1,077,000 more people join at this step, up from its earlier estimate of 970,000. That is on top of those with income over £50,000 who joined in April 2026.
  • Qualifying income is turnover, not profit. A landlord with £33,000 of rent and £9,000 of costs is over the threshold, even though the profit is well under it.
  • It is quarterly updates plus digital records, using compatible software. It is not four tax bills a year. The first update for this group is due by 7 August 2027.
  • New from the 28 October 2026 Budget: no penalty points for late submission of the first four quarterly updates in 2027 to 2028.
  • The threshold drops again to £20,000 from 6 April 2028, measured on the 2026 to 2027 tax year, which is running now.

1. Who needs Making Tax Digital from April 2027?

The rule is set out in HMRC guidance on GOV.UK, and it is short.

From 6 April 2027, you must use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax if you are a sole trader or a landlord registered for Self Assessment, and your qualifying income for the 2025 to 2026 tax year was over £30,000.

HMRC's latest figure, in a press release on 5 October 2026, is that around 1,077,000 more sole traders and landlords will have to join from April 2027. HMRC says that estimate comes from its analysis of 2024 to 2025 Self Assessment returns.

Its older policy paper put the number at around 970,000, alongside roughly 780,000 people with business or property income over £50,000 who were brought in from April 2026. The newer figure is the one to go by.

Note what the test is not based on. It is not about whether you use an accountant, whether you have a company, or whether your affairs feel complicated. It is based on a single number.

Partnerships are not in the timetable yet. HMRC says it will set out their timeline at a later date.

2. Is the £30,000 threshold turnover or profit?

Turnover. This is the single most important sentence on this page.

Qualifying income is your gross income from self employment and property before you deduct expenses. It is not your taxable profit. HMRC's own press release says turnover is counted before any tax allowances or expenses are taken off.

People assume the £30,000 test is about what they keep. It is not.

If you are a landlord receiving £33,000 a year in rent, your qualifying income is £33,000. That is true even if mortgage interest, letting fees, insurance and repairs mean your actual profit is a fraction of that.

The same applies if you have more than one source. Run a small trade with £19,000 of turnover and let a flat producing £14,000 of rent, and your qualifying income is the two added together: £33,000.

You are over the threshold, although neither source is anywhere near it on its own.

Profit never enters the test. A landlord with £33,000 of rent is in, whatever the costs.

This is what will surprise the most people in April 2027. Most coverage says "if you earn over £30,000" and leaves it there.

3. What actually changes, and what does not?

Two obligations, essentially.

Digital records. You must keep your business and property records digitally, in compatible software, rather than in a shoebox or a handwritten book.

A spreadsheet can form part of this if it is linked to compatible software. A spreadsheet on its own is not enough.

Quarterly updates. You send HMRC a summary of your income and expenses four times a year through that software. Then you finalise the year with your tax return.

For the standard tax year periods, the update deadlines are these.

6 April to 5 July 20277 August 2027

Each update covers the year so far, not just the latest three months.

What does not change is as important. You do not pay tax four times a year. HMRC says the quarterly updates are short summaries, not extra tax returns.

The payment deadlines you already know, 31 January and 31 July, stay in place for now. So do the tax rates, the allowances and the rules on what counts as an allowable expense. Our guide to the 31 January 2027 payment on account covers those dates.

4. What did the Budget on 28 October change?

Three things in Budget 2026 touch this group.

A softer first year. Taxpayers joining in April 2027 will not get penalty points for late submission of their first four quarterly updates for 2027 to 2028. A late update in the first year will not count against you under the points system.

Some groups pushed back. The Budget says the roll out to several taxpayer groups is deferred until further notice. If you are in an unusual position, check the HMRC guidance rather than assuming the April 2027 date applies.

Payments on account will change later. From April 2029, people who already make payments on account will pay smaller, more regular amounts through the year. The Budget says this follows consultation, so the detail is not final.

The Budget also tripled the trading allowance to £3,000 from 6 April 2027. That changes the tax on small side income, but it does not change the Making Tax Digital test, which runs on gross income before allowances. Our guide to the £3,000 trading allowance has the detail.

Landlords face a separate change from the same day. Property income is taxed at 24%, 44% and 49% from 6 April 2027. See the landlord property income tax rise.

5. What Making Tax Digital does not do

Worth stating plainly, because a lot of software marketing blurs this.

  • It does not change how much tax you owe.
  • It does not change the 31 January payment deadline.
  • It does not count employment income, pensions, dividends or savings interest towards the threshold. The test is on self employment and property income only.
  • It does not mean HMRC sees your bank account. You submit summary figures from software, as you do now with a tax return.
  • It does not require the most expensive package on the market. HMRC publishes a list of compatible software, and free and low cost options appear on it. Our round up of free software for UK small businesses is a starting point.

6. How do I work out my qualifying income?

You do not need software or an accountant for this test. You need your 2025 to 2026 figures.

Take the turnover figure from your self employment pages, before any expenses. Take the total property income figure from your property pages, again before expenses.

Add them together. That total is what the threshold is measured against.

Ignore your employment income, your pension, your dividends and your savings interest for this test. They do not count towards the threshold, even though they may be on the same tax return.

If the total is comfortably over £30,000, assume you are in from April 2027. HMRC also has an online checker on the guidance page that asks about the 2024 to 2025, 2025 to 2026 and 2026 to 2027 tax years.

There are exemptions, including for people who are digitally excluded. You have to apply for an exemption rather than assume it. If you are exempt, you still send a normal Self Assessment return.

7. A worked example: a landlord who thinks he is under the line

Dilip lives in Leicester and lets two flats. He also does occasional consultancy.

His numbers for the 2025 to 2026 tax year look like this.

  • Rent received on flat one: £13,200
  • Rent received on flat two: £11,400
  • Consultancy invoiced: £7,600

Total qualifying income: £13,200 plus £11,400 plus £7,600, which is £32,200.

His costs across the two flats come to about £16,000. That covers mortgage interest under the current rules, letting agent fees, insurance, gas safety checks and a new boiler in one flat. So his profit is in the region of £16,200.

Dilip assumes he is comfortably under £30,000 and not affected. He is wrong.

The test is on the £32,200, not the £16,200. He is mandated from 6 April 2027, and his first quarterly update is due by 7 August 2027.

The practical point is timing. He needs compatible software and a digital record keeping habit in place before 6 April 2027, not in the January after it.

Setting that up with no deadline pressure costs him an afternoon. Setting it up in a panic costs a lot more. The Budget's first year penalty easing helps, but it does not remove the need to keep digital records from day one.

And if Dilip were well under the line, there would be nothing here for him to do. This rule does not affect most people. It affects a specific group who can be found by one sum, which is why doing that sum now is worth five minutes.

8. Which tax year decides it?

For the April 2027 group, the threshold is not measured in the year you are in now. It is measured on the 2025 to 2026 tax year, which ended on 5 April 2026.

So the figure that decides whether you are caught has already happened. Nothing done this winter changes it.

What is still open is the return. HMRC reads the 2025 to 2026 Self Assessment return, due by 31 January 2027. If qualifying income on it is over £30,000, you are in from 6 April 2027. Dates read off HMRC guidance on 22 November 2026.

over £30,0002025 to 20265 April 202631 January 20276 April 2027
over £20,0002026 to 20275 April 202731 January 20286 April 2028

The sum that decides it is simpler than people expect, and harsher. Qualifying income is gross self employment and property income added together, before any expenses.

A sole trader with £26,000 of sales and a £6,000 room let is over the line and in from April 2027. A trader with £40,000 of sales and £15,000 of costs is in as well, despite a profit of £25,000.

If you are under £30,000 but over £20,000 this year, read our guide to the £20,000 threshold from April 2028. The year that decides that one is running now.

The honest part. HMRC says it reviews your return and writes to you once it shows you are over. The same guidance says it is still your responsibility to check and sign up if no letter arrives. A missing letter is not a defence.

You cannot sign up at all unless you are registered for Self Assessment and have filed a return in the last two years. And signing up early is allowed, which gives you time to try the software before it counts.

Frequently asked

Is the £30,000 based on profit or turnover? Turnover, or more precisely qualifying income. That is gross income from self employment and property added together, before expenses and before any tax allowances. A landlord with £33,000 of rent is over the line even if costs bring the profit down to £15,000. This is the point most people get wrong.

Do I have to pay tax quarterly under Making Tax Digital? No. The quarterly updates are short summaries of income and expenses, not tax bills. Your payment deadlines of 31 January and 31 July are unchanged. The Budget did announce that payments on account will become smaller and more regular from April 2029, but that is a separate change.

Is there a penalty if my first update is late? For the April 2027 group, the Budget on 28 October 2026 said there will be no penalty points for late submission of the first four quarterly updates in the 2027 to 2028 tax year. You still have to send them, and the tax return and payment rules still apply as normal.

What if I have two small income sources? They are added together. Two sources of £16,000 each produce qualifying income of £32,000, which is over the threshold. That is true even if one is a trade and the other is rent from a property. Employment income, pensions and savings interest are left out of the sum.

Can I use Excel for Making Tax Digital? Only with bridging software. A spreadsheet can hold your records if it is linked to compatible software that sends the updates to HMRC. A spreadsheet on its own cannot submit anything. HMRC keeps a list of compatible products on GOV.UK, and some free options appear on it.
