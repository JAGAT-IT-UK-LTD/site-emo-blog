Amazon Associates Changes 2026: What the 14 April Rules Changed for UK Affiliates

The Amazon Associates changes of 14 April 2026 are four rule changes, not a rate cut. Purchases must ship and be paid for within 180 days, paid ad traffic earns nothing, and onsite content earns only on the exact ASIN variant. The UK standard rate card still runs from 0% to 6%, so check your own before you believe a headline.

The short version

  • The Amazon Associates changes took effect on 14 April 2026. Amazon UK's changelog lists them, and the Program Policies page shows "Last updated April 14, 2026". We checked both again on 9 November 2026. Nothing newer has been added.
  • Change 1: a 180-day limit. A product must be shipped to, streamed or downloaded by, and paid for by the customer within 180 days of the purchase to earn commission.
  • Change 2: paid ad referrals are disqualified. Purchases by customers who reached Amazon through any paid or boosted advert linking to Amazon no longer qualify, whatever keywords were used. Limited exceptions apply.
  • Change 3: onsite commission applies only to Direct Qualifying Purchases of the same ASIN variant as the linked product page. The so-called halo on related purchases is gone for onsite content.
  • Change 4: original content must contain commentary, analysis or transformation that adds value. A written definition, for the first time.
  • Not in the document: any commission rate change. The UK standard rate card still runs from 0% to 6%, with Home, Books and Kitchen & Dining at 5%. Reported cuts came from publisher conversations, not from Amazon's published terms.

1. What were the Amazon Associates changes in April 2026?

Amazon publishes a comparison page in Associates Central headed "Associates Program Operating Agreement, What's Changed". It lists every update to the Operating Agreement and Program Policies effective 14 April 2026.

We first read the UK version on 3 September 2026. We read it again on 9 November 2026, seven months after the changes took effect. The list is the same, and no later update has been added.

The Program Policies page, which holds the Commission Income Statement, now shows "Last updated April 14, 2026". The main Operating Agreement page still shows 15 October 2025, because the April changes sit in the policies rather than the agreement itself. In full, the changes are these.

SectionChange
Commission Income Statement, Qualifying PurchasesAdded a 180-day time limit: products must be shipped to, streamed or downloaded by, and paid for by the customer to qualify for commission
Commission Income Statement, Qualifying PurchasesExpanded disqualified purchases to include products bought by customers referred through any paid or boosted advertisement linking to Amazon, regardless of whether prohibited keywords are used. Limited exceptions apply
Commission Income Statement, Qualifying PurchasesOnsite commission income now applies only to Direct Qualifying Purchases of the same ASIN variant as the linked product detail page
Participation RequirementsOriginal Content Definition added: original content must contain commentary, analysis or transformation for additional value
Amazon Influencer Program RegistrationCompleting registration gives access to a Storefront and a Unique Creator Link, not only to Influencer Program participants

That is the whole changelog. No rate table, no category percentages, no mention of bonuses. Hold that thought for section 3.

Two older rules did not change, and they still shape most UK earnings. A click on your link opens a 24 hour session. An item added to the basket in that session still counts if the order is completed within 89 days of the first click. 2. What does each change do to your income?

The 180-day limit mostly affects pre-orders and backordered items. A reader who pre-orders a console or a book through your link, with a release date more than six months out, now generates nothing.

Before April, a qualifying purchase counted whenever it shipped. For most UK content sites this is small. For anyone covering pre-orders, it removes a line of income.

Paid ad disqualification is the big one for a certain kind of affiliate. If you run Google, Meta or TikTok adverts that send traffic to Amazon, purchases from those visitors no longer qualify, whatever keywords you used.

The old rule banned specific keywords, such as bidding on Amazon's trade marks. The new one is about the traffic source itself. Paid to Amazon arbitrage, as a business model, is over on Associates. Organic content sites are unaffected.

The onsite ASIN restriction concerns content that appears on Amazon itself through the Influencer and Creator programmes: shoppable videos and photos on product pages.

Previously, onsite content earned on related purchases. Now it earns only when the customer buys the exact ASIN variant the content is attached to. If your video is on the blue kettle and they buy the red one, nothing.

This is what the trade press calls the end of the halo bonus. It does not change how ordinary offsite links from your own website are credited. Our comparison of the Amazon Influencer Programme and Associates covers the onsite side in more depth.

The original content definition gives Amazon a written standard for rejecting or closing accounts whose content is scraped, spun or a bare list of links. The policies now say content built on third party material must contain "significant commentary, analysis, or transformation".

A UK site publishing honest reviews with its own testing or arithmetic passes that test. A page that copies Amazon's product description under a heading does not. The Associates rules UK affiliates break without noticing lists the other ways accounts get closed.

3. Did Amazon cut UK commission rates?

Not in anything Amazon has published. This is where the coverage and the document part ways.

From April 2026 onward, publishers told Adweek and other trade outlets that Amazon had cut their commission rates. Some said categories fell by as much as half, and that milestone bonuses went at the same time. Long standing publishers were said to have kept better terms than newer ones.

None of that appears in the 14 April changelog. Commission rates are not part of the Operating Agreement text. They sit in the Standard Commission Income Statement and, for larger publishers, in individually negotiated terms.

We read the UK Standard Commission Income Statement on 9 November 2026. These are the published rates.

What that means for a UK affiliate is simple. Do not take a rate from a blog post, ours included. Sign in to Associates Central and read your own rate card, because it is the only rate that applies to you.

If it has changed since April, that is your cut. If it has not, the headlines were about someone else. Our Amazon Associates UK commission rates guide goes through the published card category by category.

4. A worked example: Priyanka's kitchen site in Bolton

Priyanka runs a small kitchen equipment review site from Bolton. She earns through ordinary Associates links on her own pages. She also has a modest Influencer storefront with a handful of shoppable videos on Amazon.

Her website links: unchanged. Say her readers buy £3,000 a month of Kitchen & Dining items through her links. At the published 5% rate, that is £150 a month, before and after 14 April.

Every one of the four changes leaves offsite, organic, original content links exactly as they were. Her reviews contain her own testing, so the original content definition is a formality.

Her onsite videos: cut. Her most watched video sits on a particular 4.5 litre air fryer. In March, a viewer who watched it and bought the 6 litre version from the same range earned her a commission. From 14 April, only the exact variant on that product page counts.

Say her onsite videos drive £800 a month of sales, and a fifth of that, £160, was related variants. At 5%, she loses £8 a month, or £96 a year. She would see it as a fall in onsite earnings with no change in views.

The paid ad experiment she was planning: cancelled. She had budgeted £200 to test Meta adverts pointing at her best review page. At 5%, she would have needed £4,000 of qualifying sales just to cover the spend.

Now those sales may not qualify at all. The rule disqualifies customers referred through any paid advertisement linking to Amazon, and a landing page that then links on is the grey area the "limited exceptions" wording does not clarify. She cannot rely on it, so the £200 stays in the bank.

5. What should a UK affiliate do now?

Three actions, all free. First, read your own rate card in Associates Central and note the rates by category. That is your real rate card.

Second, if you run any paid traffic that ends at Amazon, stop, or accept that it may earn nothing. Third, if you have onsite content, check which ASIN each piece is attached to, because that is now the only ASIN it can earn on.

Amazon commission is taxable income, whatever the rate. If your earnings pass the £1,000 trading allowance, our guide on how to declare affiliate income to HMRC walks through it.

The longer view is clear. Every one of the four changes rewards original content on your own site sending organic readers to a specific product. Amazon has written that model into its agreement.

6. The honest verdict

If you write original reviews on your own site and link to Amazon, the 14 April changes did not touch you. Check your own rate card anyway. The reported cuts were real for some publishers, and Amazon does not publish negotiated terms.

If you buy traffic to send to Amazon, that model is finished on Associates. Purchases from paid referrals no longer qualify, whatever keywords you use.

If you make onsite videos, you now earn only on the exact variant you tag. Tag the variant people actually buy. There is nothing to purchase here. The agreement is free to read and we have linked it.

Frequently asked questions about the Amazon Associates changes

What changed in the Amazon Associates agreement in April 2026? Amazon UK's changelog for 14 April 2026 lists five updates. There is a 180-day shipping and payment limit, no commission on paid or boosted advert referrals, onsite commission only on the exact ASIN variant, a new original content definition, and wider Storefront access after registration.

Did Amazon cut commission rates in 2026? Not in its published terms. The UK Standard Commission Income Statement still runs from 0% to 6%, with Home and Books at 5%. Cuts of up to half in some categories were reported by publishers about their own accounts. Sign in to Associates Central to check yours.

What is the Amazon Associates 180 day rule? Since 14 April 2026, a purchase only earns commission if the product is shipped to, streamed or downloaded by, and paid for by the customer within 180 days. It mainly hits pre-orders and backordered items with long release dates. Ordinary orders that ship within days are not affected.

Does the ASIN change affect my website links? No. It applies to onsite commission, meaning content shown on Amazon itself through the Influencer and Creator programmes. Ordinary affiliate links from your own site are credited as before, using the same 24 hour session and 89 day basket rules that applied before April.

Can I still run adverts that link to Amazon? You can, but since 14 April 2026 purchases by customers referred through any paid or boosted advertisement linking to Amazon do not qualify for commission, regardless of keywords. Amazon says limited exceptions apply but does not list them on the changelog, so treat paid traffic as unpaid.
