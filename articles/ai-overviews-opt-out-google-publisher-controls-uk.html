Google Must Let UK Sites Opt Out Of AI Overviews By 3 December. Should A Small Affiliate Site Actually Do It? The Numbers Say Probably Not Yet.

On 3 June 2026 the Competition and Markets Authority imposed its first ever conduct requirement, on Google, giving every UK publisher the right to keep its content out of Google's AI search features without being pushed down the ordinary results. Google has until 3 December 2026 to comply. The temptation for an affiliate site that has watched AI Overviews eat its clicks is to opt out on day one. This piece is about why the same rule also hands you the data to check whether that would help.

1. What the publisher requirement actually gives you

The CMA designated Google as having strategic market status in general search under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, and on 3 June 2026 imposed the publisher conduct requirement, the first conduct requirement under the regime. The CMA's own summary, published on 3 August 2026, describes it under three headings: control, transparency and attribution.

Control. Google must provide publishers, defined broadly as any party that makes content available on the web, with controls to withhold their content from three uses: training its generative AI models inside and outside search; grounding, meaning formulating and fact-checking responses, in its generative AI features inside search such as AI Overviews and AI Mode; and grounding outside search, for example the Gemini assistant. The controls covering grounding inside search must be offered at directory and page level, so you can pull one section of a site rather than the whole thing.

Transparency. Google must publish clear information on what the controls cover and how they work, explain how publisher content is used to train and ground its AI, and provide metrics for search AI features: impressions, clicks and click-through rate aggregated across features such as AI Overviews and AI Mode, plus referral data so publishers can assess the quality of traffic from those features.

Attribution. Google must attribute publisher content clearly in AI features with a clear route to the source, and publish how it selects content for attribution, monitors accuracy and handles complaints.

And the sentence that makes the whole thing usable: Google shall not penalise publishers, for example by downranking them in the rest of Search, for using the controls. The CMA's press release on 3 June called the opt-out a world first and noted it also covers fine-tuning of AI models.

The deadline is 3 December 2026, with three more months for the page-level control. The CMA says Google is already rolling out parts of both.

2. How the controls work, and what opting out does not do

What opting out does not do decides whether it is worth it. It does not remove AI Overviews or AI Mode from the results your readers see. If a query triggers an AI answer, it still does; the difference is whose content writes it and who gets the attribution link. Opt out, and a competitor who did not is quoted instead, above your blue link.

It does not restore the traffic AI features have taken. The CMA's press release frames the control as bargaining power, putting publishers "in a stronger position to negotiate content deals with Google". That is a lever for a national newspaper. A 150-page review site has no negotiation to have.

It does not change your ordinary rankings, and the rule says it must not. That is a protection against harm, not a promise of benefit.

3. The metrics Google must now show you, and why they matter more than the opt-out

Until now the most common complaint from affiliate sites has been unmeasurable: "AI Overviews are taking our clicks". Search Console lumps AI feature impressions and clicks into the general totals, so nobody could say how many visits came from being cited in an AI answer.

The transparency limb changes that. Google must provide impressions, clicks and click-through rate for search AI features, aggregated across AI Overviews and AI Mode, and referral data on the quality of that traffic. For the first time a site owner can put two numbers side by side: clicks from being cited in AI features, and clicks from ordinary results on the same queries.

That is the number that decides the opt-out. If AI citations send you converting traffic, opting out hands the citation to a rival. If they send almost nothing while the answer sits above your link, opting out costs nothing and stops your work being the raw material. Either way the answer is in the metrics, and they arrive under the same rule as the control.

4. A worked example in Bradford

Nasreen in Bradford runs a 150-page site reviewing air fryers, slow cookers and kettles, monetised through Amazon Associates and Awin. Search Console shows 1,800 clicks a day in early 2025 falling to 1,100 by summer 2026, with impressions roughly flat. She is certain AI Overviews are the cause and wants to opt out the day the control appears.

Suppose the new AI feature metrics, when they arrive, show her pages getting 40,000 impressions a month in AI Overviews and AI Mode with 900 clicks, a 2.25% click-through rate, and referral data showing those visitors convert at about half the rate of ordinary organic visitors. On her numbers, at roughly £0.04 of commission per organic visit, those 900 clicks are worth about £18 a month. Opting out loses £18 a month and removes her content from the box that sits above her listings. The box does not go away.

Now suppose instead the metrics show 40,000 impressions and 4,500 clicks, an 11% rate, because her comparison tables are being cited with a direct link. At the full £0.04 a visit that is £180 a month she would be giving to whichever competitor gets cited next. Same site, opposite decision, and only the metrics tell her which world she is in.

The page-level control, due by 3 March 2027, lets her opt out the pages where citation brings nothing and keep the comparison tables in. That is only possible once she has the page-level data.

5. How to decide, page by page

First, do nothing on day one. The control is reversible and Google must not penalise you either way, so there is no first-mover advantage, only a first-mover risk.

Second, when the metrics appear, export a month of AI feature impressions and clicks by page alongside organic clicks, and work out the value of an AI-referred visit against an organic one from your own affiliate data.

Third, sort pages into three groups: cited pages that bring meaningful clicks, keep in; cited pages that bring almost nothing, candidates for the page-level opt-out from March 2027; and pages that never appear in AI features, where the decision is irrelevant.

Fourth, separate the training control from the grounding controls. Withholding content from model training has no traffic cost either way. Withholding from in-search grounding is the one with a click cost, and the one to measure.

Fifth, if attribution is wrong or missing on a cited page, the requirement obliges Google to publish a way to flag it. A correct link is worth more than an opt-out.

6. The honest verdict

By 3 December 2026 you will be able to keep your content out of AI Overviews without being downranked. That is real and it is new. Page-level control follows by 3 March 2027.

Opting out does not remove the AI answer from above your link. It puts someone else's content in it. For a small affiliate site the opt-out is a lever with no negotiation behind it.

The metrics Google must now publish are the valuable part. Measure your AI feature clicks first, then opt out page by page where they are worthless. Do not do it on a hunch on day one.

Frequently asked

When can I opt out of AI Overviews? Google must comply with the publisher conduct requirement by 3 December 2026, with a further three months to deliver page-level controls, so by 3 March 2027. The CMA says Google is rolling out parts of the control earlier.

Will opting out hurt my rankings? The requirement says Google must not penalise publishers for using the controls, for example by downranking them elsewhere in Search. The separate fair ranking requirement, effective 17 December 2026, also forbids taking an AI opt-out into account in ranking.

Does opting out remove AI Overviews from search results? No. It removes your content from being used in them. The AI feature still appears for the query, using other sources.

Is this only for news publishers? No. The CMA defines a publisher as any party that makes content available on the web, so an affiliate site, a blog or a small business site is covered.
