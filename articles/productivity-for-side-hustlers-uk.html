Side Hustle With a Full Time Job: How to Fit It In Without Burning Out

21 July 2026 · Updated 20 November 2026 · 7 min read · Figures checked November 2026

You can run a side hustle with a full time job, but only on about 6 to 10 hours a week. Put those hours into two protected blocks, check your contract first, and tell HMRC once your side income passes £1,000 in a tax year.

Most advice treats this as a time management puzzle. It is really a question of energy, sleep and honest limits. Get those right and the hours compound.

The short version
  • A side hustle with a full time job is not a time management problem. It is a capacity problem, and most advice pretends otherwise.
  • The realistic budget is 6 to 10 hours a week, not 20. Plan for what you can sustain in February, not what you feel capable of in January.
  • Check your employment contract first. Exclusivity clauses are banned in zero hours contracts, but they can still bind you in an ordinary contract.
  • Two protected blocks a week beat seven scattered evenings. Consistency beats intensity, and it survives a bad month.
  • Tax: the trading allowance is £1,000 for 2026 to 27 and rises to £3,000 from 6 April 2027. Above it, register for Self Assessment by 5 October after the tax year ends.
  • If it is costing you sleep, it is costing you your day job and your health. No amount of side income fixes that trade.

1. Can I have a side hustle with a full time job?

Usually yes. But do this check before you spend a penny or an hour.

  • Read your employment contract. Look for exclusivity, conflict of interest, and intellectual property clauses. Some require you to declare outside work. Some prohibit competing work. Some claim ownership of anything you create while employed.
  • The IP clause is the one that catches people. If you are a developer and your contract assigns work created in the course of employment, evening software projects can get complicated. Do not build in your employer's field without checking.
  • Never use work equipment, work email or work time. That is the fastest route to a disciplinary, and it is entirely avoidable.
  • Exclusivity clauses in zero hours contracts have been unenforceable since 2015. GOV.UK says zero hours workers can ignore a clause that stops them working elsewhere. In ordinary contracts such clauses can still be enforced.

Most employers are fine with unrelated outside work. The problem is almost never the side hustle. It is not having checked.

2. How many hours do you really have?

Here is a week. It has 168 hours.

That 46 hours is where family, friends, exercise and rest have to fit. Add a partner, children, or a parent who needs help, and it drops fast.

A realistic, sustainable side hustle budget is 6 to 10 hours a week. You can do 20 for about six weeks. Then something breaks, usually your sleep or your relationships.

When that happens, most people stop entirely. That is why so many side hustles die around week seven.

Plan for eight hours a week and treat anything more as a bonus. Eight hours a week is 416 hours a year. That is a great deal of progress if it actually happens every week.

3. How should you schedule the hours?

Scattered half hours do not work for most creative or building work. The setup cost is real. Two protected blocks beat seven fragments.

The structure that works for most people:

  • One weekday evening block, 2 to 3 hours, same night every week. The same night matters more than which night, because it stops being a decision.
  • One weekend morning block, 3 to 4 hours, early, before the day fills up.
  • Loose admin time, 30 to 60 minutes scattered, for small things: replying, invoicing, listing, posting.

Protect them the way you would protect a shift. Put them in the calendar. Decline anything that clashes. Tell the people you live with, so it is not a surprise every week.

Batch by type, not by project. Do all the photography in one block and all the writing in another. Switching between kinds of work inside a two hour block wastes a large part of it.

Finish mid task. Stop when you know exactly what the next step is, and write it down. Starting is the expensive part. A note saying "next: photograph the four blue items" removes it.

4. Worked example: Marcus in Birmingham

Marcus works 9 to 5 on a £32,000 salary. He has a partner and a four year old. He wanted to build a proofreading side business.

What failed first. He tried an hour every evening after his daughter went to bed. It lasted three weeks.

By 9pm he was too tired to do accurate work, and accuracy is the whole product in proofreading. His mistakes took longer to fix than the work itself.

Seven hours a week at an effective £28 an hour is £196 a week. Over a year that is £10,192, or about £850 a month before tax. It took him five months to get there.

What he keeps after tax. His salary already uses his £12,570 personal allowance. If he claims the £1,000 trading allowance instead of expenses, £9,192 is taxable at 20%.

The change that mattered was moving the hard work to Saturday morning, when he was rested. He kept the weekday evening for one defined task.

5. Why is energy the real limit, not time?

This is the part generic productivity advice gets wrong. You do not have eight interchangeable hours. You have a few good hours and a lot of tired ones.

Match the work to the state you are actually in.

WhenGood forBad for
Weekend morning, restedWriting, learning, anything needing accuracyNothing
Weekday evening, moderateDefined tasks with a clear finishOpen ended creative work, decisions
Late evening, tiredListing, packing, tidying files, light adminAnything a client will read
Lunch breakEmail, quotes, schedulingDeep work, you will not get into it

Protect your sleep. This is not a soft point. Cutting sleep to make time is borrowing from tomorrow at a terrible rate.

The cost lands on your day job, your driving, your mood and the people around you. If the side hustle only fits by sleeping less, it does not fit yet. Make it smaller instead.

The same applies to exercise and to seeing people. A side project that costs you your health or your relationships has a negative return, however much it pays.

6. Which tools are worth using?

The trap is obvious once you name it: setting up tools feels like progress and is not progress. A perfectly configured Notion workspace has earned nobody a single pound.

What is genuinely worth having, all free:

  • One list, in one place. Any app, or paper. What matters is that there is exactly one.
  • Calendar blocks for the two sessions, on repeat.
  • A separate bank account for the side income. Many app banks offer one free, and it makes tax time far easier.
  • A simple time record for the first two months. You find out your real hourly rate rather than the one you imagine.
  • Templates for anything you write more than twice: quotes, replies, listings.

Do not buy a course, a planner, or a productivity subscription before you have earned anything. Those purchases feel like commitment and work as delay.

7. When does HMRC need to know?

The trading allowance lets you earn up to £1,000 a year from self employment tax free in 2026 to 27. Above that, you must register for Self Assessment.

The deadline to register is 5 October after the end of the tax year. For income earned in 2026 to 27, that means 5 October 2027.

The allowance rises to £3,000 from 6 April 2027, confirmed by HMRC. Small hustles below that line will not need a return for that income.

8. What should you do when it stalls?

It will stall. Everyone's does. The question is what you do in that week.

  • Shrink the session, do not skip it. Twenty minutes keeps the habit alive. A skipped week usually becomes a skipped month.
  • Do the easiest task on the list. Momentum matters more than importance when you have stalled.
  • Check whether you are avoiding one specific thing. Usually a stall is one dreaded task, often a difficult message or a price conversation. Name it and do it first.
  • Take one week off deliberately if you are genuinely exhausted. A planned break you return from beats a collapse you do not.

9. When should you stop?

Almost nothing written about side hustles says this, so it is worth saying plainly.

Stopping is a legitimate outcome. After six honest months the money may not be there. You may dread the sessions, or it may squeeze out things that matter more.

Then stopping is a decision, not a failure. You have learned what the work is actually like, and that is worth something.

Signals it is time to stop or change direction:

  • You are consistently sleeping less than seven hours to fit it in
  • Your day job performance has slipped, which risks the income you actually rely on
  • You have been at it six months with no revenue and no clear reason why
  • You dread the blocks rather than look forward to them
  • The people you live with have stopped mentioning it, which is usually not neutral

Changing the hustle is also an option. The skills mostly transfer. Someone who tried three things over two years is often further ahead than someone who forced the first one for two years.

The honest verdict

The realistic version is eight protected hours a week, in two blocks, for at least six months. Choose something you can do accurately when you are moderately tired.

That will not make you rich quickly, whatever most content promises. What it will do is compound. Eight hours every week for a year beats thirty hours a week for two months and then nothing.

The honest warning: a side hustle usually brings in a few hundred pounds a month. That is genuinely useful. It is rarely life changing, and it costs you the free time you have now.

Go in knowing the trade. Protect your sleep, your health and the people around you while you make it. If your mood or health starts slipping, take it seriously and talk to someone.

Frequently asked

Do I have to tell my employer about a side hustle? Read your contract first, because it is the real answer. Some contracts require you to declare outside work, and many do not. If the work is unrelated to your employer's business and done on your own time and equipment, it is usually fine. Ask HR in writing if the wording is unclear.

How many hours a week should a side hustle take? For most people with a full time job, 6 to 10 hours a week is sustainable. You can push to 20 for a few weeks, but sleep and relationships usually give way. Plan for eight hours in two protected blocks and treat anything extra as a bonus, not the target.

Is it better to do a little every day or one big block? For most work, blocks win. Building, writing and client work all have a setup cost, so two longer sessions get more done than seven short ones. Daily habits suit practice based goals, such as learning a language or a skill, where short regular repetition matters more than depth.

How long before I know if it is working? Give it six months of genuine, consistent effort. Less than that is not a fair test. If you pass twelve months with no revenue and no clear explanation, change direction. Keeping a simple time record from the start tells you your real hourly rate and makes this call much easier.

Do I need to register with HMRC straight away? Not straight away. Once your trading income passes the £1,000 trading allowance in a tax year, register for Self Assessment by 5 October after that tax year ends. The allowance rises to £3,000 from 6 April 2027.
