1. What is the cheapest way to get married in the UK?

The cheapest legal wedding is a statutory ceremony at a register office. In England and Wales, that costs about £155 for a couple in 2026.

The sum is simple. Each of you pays £46.50 to give notice, so £93 together. The statutory register office ceremony fee is £62. Both rose on 6 April 2026 under new national fee regulations, from £42 and £56 before.

Councils publish these fees on their own pages, and Walsall's fee list is a clear example. You will usually also want a marriage certificate, which is a small extra charge. Scotland and Northern Ireland run their own systems, so check locally there.

The £62 rate normally covers a small room on set days. A grander room or a Saturday slot costs more, and each council sets that price itself.

Nobody is suggesting you do only that. But knowing the floor helps, because everything above £155 is a choice rather than a requirement. The wedding industry's pricing depends on you not thinking about it that way.

Two practical notes. You give notice in person at your local register office, at least 29 days before the ceremony. So this is not a same week decision.

And a register office ceremony followed by a party somewhere cheap on another day is a perfectly ordinary way to do this. It is often the best value structure there is.

2. How much does a wedding cost in the UK in 2026?

The most quoted figure is £20,604, from the Bridebook 2026 UK Wedding Report. It surveyed about 7,000 couples who had married or were planning to. It excludes the engagement ring and the honeymoon.

The same report has the reassuring numbers too. 25% of couples spend £10,000 or less. The average cost per guest is £278, and the venue alone, before catering, averages £6,040.

Season matters. June is the priciest month in the report at £23,809, against £20,483 for July. The average ceremony has about 74 guests, and 41% of receptions host fewer than 60.

Other surveys land in a similar place, with slightly different methods. Treat any average as a weather report, not a target.

3. Which three decisions set the whole bill?

Ignore the hundred small line items for a moment. Three choices decide most of what you pay.

DecisionWhy it dominatesRough effect
Guest numbersEvery guest means more food, more drink, a bigger venue and more of everythingThe largest single lever, about £278 a head on average
Day and seasonVenues discount hard for days they struggle to fillJune averages £23,809; a winter weekday is usually far cheaper
Venue typeA dedicated wedding venue bundles in a premiumAverage venue hire is £6,040; a village hall or pub room costs a fraction

Guest numbers. Cutting from 100 guests to 40 does not trim the cost, it transforms it. It changes which venues you need and how much catering you buy.

At the report's £278 a head, that cut is worth about £16,680 on its own. A small wedding is not a compromise. It is the single most powerful saving available.

The day and the season. Wednesday and Thursday weddings suit couples who did the maths. Venues discount Tuesdays, Wednesdays and quiet months because they struggle to fill them, and the venue is identical.

The venue type. A village hall, a pub function room, a botanical garden or a restaurant's private room can cost a fraction. It often looks better in photographs because it is not a wedding factory.

Ask about a dry hire, where you bring in your own catering and drink. That is usually where the biggest gap opens up.

The moment a florist, caterer or car hire firm hears the word wedding, the quote rises. Ask for a party or a private event wherever you honestly can.

5. Worked example: Priya and Tom's 40 guest day

A Leicester couple, 40 guests, a Thursday in March

The plan. Priya and Tom give notice at their local register office and book the statutory ceremony room. Legal fees: £46.50 each plus £62, so £155.

The party. They hire a village hall for the afternoon at £400. A local caterer does a hot buffet at about £40 a head, so £1,600.

The rest. Drinks £600, photographer £900, a preloved dress £350, seasonal flowers £250, and cake, music and printing £600.

Total: £4,855. At the national average of £278 a guest, the same 40 people would cost about £11,120. Their choices save roughly £6,265.

Saving for it. £4,855 over twelve months is about £405 a month, or £203 each.

After the day. Priya works part time and earns under the £12,570 Personal Allowance. Tom pays basic rate tax. Priya can transfer £1,260 of her allowance to him through Marriage Allowance, cutting his tax by up to £252 a year.

Your own sums will differ by region. The structure is the point: fixed legal fees, a cheap room, food by the head, and photography protected.

6. Where do the real savings hide?

  • Do not have a full open bar. It is the quiet budget killer. A welcome drink and wine on the tables is plenty. Ask venues about corkage, because a dry hire where you supply the drink is often the largest single saving.
  • Buy the dress preloved or from a sample sale. Gowns worn once sell for a fraction of retail. Rental is perfect if you will never wear it again.
  • Skip the wedding label. Ask for a private event where you honestly can. The same catering quoted for a birthday and a wedding is often not the same number.
  • Choose seasonal flowers. Out of season blooms are imported and priced to match. A florist will tell you what is cheap that month if you ask.
  • Let people help. A friend who bakes, an uncle with a nice car, a cousin handy with a camera for the casual shots. That is not cheap, it is personal, and guests remember it.
  • Buy decorations secondhand and sell them afterwards. There is a large resale market in wedding decor for exactly this reason. Our guide to decluttering and selling covers where things actually sell.
  • Keep the honeymoon modest or later. A short UK trip a few months on spreads the cost. Our budget travel guide has the cheaper routes.

7. Where should you not cut?

Photography is the one thing that lasts after the day ends. Go cheaper than a celebrity photographer, but not the cheapest. Bad photos are the one regret that cannot be fixed.

A newer photographer with a strong portfolio is the sweet spot. Ask to see a full wedding, not a highlights reel, because anyone can produce ten good frames.

Do not go into high interest debt for a single day. A wedding paid for on a credit card you cannot clear can take years to repay. The interest will soon exceed anything you saved elsewhere.

Our guide to cashback credit cards shows why interest dwarfs any reward. That logic applies doubly here.

Do not skip the boring admin. Check whether suppliers have public liability insurance, get everything in writing, and pay deposits by credit card where you can.

Under Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act, if an item costs more than £100 and no more than £30,000, your card issuer is jointly liable if the supplier fails. That covers a deposit paid on the card too, as Citizens Advice explains. Wedding suppliers do sometimes go under, and that protection is free.

8. How do you pay for it without a loan?

Give yourself a year and the maths gets gentle.

£5,000 over twelve months

£5,000 ÷ 12 = about £417 a month between two people, so about £208 each.

That is close to what many people spend on bought coffee and lunches. Our coffee cost breakdown shows how a daily shop coffee adds up over a year.

The method that works: open a separate savings pot, pay in on payday before anything else, and the day funds itself without debt. Money left in a current account "to save later" does not get saved.

An eighteen month engagement drops it to about £278 a month, or £139 each. Long engagements are quietly the most effective budgeting tool in this guide.

A regular saver account can earn a little on that monthly pot. Our regular saver guide shows what the headline rate really pays.

One more free win is an audit of forgotten direct debits. Subscriptions you no longer use can quietly fund a chunk of the day. Our no spend guide walks through that hour.

The honest verdict

The £20,604 average is real, and so is the fact that a quarter of couples spend £10,000 or less. Which number describes your wedding comes down to three choices: how many people, which day, and what kind of venue.

Get those three right and £4,000 to £6,000 buys a lovely day for 40 people. Get them wrong and no saving on stationery will rescue the budget.

Spend properly on photography, refuse to borrow at high interest, and save monthly from a separate pot. The couples who look back happily are not the ones who spent least or most. They are the ones not still paying for it two years later.

Frequently asked

What is the cheapest way to get married in the UK? A statutory register office ceremony. In England and Wales you each pay £46.50 to give notice and then £62 for the ceremony, about £155 in total from April 2026. Allow for a certificate too, and give notice at least 29 days ahead. You can hold a cheap party on another day if you want a celebration as well.

How much should a small wedding cost in the UK? For about 40 guests, £3,255 to £6,455 is realistic with a village hall, a buffet, wine on the tables and a newer photographer. Bridebook's 2026 average of £278 a guest puts the same 40 people at about £11,120, so the venue and catering choices make the difference.

Is a weekday wedding really much cheaper? Often by thousands at the same venue, because venues struggle to fill Tuesdays and Wednesdays and price them to sell. The trade off is guest availability. That matters a lot with a long guest list and very little with a small one, where most people can take a day off.

Do I have to invite people who invited me to theirs? No, and it is the most expensive social obligation in the whole exercise. Every reciprocal invitation is a full guest cost, close to £278 each on the national average. Decide the number first and the list second, rather than the other way round, and explain it is a small day.

Are wedding insurance policies worth it? They can be, especially for a larger spend with many suppliers. Policies typically cover supplier failure and cancellation for set reasons. Read the exclusions before you buy. Remember that Section 75 already protects credit card payments on items over £100, including deposits, so insurance adds most on the parts paid another way.
