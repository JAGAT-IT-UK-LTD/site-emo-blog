1. The legal minimum, so you know the floor

A legal marriage at a register office costs about £56 in 2026: the ceremony fee plus giving notice. That is the true minimum to be legally married in the UK, and fees vary by council so check yours.

Nobody is suggesting you do only that. But it helps to know the floor, because everything above £56 is a choice rather than a requirement, and seeing it that way changes how you spend. The wedding industry's entire pricing model depends on you not thinking about it in those terms.

Two practical notes: you normally need to give notice at your local register office and there is a waiting period before the ceremony, so this is not a same-week decision. And a register office ceremony followed by a party somewhere cheap on a different day is a perfectly ordinary way to do this, and frequently the best value structure available.

2. The three decisions that set the whole bill

Ignore the hundred small line items for a moment. Three choices decide roughly 80% of what you pay.

DecisionWhy it dominatesRough effect
Guest numbersEvery guest means more food, more drink, a bigger venue and more of everythingThe largest single lever
Day and seasonVenues discount hard for days they struggle to fillSaturday summer averages £22,290; a winter weekday can be thousands less
Venue typeA dedicated wedding venue bundles in a premiumA village hall or pub function room costs a fraction

Guest numbers. Cutting from 100 guests to 40 does not cut the cost by a bit, it transforms it, because it changes which venues you need and how much catering you buy. A small wedding is not a compromise, it is the single most powerful saving available, and most couples who have done it say the day was better for it.

The day and the season. Wednesday and Thursday weddings are growing fast among couples who did the maths. Venues discount for Tuesdays, Wednesdays and off-season dates because they struggle to fill them, and the venue is identical.

The venue type. A village hall, a pub function room, a botanical garden, a family garden or a restaurant's private room can cost a fraction and often looks better in photographs precisely because it is not a wedding factory. Ask about a dry hire, where you bring in your own catering and drink, because that is usually where the biggest gap opens up.

The moment a florist, caterer or car hire firm hears the word wedding, the quote rises. Ask for a party or a private event wherever you honestly can.

That is a full, warm, proper wedding day. The same event billed as a package at a Saturday summer venue would clear £15,000, and the difference is not what the guests experience. It is the three decisions in the section above.

4. Where the real savings hide

  • Do not have a full open bar. It is the quiet budget killer. A welcome drink and wine on the tables is plenty, and guests rarely notice. Ask venues about corkage, because a dry hire where you supply the drink is frequently the largest single saving on the list.
  • Buy the dress preloved or from a sample sale. Gowns worn once sell for a fraction of retail, and rental is perfect if you will never wear it again.
  • Skip the wedding label. Ask for a private event where you honestly can. The same catering quoted for a birthday party and a wedding is frequently not the same number.
  • Choose seasonal flowers. Out-of-season blooms are imported and priced accordingly. A florist will tell you what is cheap that month if you ask.
  • Let people help. A friend who bakes, an uncle with a nice car, a cousin handy with a camera for the casual shots. Involving people is not cheap, it is personal, and it is what guests remember.
  • Buy decorations secondhand and sell them afterwards. There is a large resale market in wedding decor for exactly this reason, and our selling guide covers where things actually sell.

5. Where not to cut

Photography is the one thing that lasts after the day ends. Go cheaper than a celebrity photographer, but do not go for the cheapest, because bad photos are the one regret that cannot be fixed. A newer photographer with a strong portfolio and a second shooter is the sweet spot. Ask to see a full wedding, not a highlights reel, because anyone can produce ten good frames.

Do not go into high interest debt for a single day. A wedding paid for on a credit card at 25% interest can take years to clear, and the interest will exceed anything you saved by economising elsewhere. Our cashback card guide explains why interest dwarfs any reward, and that logic applies doubly here.

Do not skip the boring admin. Check whether suppliers have public liability insurance, get everything in writing, and pay deposits by credit card where you can. Under Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act, for anything between £100 and £30,000 your card issuer is jointly liable with the supplier if they fail to deliver. Wedding suppliers do occasionally go under, and that protection is free.

6. Funding it without a loan

Give yourself a year and the maths gets gentle.

£5,000 over twelve months

£5,000 ÷ 12 = about £420 a month between two people, so £210 each.

That is roughly what two people spend on bought coffee and lunches. Our coffee cost breakdown puts a single daily bought coffee at around £1,000 a year per person.

The method that works: set up a separate savings pot, pay in on payday before anything else, and the day funds itself without a penny of debt. Money left in a current account "to save later" does not get saved.

An eighteen month engagement drops it to £140 each a month, which is why long engagements are quietly the most effective budgeting tool in this article.

One more free win: an audit of forgotten direct debits typically finds £39 a month per person that was leaving your account for things you do not use, which is £468 a year each. Our no-spend guide covers that hour, and for a couple it funds roughly a fifth of the day above.

The honest verdict

The £20,604 average is real and so is the fact that a quarter of couples spend under £10,000. Which of those two numbers describes your wedding is decided almost entirely by three choices: how many people, which day, and what kind of venue.

Get those three right and £4,000 to £6,000 buys a genuinely lovely day for 40 people. Get them wrong and no amount of economising on stationery will rescue the budget.

Spend properly on photography, refuse to borrow at high interest, and save for it monthly from a separate pot. The couples who look back happily are not the ones who spent least or most. They are the ones who were not still paying for it two years later.

Frequently asked

Is a weekday wedding really much cheaper? Frequently thousands cheaper at the same venue, because venues struggle to fill Tuesdays and Wednesdays. The trade-off is guest availability, which matters more with a large guest list and barely at all with a small one.

Do I have to invite people who invited me to theirs? No, and this is the single most expensive social obligation in the whole exercise. Every reciprocal invitation is a full guest cost. Decide the number first and the list second, rather than the other way round.

Are wedding insurance policies worth it? They can be, particularly for a larger spend with many suppliers, and they typically cover supplier failure and cancellation for specified reasons. Read what is excluded, and remember Section 75 already protects credit card deposits over £100.

Is it cheaper to marry abroad? Sometimes, for a very small group, and the arithmetic changes fast once you count what guests must spend to attend. Legal requirements vary by country and you may need to register the marriage in the UK afterwards.

What is the single biggest saving available? Cutting the guest list. Nothing else comes close, and it is the one people resist hardest.
