The pensions dashboard connection deadline has passed. Here is what happened, and why you still cannot log in

1. What did the pensions dashboard connection deadline require?

The Pensions Dashboards Regulations 2022, as amended in 2023, set one final connection deadline of 31 October 2026.

By that date, schemes in scope had to be plugged into the dashboards system. That means their data sits behind a central search, ready to answer when someone looks for their pensions.

The Department for Work and Pensions also published staged connection dates. They spread the work by scheme size and type. Those stages were guidance, not separate legal deadlines.

The last stages ran through 2026. Schemes with 195 to 249 members were due by 30 June. Schemes with 155 to 194 members followed by 31 July, and 125 to 154 members by 31 August.

The smallest schemes in scope, with 100 to 124 members, were due by 30 September. The 31 October 2026 date was the hard legal backstop for all of them.

The window to apply to defer that deadline closed before it arrived. The Pensions Regulator says so on its own guidance page.

2. What happened by 31 October 2026?

The short answer is that most of the work was already done months before. The deadline was the finish line for the last schemes, not the start.

On 1 June 2026, Pensions Expert reported figures from the Pensions Dashboards Programme. More than 70 million pension records were connected, which was over 85% of the records in scope.

The same report said nearly 1,500 providers and schemes had joined in the latest round. The State Pension was connected earlier too.

After that, the remaining small schemes worked through the monthly staged dates above. Then 31 October 2026 passed as the final legal date.

As of 11 November 2026, we found no official final count published after the deadline. When the Programme publishes one, we will add it here. Until then, the June figure is the latest confirmed number.

For savers, the key point is simple. The deadline passing changed nothing you can see yet. It finished the plumbing behind a service that is still closed to the public.

3. Which schemes had to connect?

The duty fell on relevant occupational pension schemes with 100 or more relevant members. It also covered personal pension providers subject to FCA rules.

For workplace schemes, size was measured at the scheme year end between 1 April 2023 and 31 March 2024. A scheme that grows past 100 members later gets its own, later deadline.

That is a broad net. If you left a job years ago and kept a workplace pension, it is very likely to be covered.

It is not universal. Schemes with 99 or fewer relevant members are out of scope for now, though they can choose to connect. The State Pension has its own route into the system.

4. What happens to schemes that missed it?

Missing the deadline is a matter between the scheme and its regulator. It is not something savers need to chase.

The Pensions Regulator tells trustees to act promptly to connect every membership as soon as possible. Trustees should also consider reporting a breach of law to the regulator.

Its compliance and enforcement policy lets it issue compliance notices that set out what must be fixed. If trustees still fail to comply, it can issue penalty notices.

Those penalties can reach £5,000 for an individual and £50,000 in other cases, such as a corporate trustee. The FCA oversees personal pension providers under its own rules.

For you, the practical effect is small. A pension in a late scheme may simply not appear when dashboards first open. That is one more reason to trace old pots yourself now.

5. Can you use a pensions dashboard yet?

No. 31 October 2026 was never the day you could look up your pensions. This is where most confusion sits.

Public access starts at a separate milestone called the Dashboards Available Point. The Secretary of State for Work and Pensions sets it, after consulting the Money and Pensions Service, the regulator and the FCA.

The government has promised six months' notice before that point. Public access is expected in the 2027/28 financial year, which runs from 6 April 2027 to 5 April 2028.

As of 11 November 2026, no date had been announced. Because of the notice period, spring 2027 is the earliest it could realistically open. Without an announcement, later in that window looks more likely.

The deadline also did not move your money. No pension was transferred, merged or changed. No charges changed either.

Your data has not become public. A dashboard shows your pensions only to you, after you prove who you are. It is a finding service, not a directory.

And you never had to do anything. The legal duty sat on schemes and providers. Nobody needed to register, opt in or contact a provider because of 31 October 2026.

6. What is the pensions dashboard, and what will you see?

A pensions dashboard is a free online service that searches every connected scheme at once. You search one time, and each scheme checks whether it holds a pension for you.

What comes back is a list of your pensions. Each one shows basic details and an estimate of what it might pay.

It will not be a single pot, a transfer tool or advice. If you later want to bring pots together, read our guide on whether pension consolidation is worth it first.

The biggest value is for people who changed jobs often. The hardest part of pension admin has always been remembering a scheme exists at all.

Expect gaps at the start. Data quality varies, and a pension under an old name or address may not match on the first search. Some schemes will return possible matches to confirm later, rather than instantly.

7. A worked example: one saver this winter

Bernadette is 54 and lives in Carlisle. She has had four employers. She knows about two pensions, has old paperwork for a third, and cannot remember the provider for a two year job in her twenties.

Her latest statements show £41,200 in her current workplace pension and £18,600 in the one before. Her old paperwork for the third scheme, from 2014, shows a value of £6,300. The fourth is a blank.

So she can see about £66,100 across three pots. The fourth might be small, but it is still her money, and she has no way to check it on a dashboard yet.

Here is what the deadline did for her. If those schemes are in scope, they should now be connected. The plumbing is finished, and that is real progress.

Here is what it did not do. She still cannot sit down this winter and find the fourth pension online. The public service is not open, and no opening date has been set.

So her plan is simple. She uses the free Pension Tracing Service to find contact details for the missing scheme. Then she writes to it with her National Insurance number, her dates of employment and her maiden name.

She also updates her address and date of birth with the three schemes she knows. Matching is how a dashboard will find her later. A scheme holding a 1997 address and a maiden name may struggle to match her.

If she does nothing, she loses nothing for good. But the work she does now is the same work that makes a dashboard useful later. It also means she could act on that fourth pot a year or more sooner.

Frequently asked

Has the pensions dashboard connection deadline passed? Yes. The final legal deadline for relevant schemes to connect was 31 October 2026, and it has passed. Most records were connected earlier, with over 85% linked by June 2026. Schemes that missed it must connect as soon as possible and may face action from The Pensions Regulator.

When can I use the pensions dashboard? Not yet. Public access starts at the Dashboards Available Point, which the government expects in the 2027/28 financial year. It has promised six months' notice first. As of 11 November 2026, no date had been announced, so spring 2027 is the earliest realistic opening and later is more likely.

What happens if my pension scheme did not connect on time? That is a matter for the scheme and its regulator. The Pensions Regulator can issue compliance notices and penalties of up to £5,000 for an individual and £50,000 in other cases. Your pension is not affected, but it may not appear on a dashboard at first, so trace it yourself.

Do I need to do anything because of the deadline? No. The legal duty sat with schemes and providers, not savers. You do not need to register or opt in. It is still worth updating your address with every scheme you know and using the free Pension Tracing Service to find any old pots you have lost track of.

Will a dashboard move or combine my pensions? No. A pensions dashboard is a service for finding and viewing your pensions in one place. It does not transfer money, merge pots, change charges or give advice. If you want to combine pensions, you still do that directly with providers, ideally after checking for exit fees or valuable guarantees.
