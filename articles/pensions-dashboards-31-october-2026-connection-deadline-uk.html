Every pension scheme must connect by 31 October. You still will not be able to use a dashboard

Ruhul Amin · Published 19 September 2026 · 5 min read · Figures checked September 2026

The pensions dashboards connection deadline is 31 October 2026, and the window to apply for deferral has closed. But connection is not the same as availability, and the difference is where most of the confusion sits.

Share Facebook X WhatsApp Pinterest LinkedIn

The short version

  • The connection deadline is 31 October 2026. It is a single deadline for relevant occupational schemes, set by the Pensions Dashboards Regulations 2022 as amended in 2023.
  • It covers occupational schemes with 100 or more relevant members, plus personal pension providers subject to the FCA rules.
  • The deferral window has closed. Applications to defer the 31 October 2026 deadline are no longer open.
  • Connection is not availability. The public can only use dashboards from the Dashboards Available Point, a separate date set by the Secretary of State.
  • At least six months' notice will be given before that point, and public availability is currently expected in the 2027/28 financial year.

1. What the 31 October deadline actually requires

The Pensions Dashboards Regulations 2022, as amended by the Pensions Dashboards (Amendment) Regulations 2023, set a single connection deadline of 31 October 2026.

By that date, in-scope pension schemes must be connected to the pensions dashboards digital architecture. In plain terms, their data must be plugged into a central system so that, when someone eventually searches, the system can find their pension and return it.

The Department for Work and Pensions published a staged timetable asking schemes to connect over time, by size and type, to spread the load. That timetable is guidance rather than a legal requirement for each individual stage. The 31 October 2026 date is the hard one.

The window to apply to defer that deadline has now closed.

2. Which schemes it covers

The obligation falls on relevant occupational pension schemes with 100 or more relevant members, and on personal pension providers subject to the FCA's rules.

That is broad, and it is the reason this matters to ordinary savers rather than only to the pensions industry. If you have a workplace pension from a job you left years ago, it is very likely to be in scope.

It is not universal. Very small occupational schemes fall outside the member threshold, and the State Pension is a separate matter handled through its own route.

3. What the deadline does NOT do

This is the section that matters most, because a great deal of coverage gets it wrong.

31 October 2026 is not the day you can look up your pensions. The point at which dashboards become available to the general public is a separate milestone called the Dashboards Available Point. It is set by the Secretary of State for Work and Pensions, in consultation with the Money and Pensions Service, the Pensions Regulator and the Financial Conduct Authority.

The government has committed to giving at least six months' notice before that point. Public availability is currently expected in the 2027/28 financial year.

So the honest position is this. Schemes are being required to get ready by 31 October 2026. Savers should not expect to be using a dashboard on 1 November 2026, and any article implying otherwise is confusing two different dates.

It also does not move your money, change your pension, or create a new pension. Nothing about your existing pots changes because a scheme connects. No transfer happens. No charges change.

And it does not mean your data is suddenly public. A dashboard returns your pension information to you after you have verified your identity. It is a find and view service, not a directory.

There is one more thing the deadline does not do, and it is worth saying because it is the most common worry. It does not require you to do anything. The legal obligation sits on schemes and providers, not on savers. Nobody needs to register, opt in, or contact their pension provider because of 31 October 2026.

4. What a saver will eventually see

When dashboards do go live, the service is designed so that you search once and the system asks every connected scheme whether it holds a pension matching you. What comes back is a list of your pensions with basic information about each, including an estimate of what they might pay.

What it will not be is a single pot, a transfer tool, or advice. It is a finding service. The value is genuinely large for people who have changed jobs several times, because the hardest part of pension admin has always been remembering that a scheme exists at all. We go through what a dashboard will actually show you when it opens.

One more thing worth setting expectations on. Coverage at launch will not be complete on day one in the way people imagine. Schemes connect over a period, data quality varies between schemes, and a pension held under a former name or an old address may not match on the first search. The service is designed to return possible matches for a scheme to confirm, which means some results arrive after a delay rather than instantly.

5. A worked example: what this means for one saver right now

Bernadette is 54 and lives in Carlisle. Over her working life she has had four employers. She is confident about two pensions, has paperwork somewhere for a third, and genuinely cannot remember the name of the provider for the fourth, which was a two year job in her twenties.

Here is what the 31 October 2026 deadline does for her. By that date, if those schemes are in scope, they should be connected. That is the plumbing being finished. It is real progress and it is the necessary condition for everything else.

Here is what it does not do for her. She cannot sit down on 1 November 2026 and find that fourth pension, because the public service is not open yet, and on current expectations will not be until the 2027/28 financial year at the earliest, with at least six months' notice first.

So the practical advice for Bernadette this autumn is unglamorous and it is not "wait for the dashboard". It is to use the free government-backed tracing service to chase the missing scheme now, and to make sure the schemes she does know about hold her current address and correct date of birth, because matching is how a dashboard will find her later. A scheme holding a maiden name and a 1997 address will struggle to match her whenever the service does open.

If she does nothing, she loses nothing permanent. But the work she does now is the same work that makes the dashboard useful to her later, which makes it worth doing anyway.

Frequently asked

Can I use a pensions dashboard on 1 November 2026? No. 31 October 2026 is the deadline for schemes to connect to the system. Public access starts at the separate Dashboards Available Point, which will be announced with at least six months' notice and is currently expected in the 2027/28 financial year.

Does my scheme have to connect? Relevant occupational schemes with 100 or more relevant members, and personal pension providers subject to FCA rules, are in scope. Very small occupational schemes fall outside the member threshold.

Can schemes still apply to delay? No. The window for applications to defer the 31 October 2026 connection deadline has closed.

Will a dashboard move or combine my pensions? No. It is a service for finding and viewing pensions. It does not transfer money, consolidate pots, change charges, or give advice.
