Rent Out Your Car in the UK: What Peer to Peer Car Sharing Really Pays

Renting out your car through Turo can earn money, but our worked example keeps only about £32 to £50 a month once the platform's cut, VAT on its fee, the extra miles and tax are paid. Two of the four platforms most guides name have left the UK, and a car on finance cannot be listed on Turo at all.

The short version

  • Only two of the four names you will see still work in the UK. Turo and Hiyacar are live. Getaround stopped UK operations in 2024 and Karshare has ceased trading.
  • Turo lets UK hosts keep 60%, 70% or 80% of the trip price. The more you keep, the more you pay towards damage: £0, £250 or £1,750 per incident.
  • A car with outstanding finance cannot be listed on Turo at all. You also have to tell your own insurer, and the car must stay insured on the Motor Insurance Database.
  • Over £1,000 a year of income means a Self Assessment return. Turo now reports what hosts earn to HMRC every January.
  • Our worked example keeps about £32 to £50 a month from 72 rental days a year, once the platform, tax and wear are paid (our own arithmetic, shown below).

1. Which platforms can you actually use today?

Peer to peer car sharing means you list your own car in an app, and a stranger books it by the day. The app handles the booking, the payment and some of the cover. You hand over the keys, or the app unlocks the car.

Most UK articles on this list four names. That list is out of date. We checked each one on 15 November 2026.

PlatformUK status, November 2026What we could confirm

So in practice the choice is Turo or Hiyacar. Turo publishes its UK terms in detail, so most of the sums below use Turo. Hiyacar's own pages are thinner, and they disagree with each other.

Its owners page says you can "earn up to £650 per week". Its own blog, from 30 November 2025, says owners can make "around £650 per month". Treat both as marketing, not as a forecast.

2. How much of each booking do you keep?

Turo calls its options earnings plans. You pick one, and you can change it later, though not for trips already booked. Each plan pays you a share of the trip price and sets how much you pay towards damage before Turo reimburses the rest.

Turo UK planYou keepYour damage share per incidentExtras
More peace of mind60% of trip price£0Courtesy car for single car hosts, up to £350; some lost income cover
Balanced70% of trip price£250None
More earnings80% of trip price£1,750None

Two details shrink the headline share. First, Turo charges VAT at 20% on its own fee and shows host earnings after that VAT. On the Balanced plan, Turo's fee is 30% of the trip price, so the VAT on it is 6% of the trip price. On our reading of Turo's page, that leaves you about 64%. Check your own earnings screen.

Second, small damage is on you. Turo treats dents and scratches of 3 inches or less as wear and tear, and fading or rust as deterioration. Neither is reimbursed. Damage cover is capped at the car's value or £70,000, whichever is lower.

Turo says its damage plans are a contract with Turo, "which isn't insurance". Separate third party liability insurance covers every UK trip: up to £20 million for damage to other people's property, and unlimited cover for injury or death.

Money arrives through Stripe. Turo says Stripe takes about three business days to process a payout, then your bank takes three to five more. Trips longer than a week are paid in weekly parts.

3. Will your insurer and finance company allow it?

This is where many owners stop. Turo's UK rules for listing a car are strict:

  • The car must be 12 years old or less, worth no more than £70,000, with fewer than 130,000 miles and no more than nine seats.
  • It must have its own private or commercial insurance at all times, and show as insured on the Motor Insurance Database.
  • You must tell your insurer that the car will be used on Turo.
  • Vehicles with outstanding finance are not eligible.

That last line rules out most newer cars. If your car is on PCP or hire purchase, the finance company owns it until you pay it off, and Turo will not take it. Turo's listing page also says you must get permission from a lessor before listing a leased car.

Then there is your own insurer. Turo's liability page warns that most insurers will not cover hosts for liability while the car is out on a Turo trip. That is why Turo arranges its own. But your insurer still has to know, and some will charge more or refuse. Ask before you list, not after a claim. Our look at what UK car insurance really costs shows how little room most premiums leave.

Hiyacar's blog says it "provides comprehensive insurance during every booking, meaning your own policy isn't affected". We could not open its insurance terms to check the detail, so ask Hiyacar for the policy wording in writing.

4. What do a stranger's miles cost you?

Every rental adds miles you did not drive. Those miles wear the tyres, brakes and clutch, and they knock value off the car when you sell it. The platform does not pay you for that. It comes out of your share.

HMRC gives a useful yardstick. Its approved rate for business mileage in your own car is 55p a mile for the first 10,000 miles, raised from 45p on 5 June 2026, then 25p a mile after that. The 55p covers fuel too. Our guide to the 55p mileage rate explains the change.

In this guide we use the lower 25p as a rough stand in for the wear and lost value each extra mile costs you. That is our own choice of yardstick, not an HMRC figure for car sharing. Your real figure depends on the car.

Also count your time. Each booking means checking the car, photographing it, cleaning it and dealing with messages. That is unpaid work, a bit like the hidden hours in hosting a spare room on Airbnb.

5. Worked example: Sophie in Bristol

Sophie, a nurse who cycles to work

Sophie owns a 2019 hatchback outright, with no finance. It sits outside her flat most weekdays. She earns £30,000 a year, so she is a basic rate taxpayer. Her insurer has agreed to her listing it.

She lists it on Turo's Balanced plan at £40 a day. She gets 6 booked days a month, or 72 a year, and guests drive about 60 miles a day. The price, bookings and miles are our assumptions for this example, not Turo figures.

Per booked day: 70% of £40 is £28.00. Turo's fee is £12.00, and 20% VAT on that is £2.40. So she receives about £25.60 a day.

Over a year: 72 days at £25.60 is £1,843.20. Guests paid £2,880 in trip prices for those days.

Wear: 72 days at 60 miles is 4,320 miles. At our 25p yardstick that is £1,080. Before tax she is left with £763.20.

Tax: she is over the £1,000 trading allowance, so she must file a return. Using the allowance, and counting the full £2,880 as her gross, she pays 20% on £1,880, which is £376. If only her £1,843.20 counts as gross, she pays 20% on £843.20, which is £168.64.

What is left: between £387.20 and £594.56 a year. That is about £32 to £50 a month. Every figure here is our own arithmetic from the inputs shown.

One bad claim on the Balanced plan costs her £250, which is five to eight months of that profit. On the More earnings plan it would be £1,750.

The result moves a lot with the inputs. A car that earns £60 a day and is booked 10 days a month does much better. A car that guests drive 150 miles a day does much worse. Run the sum with your own figures before you list.

6. How is the money taxed?

GOV.UK lists "hiring personal equipment" as income the £1,000 trading allowance can cover. If your gross income from it is £1,000 or less in a tax year, you do not pay tax on it or need to tell HMRC.

Above £1,000, you must register for Self Assessment by 5 October after the tax year ends. Gross means the total before any allowances or expenses come off. If you claim the allowance, you cannot also deduct expenses. So you either take £1,000 off, or you deduct your real costs.

Which part counts as gross, the full trip price or only your share, depends on how Turo's fee is treated. That is a question for an accountant. Sophie is over £1,000 either way, as most regular hosts will be.

HMRC will see the money. Since 1 January 2024, platforms must collect seller details and report earnings to HMRC each January. GOV.UK names renting out "property or transport" as covered. The small seller exemption does not apply to transport rental. Our side hustle tax guide covers the reporting rules in full, and our driveway rental guide shows the same gross versus net trap.

One more point. The new 24% property income rate from April 2027 applies to income from land and buildings. Renting out a car is not property income, so it stays at the normal rates of 20%, 40% and 45%.

7. The honest verdict

For most car owners, renting out your car is not worth it. After the platform's cut, VAT on its fee, the miles and tax, our worked example keeps about £32 to £50 a month. One small claim can wipe out months of that.

It can make sense in a narrow case. The car is owned outright, sits unused for days at a time, lives in a busy city, and your insurer says yes in writing. Choose a plan with a low damage share, and price high enough to cover the miles.

If your car is on finance, the answer on Turo is simply no. If you want a car side hustle that does not put your own car's value at stake, the sums for Amazon Flex or low cost side hustles are worth comparing first.

Frequently asked

Can I rent out my car in the UK? Yes, through a peer to peer platform such as Turo or Hiyacar. On Turo the car must be 12 years old or less, worth up to £70,000, under 130,000 miles and free of outstanding finance. You must keep your own insurance in force and tell your insurer about the Turo use.

How much does Turo take from UK hosts? Turo keeps 40%, 30% or 20% of the trip price, depending on whether you choose the More peace of mind, Balanced or More earnings plan. It also charges 20% VAT on its fee and shows your earnings after that VAT. Plans that pay you more leave you paying more towards damage.

Do I pay tax on money from renting out my car? Only if your gross income from it is over £1,000 in a tax year. The trading allowance covers hiring out personal equipment up to that amount. Above it you must register for Self Assessment by 5 October after the tax year, and platforms now report hosts' earnings to HMRC.

Can I rent out a car that is on PCP or finance? Not on Turo. Its UK eligibility rules say vehicles with outstanding finance are not eligible. For a leased car, Turo says you must get permission from the lessor before listing. Until the finance is paid off, the finance company owns the car, so read your agreement first.
