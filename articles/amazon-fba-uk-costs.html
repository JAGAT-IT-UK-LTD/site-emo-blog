Amazon FBA UK Fees in 2026: Where 30 to 50% of Your Price Goes

Amazon FBA UK fees usually take 30 to 50% of your selling price once referral, fulfilment, storage and advertising are added. Referral fees are mostly 8% to 15%, and the fuel surcharge on fulfilment rose to 2% on 2 November 2026. Get the full stack and the VAT hit right before you buy any stock.

The short version

  • Plan on 30 to 50% of your selling price going to Amazon before you pay for the product. That is our planning range, not an Amazon figure. Anything lower is a bonus.
  • The Professional plan costs £25 a month excluding VAT, which is £30 with VAT. The Individual plan charges 75p per item sold instead.
  • Referral fees are mostly 8% to 15%, with a 25p minimum per item in most categories. Clothing, home, pet, grocery and supplements have lower rates on cheaper items since December 2025 and January 2026.
  • The fuel and logistics surcharge rose from 1.5% to 2% of FBA fulfilment fees on 2 November 2026. It is small per unit, and easy to miss in a spreadsheet built earlier in the year.
  • Storage costs more from October to December. Standard items in most categories pay £0.76 per cubic foot a month from January to September, and £1.51 from October to December. An aged inventory surcharge starts after 241 days.
  • VAT is the real cliff edge. UK sellers register once rolling turnover passes £90,000. Sellers based overseas who sell into the UK must register from the first sale.

What changed since this was first published

  • Fuel surcharge up. Old: 1.5% of fulfilment fees. New: 2% from 2 November 2026. Source: Amazon UK selling fees page.
  • Referral fee minimum corrected. Old: £1. New: 25p per item in most categories, with no minimum in books, music, video, software and video games. Source: Amazon UK selling fees page.
  • Storage fees added. Monthly storage per cubic foot is now shown from Amazon's own table, and the old long-term storage figure we could not source today has been removed.
  • Self Assessment rule updated. The £1,000 trading allowance rises to £3,000 from 6 April 2027. Source: GOV.UK.
  • VAT threshold rechecked at £90,000 on GOV.UK in November 2026. Unchanged.

1. What are the Amazon FBA UK fees, in order?

Amazon does not charge one fee. It charges a sequence of them.

Each one is worked out on something different. That is exactly why new sellers underestimate the total.

CostWhat it is in 2026Charged on
Selling planProfessional £25 a month excluding VAT, or Individual 75p per itemPer month, or per item sold
Referral feeMostly 8% to 15%, 25p minimum in most categoriesTotal sale price
FBA fulfilment feePick, pack and delivery, set by size tier and weight bandPer unit shipped
Fuel and logistics surcharge2% from 2 November 2026, up from 1.5%On top of the fulfilment fee
Monthly storageBy volume in cubic feet, much higher from October to DecemberPer month of storage
Aged inventory surchargeStarts once stock passes 241 daysAged units
Returns and removalsRefund administration fee, plus removal or disposal feesPer event
AdvertisingOptional in theory, close to unavoidable for a new listingPer click

Look at how many are per unit or per month rather than a percentage. That is the trap on cheap products.

The fixed parts do not shrink with your price. So a £9 item can lose a far bigger share of its price than a £40 one.

The refund administration fee is a good example. Amazon keeps the lesser of £5 or 20% of the referral fee when you refund a sale.

The referral fee is the one everyone budgets for. Storage, surcharges and advertising decide whether you made money.

2. Which Amazon seller plan should you pick?

The Individual plan has no monthly fee. You pay 75p per item sold, plus the normal selling fees.

The Professional plan costs £25 a month excluding VAT. With VAT at 20% that is £30 a month.

The break even point is simple. At 75p an item, 34 sales a month cost £25.50, which is already more than the Professional fee.

So if you expect to sell more than about 33 items a month, Professional is cheaper. It also unlocks the tools most FBA sellers need, such as advertising.

3. What changed in 2026?

Three changes matter if your numbers were built before this year.

Referral fees came down on cheaper items. Amazon said its 2026 changes cut fees by an average of 15p per unit sold across Europe. The cuts are category specific, not across the board.

Clothing and accessories fell to 5% up to £15 and 10% from £15 to £20, from 15 December 2025. From 5 January 2026 home products fell from 15% to 8% up to £20.

Pet clothing and food fell to 5% up to £10. Grocery and supplements also fell to 5% up to £10. Most other categories still pay 8% to 15%, so check your own category in Seller Central first.

Low Price FBA got wider. From 5 January 2026 most products priced at or below £20 qualify, with automatic enrolment. Amazon says newly eligible products save an average of 40p per unit.

A surcharge went the other way. A fuel and logistics surcharge of 1.5% was added to UK FBA fulfilment fees in April 2026. On 2 November 2026 it rose to 2%.

Per unit it is pennies. Across thousands of units it is a line in your model that you cannot leave out.

4. How much are Amazon FBA storage fees in the UK?

Storage is charged per cubic foot per month. A cubic foot is a box roughly 30cm on each side.

Storage type (non dangerous goods)January to SeptemberOctober to December
Standard size, most categories£0.76£1.51
Standard size, clothing, footwear, eyewear, bags£0.62£0.82
Oversize£0.55£0.87

Notice the jump for most standard items. The October to December rate is almost double the rest of the year.

Two more charges sit on top. An aged inventory surcharge applies to units stored for more than 241 days.

A storage utilisation surcharge can apply when your stock would take more than 22 weeks to sell at your recent rate. Amazon measures that over the last 13 weeks.

If you buy in bulk to chase a lower unit cost, both clocks start ticking. Slow stock gets more expensive every month it sits.

Worked example: Daniel's £24.99 product, line by line

Daniel sells from home in Leeds on the Professional plan. His product sells for £24.99 and sits in a category with a 15% referral fee.

The fulfilment fee is an assumption of £3.00 for a standard parcel. Your real figure depends on size and weight, so take it from Amazon's rate card or Revenue Calculator.

Revenue: £24.99

Less referral fee at 15%: −£3.75
Less FBA fulfilment fee (assumed): −£3.00
Less 2% fuel and logistics surcharge on £3.00: −£0.06
Less storage, 0.1 cubic feet for two months at £0.76: −£0.15
Less returns and write off allowance, say 3%: −£0.75
Less advertising at a modest 10% of the price: −£2.50

Amazon and selling costs total: £10.21, or about 41% of the price.

That leaves £14.78 before Daniel pays for the product. His landed cost, including shipping and duty, is £7.50 a unit.

So his profit is £7.28 a unit, about 29% of the price. That looks healthy.

Now add VAT. Once registered, £24.99 including VAT at 20% leaves Daniel only £20.83 of revenue.

Keep the same £10.21 of costs and the £7.50 landed cost. His profit falls to about £3.12 a unit, roughly 12% of the price.

Nothing about the product changed. That drop is the single biggest number in this guide.

5. When does VAT hit an Amazon seller?

This catches more UK sellers than any fee.

If you are based in the UK, you must register once taxable turnover passes £90,000 in the last 12 months. You must also register if you expect to pass it in the next 30 days alone.

It is a rolling 12 months, not a tax year. So it can arrive in the middle of a busy month.

If you and your business are based outside the UK, there is no threshold. GOV.UK says you must register whatever your turnover once you supply goods in the UK.

The effect on margin is brutal if you have not planned for it. On standard rated goods, registration takes a sixth of your price, about 16.7%, from the top.

A business running on a 20% margin can be close to breaking even the day it registers, unless prices move.

You can reclaim VAT on your own costs, including Amazon fees and UK stock. That softens it.

But if you buy from suppliers who are not VAT registered, the offset is smaller than people expect. Many accountants suggest registering early, so the price change happens on your terms. Our guide to the VAT Flat Rate Scheme shows when a simpler scheme helps.

6. How much money do you need to start Amazon FBA?

The honest answer is more than the courses suggest. The reason is simple: you pay for stock long before Amazon pays you.

Your money goes out when you place the order with your supplier. It comes back on Amazon's payout cycle, after the sale and the fees.

On a product that takes 60 days to arrive and 60 days to sell, you can wait four months between paying and being paid.

A first private label product needs enough to cover several things:

  • an opening order of a few hundred units
  • samples and testing
  • barcodes and photography
  • at least three months of advertising
  • a second order placed before the first sells out

In practice that is thousands of pounds, not hundreds. The second order is the part people forget.

The cheaper way in is retail or online arbitrage. You buy discounted stock from UK shops and resell it.

It needs less money but gives far lower margins. It takes much more of your time and leaves no brand at the end. It is a real way to learn the platform, but it is not passive.

7. What kills most new FBA sellers?

  1. Modelling on the referral fee alone. A spreadsheet with only 15% in it shows profit on products that lose money.
  2. Ignoring the cash cycle. Profitable on paper, out of cash in month five, no money for the reorder, and the listing loses rank.
  3. Buying too deep, too early. Bulk pricing is tempting until slow stock passes 241 days and starts paying the aged inventory surcharge.
  4. Forgetting the peak storage rate. Stock that lands in October pays almost double for most standard items.
  5. Choosing a product because it looks easy. Easy to source means easy for everyone else, which means a price war.
  6. Underestimating returns. Some categories have high return rates, and a returned item is not always fit to resell.
  7. Being surprised by VAT. Crossing £90,000 with prices built on pre VAT margins is a self inflicted wound.
  8. Treating advertising as optional. A new listing with no reviews and no ad spend is close to invisible.

Bulky or slow selling products are often the wrong fit for FBA. Storage is charged by volume and by time.

9. Who should not do this?

If the money is borrowed at high interest. The cash cycle is long and the first product often fails. A monthly loan payment does not survive a four month wait to be paid.

If you need income within three months. Sourcing, shipping, listing and ranking take longer than any course video suggests. If you need money soon, look at our side hustles under £100 guide instead.

If you cannot face admin. VAT returns, customs paperwork, product safety and UKCA marking are all your job. Getting them wrong is expensive.

If you rely on a course to pick your product. A product promoted to thousands of students is, by definition, about to be crowded.

10. The honest verdict

Amazon FBA is a real business with real margins. The 2026 referral cuts and wider Low Price FBA genuinely help cheaper products.

It is not passive, it is not fast, and it is not cheap to start properly.

Build your model with every line in section one, not just the referral fee. Plan on 30 to 50% of the price going to Amazon.

Then run the same model as if you were VAT registered. If this works you will be, and that version tells you whether the product is viable.

If it still profits after that, you have a product. If it only profits before VAT and before advertising, you have a hobby that costs you money.

Frequently asked

How much does it cost to sell on Amazon UK? The Professional plan costs £25 a month excluding VAT, or you pay 75p per item on the Individual plan. On top come referral fees of mostly 8% to 15%, FBA fulfilment fees by size and weight, a 2% fuel surcharge on those fees, storage and advertising. Model your exact product in Amazon's calculator.

Is Amazon FBA worth it in the UK? It can be, for small, light products that sell quickly at a healthy price. It is rarely worth it for bulky or slow items, where storage eats the margin. Test your numbers after VAT and advertising. If the product only profits before those, it is not worth starting.

Do I need to register with HMRC to sell on Amazon? If your trading income goes over £1,000 in a tax year, you register for Self Assessment, as our side hustle tax guide explains. That allowance rises to £3,000 from 6 April 2027. VAT is separate and starts at £90,000, or from the first sale for overseas sellers.

Do I need a limited company to sell on Amazon? No. Many sellers start as a sole trader and form a company later. The right choice depends on your other income, your profit and your plans. It is worth an hour with an accountant before you decide, rather than relying on a forum thread or a course.

How long until Amazon FBA makes a profit? For a private label product, expect six to twelve months, and only if the first product works. Stock takes weeks to arrive, ranking takes time and early advertising is costly. Many sellers are on their second or third product before anything meaningful lands in their bank account.
