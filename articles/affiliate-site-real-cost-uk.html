The short version

  • The advertised price is before VAT. Hostinger's UK pages say at the foot of each one that prices are listed without VAT. A UK consumer adds 20%. In the Black Friday sale on 26 November 2026, the £1.79 Premium plan is £2.15 a month in real money.

How much does an affiliate website cost in the UK: what does the advertised price buy?

Take the budget host most often recommended to UK beginners. On 26 November 2026 its UK hosting page shows four shared and cloud plans, all in a Black Friday sale.

Here is what each headline price is attached to, taken from the small print on the same page.

Single, £1.49 a month. 48 month term, £71.52 in one payment. Renews at £7.99 a month. One website.

Premium, £1.79 a month. 48 month term, £85.92 in one payment. Renews at £10.99 a month. Three websites, domain free for one year.

Unlimited, £2.49 a month. 48 month term, £119.52 in one payment. Renews at £13.99 a month.

Cloud Startup, £7.99 a month. 48 month term, £383.52 in one payment. Renews at £20.99 a month.

Every figure above excludes VAT, as the foot of the page says.

When we first wrote this guide on 29 August 2026, outside the sale, the same Premium plan was £2.99 a month, or £143.52 for 48 months. So prices move a lot with the season.

Three things are packed into that one monthly number. It is worth pulling them apart.

VAT. UK consumers pay the 20% standard rate on web hosting. The page is priced as if you can reclaim VAT, which a VAT registered business can.

If you are an individual starting a first site, you cannot. So every figure needs multiplying by 1.2. £1.79 becomes £2.15, and £85.92 becomes £103.10.

The term. The monthly figure is arithmetic, not a billing plan. The page says all plans are paid up front, and the monthly rate is the total divided by the months in the plan.

You are not paying £1.79 a month. You are paying £103.10 once and dividing it by 48 in your head.

The renewal. Premium renews at £10.99 a month before VAT, which is £13.19 with VAT, "for the same term". That is about six times the sale rate.

Renew Premium for the same 48 month term and the bill in month 49 is £633.02 with VAT, more than six times what you paid on day one.

The promotion is not a discount on an ongoing price. It is a different price that lasts four years and then stops.

If you are sure you will still be writing in 2030, the sale is a fair trade. If this is your first site and you have not published anything yet, it is a bet on yourself with four years of money.

How much does a domain cost after year one?

The free domain is real, and it is free for one year. After that you own an annual bill for as long as the site exists, even if you stop paying for hosting.

.co.uk: £0.01 for the first year, then £10.99 a year to renew. With VAT, £13.19 a year.

.com: £0.01 for the first year, then £15.99 a year to renew, plus a 15p ICANN fee shown on the page. With VAT, £19.19 a year before that fee.

Both figures are from the registrar's own .co.uk and .com pages on 26 November 2026, and both exclude VAT. In August the .com renewal was £16.99.

Two practical notes. The .co.uk page says the best value is on a three year term, so check how long the first year price applies before you pick a term.

And the cheapest Single plan is left out of the free domain offer in the host's own FAQ. The FAQ says Premium, Unlimited and Cloud Startup come with a free domain on orders of a year or more, while the Single plan card lists "Free domain" among its features.

Those two statements sit on the same page and contradict each other. Read your basket total at checkout, not the feature list.

Worked example: Priya in Watford, four years

Priya works in a garden centre in Watford. She wants to build a review site about UK garden tools, and takes the advice she has read everywhere: Premium on the 48 month term, with the free .co.uk.

Day one. £85.92 before VAT for 48 months of hosting, which is £103.10 with VAT, in one payment. Domain £0.01. Total out of her account in week one: £103.11.

Year 2. Hosting already paid. Domain renewal £10.99 before VAT, so £13.19.

Year 3. £13.19. Year 4. £13.19.

Four year total: £142.68. Over 48 months that is £2.97 a month, against an advertised £1.79. Her real monthly cost is about two thirds higher than the advert.

And in month 49, hosting renews at £13.19 a month with VAT. On the same 48 month term that is £633.02 up front. Counted a year at a time, year five is £158.26 of hosting plus £13.19 for the domain, £171.45.

Now the version nobody writes about. Suppose Priya publishes eleven articles, gets bored in month seven, and stops.

She has used roughly £15 of hosting and paid £103.10 for it. The 30 day refund window closed in month two.

That is not a scandal. It is how prepaid terms work. But it is the likely outcome for most first sites, and it belongs in the decision.

How much commission does it take to break even?

This is the sum that turns a hosting bill into a business decision. Amazon UK pays a fixed percentage of the sale price, set by product category.

On Amazon's UK commission table, Home, Kitchen and Dining, Home Improvement and Power and Hand Tools pay 5%. Luxury, clothing, shoes and watches pay 6%. Beauty and Sports pay 4%, and electronics and appliances 2.5%.

Anything not named pays 3%. Garden is not named on the table, so most of Priya's garden kit earns 3%. We use 3% below, and show 5% for anyone writing about home or tools.

Priya's four year cost: £142.68. At 3% she needs £4,756 of tracked qualifying sales over four years to cover it, about £1,189 a year. At 5% it would be £2,854.

One year on the cheapest plan after renewal: hosting £115.06 plus domain £13.19 = £128.25. At 3% that is £4,275 of tracked sales in twelve months.

Per order. On a £40 basket, 3% is £1.20. Covering £128.25 takes 107 orders, about nine every month. The £40 basket is our assumption, not a published figure.

Then there is the payment threshold. Amazon UK does not pay a bank transfer until you are owed at least £25. Payment arrives about 60 days after the end of the month the commission was earned.

At £1.20 a sale, that is 21 qualifying orders before any money reaches you, then a two month wait. A site making six sales a month is holding a balance it cannot withdraw.

That stuck balance is one reason to read how display ad revenue compares with affiliate commission. For how long the first sale usually takes, see when a new affiliate site makes its first sale.

One more rule worth knowing. Amazon's session lasts 24 hours from the click. A purchase counts if the customer buys in that window, or adds the item to their basket in that window and orders within 89 days.

So a click today that turns into a fresh visit and purchase next week pays nothing. Our guide to affiliate cookie windows explains how that compares with other networks.

We have not guessed click or conversion rates, because those depend entirely on your niche. Inventing them would make this page look more precise than it is.

What is the cheapest way to start an affiliate website?

Amazon does not inspect your hosting. Neither does Awin, nor any network we know of. What they check is that the site exists, has real content and discloses the relationship.

Awin's UK page says joining the network as a publisher is free, with no deposit or joining fee to apply. A static site on a free hosting tier meets every test above.

Google's rules on what it will rank are a bigger risk than the server. Read the site reputation policy and what it means for affiliate pages before you spend anything.

The floor. A static site on a free hosting tier, plus a .co.uk. Year one £0.01, years two to four £13.19 each. Four year total: £39.58.

Against the popular route: £142.68. The difference is £103.10 over four years. It buys you WordPress, a control panel and email, not visibility.

Paying for managed hosting is a fair choice for one reason. If you are not comfortable with a build step or a Git push, a free static tier will cost you evenings, and evenings are scarce when you have a job.

That is a genuine trade, and it is worth £100 to plenty of people. What it is not is a requirement.

Many articles that recommend a host earn a commission on the sign up, which is one reason the 48 month plan is the one you keep seeing. Read any hosting recommendation with that in mind.

Do you pay tax on a small affiliate website?

If your affiliate income is £1,000 or less gross in a tax year, the trading allowance usually means you have nothing to tell HMRC. From 6 April 2027 that allowance rises to £3,000.

But the allowance and your real costs are alternatives, not extras. You deduct one or the other.

On £600 of income and £128 of hosting, the allowance wipes out all £600 and is clearly better. In fact the allowance wins whenever your real costs are under £1,000 a year, or under £3,000 from April 2027.

Above £1,000 of gross income in 2026/27 you must register for Self Assessment, even if the allowance then covers part of it. Our guide to declaring affiliate income to HMRC walks through it.

The honest verdict

Starting an affiliate site in the UK really is cheap. It is about £13 a year cheap, not £1.79 a month cheap, and the gap between those two is where hosting sales make their money.

If you have not published anything yet, do not buy 48 months of anything, Black Friday or not. Register a domain, put a static site on a free tier, and write twelve articles.

That costs a penny in year one and about thirteen pounds a year after. If you are still writing in twelve months and the free tier is the bottleneck, buy hosting then, with a year of evidence behind the decision.

And run the break even first. £128 of yearly cost is 107 Amazon orders at a £40 basket. If you cannot picture where those orders come from, the hosting plan is not the problem to solve this week.

Frequently asked

How much does it cost to start an affiliate website in the UK? As little as about £13 a year. A .co.uk domain is £0.01 in year one, then £13.19 a year with VAT, and a static site can run on a free hosting tier. Paid shared hosting on a 48 month sale plan took our four year example to £142.68 including VAT.

Is the free domain actually free? For twelve months, on the plans that include it. From month thirteen you pay the standard renewal, £10.99 a year plus VAT for a .co.uk and £15.99 plus VAT for a .com. Set a reminder for month eleven, because auto renewal on a forgotten card is how people pay a renewal they never chose.

How much does Amazon pay affiliates in the UK? Between 0% and 6% of the sale, by category. Home, kitchen and tools pay 5%, luxury and fashion 6%, and anything not named on the table 3%. Amazon pays by bank transfer once you are owed £25, about 60 days after the end of the month the sale was made.

Can I get a refund if I change my mind? The host in our example offers a 30 day money back guarantee on hosting. Domains are often treated differently, because the registration is passed to the registry. Check the refund policy for both before you pay, not after, and keep a note of the date you bought.

Does cheap hosting hurt my Google rankings? Not directly. Page speed matters and an overloaded server can hurt it, but a well built static site on a free tier is often faster than WordPress on entry level shared hosting. Two decisions matter more: Shopify against WordPress on true cost, and which free keyword tools are worth using.
