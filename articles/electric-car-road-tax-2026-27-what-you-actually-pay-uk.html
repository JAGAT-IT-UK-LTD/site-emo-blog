Electric Car Road Tax in 2026/27: £10, Then £200, Then £640 if the List Price Was Over £50,000

Ruhul Amin · Published 5 September 2026 · 6 min read · Figures checked September 2026

Electric cars stopped being free to tax on 1 April 2025 and the second year of the new regime is now under way, with the standard rate up to £200 and the expensive car threshold for electric cars moved to £50,000. This is the rule as GOV.UK's rate table states it on 5 September 2026, what it means for a car registered in 2019 as much as one registered last month, and the three things it is regularly said to do that it does not.

The short version

  • First year: £10 for a car with 0g/km CO2, frozen at that level until 2029/30. Source: GOV.UK vehicle tax rates, cars registered on or after 1 April 2017, page updated 2 September 2026.
  • Every year after that: £200, the same standard rate as petrol, diesel and hybrid cars. £210 if you pay by monthly Direct Debit, £110 for six months.
  • The expensive car supplement is £440 a year on top, for years two to six, if the list price was over £50,000 for an electric car or over £40,000 for anything else. That is £640 a year, or £2,200 over the five years.
  • What it does NOT do: an electric car registered before 1 April 2025 never pays the supplement, whatever it cost. A car registered between 2001 and March 2017 pays £20 a year, not £200. And nothing here is the 2028 per-mile charge, which is a separate tax on top.
  • The £50,000 line is the electric-only concession, announced at Budget 2025 and now in the live table, and GOV.UK confirms it also covers electric cars registered between 1 April 2025 and 31 March 2026.
  • Our verdict: for a typical £35,000 electric car, tax is now £200 a year, identical to petrol. The list price, not the price you paid, decides whether you are in the £640 band, so check the V5C before you buy a nearly-new one.

1. The number in the table

Vehicle Excise Duty for cars registered on or after 1 April 2017 has two parts. The first tax payment, made when the car is registered, is set by CO2 emissions. For a car with 0g/km, which is every battery electric car, that figure is £10, and the government has said it will stay at £10 until at least 2029/30. From the second year the standard rate applies to everyone, whatever the fuel.

PaymentElectric, petrol, diesel and hybrid
First year, 0g/km£10
Single 12 month payment (year two onwards)£200
12 monthly Direct Debit instalments£210 total
Single 6 month payment£110 (£105 by Direct Debit)
Standard rate plus expensive car supplement, 12 months£640 (£672 by monthly Direct Debit)

Those are the figures on GOV.UK's "Vehicle tax rates" page as read on 5 September 2026, which shows a last update of 2 September 2026. The standard rate was £195 in 2025/26; it moved to £200 for 2026/27, and the Treasury's eVED consultation confirms in a footnote that the £200 figure was set at Budget 2025. The monthly Direct Debit option always costs 5% more than paying annually, on every band, which is the least-known line in the table and worth £10 a year to anyone who can pay up front.

2. The £440 supplement and the two thresholds

The expensive car supplement is an extra £440 a year charged from the second time the car is taxed and lasting five years, so years two to six of the car's life. It is triggered by the list price, which GOV.UK defines as the published price before the car was first registered and before any discount. A dealer discount, a scrappage deal or a lease incentive does not move it. Factory-fitted options ordered with the car generally count too, because they were part of the published price of that car.

There are now two thresholds. Petrol, diesel and hybrid cars cross into the supplement above a £40,000 list price. Electric cars cross above £50,000. The higher electric threshold was announced at Budget 2025, is in the live table, and GOV.UK adds one sentence that matters to recent buyers: "this also applies to electric vehicles registered between 1 April 2025 and 31 March 2026". So an electric car registered in, say, June 2025 with a £46,000 list price is under the £50,000 line and does not pay the supplement, even though the £40,000 line applied on the day it was registered.

Above the line, the sum is £200 plus £440, £640 a year, for five years: £3,200 in standard-plus-supplement over years two to six, of which £2,200 is the supplement itself. On a nearly-new purchase, the years that have already run off count. A two-year-old car that has been taxed twice has three supplement years left, not five.

3. Older electric cars: 2001 to 2017, and 2017 to March 2025

This is where most of the confusion lives, because the change on 1 April 2025 ended the exemption for every electric car, not just new ones, but it did not put them all on the same rate.

An electric car first registered between 1 April 2017 and 31 March 2025 moved onto the £200 standard rate (£195 last year). It does not pay the expensive car supplement, ever, because GOV.UK's exemption applies where the vehicle is zero emission and was registered before 1 April 2025. A 2021 Tesla with a £55,000 list price therefore pays £200 a year, while the same model registered in May 2025 pays £640 a year until its sixth year.

An electric car first registered between 1 March 2001 and 31 March 2017 sits in the older CO2 band system, where zero emission cars fall into band A, "up to 100g/km", at £20 a year. Early Nissan Leafs and Renault Zoes are in this group. They went from £0 to £20 in April 2025, not to £200, and that is the figure GOV.UK's 2001 to 2017 table still shows for 2026/27.

4. What the rule does not do

It does not backdate the supplement. If your electric car was registered before 1 April 2025, its list price is irrelevant and it will never pay the £440. Do not let a garage or a forum tell you otherwise.

It does not charge you for mileage. The pay-per-mile Electric Vehicle Excise Duty announced at Budget 2025 starts in April 2028 at 3p a mile for electric cars and 1.5p for plug-in hybrids, on top of the £200 standard rate, and is administered through the same annual renewal. Nothing about it applies in 2026/27, and the £200 does not rise to make room for it.

It does not change the plug-in hybrid position. A hybrid is an "alternative fuel" car, pays a first-year rate set by its CO2 band (£115 at 1 to 50g/km), then the same £200, and crosses into the supplement at £40,000, not £50,000. The higher threshold is for electric cars only.

And it does not apply to vans, motorcycles or Northern Ireland differently. Rates are UK-wide. Electric vans are taxed on the light goods vehicle rate rather than this table, which is a different figure for a different article.

5. A worked example in Ipswich

Tom in Ipswich is choosing between two used electric cars in September 2026. Car A is a 2023 model, list price when new £48,500, registered March 2023. Car B is a 2025 model, list price when new £52,000, registered September 2025, now one year old and about to be taxed for the second time.

Car A was registered before 1 April 2025, so the supplement can never apply. Its tax is £200 a year, £1,000 over the next five years. Car B was registered after 1 April 2025 with a list price above £50,000, so it pays £640 a year from its second tax payment for five years: £3,200 over the same period, £2,200 more than Car A. That gap is worth checking against the price difference between the two cars before Tom even thinks about range or battery health. The V5C shows the date of first registration, and the dealer, or the manufacturer's archived price list, gives the list price. If Car B's list price had been £49,000, both cars would cost £200 a year.

6. The honest verdict

For most electric cars on the road, road tax in 2026/27 is £200 a year, exactly what a petrol car pays, and £10 in the first year for a new one. The £640 band is for cars registered since April 2025 with a list price over £50,000, and it runs for five years whoever owns the car.

If you are buying nearly new, the two facts that decide your bill are the date of first registration and the original list price, both of which you can check before you pay a deposit. The per-mile charge is a 2028 problem, covered separately, and nothing about it is in this year's table.

Frequently asked

My electric car was registered in 2022. Do I pay the £440 supplement? No. GOV.UK's exemption applies to zero emission vehicles registered before 1 April 2025, regardless of list price. You pay the £200 standard rate.

Is the £50,000 threshold the price I paid? No. It is the manufacturer's published list price before first registration, before any discount, including factory options. A £5,000 dealer discount on a £52,000 car does not take it under the line.

Does an electric car registered in mid-2025 use the £40,000 or £50,000 threshold? £50,000. GOV.UK states that the higher electric threshold also applies to electric vehicles registered between 1 April 2025 and 31 March 2026.

Is this the pay-per-mile charge? No. Electric Vehicle Excise Duty, at 3p a mile, starts in April 2028 and will be charged on top of the £200 standard rate. Nothing per mile applies in 2026/27. What that charge will actually cost a driver is set out in the 3p a mile EVED charge due in April 2028.
