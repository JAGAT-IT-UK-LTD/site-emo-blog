If you searched for the pensions dashboard launch date after 31 October 2026, here is the plain answer. The deadline came and went for pension schemes. The public MoneyHelper Pensions Dashboard did not open with it, and it is not open now.

This guide explains what the deadline really was, what we know about the launch, what you will see when it opens, and the free way to find a lost pension without waiting.

The short version

  • 31 October 2026 has passed. It was the legal connection deadline for schemes and providers in scope, set by the Pensions Dashboards Regulations 2022 as amended in 2023. Source: DWP connection guidance, updated 6 August 2025, checked 11 November 2026.
  • It was not the launch. The public MoneyHelper Pensions Dashboard is still expected in financial year 2027/28. The Secretary of State sets the date, and industry gets six months' notice. As of 11 November 2026 we found no launch date announced.
  • Most records were already connected before the deadline. In June 2026 the Pensions Dashboards Programme reported more than 85% of in scope records, over 70 million, connected.
  • Schemes that missed it face The Pensions Regulator. It can issue compliance notices and penalties of up to £5,000 for an individual trustee and up to £50,000 in other cases.
  • What it will show: your pensions, including the State Pension, in one place, matched on your name, date of birth and National Insurance number.
  • What it will NOT do: show schemes with fewer than 100 relevant members unless they join voluntarily, move or merge pensions, or give advice.
  • You do not have to wait. GOV.UK's Pension Tracing Service is free online, or call 0800 731 0175, Monday to Friday 10am to 3pm.
  • Our verdict: the plumbing is (mostly) finished, the tap is not on. Trace lost pots now.

1. What happened on 31 October 2026?

The connection deadline arrived for pension schemes. It was never a day for savers to log in.

The Department for Work and Pensions' guidance, updated 6 August 2025, sets out the law. The Pensions Dashboards Regulations 2022, as amended in 2023, "introduced a single connection deadline of 31 October 2026 for relevant occupational pension schemes". The Financial Conduct Authority made a matching rule for personal pension providers.

Schemes did not all connect on the day. DWP's staged timetable brought them in by size, largest first, from spring 2025. The Pensions Regulator's own page lists the smallest in scope schemes, with 100 to 124 members, as due by 30 September 2026. It also says the window to apply to defer the 31 October 2026 deadline has closed.

By the summer, most of the work was done. Pensions Expert reported on 1 June 2026 that the Pensions Dashboards Programme had connected more than 70 million records. That was more than 85% of the pension records in scope.

We could not find a final figure for the day itself. As of 11 November 2026, neither GOV.UK nor The Pensions Regulator had published a count of how many schemes made the deadline. We will add it when an official number appears.

2. When is the pensions dashboard launch date?

There is no fixed date yet. The Pensions Dashboards Programme, part of the Money and Pensions Service, says the launch is separate from the connection deadline.

When we read its connection deadline page in September 2026 it said: "This will be decided by the Secretary of State for Work and Pensions." The Minister for Pensions has promised industry six months' notice. The current expectation is that the MoneyHelper Pensions Dashboard opens in financial year 2027/28.

That window runs from 6 April 2027 to 5 April 2028. Because of the six months' notice, the earliest realistic opening is spring 2027. Without an announcement, later in that window is more likely.

So if you see a site offering a "pensions dashboard login" today, be careful. The official service will sit on MoneyHelper and use GOV.UK One Login. No commercial dashboard can connect to the system before the public launch.

3. What happens to schemes that missed the deadline?

That is a matter between the scheme and its regulator. You do not have to do anything.

The Pensions Regulator oversees workplace schemes. Its dashboards compliance and enforcement policy lets it issue compliance notices telling trustees what to fix. If they still do not comply, it can issue penalty notices of up to £5,000 for an individual and up to £50,000 in other cases, such as a corporate trustee.

The policy also lets the regulator use its wider powers, such as information requests. For you, the practical risk is simple. A late scheme may not show up on day one, so keep your own list of pensions.

4. What will you see when it opens?

DWP's guidance says dashboards "will allow individuals to see their pensions information, including their State Pension". The aim is to reunite savers with lost or forgotten pensions too.

You will prove who you are through GOV.UK One Login. The system then sends your name, date of birth and National Insurance number to every connected scheme.

Each scheme checks for a matching record. Matches come back as a list showing the scheme, the administrator's contact details and the values it already gives in annual statements.

The State Pension line uses the same forecast you can check on GOV.UK now. For 2026/27 the full new State Pension is £241.30 a week, and your forecast shows what you are on track for.

5. What will the dashboard not do?

It will not show every pension. The duties cover occupational schemes with 100 or more relevant members. Smaller schemes may connect voluntarily, so a pot in a small employer's old scheme may never appear.

It will not find pots it cannot match. A maiden name, an old address or a mistyped National Insurance number on a 1990s record can leave a real pension invisible.

It will not move, merge or advise. If you are thinking about combining pots, read our guide on whether pension consolidation is worth it first, because some old schemes carry guarantees you can lose.

It will not replace your annual statement or your scheme's own online account. And it will not show a Child Trust Fund, which is not a pension.

6. How can you find a lost pension today, for free?

Use GOV.UK's Pension Tracing Service. It is the job the dashboard will one day automate, and it works now.

Enter the name of an old employer or pension provider. The service returns the scheme's current contact details. GOV.UK is clear on the limits: it "will not tell you whether you have a pension, or what its value is".

Then write to the administrator. Give your full name and any former names, date of birth, National Insurance number, addresses and the dates you worked there.

The phone line is 0800 731 0175, Monday to Friday, 10am to 3pm. Our full guide to the Pension Tracing Service and what paid tracing firms charge explains why you should not hand a firm a share of your pot for the same letters.

Two habits will help when the dashboard opens. Tell every scheme your current address and any name change now, because matching uses the data they hold. And check your State Pension forecast, the one part already live. Our voluntary National Insurance guide shows when filling a gap is worth thousands.

Anyone born between 2002 and 2011 should also check for a Child Trust Fund. It will never appear on the pensions dashboard.

7. A worked example in Bath

Ruth is 52 and lives in Bath. She has had six employers since 1994 and can account for four pensions. She was going to "wait for the dashboard" to find the other two.

The first missing pot is from a retail job, 1996 to 1999. The employer was part of a large group, so its scheme almost certainly had 100 or more members. It should have connected by 31 October 2026.

But Ruth married in 2001. That record will be in her maiden name, so the dashboard match may fail unless she tells the scheme.

The second pot is from a design agency with eleven staff, 2003 to 2005. If it ran its own small trust, it is out of scope. If it used a group personal pension with an insurer, the pot sits with that provider under her old name.

On the timetable above, the earliest Ruth could log in is spring 2027, and no date is set. Instead she uses the Pension Tracing Service one afternoon and finds both contact details. She writes to each with both names, her National Insurance number and her dates.

She also checks her State Pension forecast against the full rate of £241.30 a week, and spots two gap years worth looking at. Waiting a year or more for a screen that might show neither pot was the worse plan.

8. The honest verdict

31 October 2026 was a deadline for pension schemes, not for you. Most records were connected well before it, and the regulator deals with any scheme that missed it.

The pensions dashboard launch date is still not set. The current expectation is the 2027/28 financial year, with six months' notice to industry first.

When it opens it will show only what connected schemes can match to your name, date of birth and National Insurance number. Update your details with every scheme you know, trace the ones you do not through GOV.UK's free service, and treat the dashboard as a future convenience, not a reason to wait.

Frequently asked questions

Is the pensions dashboard live yet?

No. The 31 October 2026 date was the deadline for schemes to connect, not a public launch. The Pensions Dashboards Programme expects the MoneyHelper Pensions Dashboard to open in financial year 2027/28. The Secretary of State will set the date and give industry six months' notice. As of 11 November 2026, no date had been announced.

Will the pensions dashboard show my State Pension?

Yes. DWP's guidance says dashboards will show your pensions information, including your State Pension. You do not need to wait for that part. You can check your State Pension forecast on GOV.UK today, using the same data. In 2026/27 the full new State Pension is £241.30 a week, and your forecast shows your own figure.

Will every pension I have ever had appear?

Not necessarily. Occupational schemes with fewer than 100 relevant members only appear if they connect voluntarily. Any scheme that cannot match your name, date of birth and National Insurance number to its records will not return a result. Old records under a former name or address are the most common reason a real pot stays hidden.

How do I find a lost pension using my National Insurance number?

The Pension Tracing Service searches by employer or provider name, not National Insurance number. Once it gives you the scheme's contact details, write to the administrator with your National Insurance number, full name, any former names, date of birth and dates of employment. The scheme uses those to find your record and send a statement.

Is the Pension Tracing Service free?

Yes. The GOV.UK Pension Tracing Service costs nothing online, and 0800 731 0175 is a freephone number, open Monday to Friday, 10am to 3pm. Some private firms charge a share of any pot they find, but they mostly send the same letters you can write yourself after a free search.
