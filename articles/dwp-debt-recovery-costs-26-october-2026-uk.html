DWP Debt Recovery Costs: What Changed on 26 October 2026

Yes, it happened. Since 26 October 2026, DWP debt recovery can include the costs the department reasonably incurs chasing you, collected the same way as the debt. No price list has been published, so nobody can tell you the amount yet. If you owe the DWP money and have stopped opening the post, this is the change that makes waiting more expensive.

The short version

  • It is now in force. Section 99 of the Public Authorities (Fraud, Error and Recovery) Act 2025 came into force on 26 October 2026, under regulation 2(2) of SI 2026/601.
  • It added section 80G to the Social Security Administration Act 1992. legislation.gov.uk now shows it as inserted from that date.
  • The rule: costs the Secretary of State reasonably incurs in recovering a benefit debt can be recovered as if they were part of that debt.
  • No new collection method. Costs travel by the routes that already exist: benefit deductions, earnings deductions, direct deduction orders and the court route.
  • No tariff. The Act and SI 2026/601 set no amount, and we found no published schedule of costs when we checked on 11 November 2026.
  • Driving bans are separate. That power started on 24 June 2026, needs a court and at least £1,000 outstanding.
  • The cheapest move is unchanged. An affordable arrangement agreed early stops the escalation that creates costs.

1. What happened on 26 October 2026?

Section 99 of the Public Authorities (Fraud, Error and Recovery) Act 2025 came into force, as planned. Nothing delayed it.

The date was set by The Public Authorities (Fraud, Error and Recovery) Act 2025 (Commencement No. 3) Regulations 2026, SI 2026/601. It was made at 2.06pm on 8 June 2026.

We checked legislation.gov.uk on 11 November 2026. The Act page now reads that section 99 is in force at 26 October 2026 by SI 2026/601, regulation 2(2). The instrument itself shows no later amendment.

That instrument does two separate things, and they are often reported as one.

Regulation 2(1). Sections 94 to 97, 100 and 104, with Schedules 5 and 6, came into force on 24 June 2026. That block includes section 95 and Schedule 5, recovery from bank accounts, and section 96 and Schedule 6, disqualification from driving. Schedule 5 is worth reading in full, because how a direct deduction order reaches into a bank account and what it has to leave behind decides what the new power feels like in practice.

Regulation 2(2). Section 99, recovery of costs, came into force on 26 October 2026. It was the only provision starting on that date.

So the powers people link with October arrived in June. What arrived in October is narrower and quieter. It is the cost of the recovery process itself.

2. What does section 80G actually say?

Section 99 does two things. It deletes subsection (3) of section 71ZE of the Social Security Administration Act 1992, which deals with court action. It also inserts a new section 80G, headed recovery of costs.

The enacted wording is short. Costs the Secretary of State reasonably incurs in recovering an amount under sections 71 to 80, Schedule 3ZA or Schedule 3ZB can be recovered as if they were amounts recoverable under that same provision.

The explanatory note to SI 2026/601 puts it the same way. Costs may be recovered as though they were recoverable under the same methods as the debt itself.

Unpick that and it means four things.

  • Costs attach to the debt. Money the department spends pursuing you can be added to what you owe.
  • They travel by the same routes. Whatever method is used for the debt can carry the costs too.
  • It covers the new powers as well as the old. The reference to Schedules 3ZA and 3ZB is deliberate. Costs of a direct deduction order, or of going to court, are inside scope.
  • There is one limit in the text. The costs must be reasonably incurred. That word is the only test written into the section.

3. Which DWP debt recovery powers can carry costs?

Section 80G does not list methods. It points back to the provision the debt is being recovered under. In practice that means the routes below.

Recovery routeWhere it comes fromCan costs ride along?
Deductions from ongoing benefit, such as Universal CreditExisting powers in sections 71 to 80Yes, if reasonably incurred
Deductions from earningsExisting powers in sections 71 to 80Yes, if reasonably incurred
Direct deduction order on a bank accountSchedule 3ZA, in force 24 June 2026Yes, if reasonably incurred
Court route, including driving disqualificationSchedule 3ZB, in force 24 June 2026Yes, if reasonably incurred

If you are still claiming, DWP debt recovery through Universal Credit deductions is usually the first route. The new powers are aimed at people the department cannot reach that way.

4. What does the change not do?

This is the part most coverage skips. On a rule about people in debt, it matters more than the headline.

  1. It does not create a new way of taking money. Section 80G is about what is recoverable, not how. Every collection method that existed on 27 October already existed on 25 October.
  2. It does not set an amount. There is no figure in section 99, none in SI 2026/601, and we found no published tariff. Anyone quoting a number is guessing.
  3. It does not start the driving ban clock. Section 96 and Schedule 6 began on 24 June 2026. A disqualification still needs a court, at least £1,000 outstanding, and a suspended order first.
  4. It does not make silence cheaper. Costs only exist because a process ran. Escalation is what runs it.

5. Worked example: Marcus in Doncaster

Marcus, Doncaster. He stopped claiming Universal Credit in 2024 and now works for himself. He has a DWP overpayment of £1,800 outstanding. He has ignored three letters.

Before 26 October 2026. If the department escalated, whatever that escalation cost stayed with the department. Marcus owed £1,800.

From 26 October 2026. The same escalation can produce costs, reasonably incurred, that are recoverable the same way as the £1,800. His exposure is now £1,800 plus an amount nobody outside the department can yet put a number on.

What changes his position. Not paying £1,800 in one go, which he does not have. Contacting the department and agreeing an affordable amount stops the escalation that generates costs.

If he thinks the debt is wrong. That is a dispute, and a separate track. It does not resolve itself by ignoring post.

6. What should you do now?

For most people this changes nothing. It bites on people with a departmental debt who are no longer claiming and not in PAYE employment. If that is you, four steps are worth taking, in this order.

  • Open the post. Every safeguard in this legislation is triggered by engagement. None is triggered by silence.
  • Ask for an affordable arrangement. Do not agree a figure you cannot keep. A broken arrangement moves a case along, and movement creates cost.
  • Challenge it if it is wrong. Overpayment decisions can be disputed. That is separate from repayment, and you can do both.
  • Get free advice. Citizens Advice, StepChange and National Debtline cost nothing. Do not pay a company to talk to the department for you.

If other debts are piling up too, it is worth knowing how a debt relief order compares with bankruptcy before you promise payments to anyone.

The honest verdict

This was a real change on a real date, and it is smaller than it sounds. Section 99 did not hand the department a new weapon. It changed the accounting. Since 26 October 2026, the reasonable cost of pursuing a benefit debt can be pursued alongside it.

The honest problem is that the size is unknown. No tariff has been published, so nobody outside the department can tell you how much this adds. What is knowable is the direction. Costs grow when a case escalates, and picking up the phone early is the one lever you still hold.

Frequently asked

Can the DWP add recovery costs to my debt? Yes, since 26 October 2026. Section 80G of the Social Security Administration Act 1992 lets the department recover costs it reasonably incurs chasing a benefit debt. They are collected as if they were part of the debt, through the same methods. No fixed charge or tariff has been published.

Does this mean the DWP can take my driving licence? Not because of this change. Driving disqualification sits in section 96 and Schedule 6, which began on 24 June 2026. It needs a court, at least £1,000 still owed, and a suspended order before any immediate one. Section 99 only deals with the cost of recovery, not with new penalties.

How much will the recovery costs be? Nobody has published a figure. There is no amount in the Act and none in SI 2026/601, and we found no published schedule when we checked in November 2026. The only legal test is that costs must be reasonably incurred. Treat any specific number you see quoted as speculation until the department publishes one.

Can costs be added to a debt I am already repaying by agreement? Costs come from a recovery process running. If you keep to an arrangement and no extra action is taken, there should be little to generate them. This is general information, not legal advice. If a new charge appears while you are keeping to a plan, take the letter to free debt advice.

Who is the DWP debt recovery team? DWP Debt Management collects most benefit overpayments. Use the phone number or address printed on your own letter, so you reach the team handling your case. If you cannot find it, Citizens Advice or National Debtline can help you contact the right office at no cost.
