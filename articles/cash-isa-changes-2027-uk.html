The Cash ISA Cut Is Scrapped: £20,000 Stays, and Now It Matters More

Ruhul Amin · Published 3 September 2026 · Updated 15 November 2026 · 10 min read · Figures checked November 2026

The planned cut to £12,000 is off. Budget 2026 kept the cash ISA limit at £20,000 from 6 April 2027 for every saver, whatever their age. And because tax on savings interest outside an ISA rises to 24%, 44% and 49% from that date, a cash ISA is now worth more than it was.

The short version

  • The cash ISA cut to £12,000 is scrapped. Budget 2026 on 28 October kept the cash ISA limit at £20,000 from 6 April 2027, for every saver of every age.
  • Nothing changes on 6 April 2027 for cash ISAs. The total ISA allowance stays at £20,000 and you can still put all of it in cash, as you can today.
  • The over 65 exemption no longer matters, because there is no lower limit for anyone else to be exempt from.
  • The reason a cash ISA now matters more: tax on savings interest outside an ISA rises to 24%, 44% and 49% from 6 April 2027, up from 20%, 40% and 45%. The Personal Savings Allowance stays at £1,000 and £500.
  • The Lifetime ISA charge for buying a first home above £450,000 fell to 20% from midnight on 29 October 2026. Other early withdrawals still cost 25%.

What changed since this was first published

  • 15 November 2026: the cut is off. This page first explained the planned £12,000 cap for under 65s. Budget 2026 reversed it, so the article has been rewritten around the rules that now apply.
  • Savings tax rise added. Budget 2026 set the savings rates for 2027 to 2028 at 24%, 44% and 49%, which is why a cash ISA is now worth more than before.
  • Lifetime ISA change added. The 20% charge for homes above the £450,000 cap, and the new First Time Buyer ISA planned for April 2029.

1. Cash ISA changes 2027: what Budget 2026 decided

The short answer is that the big cash ISA change for 2027 is not happening. The Budget 2026 document says the government "has decided not to reduce the cash ISA limit, so that it remains at £20,000".

That reverses a decision from Budget 2025. Back then, the plan was to cap cash ISA saving at £12,000 a year for under 65s from 6 April 2027. The other £8,000 could only have gone into stocks and shares or other non-cash ISAs.

The old plan, Budget 2025What applies now, Budget 2026
Total ISA allowance from 6 April 2027£20,000£20,000
Most you can put in cash, under 65£12,000£20,000
Most you can put in cash, 65 and over£20,000£20,000
Junior ISA allowance£9,000£9,000
Lifetime ISA allowance, inside the £20,000£4,000£4,000

The Annex A rates table published with the Budget lists the ISA subscription limit at £20,000 for both 2026 to 2027 and 2027 to 2028. The Junior ISA stays at £9,000.

The cash ISA cut was due to start on 6 April 2027. It was cancelled five months before it began, and nothing about your cash ISA changes on that date.

Chancellor John Healey also said the government will work with the industry on a new incentive for people opening a stocks and shares ISA for the first time. No design or date has been published yet, so treat it as a plan, not a product.

2. Who this helps, and who it does not touch

Not affected either way: anyone who saves less than £12,000 a year into a cash ISA. That was always most savers. If you put away a few hundred pounds a month, the cut would never have reached you, and its reversal does not either.

Helped: under 65s who want to put more than £12,000 a year in cash. Typically that is someone with a lump sum from a house sale, an inheritance or a bonus, or a large emergency fund they want kept safe.

No change for over 65s. They were always going to keep the full £20,000, and they still do. The only difference is that everyone else now has the same limit. Savers in that age group are also the most likely to have money they have lost track of, and the free service for tracing an old pension pot is worth an hour.

3. What happened to the transfer rule

The old plan came with a second rule. Under 65s would not have been able to transfer money from a stocks and shares ISA or an Innovative Finance ISA into a cash ISA. It was there to stop people getting round the £12,000 cap.

With the cap gone, that rule has nothing left to protect. The Budget 2026 documents do not set out any transfer restriction, and the ISA reform factsheet that described it is no longer listed in the GOV.UK collection of fiscal event factsheets.

The HMRC guide to ISAs still says you can transfer savings to a different type of ISA or the same type, at any time. That is the rule today. If you are planning a large move from investments into cash, check with your provider that it can be done as a transfer, because withdrawing the money loses the tax wrapper.

Anyone planning that kind of move should first know what they actually hold. The 31 October 2026 dashboard connection deadline is what will eventually put every pension pot on one screen.

4. Why a cash ISA matters more from April 2027

The same Budget made savings held outside an ISA more expensive. From 6 April 2027 the income tax rates on savings interest go up by 4 percentage points in total. Budget 2025 had announced 2 points, and Budget 2026 added 2 more.

Tax on savings interest2026 to 20272027 to 2028
Savings basic rate20%24%
Savings higher rate40%44%
Savings additional rate45%49%
Personal Savings Allowance, basic rate£1,000£1,000
Personal Savings Allowance, higher rate£500£500
Starting rate for savings band£5,000 at 0%£5,000 at 0%

These rates apply across the whole UK, including Scotland. They will be legislated in Finance Bill 2026 to 2027.

Interest inside a cash ISA is tax free, whatever the rate. So every pound of interest you move from outside an ISA to inside one now saves 24p, 44p or 49p, instead of 20p, 40p or 45p.

Most people still pay nothing. The Budget itself says a basic rate taxpayer would need over £25,000 in savings, on top of any ISA, before paying tax. That fits an interest rate of about 4%, in line with the 4% Bank Rate set on 5 November 2026. Our guide to how much interest you can earn before tax bites walks through the allowance in full.

The people who should care are higher rate taxpayers, whose allowance is only £500, and anyone with savings well above £25,000. For them, the full £20,000 cash limit is now the most useful tax shelter they have.

5. Worked example: four different savers

Each example assumes an interest rate of 4% a year, close to Bank Rate. Your own account rate will differ, so scale the sums to it.

Aisha, 34, saves £300 a month. That is £3,600 a year. Neither the cut nor its reversal ever touched her, and her interest sits well inside her £1,000 allowance. She should spend her energy on finding a better rate.

Tom, 41, a higher rate taxpayer, has £40,000 from a flat sale for a purchase in two years. Under the old plan he could have sheltered only £12,000 in cash in 2027 to 2028. With £28,000 outside, earning £1,120, he would pay 44% on £620, which is £272.80 a year.

Under the rules that now apply, he puts £20,000 in a cash ISA before 5 April 2027 and another £20,000 from 6 April 2027. All £40,000 is sheltered within a few weeks, and his tax on the interest from 2027 to 2028 is nothing.

Priya, 52, a basic rate taxpayer, has £60,000 in an ordinary savings account. It earns £2,400. After her £1,000 allowance, £1,400 is taxed: £280 this year at 20%, rising to £336 from April 2027 at 24%.

If she moves £20,000 into a cash ISA this tax year, only £600 of interest is taxed in 2027 to 2028, which is £144. A second £20,000 after 6 April 2027 brings her outside interest down to £800, under her allowance, and her tax to nothing.

Margaret, 67, has £20,000 to place. Nothing changes for her. She could always use the full cash allowance, and she still can.

6. The Lifetime ISA: the 20% charge and what replaces it

Budget 2026 made two changes for people saving for a first home.

The withdrawal charge for homes above £450,000 is now 20%, not 25%. It applies from midnight on 29 October 2026. GOV.UK now says you will pay a 20% withdrawal charge if the property costs more than £450,000, and nothing on homes at or under that price.

The charge for any other early withdrawal stays at 25%. That covers taking money out before 60 for anything other than a first home or terminal illness.

The difference is bigger than it looks. Pay in £4,000, collect the £1,000 bonus, and you hold £5,000. A 25% charge takes £1,250, leaving £3,750, so you lose £250 of your own money. A 20% charge takes £1,000, leaving £4,000. You lose the bonus but get every pound you paid in back.

A new First Time Buyer ISA is planned for April 2029. It will replace the Lifetime ISA for new savers, with up to £4,000 a year, a 25% bonus on homes worth up to £450,000, a bonus capped at £20,000 and no upper age limit. Existing Lifetime ISA holders can carry on under the current rules. A consultation opened on 28 October 2026. Our guide on whether a Lifetime ISA is worth it covers the rest of the trade off.

An important caution

Nothing in this article is advice about what you personally should do. Moving money from cash into investments changes your risk, and money you might need within a few years generally does not belong in the stock market. If the sums are significant, free guidance from MoneyHelper, or a conversation with a regulated adviser, is worth the effort. A wedding is the clearest example of money with a date on it, and what a UK wedding actually costs once the quiet extras are counted decides whether you are saving for two years or five. That horizon, not the ISA rules, is what tells you to stay in cash.

7. What to do before and after 6 April 2027

  1. Check whether any of this affects you. If your savings interest sits inside your Personal Savings Allowance, the tax rise costs you nothing. Go back to comparing rates.
  2. Use this year's allowance if you have spare cash outside an ISA. The 2026 to 2027 allowance ends on 5 April 2027 and cannot be carried forward. A fresh £20,000 starts the next day.
  3. Move the money that earns the most taxable interest first. For a higher rate taxpayer, every £100 of interest moved inside saves £44 from April 2027.
  4. Do not rush into investing because of a headline. The policy nudge towards stocks and shares is gentler now. Money you need soon still belongs in cash, ISA or not.
  5. Keep chasing the rate. Whatever the wrapper, an uncompetitive rate costs most people more each year than any of these rule changes. Our guide to Premium Bonds against a savings account shows how wide that gap can be.

Savings for a child do not use your allowance at all, which is the case for the separate 9,000 pound Junior ISA allowance and whether it is worth using. And savings outside an ISA still count when help is means tested, which is the point of the 16,000 pound capital limit that stops a Housing Benefit claim. If you carry expensive debt, clearing a balance charging 20% or more beats almost any savings rate, as our cashback card guide explains.

8. Four misunderstandings worth clearing up

"The cash ISA limit drops to £12,000 next April." No. That was the plan. Budget 2026 cancelled it, and the limit stays at £20,000.

"Only over 65s keep £20,000." No. Everyone aged 18 or over keeps the full £20,000 cash limit.

"Savings tax rises, so my ISA interest will be taxed." No. Interest inside an ISA stays tax free. The new 24%, 44% and 49% rates apply only to interest outside an ISA above your allowances. Money held outside an ISA is reported in its own way, and the crypto reporting rules HMRC now applies are the sharpest example of that.

"The £20,000 business threshold is the same thing." No. The Making Tax Digital threshold from 2028 and who it catches is about income, not savings. Before the Budget we set out what was expected in what to expect from the Autumn Budget on 28 October 2026, and the cash ISA reversal was the biggest surprise for savers.

9. The honest verdict

For most UK savers, nothing changes. The cash ISA limit stays at £20,000, as it is today, and most people never fill it.

The real change is outside the ISA. From April 2027 interest above your allowance is taxed at 24%, 44% or 49%. If you are a higher rate taxpayer, or you hold well over £25,000 in ordinary savings, using the full cash allowance this year and next is the simplest saving available.

What none of this is, is a reason to put money you need soon into the stock market. Your own time horizon should decide where the money sits, not a rule change.

Frequently asked

Is the cash ISA allowance still £20,000 in 2027? Yes. Budget 2026 on 28 October kept the cash ISA limit at £20,000 from 6 April 2027, reversing the planned cut to £12,000. The total ISA allowance is also £20,000, so you can put the whole amount in cash, split it across ISA types, or use only part of it.

Do the ISA changes apply in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland? Yes. ISA rules are set for the whole UK, so the £20,000 cash limit applies everywhere. The higher savings tax rates of 24%, 44% and 49% from April 2027 also apply across the UK, including Scotland, even though Scotland sets its own rates on earned income.

Can I still transfer from a stocks and shares ISA to a cash ISA? Yes, under the current HMRC rules you can transfer to a different type of ISA at any time. The planned ban for under 65s was tied to the £12,000 cap, which has been dropped. Always use your provider's transfer process rather than withdrawing, or you lose the tax wrapper on that money.

How much savings interest can I earn tax free? Basic rate taxpayers have a £1,000 Personal Savings Allowance and higher rate taxpayers £500. Additional rate taxpayers have none. If your other income is low, the £5,000 starting rate band can cover more. Interest inside an ISA does not use any of these allowances and is never taxed.
