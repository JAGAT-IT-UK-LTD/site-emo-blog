Twenty Unpaid Minutes a Day Is Enough to Put You Below the Minimum Wage

Most minimum wage underpayment is not an employer deciding to pay below the rate. It is unpaid time at the edges of a shift, or a deduction nobody thought about, quietly dragging the effective hourly rate under the line. Here is how to do the sum on your own payslip, using the rates that applied from 1 April 2026.

The short version

  • From 1 April 2026: 21 and over £12.71, 18 to 20 £10.85, under 18 and apprentices £8.
  • The test is your average rate across the pay reference period, not the rate written in your contract.
  • Unpaid working time counts. Setting up, closing down, handovers and mandatory training are working time.

  • Twenty unpaid minutes a day on £12.71 is about £1,100 a year. That is the size of a problem that looks like nothing.

1. The rates from 1 April 2026

These are the hourly rates almost all workers are legally entitled to, in force from 1 April 2026.

Aged 21 and above, the National Living Wage rate: £12.71

Aged 18 to 20 inclusive: £10.85

Aged under 18, above compulsory school leaving age: £8

Apprentices aged under 19: £8

Apprentices aged 19 or over, in the first year of the apprenticeship: £8

Two details in that list do real work. An apprentice aged 19 or over moves onto the ordinary rate for their age after the first year, a change many payroll systems miss because it is not tied to a birthday. And a worker turning 21 moves to £12.71 from the start of the next pay reference period, not on the birthday itself.

What the rate becomes in April 2027 is a separate question, covered in our piece on the Low Pay Commission decision due in October.

2. The sum to do on your own payslip

The legal test is not the rate printed on your contract. It is whether your pay for the pay reference period, divided by the hours worked in it, comes to at least the rate for your age. The pay reference period is however often you are paid.

The calculation. Total pay for minimum wage purposes, divided by total hours worked in the same period, must be at or above your rate.

Use your own two numbers rather than the contract, because three things make them differ:

  • Hours worked is not hours rostered. If you regularly work beyond your shift, the denominator is bigger than the rota says.
  • Some pay does not count. Elements that are not consideration for the work done are stripped out.
  • Some deductions reduce the numerator. Covered below.

GOV.UK publishes a minimum wage calculator, and for anything borderline it beats a rough sum.

3. The unpaid time that breaches it

This is where most genuine underpayment comes from, and almost none of it is deliberate. Working time includes time you are required to be at work and working, not just the hours between the start and end of a rostered shift. In practice that catches:

  • Opening up and closing down. Arriving early to set up, staying late to cash up.
  • Handovers. Overlap at shift change that the rota does not pay for.
  • Security checks and changing into required uniform on site.
  • Mandatory training, including online modules expected outside paid hours.
  • Travel between assignments during the working day, which is working time even though the commute at either end is not.

None of these looks like a wage problem on its own. Together, twenty minutes a day is the difference between being paid the minimum wage and not being paid it.

4. Worked example: Leanne in Blackpool

Leanne, Blackpool. Aged 24, so her rate is £12.71. She is contracted and paid for 40 hours a week. In practice she arrives ten minutes early to set up and stays ten minutes after her shift to close down, five days a week.

What she is paid. 40 hours at £12.71 is £508.40 a week.

What she actually works. 40 hours plus 100 minutes, which is 41 hours 40 minutes.

Her effective rate. £508.40 divided by 41.67 hours is £12.20 an hour. That is 51p below the rate she is legally entitled to.

The size of it. To be at £12.71 for 41.67 hours she should receive £529.58. The shortfall is £21.18 a week, which is about £1,101 a year.

Why this is common. Nobody involved thinks of it as a pay dispute. Her contract says £12.71, her payslip says £12.71, and the twenty minutes is treated as goodwill. The law looks at the division sum.

5. Deductions, and the accommodation exception

Deductions for the employer's own use or benefit reduce the pay that counts towards the minimum wage. The recurring examples are a uniform you must buy, tools you must provide, and till shortages taken from wages. A deduction that takes your effective rate below the minimum is unlawful even if you agreed to it in writing.

Accommodation is treated differently, and is the only benefit that counts at all. GOV.UK is explicit that no other company benefit, not food, not a car, not childcare vouchers, counts towards the minimum wage. It works through an offset:

From April 2026 the accommodation offset is £11.10 a day, or £77.70 a week.

If the employer charges more than the offset, the excess is taken off the pay that counts for minimum wage purposes.

If the charge is at or below the offset, it has no effect on the calculation.

If the accommodation is free, the offset rate is added to the worker's pay for the calculation.

If your sum comes out short, raise it with your employer in writing with the arithmetic attached. Most cases are payroll error, and an employer shown the division sum usually corrects it. If that fails, HMRC enforces the minimum wage and you can complain to it, including anonymously.

The honest verdict

The minimum wage is one of the few workplace rights you can check yourself in under a minute, and almost nobody does, because the number on the contract looks right.

Do the division. Take one payslip, take the hours you genuinely worked including the minutes at either end, and divide. If the answer is below £12.71 and you are 21 or over, you have found something worth raising, and on a full time job the annual figure is likely to be in the hundreds or low thousands. Raise it in writing with the sum attached. The arithmetic is the whole argument, and it is usually an error rather than a fight.

Frequently asked

My contract says £12.71. Can I still be underpaid? Yes, and this is the most common form of it. The legal test is pay divided by hours actually worked in the pay reference period. Unpaid time at the edges of a shift, or a deduction for something like a required uniform, can take the effective rate below the contractual one.

Can my employer charge me for accommodation? Yes, but the accommodation offset limits the effect. From April 2026 it is £11.10 a day or £77.70 a week. A charge above the offset reduces the pay that counts towards the minimum wage by the excess. Free accommodation adds the offset to your pay for the calculation.

What do I do if the sum comes out short? Raise it with your employer in writing with the calculation attached, because most cases are payroll error and get corrected. If that does not resolve it, HMRC enforces the National Minimum Wage and you can complain to it, including anonymously. Acas gives free advice on raising it.
