Best Budget Smartphone in the UK: Cost Per Year, Not Price

Published 21 July 2026 · Updated 14 November 2026 · 11 min read · Figures checked November 2026

The best budget smartphone in the UK right now is the Google Pixel 10a at £399, because it gets seven years of updates, to at least March 2033. If £399 is too much, Samsung's Galaxy A17 5G at £259 still promises six years of security updates. Judge any phone on its cost per supported year, not its sticker price.

That one number decides when your banking app stops working. This guide shows how to compare phones on it, which models win in November 2026, and why buying outright with a SIM only deal beats a contract.

1. What actually matters in a budget smartphone?

Every budget phone review leads with the camera and the processor. Both matter far less than you think.

A modern £250 phone is fast enough for messaging, maps, video and banking. Every phone camera is decent in daylight.

What decides how long the phone stays usable is the security update commitment.

When updates stop, the phone still works. But new security holes never get patched. Over time, banking apps, payment apps and some workplace systems start refusing to run on it.

That is when you are forced to replace it, even if the hardware is fine.

There is good news here. Since 29 April 2024, the UK product security regime has required makers of connectable products to publish a minimum security update period, with an end date, in plain English. So the number is out there. Almost nobody checks it before buying.

It is the single most valuable thirty seconds of research a phone buyer can do.

3. Which budget phones are worth buying in the UK in 2026?

Google Pixel 10a, £399 on the Google Store, in an offer that ends on 1 December 2026. Google promises seven years of OS and security updates. The Pixel camera is the strongest in this bracket because Google does the work in software. Google says the battery lasts over 30 hours. If you keep phones a long time, this is the obvious pick.

Google Pixel 9a, £349 on the Google Store in the same offer. It is last year's model, with seven years of updates counted from April 2025. That leaves about five and a half years from now, which is still more than most phones get new.

Samsung Galaxy A17 5G, £259 for 128GB on Samsung UK. Six generations of OS upgrades and six years of security updates, counted from its first global launch. It is the cheapest phone from a big maker with that kind of promise.

Samsung Galaxy A37 5G, £419 for 256GB on Samsung UK. Same six and six support as the A17, with double the storage. Worth a look if you want more room for photos, but it costs more per supported year.

Nothing Phone (4a), from £399 for 8GB and 256GB on Nothing's own store, or £449 with 12GB. Distinctive design and clean software. Note the gap in its support: six years of security patches but only three Android upgrades. The phone stays safe longer than it stays current.

Samsung Galaxy A57 5G, £599 for 256GB on Samsung UK. A good phone, but at that price it is no longer a budget pick, and it costs £100 per supported year.

4. Is it cheaper to buy a phone outright or on contract?

Buying outright is almost always cheaper. A 24 month contract bundles the handset and the airtime into one payment, and the cost of the credit sits inside the monthly figure.

Contracts can also keep charging the full amount after the handset is paid off unless you act. Set a reminder for the month your contract ends.

Since 17 January 2025, Ofcom has banned inflation linked price rise terms in new phone contracts. Any planned rise must now be shown in pounds and pence before you sign. Read that line before you agree.

If you cannot find £399 upfront, a 0% purchase credit card or a retailer instalment plan on the handset alone, paired with a cheap SIM, often still beats the contract. Check the rate is genuinely 0% and that you can clear it inside the promotional period. This is general information, not financial advice.

5. Is a refurbished phone worth it?

Often, yes. A refurbished phone one generation back can be the best value in the whole market, as long as plenty of its support window is left.

The key is to count support from the phone's original launch, not from the day you buy it. A refurbished Pixel 9a still has updates to April 2032. A refurbished phone from 2021 may have almost nothing left.

What to insist on:

  • A minimum 12 month warranty. Reputable sellers offer this. Anyone offering 30 days is selling you someone else's problem.
  • A stated battery health figure, ideally 85% or above. The battery is the part that ages, and a replacement costs real money.
  • A clear grading scale with photographs of the actual unit where possible.
  • Check the IMEI with a blacklist checker before you use it, to confirm it is not reported lost or stolen.
  • Check the remaining support window on the maker's own page.

Buying refurbished from a marketplace seller with no warranty and no battery figure is how people end up replacing the phone in eight months.

6. Which specs can you safely ignore?

  • Megapixels. A huge headline sensor on a budget phone usually combines pixels to make a smaller image anyway. Sensor size and processing matter more than the number.
  • 120Hz on a cheap panel. Nice, but not worth paying much extra for. A good 60Hz AMOLED beats a poor 120Hz LCD.
  • Benchmark scores. Relevant for gaming. Irrelevant for WhatsApp, maps and banking, which is what most people do.
  • Storage above 128GB, if you use cloud photos or the phone takes a microSD card. Many no longer do, so check.
  • AI features. In 2026 most of them are marketing. Judge the phone on the screen, the battery, the camera in poor light and the support window.

What is worth paying for: battery life, an AMOLED screen, an IP rating for water resistance, and the update commitment.

Myths that cost people money

"Buying the phone on contract spreads the cost for free." It does not. The cost of the credit is built into the monthly figure. It is just not labelled that way.

"I need the latest model." Last year's model often drops in price when the new one lands. On the Google Store today, the Pixel 9a is £349 against £399 for the Pixel 10a. The gap between adjacent budget generations is usually small.

"Cheap phones are fine, I only use WhatsApp." Right up to the point where your bank drops support for your Android version. Then you have to replace it anyway. The support window is the whole argument.

"Cheap always means short support." Not any more. Samsung's £259 Galaxy A17 5G promises six years. The risk now sits with unbranded phones that publish no date.

The honest verdict

Buy this if you keep phones for years: the Pixel 10a at £399 is the best budget smartphone in the UK right now. Seven years of updates spreads the cost further than almost anything else, and the camera is the best at the price. Buy before 1 December if you can, while the offer runs.

Better option if money is tight: the Galaxy A17 5G at £259. Six years of security updates for the lowest cost per year in our table. A refurbished Pixel 9a with a 12 month warranty and 85% plus battery health is also a strong buy.

Do not buy a sub £150 phone with an unstated update policy, unless the alternative is no phone. It is a two year purchase priced like a five year one.

And whatever you buy, buy the handset separately and pair it with a SIM only deal. That single decision is worth more than any handset choice on this page.

Frequently asked

What is the best budget smartphone in the UK? For most people, the Google Pixel 10a at £399. It gets seven years of updates from March 2026 and has the best camera at the price. If your budget is nearer £250, the Samsung Galaxy A17 5G at £259 promises six years of security updates, which is rare at that price.

How do I check how long my phone will get updates? Look on the maker's own support page. Google lists update periods for every Pixel, and Samsung and Nothing state them on each product page. Since April 2024, UK law has required makers to publish a minimum security update period with an end date, so if you cannot find one, treat that as a warning.

Is it cheaper to buy a phone outright in the UK? Usually, yes. In our example, a Pixel 10a bought outright with an £8 SIM only plan costs £591 over two years, against £745 for a typical contract. You also own the phone from day one and can change network whenever a better SIM deal appears.

Is an iPhone better value than these? It depends on the price you pay and on whether you want iOS. Apple, like every maker selling here, has to publish a minimum security update period, so compare it on cost per supported year exactly as in our table. A refurbished iPhone can close the price gap, and our refurbished iPhone guide runs those sums.

What about eSIM? Most current phones from these makers support eSIM, so check the spec page, and most UK networks now offer it. It makes switching provider faster, which helps if you move between rolling SIM only deals. Check your network supports eSIM on your plan before you cancel the old physical SIM.
