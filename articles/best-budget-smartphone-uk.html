1. The only spec that actually matters

Every budget phone review leads with the camera and the processor. Both matter far less than you think, because a modern £200 phone is fast enough for messaging, maps, video and banking, and every phone camera is decent in daylight.

What actually determines how long the phone is usable is the security update commitment.

When updates stop, the phone still works. What changes is that newly discovered vulnerabilities never get patched, and over time banking apps, payment apps and some workplace systems start refusing to run on it. That is when you are forced to replace it, regardless of whether the hardware is fine.

Manufacturers publish this and almost nobody checks it before buying. It is the single highest value thirty seconds of research available to a phone buyer.

3. What to look at in 2026

Google Pixel 9a, around £399. Best camera in the price bracket by a clear margin, because Google does the work in software rather than hardware. Seven years of updates. Battery life is strong. The screen is good rather than spectacular and the charging speed is unremarkable. If you keep phones a long time, this is the obvious pick.

Samsung Galaxy A56 5G, around £349. The best all rounder. Full HD Super AMOLED screen, 50MP triple camera, 5G, and Samsung's six years of updates. If you already use Samsung's ecosystem or you want the nicest screen for the money, this is it.

Nothing Phone 3a, around £329. Distinctive design, clean software, good value. Note the asymmetry in its support: six years of security patches but only three OS upgrades, so the phone stays safe longer than it stays current.

5. Refurbished, and how not to get burned

A refurbished phone one generation back is often the best value in the entire market. A refurbished Pixel from last year at £220 with five years of updates remaining is a better buy than any new £220 phone.

What to insist on:

  • A minimum 12 month warranty. Reputable sellers offer this. Anyone offering 30 days is selling you someone else's problem.
  • A stated battery health figure, ideally 85% or above. Battery is the part that ages, and a replacement is £50 to £90.
  • A clear grading scale with photographs of the actual unit where possible.
  • Check the IMEI against a blacklist checker before you use it, to confirm it is not reported lost or stolen.
  • Check the remaining support window from the original release date, not from when you bought it.

Buying refurbished from a marketplace seller with no warranty and no battery figure is how people end up replacing the phone in eight months.

6. Specs you can safely ignore

  • Megapixels. A 200MP sensor on a budget phone bins its output down to 12MP anyway. Sensor size and processing matter, the headline number does not.
  • 120Hz on a cheap panel. Nice, not worth paying £60 for. A good 60Hz AMOLED beats a poor 120Hz LCD.
  • Benchmark scores. Relevant for gaming. Irrelevant for WhatsApp, maps and banking, which is what most people do.
  • Storage above 128GB, if the phone takes a microSD card or you use cloud photos. Many do not any more, so check.
  • AI features. In 2026 most of them are marketing. Judge the phone on the screen, the battery, the camera in poor light and the support window.

What is worth paying for: battery capacity, an AMOLED screen, an IP rating for water resistance, and the update commitment.

Myths that cost people money

"Buying the phone on contract spreads the cost for free." It does not. The interest is baked into the monthly figure and is typically the equivalent of a 15 to 25% APR, it just is not labelled that way.

"I need the latest model." The previous generation of the same phone is usually 30 to 40% cheaper within months of a launch, and the differences between adjacent generations of budget phones are very small.

"Cheap phones are fine, I only use WhatsApp." Right up to the point where your bank drops support for the Android version and you have to replace it anyway. The support window is the whole argument.

The honest verdict

Buy this if you keep phones for years: the Pixel 9a at about £399 is the clearest value in the UK market right now, purely because seven years of updates spreads the cost further than anything else available.

Better option if money is tight: a refurbished previous generation Pixel or Galaxy at £200 to £250 with a 12 month warranty and 85% plus battery health. Best pound for pound buy in the whole market.

Do not buy a sub £150 phone with an unstated update policy, unless the alternative is no phone. It is a two year purchase priced like a five year one.

And whatever you buy, buy the handset separately and pair it with a SIM only deal. That single decision is worth more than any handset choice on this page.

Frequently asked

How do I check how long my current phone is supported? On Android, Settings, About phone, Android version, and look for the update policy. Google, Samsung and Nothing all publish end dates on their own support pages.

Is an iPhone SE better value than these? Apple's support is excellent, typically five to seven years in practice. The comparison comes down to price at the time and whether you want iOS. On raw cost per supported year they are competitive.

Does buying outright void anything? No. You get the full manufacturer warranty either way, and an unlocked handset is worth more when you sell it.

What about eSIM? Most current phones support it and most UK networks now offer it. It makes switching provider faster, which is useful if you plan to move SIM deals regularly.
