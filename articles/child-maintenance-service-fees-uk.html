The Child Maintenance Service charges both parents, and the money goes to neither of them

Ruhul Amin · Published 13 September 2026 · 7 min read · Figures checked September 2026

Collect and Pay is the option the service reaches for when payments go wrong, and it costs both sides at once. Direct Pay costs nothing. Over a year the difference is larger than most people expect, and it is worth knowing before the letter arrives.

Share Facebook X WhatsApp Pinterest LinkedIn

The short version

  • Direct Pay is free. GOV.UK: the paying parent pays the receiving parent directly and you do not pay any collection fees.
  • Collect and Pay charges both. 20 per cent added to the payment for paying parents, 4 per cent taken off the payment for receiving parents.
  • You cannot dodge it by paying directly once you are on Collect and Pay. GOV.UK says so in terms.
  • Five rates decide the calculation: nil below £7 gross a week, flat £7 up to £100 or on benefits, reduced from £100.01, basic from £200 to £3,000, and a default rate of £38, £51 or £64 where income is not provided.
  • Shared care cuts the bill in four steps, from 14.29 per cent at 52 nights a year to 50 per cent plus a further £7 a week at 175 nights.
  • Maintenance is not taxed and does not affect benefits, including Universal Credit.

1. The two payment routes, and which one you get

Once the Child Maintenance Service works out an amount, there are only two ways of moving the money. On Direct Pay the paying parent pays the receiving parent directly, and GOV.UK is explicit that you do not pay any collection fees. On Collect and Pay the service takes the money and passes it on, and there are fees for that.

When you apply, both parents can say which they would prefer. What decides it in practice is behaviour. GOV.UK says that if the paying parent does not pay in full and on time, the Child Maintenance Service can change the payment method to Collect and Pay. It is not presented as a punishment, but the effect is that a late payment history moves both parents onto the charged route.

Collect and Pay is not just a bank transfer service either. The CMS can take the payment directly from the paying parent's earnings by arrangement with their employer, from their bank account by Direct Debit, or from their benefits or pension.

One thing people try and it does not work: GOV.UK says that if you use Collect and Pay, you cannot avoid collection fees by paying the other parent directly. The fee follows the case, not the transaction.

2. The fees in pounds, not percentages

The two fees are small sounding and they sit on opposite sides of the same payment. The paying parent has 20 per cent added to the payment. The receiving parent has 4 per cent taken off it. Neither of those percentages goes to the other parent.

Kirsty and Dean in Rotherham have one child. Suppose the calculation comes out at £70 a week. That is an illustration, not a quoted rate, because the basic rate depends on gross income and the government's own child maintenance calculator is the place to get your figure.

On Direct Pay, Dean pays £70 and Kirsty receives £70.

On Collect and Pay, Dean pays £70 plus 20 per cent, which is £84. Kirsty receives £70 less 4 per cent, which is £67.20. The difference between what leaves his account and what lands in hers is £16.80 a week.

Over a year that is £16.80 multiplied by 52, which is £873.60. Dean is out of pocket by £728 a year against the calculation and Kirsty is short by £145.60. Both of those are real, and neither is visible in the headline figure on the letter.

Now add shared care. If the child stays with Dean for 104 nights a year, GOV.UK's table applies a 28.57 per cent reduction, which on £70 is £20.00, taking the calculation to £50.00 a week. The bands are 14.29 per cent at 52 to 103 nights, 28.57 per cent at 104 to 155, 42.86 per cent at 156 to 174, and 50 per cent plus a further £7 a week at 175 nights or more. Payments cannot go below £7 a week.

3. How the underlying amount is set

The fee is a percentage of the calculation, so the calculation is worth understanding. GOV.UK sets out five rates based on the paying parent's gross weekly income, which the CMS takes from HMRC rather than from either parent's say so.

  • Nil rate: gross weekly income below £7. Nothing is payable.
  • Flat rate: £7 to £100, or where the paying parent gets benefits. £7 a week.
  • Reduced rate: £100.01 to £199.99, worked out by formula.
  • Basic rate: £200 to £3,000, worked out by formula.
  • Default rate, used where income is unknown or not provided: £38 for one child, £51 for two, £64 for three or more.

Above £3,000 gross a week the CMS calculation stops and the receiving parent can apply to the courts for extra.

Two practical points. The first payment is usually made within 12 weeks of applying, so there is a gap to plan for. And the amount is reviewed every 12 months at the annual review, which happens whether or not anything has changed. If you think a decision is wrong you can ask for a mandatory reconsideration, but GOV.UK gives you one month from the decision to do it.

On the application fee: GOV.UK's How to apply page, read on 13 September 2026, lists what you need in order to apply and does not mention a fee anywhere. We are not going to quote a figure we cannot see on the live page. Check it at the point of applying rather than trusting a number from a secondary source.

The verdict

If you can make Direct Pay work, it is worth real money to both of you, and on the illustration above that is £873.60 a year that stays inside the family rather than leaving it. That is the honest headline.

It is also the part of this that has to be said carefully. Direct Pay requires a level of contact and reliability that is not safe or realistic for everyone, and GOV.UK builds in protections for exactly that reason, including non geographic sort codes and the ability to share bank details through your online account without contacting the other parent at all. If Collect and Pay is what keeps the money arriving, the fees are the price of that, and it is a reasonable price to pay.

What you should not do is assume the fees are trivial because the percentages are small. Twenty per cent on top and four per cent off the top is a twenty four point eight per cent spread between the two parents, every single week.

Frequently asked

Do I pay tax on child maintenance? No. GOV.UK says child maintenance payments will not affect any benefits you and your children get, including Universal Credit, and that you will not have to pay tax on them.

Can I move from Collect and Pay back to Direct Pay? You can ask. Your online account has the option to request a change of payment method, but the CMS decides, and a history of missed payments is what moved the case in the first place.

What if the paying parent will not give their income? The default rate applies: £38 a week for one child, £51 for two, £64 for three or more. The CMS can also approach an employer, Jobcentre Plus, or the paying parent's bank.

Does a night at grandparents count as shared care? The table counts nights the child stays overnight with the paying parent, based on a court order or an agreement between the parents. GOV.UK's factsheet covers local authority overnight care separately.
