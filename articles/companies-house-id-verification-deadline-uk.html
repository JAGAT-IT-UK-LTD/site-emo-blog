1. What happened at the Companies House ID verification deadline?

When this guide first ran in August, it counted down to a November backstop. That date has now passed, so here is what actually happened.

Identity verification became a legal requirement on 18 November 2025. New directors and new people with significant control (PSCs) had to verify from that day. Existing ones got a twelve month transition.

For existing directors, the deadline was the company's next confirmation statement. Companies House's own table puts it plainly: verify "alongside confirmation statement due between 18 November 2025 and 17 November 2026". Every company has to file one a year, so by 17 November 2026 every existing director's turn had come.

On 18 November 2026, one year on, Companies House published its results. The next day it updated its main verification guidance "for the end of the 12 month identity verification transition period". The older page that set out the transition windows now says the period "has now passed".

So there is no countdown left. You are either verified, or you are late and inside the enforcement process.

In total 12.18 million appointments were verified by November. That is 85.55% of the appointments that were due, and 82.94% of all appointments on the register.

Note the word appointments, not people. One person who is director and PSC of two companies counts four times.

At company level, Companies House says around 90% of live companies have complied. 88.1% have met the requirement linked to their confirmation statement. Another 11.4% are on what it calls a compliance or enforcement pathway.

The summer worry was the run rate. At the end of June, PSCs needed close to double their pace to finish on time. They added nearly two million verified appointments in the months after. Not everyone made it, but the gap closed a long way.

The checks also found problems. Companies House says verification led to over 75,000 corrections to personal data and flagged 14,000 inaccurate PSC addresses.

3. Why were PSCs so far behind?

Verification has two steps that people often treat as one. First you prove who you are and get an eleven character personal code. Second you connect that code to each role you hold.

The first step is a one off. The second is per role, per company.

For directors, step two was forced. A company cannot file its confirmation statement unless every director's code is supplied. Miss it and the filing bounces, so you notice at once.

For PSCs, nothing was forced. The code goes through a separate service, "Provide identity verification details for a PSC", on its own clock. Nothing blocks you. You simply fall out of compliance.

That is why the sole director who is also the sole shareholder was the most exposed person of all. They filed the confirmation statement, saw it accepted, and reasonably thought they were finished.

The filing it blocks has its own price, and what a confirmation statement and other Companies House filings cost in 2026 is set out in our fees guide. The online fee is £50, or £110 on paper.

The timing rules that caught people out

Filing early did not move the PSC window. If you were both director and PSC, your PSC window was fourteen days starting the day after the confirmation statement date. Companies House said directly that filing early does not change it.

A PSC who is not a director had a birthday window. The code was due within the first fourteen days of your birth month. Born on 22 January, your window opened on 1 January.

New PSCs still have fourteen days. Anyone added as a PSC now must give their code when added, or within fourteen days of being added. That rule did not end with the transition.

4. I missed the deadline. What happens now?

You are not alone, and it is fixable. But the soft phase is over.

Companies House says it has started targeted enforcement. It begins with reminders and a chance to explain. Continued non compliance can lead to financial penalties, prosecution and referral to the Insolvency Service.

These are not empty words. Financial penalty warning letters began in July 2026. The Insolvency Service secured its first convictions and disqualifications in September for acting as a director with an unverified identity.

Continuing to act as a director without verifying is an offence under section 167M of the Companies Act 2006. Companies House says the company may be in breach too, and it will write to the company.

There is a public cost coming as well. Companies House says that in future it will put notes on the public register where verification was not completed by the legal deadline. Customers, lenders and landlords read that register.

By the end of March 2027, Companies House expects every company to have met the requirement or be on a pathway to compliance or enforcement. In plain terms, the letters will keep coming until you act.

The gap between no penalty and £250 is not skill. It is opening one envelope within 28 days.

5. Worked example: Nadia in Stevenage

Nadia Rahman is sole director and sole shareholder of a small design company. Her confirmation statement date was 12 September 2026.

Being organised, she verified through GOV.UK One Login in late August and got her code. She filed the confirmation statement on 1 September, eleven days early, and paid the £50 online fee. She marked it done.

She was not done. As a PSC her second window ran from 13 to 26 September 2026. Filing early did not move it, and nothing blocked her.

In November the first she hears of it is a letter. It goes to her home address, because Companies House usually sends PSC non compliance letters to the residential address, not the company's.

What it costs depends on what she does next. She logs in, enters her existing personal code in the PSC service, and is finished the same evening. Because she acted within 28 days of the notice, she pays nothing.

Had she ignored it, a first minor offence would cost £250. A second would cost £500. Her total spend on doing it properly: the £50 she already owed for the confirmation statement, and nothing more.

6. How do I verify my identity now, and what does it cost?

Verifying is free, and the routes have not changed. Use the free "Verify your identity for Companies House" service through GOV.UK One Login.

  1. The app. Scan your photo ID with your phone.
  2. Online questions. Answer security questions on the website.
  3. The Post Office. Enter your photo ID details online, download the customer letter, then take that letter and your original photo ID to a participating branch. A photocopy or phone picture will not do. The letter expires, so go promptly.

An accountant or solicitor registered as an Authorised Corporate Service Provider can verify you instead. They may charge, which buys convenience, not compliance.

Never post or email identity documents to Companies House. Its guidance says so plainly, and any email asking you to is a scam.

Then do three checks today:

  1. Look up your company on the free Companies House register. Check your confirmation statement date and whether it was filed.
  2. Check whether you are recorded as a PSC as well as a director. Own more than 25% of the shares or votes and you almost certainly are. If so, make sure your code went in through the PSC service too.
  3. Director of more than one company? Repeat both checks for each. One personal code, a separate connection for every role.

If you genuinely could not do it in time, make a written representation within the period your letter gives. Companies House says it is most likely to accept one where circumstances were exceptional and linked to the due date. An accepted representation usually pauses enforcement for up to two months.

7. What is the next Companies House deadline?

For most small company owners, nothing big until late 2027. Companies House updated its transition plan on 23 October 2026.

  • People who file for a company, known as presenters, must verify no earlier than November 2027, with at least six months' notice.
  • Third party agents such as accountants must register as Authorised Corporate Service Providers to file, also no earlier than November 2027.
  • Limited partnerships, corporate directors and officers of corporate PSCs come later, with no date set yet.

Your ordinary yearly filings carry on as before. If you run your side business through a company, our guide to running affiliate income through a limited company covers the wider admin, and what dividend tax you owe covers paying yourself.

The honest verdict

There is still nothing to buy here. Verification is free and takes minutes for most people. If someone emails offering to "protect your director status" for a fee, that is the fee talking.

Be fair to the numbers too. In June, 41.86% of PSC appointments looked like a mess in the making. By November, 85.55% of all appointments due were verified. Most people got there.

If you did not, act this week. The real risk was never the £250. It is a note against your name on a public register, when one short online filing would have kept it clean.

Frequently asked

When was the Companies House ID verification deadline? There was no single date. Verification became law on 18 November 2025. Existing directors had to verify with their next confirmation statement, and the last ones in the transition were due by 17 November 2026. PSCs had separate fourteen day windows. All of those windows have now passed.

What happens if I have not verified my identity? Companies House will send reminders, then warning notices. Act within 28 days of a warning and there is no penalty. Ignore it and you face penalties from £250, possible prosecution or referral to the Insolvency Service, and in future a note on the public register against your name.

Is Companies House identity verification free? Yes. Verifying through GOV.UK One Login, by app, online questions or at a participating Post Office, costs nothing. An accountant or solicitor registered as an Authorised Corporate Service Provider can do it for you, but they may charge a fee for that convenience.

Do I have to verify again for each company? No. You verify once and get one personal code, and Companies House says not to verify again unless told to. But you must connect that code to every role at every company separately, including the PSC role, which uses its own service.

I am a sole trader. Does any of this apply to me? No. Companies House covers limited companies, LLPs and similar registered bodies. If you never set up a company, nothing here applies to you, however much you earn. Your deadlines are with HMRC instead, and our side hustle tax guide explains when you need to register.
