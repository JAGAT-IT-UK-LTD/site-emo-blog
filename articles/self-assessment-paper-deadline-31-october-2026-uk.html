Paper Tax Return Deadline Missed? File Online by 31 January 2027 and Skip the £100 Penalty

1. The paper tax return deadline has passed. What now?

The paper tax return deadline for the tax year 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026 was 11:59pm on 31 October 2026. That date was about receipt, not posting. A form still in the post on 1 November was late.

If you have not filed yet, nothing is lost. GOV.UK gives a second deadline for the same return: an online return must be submitted by 11:59pm on 31 January 2027.

HMRC's Self Assessment Manual says it plainly. Customers who cannot meet the 31 October paper deadline still have until 31 January to file online. File online by then and no late filing penalty applies.

So the practical answer is short. Stop thinking about the paper form. Set up or find your Government Gateway login this week, and file online well before the January rush.

Online also does the arithmetic for you. It shows your bill as you go, pre-fills PAYE and State Pension figures HMRC already holds, saves your progress and gives you a confirmation receipt. A new Government Gateway account can take a few days to set up, which is the one real reason not to leave it to the last week of January.

2. What if your paper return arrived late?

This is where people lose money. HMRC's manual says paper returns received by post or by hand on or after 1 November attract a late filing penalty. The penalty is issued once the return is captured on HMRC's system.

That penalty is £100, even if you owe no tax at all. An identical return filed online on the same day would have been three months early.

There is one narrow exception for hand delivery. A return found in an HMRC office letterbox when it is first opened on the first working day after 31 October is treated as delivered on 31 October.

If your paper return did arrive late and a penalty letter comes, you can appeal only with a reasonable excuse. HMRC reads that narrowly: serious illness, a bereavement, a failure of HMRC's own systems. Not realising the paper date was earlier does not count.

If you have not posted anything yet, do not post it now. File online instead.

3. What are the penalties and the interest rate now?

The late filing penalties are the same for paper and online. Only the start date differs. From the GOV.UK penalties page:

How lateLate filing penalty
1 day£100, even if no tax is due
3 months£10 a day, up to £900
6 months5% of the tax due or £300, whichever is greater
12 monthsA further 5% or £300, whichever is greater

Late payment is a separate charge. HMRC adds 5% of the unpaid tax at 30 days, another 5% at six months and another 5% at twelve months. Interest runs on top from 1 February 2027.

The interest rate has just changed. HMRC's late payment interest rate is 8.00% from 24 November 2026, up from 7.75% since 9 January 2026. It is set at Bank Rate plus 4 percentage points, and the Bank of England raised Bank Rate to 4% on 5 November 2026.

On £2,000 of tax paid three months late, from 1 February to 30 April 2027, interest at 8.00% comes to about £39 on our arithmetic. The 5% penalty at 30 days adds £100. If Bank Rate moves again at the 17 December decision, the interest rate will follow it.

4. Who can still file on paper without a penalty?

A very short list, now that 31 October has gone.

Returns that cannot be filed online at all. HMRC's manual names returns such as the Trustees of Registered Pension Schemes return (SA970) and the Non-Resident Companies return (SA700). For these, a paper return delivered after 31 October but on or before 31 January is treated as delivered on time.

People who received a late notice to file. If HMRC sent your notice to file after 31 July 2026, different deadlines apply. Your letter gives your date.

People who genuinely cannot get online. No device, no reliable internet, or a disability that makes the online service unusable. Ring the Self Assessment helpline before you post anything. Ask what your deadline is and whether you can get help to file online, because a paper return sent now would normally be late.

Everyone else, which is almost every side hustle, landlord and freelance filer, should file online. It costs nothing and it keeps you inside the deadline.

5. A worked example in Plymouth

Liam in Plymouth is a full-time care worker. He earned £4,800 from weekend furniture restoration in 2025 to 2026, his second year of trading. His employer already collects the PAYE tax on his wages, so his return is only for the side income.

After the £1,000 trading allowance his taxable profit is £3,800. As a basic rate taxpayer he owes 20%, which is £760. Class 4 National Insurance for 2025 to 2026 is 6% on profits above £12,570, so for him it is nil.

What went wrong. Liam meant to post a paper return. He filled it in, then the form sat on his kitchen table through October. On 3 November he found it again.

The wrong move. If he posts it now, it arrives after 31 October. HMRC's manual says that attracts the £100 late filing penalty, on top of his £760 tax.

The right move. He logs into the Government Gateway account he set up last year. His care job's PAYE figures are pre-filled. He enters £4,800 of income, claims the trading allowance, and the screen shows £760. He files on 15 November 2026, well inside the 31 January 2027 online deadline.

The bonus. Because he owes under £3,000 and files before 30 December, he asks for it to be collected through his 2027 to 2028 tax code. That is about £63 a month from his wages from April 2027, instead of £760 in one go in January.

Same tax, £760. Filing online costs him nothing, avoids £100 and spreads the bill over a year. Posting the paper form late would have cost him £100 for nothing.

6. Which Self Assessment dates are still ahead?

24 November 2026. HMRC's late payment interest rate rises to 8.00%. It matters only if you pay late.

30 December 2026. The last day to file online and have a bill under £3,000 collected through your tax code. GOV.UK says the return must be submitted by 11:59pm that day.

31 January 2027. Online filing deadline for 2025 to 2026. It is also the payment date for the balancing payment and any first payment on account for 2026 to 2027. Our guide to what you pay on 31 January 2027 and how payments on account work sets out the sums.

31 July 2027. The second payment on account for 2026 to 2027, if you have to make them.

Two changes sit further out. Making Tax Digital for Income Tax already applies from 6 April 2026 to people whose qualifying self-employment and property income was over £50,000 in 2024 to 2025. From 6 April 2027 it extends to those over £30,000 in 2025 to 2026. The return covered here, for 2025 to 2026, is still an ordinary Self Assessment return.

And the Budget on 28 October 2026 raised the trading allowance from £1,000 to £3,000 from 6 April 2027. That does not help Liam's 2025 to 2026 return, which still uses £1,000. Our Autumn Budget 2026 summary covers what else changed.

7. The honest verdict

If you missed the paper tax return deadline, file online by 31 January 2027. There is no penalty for that, and HMRC works out the bill for you.

Do not post a paper return now. Unless your return cannot be filed online, a paper form that arrives after 31 October costs £100 on day one.

Neither route changes when the tax is due: 31 January 2027. If you owe under £3,000 and want it spread through your tax code, file online by 30 December.

Frequently asked

I missed the 31 October paper deadline. Will I be fined? Not if you file online by 11:59pm on 31 January 2027. HMRC's manual says people who cannot meet the paper deadline still have until 31 January to file online. The £100 penalty applies if a paper return arrives late, so switch to the online service rather than posting the form.

Can I still send a paper tax return for 2025 to 2026? Only in narrow cases. Returns that cannot be filed online, such as SA970 or SA700, are treated as on time if delivered by 31 January. If HMRC sent your notice to file after 31 July, your letter gives a later date. For almost everyone else, a paper return sent now is late.

Does missing the paper deadline mean I pay my tax early? No. The tax for 2025 to 2026 is due by 11:59pm on 31 January 2027, whichever way you file. The paper date only ever brought the filing deadline forward. Interest and late payment penalties start only if you pay after 31 January, at 8.00% interest from 24 November 2026.

I owe £1,500. Can it come out of my wages instead? Yes, if you already pay tax through PAYE, owe less than £3,000, and file online by 11:59pm on 30 December 2026. HMRC then collects the bill through your 2027 to 2028 tax code, spread over the year, instead of as one payment on 31 January. Miss 30 December and you pay another way.

What if my paper return was posted before 31 October but arrived after? The deadline was receipt, not posting, so HMRC treats it as late and a £100 penalty is likely. You can appeal if you think you have a reasonable excuse, and HMRC decides each case on its facts. Keep your proof of posting, because HMRC will want to know what happened and when.
