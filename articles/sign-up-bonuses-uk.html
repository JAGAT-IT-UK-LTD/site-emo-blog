1. Why do companies pay a sign up bonus at all?

Banks, payment apps and cashback sites spend a fortune winning customers. They can give that money to Google and Meta for adverts, or hand some of it to you.

To them the cost is similar. The only question is whether the money goes to an ad platform or to you.

That explains both why these offers exist and why they come with conditions. The company is buying a customer, so it wants proof it got one: a deposit, a spend, a direct debit, or keeping the account open for a while.

Other banks also run offers, and they come and go. Our guide to pricing a current account switch bonus shows how to judge any offer you see.

The Current Account Switch Service moves your balance, direct debits and standing orders to the new bank in seven working days. It closes the old account and redirects payments.

It carries a guarantee. If anything goes wrong, the banks refund any interest or charges you incur as a result. A partial switch is not covered and usually does not qualify for a bonus.

Read the conditions before you start, not after. A bonus you fail to qualify for costs you the switch and pays you nothing.

Two practical points. Switching offers appear and disappear constantly, so check the current market rather than any list written months ago. And if you are about to apply for a mortgage, time it carefully.

Your money is safe while it moves. FSCS protection covers £120,000 per person per banking licence, the limit in force since 1 December 2025.

A word on Klarna. It sometimes offers a small joining reward. It is a buy now pay later service, which is a form of borrowing.

Only use it if you would spend the money anyway and can clear it in full. Do not take on debt to chase a small bonus. That is where these schemes stop being free money and start making the company its profit.

The FCA has been clear about the risks of BNPL. A missed payment costs far more than any joining reward.

5. Does cashback actually repeat?

Yes. Cashback keeps paying because it rewards spending you were going to do regardless. You click through the cashback site first, shop as normal, and a percentage comes back.

  • TopCashback: free to join, pays on shopping, insurance, broadband and switching. It is useful because you were buying those things anyway. The switching category pairs well with our guide to switching household bills.
  • JamDoughnut (code YVQD): free to join, with instant cashback on gift cards for everyday shops. That suits regular supermarket or fuel spending.

Stacked sensibly, cashback on normal weekly spending returns a modest amount every month with no extra work. It will not make you rich. It is close to the only truly passive part of this exercise.

Two habits decide whether it works. Click through before you buy, every time, because a direct purchase earns nothing.

And do not buy things to earn cashback. 5% back on something you did not need is a 95% loss.

A cashback credit card can add a second layer, but only if you clear it in full. Our guide to cashback credit cards does that maths.

6. What are the catches nobody mentions?

  1. Conditions come first, the bonus comes later. Most require a deposit, a spend, direct debits, or keeping the account open for months. Read them before you start.
  2. Some are financial products with credit checks. Opening several accounts in a short window leaves a pattern on your file. Space them out, and avoid the months before a mortgage application. Our guide to free credit score services shows how to watch your file.
  3. Offers change without notice. The figure you saw last month may differ today. Nothing in the tables above is a quote.
  4. Past customers are often excluded. NatWest's offer, for example, excludes anyone who held a NatWest current account on 6 May 2026.
  5. Do not open accounts you will never use. Dormant accounts and forgotten cards are a small security risk. They also breed the forgotten direct debits our no spend guide is about.
  6. Cashback is not instant. It tracks in days, confirms in weeks, and pays after a holding period that can run to months. Treat it as delayed, not as income.
  7. Missed tracking happens. Ad blockers and cookie settings can stop a click registering. Most sites have a missing cashback claim process, and it is worth using.

7. Worked example: Sam's six weeks

Sam, a basic rate taxpayer in Bristol, does "all of them" once

Week one. Sam opens two app accounts, checks the joining offer shown in each app on the day, and completes the qualifying tasks. Sets a reminder for any condition that takes 30 days. Time: about an hour.

Week two. Sam applies for a NatWest Reward account and asks for a full switch. The Switch Service moves the direct debits in seven working days. Sam then pays in a monthly salary of £1,900, which clears the £1,250 condition. Time: twenty minutes. Bonus: £200.

Weeks three to six. Sam joins a cashback site and installs its reminder. Nothing extra is bought. The shopping Sam was already doing goes through it, and so does the car insurance renewal, often one of the best paying categories.

The total: £200 from the switch, plus whatever the app offers and cashback paid on the day. It arrives in stages, not at once.

The tax check. Sam also keeps £5,000 in savings. At an illustrative 4%, that earns £200 interest a year, well inside the £1,000 Personal Savings Allowance. The £200 switch bonus is not added to it.

Then what? Month two produces almost nothing, because the accounts cannot be opened again. Cashback continues quietly at a small monthly figure. That drop is not a failure. It is what a one off looks like from the inside, and it is the moment the "£1,000 a month" posts never show.

8. Is a sign up bonus taxable in the UK?

Usually not, for a personal customer. Bank switch bonuses, app joining rewards and cashback on your own spending are generally treated as an incentive or a discount, not as income.

Savings interest is different. Interest, including any bonus rate on a savings account, counts toward your Personal Savings Allowance. GOV.UK sets that at £1,000 for basic rate taxpayers, £500 for higher rate and nothing for additional rate. Our guide to tax on savings interest explains the rest.

Referral income is different again. Once you earn from referring others and your total side income passes £1,000 gross in a tax year, that is trading income. You may then need to register for Self Assessment.

It is gross, across all your side activities combined, as set out in our side hustle tax guide. Our trading allowance guide covers the announced changes.

Keep a simple record from the start: date, source, amount. If referrals ever become meaningful, rebuilding it later is miserable. General information, not tax advice.

The honest bottom line

Sign up bonuses and cashback are real UK money that most people ignore. Clearing the sensible ones once can put a few hundred pounds in your pocket over a few weeks. A bank switch is the biggest single item, at £175 to £200 on the offers checked today, and takes twenty minutes.

Turning it into a monthly figure depends on referrals, which depends on reach, which takes years to build. Treat this as a smart one off top up plus a small cashback habit. Do it without taking on any debt, and it is a worthwhile use of an afternoon.

Just ignore anyone promising thousands a month for nothing. They are earning from an audience, and the audience is the part they are not selling you.

Frequently asked

Which bank gives £200 for switching? On 20 November 2026, NatWest's own page offers £200 for a full switch to a Select or Reward account. You must pay in £1,250 within 60 days and log in to the app. Anyone with a NatWest current account on 6 May 2026 is excluded. Offers change monthly, so check the page before you apply.

Will switching bank accounts hurt my credit score? A current account application usually involves a credit check and leaves a footprint on your file. One switch is rarely a problem. Several applications in a short window can be, especially in the months before a mortgage application, so space them out and check your file with a free credit score service first.

Can I get a switching bonus from the same bank twice? Usually not. Most offers exclude anyone who has held an account with that bank since a set date or has had a switch bonus before. Nationwide, for example, excludes anyone who has already had one of its switch offers. Banks check, so read the exclusions before you start the switch.

How long does cashback take to arrive? Weeks to months, depending on the retailer's own confirmation period. It usually tracks within days, then sits as pending until the retailer confirms the sale was not returned. Insurance and broadband are typically the slowest categories and also the largest. Treat cashback as delayed money, never as next month's income.

Do I pay tax on a bank switching bonus? For a personal customer, a switching bonus or joining reward is generally not taxed as income. Savings interest is different: it counts toward the Personal Savings Allowance of £1,000 for basic rate and £500 for higher rate taxpayers. Referral earnings from others count toward the £1,000 trading allowance instead.
