1. What is actually suspended, and what is not

The FCA confirmed its motor finance consumer redress scheme on 30 March 2026. It was challenged almost immediately. On 2 July 2026 the Upper Tribunal made an order suspending parts of the scheme, on terms the FCA agreed with the four commercial parties bringing the challenge: Consumer Voice, represented by Courmacs Legal, Volkswagen Financial Services, Mercedes Benz Financial Services and Crédit Agricole Auto Finance.

What the suspension does is narrow and specific. Firms are not required to calculate redress, pay redress, or send communications about compensation owed, in line with the scheme timetable, until the tribunal process concludes.

What it does not do is switch the scheme off. The FCA published a list of retained rules firms must still follow. Lenders must carry on identifying relevant complaints and agreements, gathering the commission and disclosure data they need, and cooperating with the Financial Ombudsman Service on cases already referred to it.

One part actively works in your favour. Lenders must still tell complainants who are not owed compensation that they are not owed it, so that some people get an answer rather than nothing. Two exceptions apply, both themselves part of the legal challenge: cases the firm considers were out of time, and contractual tie cases where the lender relies on the visible links exception.

A pause on paying is not a pause on the clock. The complaint window and the tribunal timetable are two different things, and only one of them is frozen.

2. The dates that still apply to you

These come straight from the FCA's consumer guidance on the partial suspension.

Your situationWhat happens and when
Agreement began on or after 1 April 2014 and you complained by 30 June 2026If you are not owed compensation, your lender should tell you by 18 November 2026
Agreement began before 1 April 2014 and you complained by 31 August 2026If you are not owed compensation, your lender should tell you by 18 January 2027
You complain after those datesIf you are not owed compensation, the lender should tell you within 5 months of receiving the complaint
You have never complained and are never contactedYou can complain to your lender directly up to 31 August 2027

Note what is missing from that table. There is no date on which money arrives. That is the honest position, and anybody telling you otherwise in September 2026 is guessing.

3. Who is eligible and how much is involved

The scheme covers agreements from 6 April 2007 to 1 November 2024 where the lender paid commission to the broker, usually the dealer. You are considered for compensation only if you were not told about at least one of three arrangements:

  1. A discretionary commission arrangement, where the broker could adjust your interest rate to earn more commission.
  2. A high commission arrangement, meaning at least 39% of the total cost of credit and 10% of the loan.
  3. A contractual tie giving the lender exclusivity or first refusal, unless the lender can prove there were visible links with the manufacturer and dealer.

Some cases are treated as fair and get nothing. Commission of £120 or less before 1 April 2014, or £150 or less from that date, is treated as too small to have influenced anyone, as are agreements where you were charged no interest.

On the numbers, the FCA's final policy puts 12.1 million agreements in scope, down from 14.2 million at consultation, with redress of around £7.5 billion at an assumed 75% take-up and an average of £829 per agreement. In roughly one in three cases, compensation is capped.

Worked example: the Hollis family in Wakefield

The Hollises bought a used estate car on hire purchase in 2019. They complained to their lender in May 2026, well inside the 30 June window for post 2014 agreements.

Where they actually stand: if the lender decides they are not owed anything, it should tell them by 18 November 2026. If they are owed something, the lender need not work out how much or pay it until the tribunal case ends. That means a hearing in December or February, judgment some months later, and payments from 2027 if the scheme is upheld.

The number they should ignore: the £829 average. It is a mean across 12.1 million agreements of wildly different sizes. Their figure depends on the commission paid on their deal and which unfair feature applied.

What is worth doing: nothing costly. Their complaint is logged and dated. Chasing it weekly will not move a tribunal.

4. What to do this month

If you have not complained, complain now, and do it yourself. The FCA publishes a step by step process and a list of lender contact details. It is free. Claims management companies and law firms may charge over 30% of anything you get, and the FCA has set up a taskforce with the Solicitors Regulation Authority, the Advertising Standards Authority and the Information Commissioner's Office over poor practice in that market. Do not sign up with more than one firm, because you can be charged more than once.

If you already signed with a claims firm and want out, any cancellation fee has to be reasonable and reflect work actually done. Most claims are still at an early stage, so check the bill carefully. The FCA publishes a template complaint letter for this.

Ignore anyone promising a payout date. If the scheme is overturned, the FCA has said it may instead tell lenders to handle complaints individually with an eight week response window, and that consulting on a revised scheme could push compensation to 2028 or beyond.

5. The honest verdict

If you were waiting on car finance money this year, you are not getting it this year. That is the plain reading of the FCA's own consumer page, and it is better to know now than to keep refreshing your banking app.

Still worth five minutes: getting a complaint on file, because it is free, it costs nothing if the answer is no, and 31 August 2027 will arrive faster than it sounds. Not worth your money: paying a third of a payout that may never come, for a form you can fill in yourself.

The most useful line in the suspension notice is the least reported. Your lender still has to tell you if you are not getting anything, by a fixed date. A clear no is worth having.

Frequently asked

Is the compensation scheme cancelled? No. Parts of it are suspended by order of the Upper Tribunal pending a hearing in December 2026 or February 2027. The FCA has said it will defend the scheme.

Do I still need to complain if my lender might contact me? Firms only have to contact people who have not complained if those people are potentially owed money, or are timed out of the scheme. If you are not contacted, complaining yourself by 31 August 2027 is how you stay in.

Does this cover a car I bought outright with savings? No. The scheme is about motor finance agreements where the lender paid commission to a broker. Cash purchases are outside it.

What if my agreement was a very large loan? Claims for loans higher than 99.5% of other loans in that year are excluded from the scheme, but those consumers can still complain to the lender and go to the Financial Ombudsman Service.
