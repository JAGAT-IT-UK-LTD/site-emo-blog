UK money calculators: work out the sums yourself

Updated 15 November 2026 · Rates checked November 2026

Four quick sums

  • Side hustle tax: do you need to tell HMRC, and what is left after tax, in 2026 to 2027 and from April 2027 when the trading allowance rises to £3,000.
  • Savings tax: what your interest costs in tax now, and from 6 April 2027 when savings tax rises to 24%, 44% and 49%.
  • Payback: how many years before anything you buy to save money pays for itself.
  • Water on a meter: what a leak, a long shower habit or a garden hose costs you a year.

Every calculator runs in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere or saved. The rates come from HMRC and the water company pages linked under each one, checked on 15 November 2026.

These are estimates for a typical case in England, Wales and Northern Ireland. Scotland has its own income tax bands for wages and self employed profit, so the side hustle answer will differ there. For your own position, check with HMRC or a qualified adviser.

1. Side hustle tax: what do you owe?

The trading allowance lets you earn a set amount from selling, freelancing or gig work before tax. It is £1,000 in 2026 to 2027 and rises to £3,000 from 6 April 2027. Above it, you either deduct the allowance or your real expenses, whichever is higher.

How it is worked out: personal allowance £12,570 (reduced by £1 for every £2 of income over £100,000), 20% up to £50,270, 40% up to £125,140, 45% above. Class 4 National Insurance at 6% on profit between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above. These are HMRC's rates for both years. Our own arithmetic, not HMRC's.

2. Savings tax: what will your interest cost?

Most savers pay no tax on interest, thanks to the Personal Savings Allowance and the starting rate for savings. From 6 April 2027 the tax on anything above them rises by four points.

How it is worked out: interest is balance times rate for a full year. Then any unused personal allowance, the £5,000 starting rate band (reduced by other taxable income), and the Personal Savings Allowance of £1,000 for basic rate, £500 for higher rate and nothing for additional rate taxpayers. What is left is taxed at 20%, 40% or 45% in 2026 to 2027, and 24%, 44% or 49% from 6 April 2027. Our own arithmetic, using HMRC's rates. Interest inside an ISA is not taxed at all.

More: savings tax 24% from April 2027: what to do now and the cash ISA rules for 2027.

3. Payback: is it worth buying?

A heat pump, solar panels, a water butt or a new boiler all promise to save money. The test is simple: how many years until the saving covers the cost, and does the thing last that long?

How it is worked out: years to pay back is the cost divided by the yearly saving. Net gain is the yearly saving times the years it lasts, minus the cost. It ignores interest you could have earned on the money and any price changes, so a payback close to the lifetime is a no.

More: solar panels payback, water butt payback and the shower head that pays back fastest.

4. Water on a meter: what does it cost?

On a water meter you pay for every cubic metre twice: once for the clean water and once for taking it away. A dripping toilet or a long daily shower adds up faster than most people expect.

How it is worked out: litres a day times 365, divided by 1,000 to get cubic metres, times your price. The default price is Severn Trent's 2026 to 2027 metered water charge of £2.5503 plus used water at £1.6255 on 95% of the water supplied. Every company charges differently, so put in your own from your bill.
